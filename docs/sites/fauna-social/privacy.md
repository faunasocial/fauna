# Privacy

*Illustration: turtle*

The short version: we designed both this website and Fauna itself so there is
almost nothing to write a privacy policy *about*.

## This website

- No cookies.
- No analytics, no trackers, no fingerprinting.
- No third-party requests at all — fonts, images, and styles are served from
  this site itself.
- No accounts, no forms, nothing to submit.

These are static pages. We learn nothing about you when you read them. The
company hosting the site may keep standard technical access logs (IP address,
time, page requested) for security and capacity purposes, as virtually all
hosting does; we add nothing on top and run no processing of our own.

## The Fauna app

Fauna is built so that nothing of yours rests with us:

- **{legalName} runs no servers for users.** Your data lives on your own nest —
  hardware at home, or a small server hosted wherever suits you. We hold no
  account of yours, no credentials, and no copies. We maintain three servers of
  our own, used to test new releases of the nest software and to let reviewers
  from the app stores try the apps; only one of them, fauna.build, has accounts
  that people sign in to: reviewers and our own testers.
- **No telemetry.** The app sends us no usage data, crash reports, or
  identifiers.
- **Encrypted on your own nest.** Your content is stored encrypted on the
  server you control, apart from what you publish to everyone, which is public
  by design; messages between people on Fauna are end-to-end encrypted by
  default; a group may instead choose to be a community room, which its home
  nest can read. Your nest sees what any server that delivers your messages
  must: who you're connected to — your contacts, whom you follow, the groups
  you're in — and when and how much you send. Beyond what you publish to
  everyone and the mail and messages your nest passes to and from email and
  other networks, what any machine may read is a permission you granted and can
  revoke in the app.
- **Nothing pulling the other way.** Our [bylaws](/organization/bylaws) forbid
  charging users, selling advertising or user data, and paying members — so
  there is no business model quietly pressing against this page.

**The apps.** Fauna's apps send data to seven places and nowhere else. To your
own nest: your handle and your identity's public key; an identifier each
install of the app makes for itself, so your nest knows which of your devices
is online; what you create and the settings you choose — posts, messages,
files, calendar, contacts — stored encrypted there, apart from what you publish
to everyone, which is public by design; the sign-in of an account you link on
another network, which your nest keeps readable so it can read that network for
you, until you unlink it; the age range of your account and how it was
established, where the app carries one, which your nest's admin can see; on a
supervised account, the minutes of use a guardian's screen-time limit counts,
with your device's time-zone offset and, when the guardian has switched it on,
how many items each category blocked, never what; from the web, Android, Linux,
Windows and terminal apps today, a registration so your nest can wake the app
with a notification; and, if you look after a child's account, which of their
devices is yours. To your device's own services: on the web app the browser's
notification service, and on an Android phone the notification app you chose,
which carry those notifications encrypted; on an Android phone the store's age
signal — and, once the iPhone age check and the attestations are in use, those
too — read on the device and sent only to your nest; and, on an iPhone or Mac,
a copy of your identity key to Apple's iCloud Keychain if you turn that on,
which is off by default. When the app rents a server, manages your domain or
imports your old mailbox for you, to the provider you chose, with the
credential you gave it (on the web app, where a provider refuses calls from a
browser, such a call will pass through a relay of ours, which parses and keeps
nothing it forwards); and when you link a Bluesky account, your browser signs
in at the server that holds that account. And, from the desktop and terminal
apps, to the site that publishes our releases — only to learn whether a newer
version is out, when you ask and once at sign-in. And, directly, from the
Linux, Windows and terminal apps to your own other devices, and from those and
the Mac app to the devices of people you share with in person — from the iPhone
and Android apps only what pairing in person itself exchanges, never a file.
And, when you enter a handle or retire a nest — and, on the web app, when it
reconnects to your nest — to a public DNS service (Cloudflare's), only to look
up that domain's records. And, when you load the pictures a message or mail
links to from elsewhere, or open an article from someone you follow or a nest's
web page, to the sites that host those pictures. Nothing stays with us.

The filters you set for posts and messages run on your own device, answer to
you, and report to no one. On a supervised account the guardian's rules run on
the device too, and when the guardian has switched Notify on the app tells them
how many items each category of their rules blocked, never what. Incoming mail
is checked for spam and malware on your nest before it is filed, under
thresholds your nest's admin sets, as every mail server does; your own spam
filter runs where your mail is opened — in the Fauna app, or in your nest's
mail bridge while a mail app of yours is signed in — never at delivery. Where
an admin removes content under a legal obligation, or blocks a reported item,
on their nest, a notice stands in its place, the author of a post can appeal,
and the act is recorded; nothing is removed silently. The [terms page](/terms)
says who can block what.
