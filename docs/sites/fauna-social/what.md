# What you get with Fauna

One app, one identity and a small server replace many walled-in accounts.

## A feed you compose

*Illustration: fox*

Follow people across the open networks. Build feeds from rules you can see and change — not an engagement machine guessing what will keep you scrolling.

## Messages and mail, one inbox

*Illustration: duckling*

A thread is a thread, whether it travels as end-to-end-encrypted Fauna messaging or as ordinary email at your own domain. Apple Mail and Thunderbird still work.

## Calendar and contacts

*Illustration: rabbit*

Served over CalDAV and CardDAV, so Apple Calendar, Thunderbird and your phone sync natively.

## Photos and files

*Illustration: mouse*

A photo library and folder sync across your machines — at home in Finder, in Explorer, and in the Files app on your phone.

## Backups that answer to you

*Illustration: frog*

Snapshots you can browse file by file, kept off-site on another server if you like. A friend storing your backup cannot read a byte of it.

## Your own website

*Illustration: beaver*

Flip a switch and publish at your own domain, from a folder you sync and the posts you choose.

## Safety

*Illustration: owl*

On-device content scanning blocks content transparently. Parents can set rules for their children, and a region's authority will be able to publish rules for its region — always visibly, with the rule named.

## What works where, today

*Illustration: hedgehog*

Fauna is in closed alpha and the seven apps do not all do everything yet. Rather than
tell you they do, we publish [what works where](/features/) — every feature on every
app, marked from the tests that actually ran, blanks and all.
