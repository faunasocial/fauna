# Store review — the tracks, the states and the one record of what each store is reviewing

Status: ratified 2026-10-18 for the tracks, the states and the record (refutable by the owner); the `:test` hold (§ The `:test` hold), proposed the same day, ruled by the owner 2026-10-20 as written (§ Implementation status today); § During a review proposed 2026-10-29, refutable by the owner, two of its points open
Authority: the store review process for the three app stores as one process — which tracks and audiences each store has and which of them pass a review, what a review needs from the organisation and what the organisation does while one is open, the states a submission passes through, and **the one record of every store submission's live state** (`installer/store-review.toml`), written by the session that submits and updated when the store answers, so that "is anything in review?" is answered by reading it. ## Why this doc exists

Asked "is any store reviewing a Fauna build?" on 2026-10-17, the fleet could not answer from any document: a Play build on internal testing was recorded in one doc's status bullet, the absence of a TestFlight upload in another's, the absence of a Microsoft Store submission in a third's table, and whether a later Play upload had happened was recorded nowhere. The question matters because the organisation's acceptance nest follows the `:test` channel and a store reviewer signs in there ([`../org-nests.md`](../org-nests.md) § fauna.build): moving `:test` while a review is open changes the nest under the reviewer. So the review process is defined once, here, and every submission's state lives in one tracked record that a session writes at the console and a gate can read.

## The tracks

Each store serves a build through tracks; a track has an audience, and some tracks put the build through the store's review before it serves. The organisation uses the pre-release track of each store for the alpha and the public track for the first public release; the per-store choice and its reasons are each channel doc's.

| Store | Track (the name the record uses) | Audience | Reviewed before it serves | Channel doc |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Google Play | `internal-testing` | the email list of invited testers (at most 100) | **no** — the release is available to the list within minutes | [`android.md`](android.md) § Goal |
| Google Play | `closed-testing` | invited testers or a Google group | yes — Google's app review of the release | [`android.md`](android.md) |
| Google Play | `open-testing` | anyone who opts in | yes | [`android.md`](android.md) |
| Google Play | `production` | everyone | yes | [`android.md`](android.md) |
| App Store | `testflight-internal` | App Store Connect team members holding a role (at most 100) | **no** — the team's smoke install of a build before it is submitted | [`ios.md`](ios.md) § The channel |
| App Store | `testflight-external` | invited testers or a public link (at most 10,000; a build serves for 90 days) | yes — **Beta App Review** of a version's first build; the alpha audience | [`ios.md`](ios.md) § The channel |
| App Store | `app-store` | everyone | yes — App Review | [`ios.md`](ios.md) |
| Microsoft Store | `private-audience` | the known-user group of invited Microsoft accounts | yes — **certification**, the same as the public track's; only the visibility differs | [`windows.md`](windows.md) § Store distribution |
| Microsoft Store | `public` | everyone | yes — certification | [`windows.md`](windows.md) § Store distribution |

Two of the nine tracks serve without a review, and both are for the organisation's own people: Play's internal testing (the alpha audience on Play) and TestFlight's internal testing (never an audience; [`ios.md`](ios.md) § The channel rejects it for the alpha). Every other track is reviewed, and a reviewed track is where the hold below can bind.

**What a review needs from the organisation — the same four things at every store.** (1) The acceptance nest serving the version under review, so the reviewer meets a nest at least as new as the app — the release order's half (a) ([`../release-table.md`](../release-table.md) § Tag gate and release table). (2) That store's own reviewer account on the acceptance nest — `google-reviewer`, `apple-reviewer`, `microsoft-reviewer` — minted by the organisation ([`../org-nests.md`](../org-nests.md) § fauna.build → *Accounts*). (3) The review-access notes in that store's own form, naming the nest and the handle and carrying the secret only in the console's review field (§ The review-access notes, below). (4) The build graded as the flavour the store takes and the store's declarations matching it — a public text, through its round ([`../public-texts.md`](../public-texts.md) § The rule). What each store's reviewer checks beyond these is that store's policy, read in its channel doc.

## The review-access notes — one text, entered in three forms (approved in public-texts round 7, 2026-10-29; entered by the owner at each store's upload of the version it describes)

The third of the four needs above is a public text (`store-notes-review-access`, the companion of `store-notes-certification` in the same console fields; [`../public-texts.md`](../public-texts.md) § The rule), and its one source is this section. Each console gets the same steps with its own store's handle; the two bracketed lines are per store, the Microsoft Store's form having no password field and the Windows app no lock control. The secret rides only in the console's review field, never here. The handle is written in the consoles in full — the local part, an at sign, the nest's domain — by the owner, who instantiates the template at entry; the tree spells it only in two halves and never holds the joined form, which its own guard refuses.

> **Signing in.** Fauna signs in with a secret key, not a password. The review account for this store is the handle **`<store>-reviewer`** on the nest **fauna.build** (`google-reviewer` on Google Play, `apple-reviewer` on the App Store, `microsoft-reviewer` on the Microsoft Store); the secret key is the 64-character value [Google Play, App Store: in this form's password field | Microsoft Store: on the last line of these notes].
> 1. Open the app. On **Set Up Your Identity** choose **Import from Another Device**.
> 2. Paste the secret key into the secret-key field and press **Import**.
> 3. On the handle screen type the handle — `<store>-reviewer`, an at sign, `fauna.build` — and press **Check**. The app answers *Welcome back* and shows the line **By continuing you agree to the Fauna terms and content guidelines** with the link. Press **Continue**.
> 4. Open **Feed**: it shows posts from our test account *tester*, who is also in your **Contacts** and in **Conversations**. On a post or a message from someone else, **Report post** and **Report message** are in its ⋯ menu; **Report account** and **Block** are on every other account's profile. A report hides what you reported from you — it is not deleted — and unblocking an account also removes it from your Contacts. The **Terms and content guidelines** link is in Settings; **Delete Account** and **Sign Out** are under Settings → Account.
>
> [Google Play and the App Store only:] **Please do not press *Lock for 24 Hours* under Settings → Sessions, nor *Lock my account for 24 hours* on the *Set Up Your Identity* screen:** either, once confirmed, locks the review account for a day and nothing can unlock it earlier. If it happens, tell us in your review response and we restore the account within the day.

*Why these words:* every step names the control as the app labels it (`onboarding.identity_choice.title` and `import_existing`, `identity_import.import`, `common.check`, `onboarding.handle_check.outcome.already_on_nest_handle_matches`, `onboarding.terms.notice`, `common.feed`, `common.contacts`, `conversations.list.title`, `feed.report_post`, `conversations.detail.report_message`, `profile.report`, `profile.block`, `settings.terms_link`, `settings.sign_out`, `sessions.lockout_button`, `onboarding.emergency_lockout_entry` in `i18n/strings/en.yaml`), because a reviewer reads the notes with the app open and matches labels, not concepts; step 2 says "the secret-key field" because the apps label it differently (Android's field is *Secret key*, the Apple apps' placeholder *64-character hex secret key*). Block is on profiles only — no post's or message's ⋯ menu carries it — and the report and unblock side effects are named because a reviewer who tries them on the seeded content would otherwise read its disappearance as a defect (§ Implementation status today, the reviewer-journey bullet). The lock is named because the walkthrough found it to be the one thing a reviewer meets with no text to prepare them, at both its doors ([`../../ui/sessions.md`](../../ui/sessions.md): the Sessions page and its signed-out door); the Microsoft Store's notes omit it because the Windows app has neither door yet (`sessions.md` § Implementation status today), and the warning is added to them when it does. The lock has no admin lift ([`../org-nests.md`](../org-nests.md) § fauna.build → *Accounts*); the restore is the re-creation (*Disposable*), and "within the day" holds even without it, since the lock ends by itself after 24 hours. No step covers the one-tap trust prompt: the offer is made on an account's first sign-in, and the provisioning drive's seeding leg is that sign-in, so a reviewer never meets it ([`../../behavior/onboarding.md`](../../behavior/onboarding.md) § 3b-ter). The secret is never named as a "password": a reviewer who types it into a field called password finds none, and the one place a password-shaped field exists in Fauna (the terminal app's store passphrase) is not it.

## The states

A submission is one build of one version at one store. It passes through these states, in this order where the arrows hold; the record (§ The record) keeps only the current one, and git history keeps the rest.

| State | Meaning | Who writes it |
|---|---|---|
| `built` | an upload candidate exists: built from the recorded public commit, graded, its hash and identity recorded in the channel doc ([`../release-integrity.md`](../release-integrity.md) § Release signing → *A store upload is built from a recorded public commit*) | the session that built it |
| `uploaded` | the store holds the build — processing, or sitting on a track without having been submitted or released; the store texts approved for this version are entered in the same sitting ([`../public-texts.md`](../public-texts.md) § Rulings, 16) | the person at the console, the session recording |
| `in-review` | the build was submitted to a reviewed track and the store has not answered | the person at the console |
| `approved` | the store answered yes and the build is not yet serving (a rollout not started, an invitation not sent) | the person at the console |
| `rejected` | the store answered no; the reason, in the store's words, goes in the note. The version string is spent: a fix ships as the next patch, never a re-upload ([`../product-version.md`](../product-version.md) § Reships) | the person at the console |
| `withdrawn` | the organisation took the build out of the path before the store answered — a discarded draft, a cancelled submission, a candidate the owner set aside | the person or session that did it |
| `serving` | the track's audience can install it | the person at the console |
| `superseded` | a later version serves the same track, or the store no longer offers this one (a TestFlight build past its 90 days) | the session that records the later version |

Only `in-review` is an **open review**. `built`, `uploaded`, `approved` and `serving` are not: no store is deciding anything about them. A build that `serving` an audience under a nest that has since moved on is the testers' problem the release order already answers — the next version's build follows the stage promptly ([`../release-table.md`](../release-table.md) § Tag gate and release table, half (a)) — and never a reason to hold the stage.

## The record

**`installer/store-review.toml` is the one place the live state of every store submission is kept** — one `[[submission]]` table per (store, version), the newest state replacing the previous one in place. It is bookkeeping the tree ships beside `installer/official-apps.json`, so it carries no secret, no address, no tester and no internal identifier; the store, the version, the build identity, the track, the flavour, the state, two dates and a short note are all it holds. Its grammar is pinned by `tests/e2e-unified/tests/test_store_review_record.py` (tier_1: the enumerations above, one table per (store, version), the dates in order, the note free of addresses).

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| `store` | `play`, `app-store` or `microsoft-store` |
| `version` | the product version string ([`../product-version.md`](../product-version.md)) |
| `build` | the store's own build identity: Play's `versionCode`, App Store Connect's `CFBundleVersion` (pinned at `1`), the Microsoft package version (`0.1.3.0`) |
| `track` | the track name from § The tracks, one of that store's |
| `flavour` | the build flavour the recipe names ([`../dynamic-features.md`](../dynamic-features.md) § The App-Store escape hatch → *The store flavors*): `release`, `store-safe`, `store-no-payments` |
| `state` | one of § The states |
| `since` | the date the state was entered |
| `read` | the date of the console reading the state rests on — the readback rule ([`store-listing-contact.md`](store-listing-contact.md) § The readback rule); for `built`, the date of the grade |
| `note` | one or two sentences: the store's own words on a rejection, what was discarded, the audience list's name; never a person, an address or a secret |

**The writing rule.** The session that builds a candidate writes its `built` row in the commit that records the build's provenance in the channel doc. Every console act that changes a state — the upload, the submission, the rollout, the store's answer — is written in the same session by the session guiding the person at the console, from what the console shows after the act, dated in `read`; a reading taken later (an owner's console check, a review answer that arrived by mail) updates `state` and `read` the day it is read. A store with no table has nothing built or uploaded. No session reads a store console it has no access to: the owner's reading is the source, and a state nobody has read back is not written. **`just store-review <store> <version> <state> --read <date> …` is the record's only writer** (`scripts/store-review.py`, its grammar in `scripts/_store_review.py`): it refuses a name outside the lists above, a `read` before `since` or in the future, and a note with an address, and renders the whole file with a digest of its tables in the header, so a hand edit — misspelled or well spelled — fails the tier_1 pin.

## The `:test` hold

**The rule itself is the release order's and lives in its paragraph:** `:test` does not move past a version while a store review of that version's app build is open ([`../release-table.md`](../release-table.md) § Tag gate and release table, half (a) of *The nest precedes the apps* — ruled 2026-10-20 by the owner as proposed). This section owns what that rule reads and what happens when it bites.

**What counts as open:** a `[[submission]]` of the version `:test` currently serves whose `state` is `in-review`, at any store, on any reviewed track. Nothing else holds: a `serving` pre-release build, an `approved` one awaiting its invitation, an `uploaded` one nobody submitted, and every build on an unreviewed track (Play internal testing, TestFlight internal) are not reviews. **When it binds:** only while a reviewer can meet a nest the app under review was not built against — throughout 0.1.x, which owes no compatibility ([`../version-compatibility.md`](../version-compatibility.md) § Dimension 2, the fifth exception), and at every major bump thereafter; within a major version from 0.2.0 on, the compatibility rule makes the mismatch safe and the hold lapses to a courtesy. And only once the acceptance nest serves reviewers at all: the acceptance nest does not exist today — the test nest `test.fauna.build` serves `:test` since 2026-10-20, but a reviewer signs in to the acceptance nest, not there ([`../org-nests.md`](../org-nests.md) § Implementation status today) — so no review is open against it and the rule does not reach a stage before the nest does.

**What the stage does when a review is open:** by default it waits — the next version's first public image dispatch is held until the last open review of the previous version closes, in whichever way the store closes it. **The owner's alternative, theirs alone and in the moment:** withdraw the open review (cancel the submission in that console and write `withdrawn`), stage, and let that version's store release go unmade — the string stays spent, the store ships the next train, and the reviewer is sent the next version's build against the nest that now serves it. The choice is a trade of calendar days against one store's release of one version, which nobody but the owner weighs; a session never withdraws a review and never bypasses the hold. **Enforcement:** the stage dispatch reads the record and refuses while it holds, printing the open reviews and the owner's one way out — `fauna-publish stage <version>`, the only path that dispatches the public `build-nest-image.yml`, the version release's stage step. It reads the version `:test` serves live, off the image's `org.opencontainers.image.version` label, and the record off private `origin/main`, refusing a record `just store-review` did not write; the predicate is `scripts/_store_review.py::stage_hold`. No flag lifts a hold: only a recorded store answer or the owner's recorded withdrawal does. The tool cannot see whether the acceptance nest serves reviewers yet; it needs not, since before it does no review is open against it.

## During a review — the organisation's duties while a submission is `in-review` (proposed 2026-10-29; its two open points ruled by the owner 2026-11-01)

The four needs (§ The tracks) are what the organisation hands over before a submission; this section is what it does while one is `in-review` (§ The states). Each duty is the organisation's practice — the owner's, or a session's guiding them — and each acts on the acceptance nest under its re-creation rules ([`../org-nests.md`](../org-nests.md) § fauna.build → *Disposable*), which bound duty 1 and the re-creations below, and which carry two exemptions for an open review (the last paragraph).

1. **Every report a reviewer files is resolved before the review closes and before any re-creation drops it.** Apple's 1.2 asks for "timely responses to concerns" and Microsoft's 11.12 for a report path "to the developer for review" ([`../../behavior/terms-and-content-guidelines.md`](../../behavior/terms-and-content-guidelines.md) § The stores' requirements against the admin's levers), and a reviewer tests both by filing one: the acknowledgement names fauna.build, and the outcome reaches the reviewer's Notifications only once the nest's admin resolves the row ([`../../behavior/moderation.md`](../../behavior/moderation.md) § User-initiated reporting → *What the reporter is told*). So the admin resolves each one from the reports queue on the admin's Nest page ([`../../behavior/admin.md`](../../behavior/admin.md) § N. Nest, the *Reports queue* section) as *acted* or *dismissed* — dismissed being the honest answer to a test report of seeded content. The admin resolves them through the headless reports pass, which spends no complex connection while the review is open, and runs it at least daily from submission until the review closes, so a reviewer's report is answered within a day.
2. **A reviewer's lock is lifted by a re-creation, the same day the reviewer asks.** A lock has no admin lift ([`../org-nests.md`](../org-nests.md) § fauna.build → *Accounts*); when a review response reports one, the organisation re-creates the acceptance nest, which re-admits and re-seeds every account from its kept file, and writes why in the re-creation record and in the submission's `note` (`just store-review <store> <version> in-review --read <date> --note "…"`). That is the restore § The review-access notes promise in their lock paragraph, approved in public-texts round 7; the lock's own 24 hours bounds it if the re-creation cannot run.
3. **The seeding is more than an hour old when a submission goes up.** The provisioning drive's seeding sign-ins leave each reviewer account's Sessions page with unmarked *App sign-in* rows for up to an hour ([`../../behavior/devices.md`](../../behavior/devices.md) § The client's own session), which a reviewer reads as someone else signed in. So the re-creation every submission follows ([`../org-nests.md`](../org-nests.md) § fauna.build → *Accounts*) completes at least an hour before the submission, never minutes before; a sentence in the notes is no substitute, since nothing sends a reviewer to the notes for it.

**Re-creations during a review.** A re-creation drops every account's content — the reviewer's own posts, the queue's open reports, the reviewer's reports and blocks with the hides they caused — re-seeds the first run, and reopens duty 3's hour, in front of a reviewer who may be mid-session. The organisation's own re-creations are its to time: none runs while a submission is `in-review` but duty 2's, and before it the reviewers' open reports are resolved (duty 1). The calendar's are not: its timer re-creates a day before the weekly cadence lapses, or when `:test` names a digest the box was not re-created on ([`../org-nests.md`](../org-nests.md) § Implementation status today, the calendar bullet). The `:test` hold keeps a new version off `:test` while a review of the served one is open (§ The `:test` hold), so the digest trigger can fire mid-review only on a same-version re-stage; the weekly one holds while the review is open (the last paragraph).

**The two exemptions an open review carries — the owner's rulings (2026-11-01).** **(a) The admin's reports pass.** Duty 1 runs through a headless shared-Rust client that lists and resolves the open reports and nothing else, exempt from the one-connection rule while the review is open — the exemption and the client's bounds are the re-creation rules' ([`../org-nests.md`](../org-nests.md) § fauna.build → *Disposable*); the full admin app still spends the connection. **(b) The calendar holds.** The weekly re-creation defers while the record holds an `in-review` submission of the version the box serves, and its miss check exempts that window (same section); duty 2's re-creation is the only one a review sees.
