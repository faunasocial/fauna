## Why this doc exists

Asked "is any store reviewing a Fauna build?" on 2026-10-17, the fleet could not answer from any document: a Play build on internal testing was recorded in one doc's status bullet, the absence of a TestFlight upload in another's, the absence of a Microsoft Store submission in a third's table, and whether a later Play upload had happened was recorded nowhere. The question matters because the organisation's acceptance nest follows the `:test` channel and a store reviewer signs in there ([`../org-nests.md`](../org-nests.md) § fauna.build): moving `:test` while a review is open changes the nest under the reviewer. So the review process is defined once, here, and every submission's state lives in one tracked record that a session writes at the console and a gate can read.

## The tracks

Each store serves a build through tracks; a track has an audience, and some tracks put the build through the store's review before it serves. The organisation uses the pre-release track of each store for the alpha and the public track for the first public release; the per-store choice and its reasons are each channel doc's.

| Store | Track (the name the record uses) | Audience | Reviewed before it serves | Channel doc |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Google Play | `internal-testing` | the email list of invited testers (at most 100) | **no** — the release is available to the list within minutes | [`android.md`](android.md) § Goal |
| Google Play | `closed-testing` | invited testers or a Google group | yes — Google's app review of the release | [`android.md`](android.md) |
| Google Play | `open-testing` | anyone who opts in | yes | [`android.md`](android.md) |
| Google Play | `production` | everyone | yes | [`android.md`](android.md) |
| App Store | `testflight-internal` | App Store Connect team members holding a role (at most 100) | **no** — the team's smoke install of a build before it is submitted | [`ios.md`](ios.md) § The channel |
| App Store | `testflight-external` | invited testers or a public link (at most 10,000; a build serves for 90 days) | yes — **Beta App Review** of a version's first build; the alpha audience | [`ios.md`](ios.md) § The channel |
| App Store | `app-store` | everyone | yes — App Review | [`ios.md`](ios.md) |
| Microsoft Store | `private-audience` | the known-user group of invited Microsoft accounts | yes — **certification**, the same as the public track's; only the visibility differs | [`windows.md`](windows.md) § Store distribution |
| Microsoft Store | `public` | everyone | yes — certification | [`windows.md`](windows.md) § Store distribution |

Two of the nine tracks serve without a review, and both are for the organisation's own people: Play's internal testing (the alpha audience on Play) and TestFlight's internal testing (never an audience; [`ios.md`](ios.md) § The channel rejects it for the alpha). Every other track is reviewed, and a reviewed track is where the hold below can bind.

**How an invited tester reaches a build on Play's internal testing.** The audience is a list of Google accounts entered in the Play Console; the organisation sends each listed tester the track's opt-in link, which the Console gives on the track's testers tab. The tester opens that link on the Android device, signed in to Google with the listed account, accepts the invitation, and installs the app from Google Play through the link the opt-in page then shows; an account not on the list cannot accept, and the install afterwards updates through Play like any other app. The public install guide states this path in the tester's words (`docs/guides/install.md` § Android).

**What a review needs from the organisation — the same four things at every store.** (1) The acceptance nest serving the version under review, so the reviewer meets a nest at least as new as the app — the release order's half (a) ([`../release-table.md`](../release-table.md) § Tag gate and release table). (2) That store's own reviewer account on the acceptance nest — `google-reviewer`, `apple-reviewer`, `microsoft-reviewer` — minted by the organisation ([`../org-nests.md`](../org-nests.md) § fauna.build → *Accounts*). (3) The review-access notes in that store's own form, naming the nest and the handle and carrying the secret only in the console's review field (§ The review-access notes, below). (4) The build graded as the flavour the store takes and the store's declarations matching it — a public text, through its round ([`../public-texts.md`](../public-texts.md) § The rule). What each store's reviewer checks beyond these is that store's policy, read in its channel doc.

## The review-access notes — one text, entered in three forms (approved in public-texts round 7, 2026-10-29; entered by the owner at each store's upload of the version it describes)

The third of the four needs above is a public text (`store-notes-review-access`, the companion of `store-notes-certification` in the same console fields; [`../public-texts.md`](../public-texts.md) § The rule), and its one source is this section. Each console gets the same steps with its own store's handle; the two bracketed lines are per store, the Microsoft Store's form having no password field and the Windows app no lock control. The secret rides only in the console's review field, never here. The handle is written in the consoles in full — the local part, an at sign, the nest's domain — by the owner, who instantiates the template at entry; the tree spells it only in two halves and never holds the joined form, which its own guard refuses.

> **Signing in.** Fauna signs in with a secret key, not a password. The review account for this store is the handle **`<store>-reviewer`** on the nest **fauna.build** (`google-reviewer` on Google Play, `apple-reviewer` on the App Store, `microsoft-reviewer` on the Microsoft Store); the secret key is the 64-character value [Google Play, App Store: in this form's password field | Microsoft Store: on the last line of these notes].
> 1. Open the app. On **Set Up Your Identity** choose **Import from Another Device**.
> 2. Paste the secret key into the secret-key field and press **Import**.
> 3. On the handle screen type the handle — `<store>-reviewer`, an at sign, `fauna.build` — and press **Check**. The app answers *Welcome back* and shows the line **By continuing you agree to the Fauna terms and content guidelines** with the link. Press **Continue**.
> 4. Open **Feed**: it shows posts from our test account *tester*, who is also in your **Contacts** and in **Conversations**. On a post or a message from someone else, **Report post** and **Report message** are in its ⋯ menu; **Report account** and **Block** are on every other account's profile. A report hides what you reported from you — it is not deleted — and unblocking an account also removes it from your Contacts. The **Terms and content guidelines** link is in Settings; **Delete Account** and **Sign Out** are under Settings → Account.
>
> [Google Play and the App Store only:] **Please do not press *Lock for 24 Hours* under Settings → Sessions, nor *Lock my account for 24 hours* on the *Set Up Your Identity* screen:** either, once confirmed, locks the review account for a day and nothing can unlock it earlier. If it happens, tell us in your review response and we restore the account within the day.

*Why these words:* every step names the control as the app labels it (`onboarding.identity_choice.title` and `import_existing`, `identity_import.import`, `common.check`, `onboarding.handle_check.outcome.already_on_nest_handle_matches`, `onboarding.terms.notice`, `common.feed`, `common.contacts`, `conversations.list.title`, `feed.report_post`, `conversations.detail.report_message`, `profile.report`, `profile.block`, `settings.terms_link`, `settings.sign_out`, `sessions.lockout_button`, `onboarding.emergency_lockout_entry` in `i18n/strings/en.yaml`), because a reviewer reads the notes with the app open and matches labels, not concepts; step 2 says "the secret-key field" because the apps label it differently (Android's field is *Secret key*, the Apple apps' placeholder *64-character hex secret key*). Block is on profiles only — no post's or message's ⋯ menu carries it — and the report and unblock side effects are named because a reviewer who tries them on the seeded content would otherwise read its disappearance as a defect (§ Implementation status today, the reviewer-journey bullet). The lock is named because the walkthrough found it to be the one thing a reviewer meets with no text to prepare them, at both its doors ([`../../ui/sessions.md`](../../ui/sessions.md): the Sessions page and its signed-out door); the Microsoft Store's notes omit it because the Windows app has neither door yet (`sessions.md` § Implementation status today), and the warning is added to them when it does. The lock has no admin lift ([`../org-nests.md`](../org-nests.md) § fauna.build → *Accounts*); the restore is the re-creation (*Disposable*), and "within the day" holds even without it, since the lock ends by itself after 24 hours. No step covers the one-tap trust prompt: the offer is made on an account's first sign-in, and the provisioning drive's seeding leg is that sign-in, so a reviewer never meets it ([`../../behavior/onboarding.md`](../../behavior/onboarding.md) § 3b-ter). The secret is never named as a "password": a reviewer who types it into a field called password finds none, and the one place a password-shaped field exists in Fauna (the terminal app's store passphrase) is not it.

## The states

A submission is one build of one version at one store. It passes through these states, in this order where the arrows hold; the record (§ The record) keeps only the current one, and git history keeps the rest.

| State | Meaning | Who writes it |
|---|---|---|
| `built` | an upload candidate exists: built from the recorded public commit, graded, its hash and identity recorded in the channel doc ([`../release-integrity.md`](../release-integrity.md) § Release signing → *A store upload is built from a recorded public commit*) | the session that built it |
| `uploaded` | the store holds the build — processing, or sitting on a track without having been submitted or released; the store texts approved for this version are entered in the same sitting ([`../public-texts.md`](../public-texts.md) § Rulings, 16) | the person at the console, the session recording |
| `in-review` | the build was submitted to a reviewed track and the store has not answered | the person at the console |
| `approved` | the store answered yes and the build is not yet serving (a rollout not started, an invitation not sent) | the person at the console |
| `rejected` | the store answered no; the reason, in the store's words, goes in the note. The version string is spent: a fix ships as the next patch, never a re-upload ([`../product-version.md`](../product-version.md) § Reships) | the person at the console |
| `withdrawn` | the organisation took the build out of the path before the store answered — a discarded draft, a cancelled submission, a candidate the owner set aside | the person or session that did it |
| `serving` | the track's audience can install it | the person at the console |
| `superseded` | a later version serves the same track, or the store no longer offers this one (a TestFlight build past its 90 days) | the session that records the later version |

Only `in-review` is an **open review**. `built`, `uploaded`, `approved` and `serving` are not: no store is deciding anything about them. A build that `serving` an audience under a nest that has since moved on is the testers' problem the release order already answers — the next version's build follows the stage promptly ([`../release-table.md`](../release-table.md) § Tag gate and release table, half (a)) — and never a reason to hold the stage.

## The record

**`installer/store-review.toml` is the one place the live state of every store submission is kept** — one `[[submission]]` table per (store, version), the newest state replacing the previous one in place. It is bookkeeping the tree ships beside `installer/official-apps.json`, so it carries no secret, no address, no tester and no internal identifier; the store, the version, the build identity, the track, the flavour, the state, two dates and a short note are all it holds. Its grammar is pinned by `tests/e2e-unified/tests/test_store_review_record.py` (tier_1: the enumerations above, one table per (store, version), the dates in order, the note free of addresses).

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| `store` | `play`, `app-store` or `microsoft-store` |
| `version` | the product version string ([`../product-version.md`](../product-version.md)) |
| `build` | the store's own build identity: Play's `versionCode`, App Store Connect's `CFBundleVersion` (pinned at `1`), the Microsoft package version (`0.1.3.0`) |
| `track` | the track name from § The tracks, one of that store's |
| `flavour` | the build flavour the recipe names ([`../dynamic-features.md`](../dynamic-features.md) § The App-Store escape hatch → *The store flavors*): `release`, `store-safe`, `store-no-payments` |
| `state` | one of § The states |
| `since` | the date the state was entered |
| `read` | the date of the console reading the state rests on — the readback rule ([`store-listing-contact.md`](store-listing-contact.md) § The readback rule); for `built`, the date of the grade |
| `note` | one or two sentences: the store's own words on a rejection, what was discarded, the audience list's name; never a person, an address or a secret |

**The writing rule.** The session that builds a candidate writes its `built` row in the commit that records the build's provenance in the channel doc. Every console act that changes a state — the upload, the submission, the rollout, the store's answer — is written in the same session by the session guiding the person at the console, from what the console shows after the act, dated in `read`; a reading taken later (an owner's console check, a review answer that arrived by mail) updates `state` and `read` the day it is read. A store with no table has nothing built or uploaded. No session reads a store console it has no access to: the owner's reading is the source, and a state nobody has read back is not written. **`just store-review <store> <version> <state> --read <date> …` is the record's only writer** (`scripts/store-review.py`, its grammar in `scripts/_store_review.py`): it refuses a name outside the lists above, a `read` before `since` or in the future, and a note with an address, and renders the whole file with a digest of its tables in the header, so a hand edit — misspelled or well spelled — fails the tier_1 pin. In the same act it writes the submission's artifacts into the release table — which states map to which is [`../release-table.md`](../release-table.md) § Tag gate and release table, *The artifacts of a version* — so both files are committed together.

## The store legs — how a release reaches the three stores by itself (designed 2026-11-12, refutable by the owner)

The ruling is [`../product-version.md`](../product-version.md) § Bumping, *A release reaches the three stores by itself*; this section is the one shape the three legs follow. Each channel doc keeps only its store's mechanics and points here.

**Where a leg runs: in the public repository's tag-triggered release runs, its build in a job that holds no credential and every act that needs a store credential as STEPS of the run's one job that already waits on the owner's approval.** Google Play and the Microsoft Store are steps of `release.yml`'s `release` job (the `release` Environment, a Linux runner: both stores are reached over their documented HTTP API); the App Store is steps of `release-macos.yml`'s `sign` job (the `release-macos` Environment, a macOS runner: the upload is Apple's own tool). The store builds are jobs beside the existing build jobs: the Android bundle (unsigned), the iOS app (unsigned, the store-safe flavour), and the two Store packages, for which `release.yml` calls `release-store-windows.yml` as a reusable workflow while that file keeps its dispatch form for dry runs. Their artifacts carry names the publish step never takes for a download, pinned by the workflow tests. **Why steps and never a job of their own:** GitHub asks for an Environment's approval once per JOB that names it, never once per run (read 2026-11-12: its documentation describes the approval of a job and offers no run-wide approval), so a store job of its own would cost the owner a fourth, fifth and sixth click, while steps of the two jobs already approved cost none. The owner's one message at the tag ([`../release-attempt.md`](../release-attempt.md) § The release attempt, step 5) therefore covers the stores with the same three approvals. **Why no fleet machine:** a store credential that can publish rests only where the required reviewer stands in front of it ([`../release-integrity.md`](../release-integrity.md) § Honest residual, the Environments paragraph), so a compromised push machine, or a compromised macOS host, cannot put a build before a tester or the public. The fleet machines keep what they have today: each rehearses its store build through the same `just` recipe the workflow job calls, as for every other release leg.

**Inside the approved job the store steps come last and each one converges.** The downloads are signed and published first, as today. Each store step then reads the store before it acts and resumes from the state the store already holds (this version uploaded: go on to the track; on the track: go on to the review), so a re-run of the job after a red uploads nothing twice and spends no second version string ([`../product-version.md`](../product-version.md) § Version strings are never reused or re-meant). A red store step reds the job, which is a red of the release; its re-run is one more approval, the price of the red. A `workflow_dispatch` dry run performs no store act: the steps check their inputs (the texts, the bundle's version, the signature) and stop.

**A version already tagged is taken into the stores by the same steps, dispatched (owner-directed 2026-11-12: 0.1.6 is the legs' first real run, ahead of 0.1.7).** `release.yml` and `release-macos.yml` take a stores-only dispatch that names the version's recorded public commit, the shape `release-store-windows.yml`'s `commit` input already has: the store builds are made from that commit ([`../release-integrity.md`](../release-integrity.md) § Release signing → *A store upload is built from a recorded public commit*), the approved job runs its store steps alone and publishes no download, and each such run costs one approval. It is how 0.1.6 reaches the stores before the first tag whose run carries the legs, and how a leg that went red after its release's downloads were out is finished without publishing them again. The steps converge here as everywhere: App Store Connect already holds 0.1.6 build 1, so that run goes straight on to the groups and the review.

**How the iOS store app is signed: built unsigned, then signed bundle by bundle by the tree's own signer in the approved job (corrected 2026-11-12, refutable by the owner; the first shape, signing by the exporter, was refuted by measurement — § Implementation status today).** The build job archives `Fauna-iOS` in the Release configuration with signing off (`CODE_SIGNING_ALLOWED=NO`), the store-safe flavour, and hands over the archived app and its debug symbols as one artifact; it holds no credential and no Environment. The `sign` job compiles nothing: a signer in the tree, the iOS twin of the macOS leg's inside-out signer ([`macos.md`](macos.md) § Build Pipeline), takes that app, and for each of the four bundles, extensions first and the app last, embeds the bundle's App Store profile and signs it with the Distribution identity over an entitlements set it states itself — never `--deep`, never the exporter. **The entitlements set of a bundle is computed, never typed:** the keys of the entitlements file the bundle's target names in its Release configuration at the built commit (a bundle whose target names none claims none), plus the identity keys read out of the bundle's own profile (the application identifier, the team identifier and whatever else of the profile's the reference below shows a store signature to carry). A key of the tracked file that the profile does not grant with that value is a red before anything is signed, never dropped: a signature silently missing a claim is the failure this shape replaces. Before signing, the signer also refuses a profile that is expired, that names another bundle identifier or team, or that does not carry the certificate of the identity it is about to sign with. It then packages the `.ipa` itself, reads every bundle back (the signature verifies, the authority is the Distribution identity, the profile is embedded, the signed entitlements equal the computed set, the version and the build are the release's), and hands the package to Apple's own upload tool, which validates it before the upload; the Distribution identity sits in a throwaway keychain of its own that an always-run step deletes. **The reference:** on macOS, where the identity and the profiles are installed, the archive test's signed leg ([`ios.md`](ios.md) § Signing, *What the archive proves*) signs the same commit both ways — at build time and exported by Xcode, and unsigned then signed by the signer — and holds the two equal bundle by bundle, key for key and value for value; a difference is a fault of the signer's computation, fixed there. Apple's validation of the uploaded package is the second reader. `just ios-store-upload` on macOS runs the same two halves the workflow calls (build unsigned, then the signer), so the rehearsal signs what the release signs. **Why not the alternatives.** *Archiving signed inside the approved job* would run the Swift compiler, the project's build phases and package resolution — everything the built commit can make a build do — in the one job where the Distribution identity is usable, when the reason the build has a job of its own is that nothing built runs beside a credential; it would also sign bytes no credential-free job had produced and recorded. *Stamping the entitlements onto the unsigned app with an ad-hoc signature so that the exporter carries them over* needs the same computed set and adds a behaviour of the exporter Apple does not document. The signer costs a script of our own on the signing path, which the macOS leg already has, and both invariants hold as before: no publishing credential outside `release-macos`, no further approval.

**A refusal of permission is waited out (owner-directed 2026-11-12: "Can we not just retry automatically?").** A store that answers a leg's credential with a refusal of permission — Google Play's "the caller does not have permission", which the service account gets until Play has applied the release permissions it was granted, up to 48 hours by Google's word ([`android.md`](android.md) § Signing model) — is waited out by the leg itself: it tries again every 5 minutes, prints each try, and goes red after 20 minutes, a third of the approved `release` job's 60-minute limit, naming the stores-only dispatch above as what finishes it; any other failure is red at once, and a leg that reads the store first makes every try after a refusal safe. A red Play upload does not hold back the Microsoft Store step after it — the two stores are independent and both converge, so one stores-only run finishes both — while the job stays red. A wait longer than one job therefore costs the owner one approval of one stores-only run and nothing else. Every try rides in `store-legs.json` beside the legs (`tries`: the store, the UTC minute, `refused-permission` / `done` / `red`, a note), written red or not; the attempt's `stores` step records each run's count and the minutes from the first refusal to the last try on its step, and the review reports that wait as its own figure ([`../release-attempt.md`](../release-attempt.md) § The release attempt).

**The credentials, each named once and resting only in its Environment.** A credential that can write to a store is never on a dev machine, in a repository or in a session's reach; the owner mints it in a browser on the host and enters it as an Environment secret.

| Store | The write credential | What it must be allowed | Rests in | The reader the fleet keeps |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| App Store | a second App Store Connect API key (Team key), role **App Manager** | upload a build, manage the TestFlight groups' builds, submit to Beta App Review and App Review (Apple's help names Account Holder, Admin or App Manager for a submission and for external testers; the Developer role is not among them — read 2026-11-12) | `release-macos`, with the Apple Distribution identity (`.p12` and its password) and the four App Store profiles the signing step needs | the existing Developer-role key on macOS (`just ios-store-builds`), which also stays the notarization key |
| Google Play | a service account, invited in the Play Console with no account permission and, on the Fauna app, the two release permissions *Release apps to testing tracks* and *Release to production, exclude devices, and use Play App Signing* — with which the console forces four more that cannot be unticked (as granted 2026-11-12): *View app information* and *View app quality information* (both read-only), *Manage policy declarations* and *Manage deep links*; *Manage testing tracks and edit tester lists* is not granted | upload a bundle, release on a testing track, promote to production | `release`, with the upload keystore and its password (the signing step) | the read-only service account on Linux |
| Microsoft Store | a Microsoft Entra application associated with the Partner Center account in the **Manager** role (the role Microsoft's submission API documentation requires — read 2026-11-12): tenant id, client id, client secret | create, fill and submit a submission | `release` | none with a credential: the public catalogue reads `live`; the private audience is read on Windows by the Store client of an audience member ([`../packaging-witnesses.md`](../packaging-witnesses.md), the Windows Store install) |

Three consequences. The Android upload keystore is copied into the `release` Environment with the service account's key, and its copy on Linux is removed once the Play leg's first run is green (it is recoverable through Google and is no publishing credential by itself, so Linux keeps it for the hand-run uploads until then; the owner's backup is unchanged). The Entra client secret expires on the date chosen at its creation, at most two years on: [`windows.md`](windows.md) records the date and a watch row renews it a month ahead. And the reader on macOS is more than a reader: a Developer-role key can upload a build and manage internal testers, so a compromised macOS can put a build before the team's own internal group and nobody else; a key of a role that reads builds and cannot upload replaces it if Apple offers one.

**How far each leg goes, and which state it records.** `furthest` is one tracked setting per store, `installer/store-legs.toml` (`testing` or `public`); `public` is written only on the owner's word for that store's first public release, and every 0.1.x release is `testing`.

| Store | At every release (`testing`) | When `furthest = "public"` | What the store does by itself afterwards |
|---|---|---|---|
| Google Play | sign the bundle with the upload key (the JDK's own signer), upload it in an edit, release it on `internal-testing` with the version's notes, commit the edit: **`live-test`** within minutes, no review | a `production` release of the same version code at full rollout: **`review`** | publishes on approval (managed publishing stays off): **`live`** |
| App Store | sign the four bundles and package the `.ipa`, upload with Apple's own tool, wait for processing (bounded; a build still processing at the bound is a red and the re-run resumes), attach the build to the internal group and to the external group with the version's *What to Test*, submit it to Beta App Review: **`review`** — only when the acceptance nest answers from where the step runs, outside its firewall (a reviewer signs in there, and a review sent while it cannot be reached is rejected and the version string spent — [`../org-nests.md`](../org-nests.md) § fauna.build, *Exposure*; the step only reads, one bounded unauthenticated request); otherwise the step submits nothing, records that it waits (the external track `uploaded`, its note naming the acceptance nest), and the job's re-run after the owner lifts the firewall submits | an App Store version with that build and the version's *What's New*, submitted to App Review with release after approval — only when `:latest` already serves the version (read from the public registry; otherwise the step records that it waits, and the job's re-run after the promote submits) | serves the external group on approval: **`live-test`**; releases the public version on approval: **`live`** |
| Microsoft Store | a submission cloned from the last published one: the two packages, the visibility unchanged (private audience), the version's *What's new* in the listing, publish as soon as certification passes, submit: **`review`** | the same submission with the visibility set to public | publishes on certification: **`live-test`** (private audience) or **`live`** (public) |

**What stays a human's, named exactly — each done once, and again only when the store changes its question; none per release.** At every store: accepting the store's own developer agreement when the store revises it (a store refuses uploads until the account holder accepts; the leg's red quotes the store's refusal and that is the ask), and deciding who the testers are (the lists live in the consoles only — § The tracks, [`ios.md`](ios.md) § The channel). **App Store:** the age-rating questionnaire, the App Privacy answers and the export-compliance attestation ([`../export-compliance.md`](../export-compliance.md)), each the organisation's representative's statement. **Google Play:** the *App content* declarations (data safety, content rating, target audience, app access with the review account). **Microsoft Store:** the FIRST submission, with its age-rating questionnaire — Microsoft's API serves only an app that already has a published submission (read 2026-11-12) — and the membership of the known-user group. The review account's secret is entered by the owner in each console's own review field once, where the store keeps it across builds (§ The review-access notes); a leg never holds or sends it. Everything else a console visit did for an upload — the upload, the track, the notes, the submit — is the leg's.

**The texts a store shows come from the tree at the tag, and a leg refuses without them.** The per-release texts — *What's new* (Play, the Microsoft Store, the App Store's public version) and *What to Test* (TestFlight) — are files under `installer/store-texts/<store>/`, landed by the version's public-texts round ([`../public-texts.md`](../public-texts.md)) and listed in the inventory; each names the version it was approved for, or says it is version-neutral. A store step whose text is missing, or approved for another version, is red before it touches the store. No leg composes, shortens or translates a text.

**No third-party tool is on the path.** Each store is reached by Apple's own upload tool or by the store's documented HTTP API from the tree's own scripts, with the token code the read-only readers already use; signing is the platform's own signer. A need for anything else is a queue entry of the bump round, never an install.

**The record: the leg reports, the attempt writes.** A job of the public repository cannot write this record, so each store step adds its result to one run artifact, `store-legs.json` — store, version, build, track, the state reached, the stamp of the store's own reading, the store's identifiers for the upload; no secret, no address, no tester. The attempt's `stores` step ([`../release-attempt.md`](../release-attempt.md) § The release attempt) reads it by its fixed grammar, as data and never as instructions, and writes each state through `just store-review`, which stays the record's only writer (§ The record) and writes the release table in the same act. The states a store reaches afterwards (a review's answer, the serving) are read by the fleet's readers above on the Versions reading's period and written the same way, so no session remembers to look and nobody relays a console: the hook is a pass of the fleet broker's readings loop, whose registry holds one reader per store audience with the tracks it reads and the machine it runs on — each a store build row's work, added in the commit that lands it — and a reader's answer is one leg in the legs' grammar, refused whole when it is off it or names another submission; the same reading twice writes nothing. The broker's pass runs the readers registered for any machine and for its own, Linux; a reader registered by another machine's name — the Microsoft Store's private audience on Windows, the App Store's reader on macOS — runs in that machine's own service agent, in a thread of its own, and lands its writes the same way, so no reader runs twice or where it was not registered. That pass reads every fifteen minutes rather than every minute: a reading there may start a process (on Windows, a PowerShell of about six seconds), and a store moves in hours — a review's answer, or the Store's automatic update of an app already installed. § The states' *who writes it* column is therefore the leg and the readers for every act a leg performs, and stays the person at the console only for the acts named as a human's above.

## The `:test` hold

**The rule itself is the release order's and lives in its paragraph:** `:test` does not move past a version while a store review of that version's app build is open ([`../release-table.md`](../release-table.md) § Tag gate and release table, half (a) of *The nest precedes the apps* — ruled 2026-10-20 by the owner as proposed). This section owns what that rule reads and what happens when it bites.

**What counts as open:** a `[[submission]]` of the version `:test` currently serves whose `state` is `in-review`, at any store, on any reviewed track. Nothing else holds: a `serving` pre-release build, an `approved` one awaiting its invitation, an `uploaded` one nobody submitted, and every build on an unreviewed track (Play internal testing, TestFlight internal) are not reviews. **When it binds:** only while a reviewer can meet a nest the app under review was not built against — throughout 0.1.x, which owes no compatibility ([`../version-compatibility.md`](../version-compatibility.md) § Dimension 2, the fifth exception), and at every major bump thereafter; within a major version from 0.2.0 on, the compatibility rule makes the mismatch safe and the hold lapses to a courtesy. And only once the acceptance nest serves reviewers at all: fauna.build exists since 2026-10-23 but stays behind its cloud firewall until the owner lifts it ([`../org-nests.md`](../org-nests.md) § Implementation status today), so no reviewer can sign in yet, no review is open against it, and the rule does not reach a stage before the nest serves.

**What the stage does when a review is open:** by default it waits — the next version's first public image dispatch is held until the last open review of the previous version closes, in whichever way the store closes it. **The owner's alternative, theirs alone and in the moment:** withdraw the open review (cancel the submission in that console and write `withdrawn`), stage, and let that version's store release go unmade — the string stays spent, the store ships the next train, and the reviewer is sent the next version's build against the nest that now serves it. The choice is a trade of calendar days against one store's release of one version, which nobody but the owner weighs; a session never withdraws a review and never bypasses the hold. **Enforcement:** the stage dispatch reads the record and refuses while it holds, printing the open reviews and the owner's one way out — `fauna-publish stage <version>`, the only path that dispatches the public `build-nest-image.yml`, the version release's stage step. It reads the version `:test` serves live, off the image's `org.opencontainers.image.version` label, and the record off private `origin/main`, refusing a record `just store-review` did not write; the predicate is `scripts/_store_review.py::stage_hold`. No flag lifts a hold: only a recorded store answer or the owner's recorded withdrawal does. The tool cannot see whether the acceptance nest serves reviewers yet; it needs not, since before it does no review is open against it.

## During a review — the organisation's duties while a submission is `in-review` (proposed 2026-10-29; its two open points ruled by the owner 2026-11-01)

The four needs (§ The tracks) are what the organisation hands over before a submission; this section is what it does while one is `in-review` (§ The states). Each duty is the organisation's practice — the owner's, or a session's guiding them — and each acts on the acceptance nest under its re-creation rules ([`../org-nests.md`](../org-nests.md) § fauna.build → *Disposable*), which bound duty 1 and the re-creations below, and which carry two exemptions for an open review (the last paragraph).

1. **Every report a reviewer files is resolved before the review closes and before any re-creation drops it.** Apple's 1.2 asks for "timely responses to concerns" and Microsoft's 11.12 for a report path "to the developer for review" ([`../../behavior/terms-and-content-guidelines.md`](../../behavior/terms-and-content-guidelines.md) § The stores' requirements against the admin's levers), and a reviewer tests both by filing one: the acknowledgement names fauna.build, and the outcome reaches the reviewer's Notifications only once the nest's admin resolves the row ([`../../behavior/moderation.md`](../../behavior/moderation.md) § User-initiated reporting → *What the reporter is told*). So the admin resolves each one from the reports queue on the admin's Nest page ([`../../behavior/admin.md`](../../behavior/admin.md) § N. Nest, the *Reports queue* section) as *acted* or *dismissed* — dismissed being the honest answer to a test report of seeded content. The admin resolves them through the headless reports pass, which spends no complex connection while the review is open, and runs it at least daily from submission until the review closes, so a reviewer's report is answered within a day.
2. **A reviewer's lock is lifted by a re-creation, the same day the reviewer asks.** A lock has no admin lift ([`../org-nests.md`](../org-nests.md) § fauna.build → *Accounts*); when a review response reports one, the organisation re-creates the acceptance nest, which re-admits and re-seeds every account from its kept file, and writes why in the re-creation record and in the submission's `note` (`just store-review <store> <version> in-review --read <date> --note "…"`). That is the restore § The review-access notes promise in their lock paragraph, approved in public-texts round 7; the lock's own 24 hours bounds it if the re-creation cannot run.
3. **The seeding is more than an hour old when a submission goes up.** The provisioning drive's seeding sign-ins leave each reviewer account's Sessions page with unmarked *App sign-in* rows for up to an hour ([`../../behavior/devices.md`](../../behavior/devices.md) § The client's own session), which a reviewer reads as someone else signed in. So the re-creation every submission follows ([`../org-nests.md`](../org-nests.md) § fauna.build → *Accounts*) completes at least an hour before the submission, never minutes before; a sentence in the notes is no substitute, since nothing sends a reviewer to the notes for it.

**Re-creations during a review.** A re-creation drops every account's content — the reviewer's own posts, the queue's open reports, the reviewer's reports and blocks with the hides they caused — re-seeds the first run, and reopens duty 3's hour, in front of a reviewer who may be mid-session. The organisation's own re-creations are its to time: none runs while a submission is `in-review` but duty 2's, and before it the reviewers' open reports are resolved (duty 1). The calendar's are not: its timer re-creates a day before the weekly cadence lapses, or when `:test` names a digest the box was not re-created on ([`../org-nests.md`](../org-nests.md) § Implementation status today, the calendar bullet). The `:test` hold keeps a new version off `:test` while a review of the served one is open (§ The `:test` hold), so the digest trigger can fire mid-review only on a same-version re-stage; the weekly one holds while the review is open (the last paragraph).

**The two exemptions an open review carries — the owner's rulings (2026-11-01).** **(a) The admin's reports pass.** Duty 1 runs through a headless shared-Rust client that lists and resolves the open reports and nothing else, exempt from the one-connection rule while the review is open — the exemption and the client's bounds are the re-creation rules' ([`../org-nests.md`](../org-nests.md) § fauna.build → *Disposable*); the full admin app still spends the connection. **(b) The calendar holds.** The weekly re-creation defers while the record holds an `in-review` submission of the version the box serves, and its miss check exempts that window (same section); duty 2's re-creation is the only one a review sees.
