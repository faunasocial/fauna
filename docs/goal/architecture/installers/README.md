## The channel contract

Getting the app is **one promise fulfilled through a different channel per platform: getting the app the way your platform provides it — served by your nest in the browser, installed on a computer, installed from the store on a phone — ends with the app open and you signed in to your account.** Which channel a platform uses, and how it works, is per-platform mechanism owned by the per-platform docs below; the promise itself is uniform across all seven apps, stated once here, and witnessed per platform by that platform's own obtain-then-drive test.

A platform without a channel yet does not weaken the contract — it simply has no witness, and the feature catalog's column for it stays honestly short until the channel and its test exist. Nothing here is a per-platform behavior fork: the differences between channels end the moment the app is open.

## Knowing a newer version is out

**The promise (ratified 2026-09-26, user-ruled): on a platform where you install the app yourself, you can ask the app whether a newer version is out, and when one is, it says so and where to get it.** **Amended by the user 2026-10-03: asking is the floor, and the app also looks once each time you sign in and only tells you.** So an app that checks does two things and no more: it answers when you ask, and once per sign-in it learns whether a newer version is out and shows the same notice. It never polls on a timer, never downloads an update and never replaces itself (the `fauna-update` loop is the nest's and the sync binaries', not an app's) — the 2026-09-26 sentence "the check is yours to trigger — never a background poll" is superseded by this one in exactly that respect: one unasked look per sign-in is allowed, because a person who never opens Settings must still learn of a security fix. The four self-installed apps behave alike: no desktop differs. **Where the unasked look reads from:** the target is the nest the app has just signed in to, which already follows the release channel, so the look contacts no third party; until the nest carries that field it reads the release host as the asked check does. The asked check always reads the release host directly. And and what counts as *newer* is the one shared `fauna_core::version::is_newer` on every app that checks, so no two apps can disagree about it. Where the door sits (a Settings → General row, the app menu) and what the check reads are per-platform mechanism, owned per platform: [`../apps/linux.md`](../apps/linux.md) § Update check, [`../apps/windows.md`](../apps/windows.md) § Auto-Updates, [`../apps/macos.md`](../apps/macos.md) § Tech Stack (Sparkle's user-initiated check, whose `.dmg` channel is [`macos.md`](macos.md)), [`../apps/tui.md`](../apps/tui.md) § Update check. What the feed looks like and what "newer" means are decided once, in `fauna_core::version` (`latest_release_api_url`, `newer_release_from_latest_json`, `release_page_url`), never per app; the round trip and the once-per-sign-in look are likewise one shared entry point, `fauna_client::update_look` (`fetch_latest_release`, `look_at_sign_in_over_http`), which each app calls from its post-sign-in hook — so when the nest carries the newest version, that entry point is the one place that changes.

**Scope — a desktop promise, drawn by who delivers updates.** **linux, windows and macOS owe it.** linux and windows have it today; macOS carries the wiring — the app-menu item over Sparkle — which cannot find a newer version until its appcast feed is live ([`macos.md`](macos.md) § Upgrade → *Desktop App*, § Implementation status today). **The terminal app owes it since it has a channel, and has it (2026-09-26)**: a notice that says "get it here" needs a *here*, which the archives [`tui.md`](tui.md) ratified now are — the release page the notice names; the door is the Settings root's About block ([`../apps/tui.md`](../apps/tui.md) § Update check). **Absent by design on web, iOS and Android, declared here:** the web app is served by your nest and is always the nest's own version, so there is nothing for it to be newer than (a newer *nest* is the nest's upgrade story, not the app's); the two phone apps are updated by their stores, which own the notice. On those three the *behaviour itself* is absent, which is the citation the catalog's `absences` requires; [`../apps/web.md`](../apps/web.md) § Implementation status today, [`../apps/ios.md`](../apps/ios.md) § Key Differences from macOS and [`../apps/android.md`](../apps/android.md) § Key Differences from iOS index the absence.

## Implementation status today

The contract holds today on web (served), macOS, Windows and Linux (package installs) and tui (the archive channel, built 2026-09-26 and witnessed by its install-then-drive test; its first artifact ships with the first tagged release); Android's store record exists with nothing uploaded, and iOS's channel — TestFlight for invited testers, then the App Store — is ratified with nothing uploaded ([`ios.md`](ios.md) § Implementation status today). The current per-channel facts are owned by [`../feature-catalog.md`](../feature-catalog.md) § Columns today, and the per-column witness state is computed by `just features-parity` — neither is restated here.

**The update notice is built on linux, windows, macOS and tui, and witnessed on tui (2026-09-26) and linux (2026-10-05)**: the catalog's [`app-version-and-updates`](../../../features/app-version-and-updates.md) outcome 2 cites § Knowing a newer version is out and is walked by `tests/e2e-unified/tests/test_app_version_and_updates.py` against a harness-served release feed (the compile-gated `FAUNA_E2E_RELEASE_FEED_URL` seam — only the feed's origin is stubbed; the endpoint path, the JSON shape and the semver rule are the shared production ones). Linux paints the three approved ids on Settings → General, its notice drawn by both the asked check and the sign-in look, and reaches the feed through the same compile-gated seam. Windows and macOS carry a check but no ids, and neither is a paint away: windows has no version row and its check keeps a feed URL of its own, macOS shows no version and its check is Sparkle — so the walk skips both as unbuilt until each is built over the shared FFI face for `fauna_client::update_look` — built 2026-10-05: `fauna-ffi`'s `version` module exports the asked check (`check_for_newer_release`, its three answers newer / up to date / failed), the sign-in look (`look_at_sign_in`) and the production feed origin (`release_feed_origin`), the same functions tui and linux call directly. **Its three declared absences are applied on the page (2026-09-26):** they are outcome-level (the page's outcome 1, the version row, is owed on every app), so they ride the per-outcome absence ([`../feature-catalog.md`](../feature-catalog.md) § The coverage contract, built the same day) rather than a split — `web (outcome 2)`, `ios (outcome 2)` and `android (outcome 2)` cite this section, with one `outcome-absence-added` record each naming the 2026-09-26 approval, so those three columns are judged on the version row alone.

**The once-per-sign-in look is built on linux and tui through the one shared entry point, witnessed on tui, and macOS is wired past the rule (ruled 2026-10-03; raised 2026-10-01; tui and the shared entry point built 2026-10-05).** § Knowing a newer version is out now allows one unasked, notify-only look per sign-in on every app that checks. Linux and the terminal app have it, both through `fauna_client::update_look` ([`../apps/linux.md`](../apps/linux.md) and [`../apps/tui.md`](../apps/tui.md) § Update check), and `test_app_version_and_updates.py` walks it on tui and linux in both directions. Windows checks only when asked and owes the sign-in look. The macOS leg is Sparkle, configured to check by itself, download in the background and replace the app in place, with a Settings toggle for each ([`macos.md`](macos.md) § Upgrade → *Desktop App*; its feed is not live, so nothing runs yet): it owes the removal of automatic download, in-place replacement and both toggles, keeping the asked check and the sign-in notice. No nest field carries the newest version yet, so every unasked look reads the release host. The catalog's outcome still claims only the ask: the sign-in outcome needs web, iOS and Android declared absent from it, which waits on the user's approval of that absence. The windows and macOS sections are corrected as each leg lands. Tracked internally.
