## The channel contract

Getting the app is **one promise fulfilled through a different channel per platform: getting the app the way your platform provides it — served by your nest in the browser, installed on a computer, installed from the store on a phone — ends with the app open and you signed in to your account.** Which channel a platform uses, and how it works, is per-platform mechanism owned by the per-platform docs below; the promise itself is uniform across all seven apps, stated once here, and witnessed per platform by that platform's own obtain-then-drive test.

A platform without a channel yet does not weaken the contract — it simply has no witness, and the feature catalog's column for it stays honestly short until the channel and its test exist. Nothing here is a per-platform behavior fork: the differences between channels end the moment the app is open.

## Knowing a newer version is out

**The promise (ratified 2026-09-26, user-ruled): on a platform where you install the app yourself, you can ask the app whether a newer version is out, and when one is, it says so and where to get it.** **Amended by the user 2026-10-03: asking is the floor, and the app also looks once each time you sign in and only tells you.** So an app that checks does two things and no more: it answers when you ask, and once per sign-in it learns whether a newer version is out and shows the same notice. It never polls on a timer, never downloads an update and never replaces itself (the `fauna-update` loop is the nest's and the sync binaries', not an app's) — the 2026-09-26 sentence "the check is yours to trigger — never a background poll" is superseded by this one in exactly that respect: one unasked look per sign-in is allowed, because a person who never opens Settings must still learn of a security fix. The four self-installed apps behave alike: no desktop differs. **Where the unasked look reads from:** the target is the nest the app has just signed in to, which already follows the release channel, so the look contacts no third party; until the nest carries that field it reads the release host as the asked check does. The asked check always reads the release host directly. And and what counts as *newer* is the one shared `fauna_core::version::is_newer` on every app that checks, so no two apps can disagree about it. Where the door sits (a Settings → General row, the app menu) and what the check reads are per-platform mechanism, owned per platform: [`../apps/linux.md`](../apps/linux.md) § Update check, [`../apps/windows.md`](../apps/windows.md) § Auto-Updates, [`../apps/macos.md`](../apps/macos.md) § Tech Stack (a Settings → General row, with the app menu as a second door; upgrading is [`macos.md`](macos.md) § Upgrade → *Desktop App*), [`../apps/tui.md`](../apps/tui.md) § Update check. What the feed looks like and what "newer" means are decided once, in `fauna_core::version` (`latest_release_api_url`, `newer_release_from_latest_json`, `release_page_url`), never per app; the round trip and the once-per-sign-in look are likewise one shared entry point, `fauna_client::update_look` (`fetch_latest_release`, `look_at_sign_in_over_http`), which each app calls from its post-sign-in hook — so when the nest carries the newest version, that entry point is the one place that changes.

**Scope — a desktop promise, drawn by who delivers updates.** **linux, windows and macOS owe it, and have it.** **The terminal app owes it since it has a channel, and has it (2026-09-26)**: a notice that says "get it here" needs a *here*, which the archives [`tui.md`](tui.md) ratified now are — the release page the notice names; the door is the Settings root's About block ([`../apps/tui.md`](../apps/tui.md) § Update check). **Absent by design on web, iOS and Android, declared here:** the web app is served by your nest and is always the nest's own version, so there is nothing for it to be newer than (a newer *nest* is the nest's upgrade story, not the app's); the two phone apps are updated by their stores, which own the notice. On those three the *behaviour itself* is absent, which is the citation the catalog's `absences` requires; [`../apps/web.md`](../apps/web.md) § Implementation status today, [`../apps/ios.md`](../apps/ios.md) § Key Differences from macOS and [`../apps/android.md`](../apps/android.md) § Key Differences from iOS index the absence.

## Implementation status today

The contract holds today on web (served), macOS, Windows and Linux (package installs) and tui (the archive channel, built 2026-09-26 and witnessed by its install-then-drive test; its first artifact ships with the first tagged release); Android's store record exists with nothing uploaded, and iOS's channel — TestFlight for invited testers, then the App Store — is ratified with nothing uploaded ([`ios.md`](ios.md) § Implementation status today). The current per-channel facts are owned by [`../feature-catalog.md`](../feature-catalog.md) § Columns today, and the per-column witness state is computed by `just features-parity` — neither is restated here.

**The update notice is built on linux, windows, macOS and tui, and witnessed on tui (2026-09-26), linux (2026-10-05), macOS and windows (2026-10-08)**: the catalog's [`app-version-and-updates`](../../../features/app-version-and-updates.md) outcome 2 cites § Knowing a newer version is out and is walked by `tests/e2e-unified/tests/test_app_version_and_updates.py` against a harness-served release feed (the compile-gated `FAUNA_E2E_RELEASE_FEED_URL` seam — only the feed's origin is stubbed; the endpoint path, the JSON shape and the semver rule are the shared production ones). Linux, macOS and windows paint the three approved ids on Settings → General, their notice drawn by both the asked check and the sign-in look, and reach the feed through the same compile-gated seam (macOS and windows through the FFI face below, both 2026-10-08). The shared FFI face for `fauna_client::update_look` was built 2026-10-05: `fauna-ffi`'s `version` module exports the asked check (`check_for_newer_release`, its three answers newer / up to date / failed), the sign-in look (`look_at_sign_in`) and the production feed origin (`release_feed_origin`), the same functions tui and linux call directly. **Its three declared absences are applied on the page (2026-09-26):** they are outcome-level (the page's outcome 1, the version row, is owed on every app), so they ride the per-outcome absence ([`../feature-catalog.md`](../feature-catalog.md) § The coverage contract, built the same day) rather than a split — `web (outcome 2)`, `ios (outcome 2)` and `android (outcome 2)` cite this section, with one `outcome-absence-added` record each naming the 2026-09-26 approval, so those three columns are judged on the version row alone.

**The once-per-sign-in look is built on linux, macOS, windows and tui through the one shared entry point, and witnessed on all four (ruled 2026-10-03; raised 2026-10-01; tui and the shared entry point built 2026-10-05, macOS and windows 2026-10-08).** § Knowing a newer version is out now allows one unasked, notify-only look per sign-in on every app that checks. Linux and the terminal app have it through `fauna_client::update_look` directly, macOS and windows through its `fauna-ffi` face ([`../apps/linux.md`](../apps/linux.md) and [`../apps/tui.md`](../apps/tui.md) § Update check, [`../apps/macos.md`](../apps/macos.md) § Tech Stack, [`../apps/windows.md`](../apps/windows.md) § Auto-Updates), and `test_app_version_and_updates.py` walks it on all four in both directions. The macOS reshape (2026-10-08) retired the automatic check, background download, in-place replacement and both Settings toggles, and the update framework behind them left the bundle and the release pipeline the same day ([`macos.md`](macos.md) § Implementation status today). No nest field carries the newest version yet, so every unasked look reads the release host. The catalog's outcome still claims only the ask: the sign-in outcome needs web, iOS and Android declared absent from it, which waits on the user's approval of that absence. Tracked internally.
