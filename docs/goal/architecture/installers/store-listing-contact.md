## Why this doc exists

For two months the store tracks carried one sentence — *declaring DSA non-trader keeps the organisation's contact details off the store listing* — from file to file without a source. It is true of one store, false of another, and beside the point on the third, and nobody found out until the first Play release had shipped and the Play Console showed the developer phone under *information shown as part of your Play developer profile*. What a store publishes about the organisation is a privacy fact, so it is owned once, here, with a dated primary source per store, and every store track reads it before filling a contact or trader field.

## The three stores

Each store is answered on three points: **(a)** which organisation contact fields the store publishes; **(b)** whether a DSA trader-status declaration changes any of them; **(c)** what was declared on this organisation's account. *Unknown* is recorded as unknown, never inferred.

### Google Play

- **(a) Published, for an organisation account:** legal name, legal address, developer email address and developer phone number. Google's *Required information to create a Play Console developer account* (support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/13628312, read 2026-10-10): "Google will display your legal name, legal address, developer email address, and developer phone number on Google Play"; the separate *contact* email and phone, which Google uses to reach the developer, "are NOT shown to users on Google Play". *Contact information requirements for developer accounts* (answer/10840893, read 2026-10-10) places both published fields on the developer profile: "Developer phone number, shown as part of your developer profile on Google Play". The Developer Program Policy (answer/17517561, § Play Console Requirements item 2.1.4, read 2026-10-10) binds the same pair: "Developer email address and phone number shown on Google Play where applicable". Organisation type, size and website are "not shown on Google Play" (*View and manage your developer account information*, answer/13634081, read 2026-10-10), and the same page says where to check: "To see all of the information that may be shown publicly on Google Play, go to the Developer profile page". A second, per-app contact set — the *Store listing contact details* under Store settings (email, phone, website) — appears on the app's own listing page and is filled per app.
- **(b) Trader status:** since November 2026 the Console requires a declaration, and on Play it changes no published contact field of a non-trader. Until Google's mail of 2026-11-03 none was found: none of the four Google pages above mentions trader status or the Digital Services Act, five searches of Play Console Help on 2026-10-10 surfaced no Google-authored page on a trader declaration, and the hunt for the form in the Console on 2026-08-22 found nothing. Google's policy-update mail to Play developers of 2026-11-03 announced it: "Starting in November 2026, you must declare your trader status in Play Console in accordance with new Console requirements." Google's *Trader status declaration* (support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/17594752, read 2026-11-05) places it at account level — "Go to Manage account > Account details > About you" — and a trader also confirms "the trader status for each app in your portfolio" under *Monitor and improve > Policy and programs > App content*; "Existing developer accounts must submit the declaration by February 1, 2027", "regardless of where apps are targeted or distributed". The form, read back by the owner on 2026-11-05, is one choice: trader, yes or no. For a non-trader, "In countries and regions where displaying trader status is required (for example, the EEA and the UK), your declaration will be visible to consumers", and "we will inform consumers in these regions that consumer rights stemming from consumer protection laws do not apply to contracts between you and them." For a trader, from March 1, 2027, "Your contact details will be shown", with its DUNS number in the *App support* section and a link to the app's Terms of Service, and in the EEA and the UK its name replaces Google in the *Offered by* field. Nothing on the page changes the developer email and phone of (a), which Google shows for every organisation account, trader or not.
- **(c) Declared: non-trader, on 2026-11-05.** None was found to make before 2026-11-03. The owner declared the organisation's account non-trader on 2026-11-05, as § The classification question records they declare wherever a store offers the choice, and read it back in the Console the same day: the account page shows *Trader status: Non-trader* (read in the Norwegian-language Console, whose labels are *Status som næringsdrivende* and *Ikke-næringsdrivende*). The account is an organisation account; its published pair is the developer email role mailbox and the organisation's phone number ([`android.md`](android.md) § Implementation status today). Read back by the owner in the Console on 2026-10-09: developer email and developer phone listed under *information shown as part of your Play developer profile*. The public developer-profile page itself has not been read back yet: the signed-out listing answered HTTP 404 on 2026-11-05, the app having no public track (§ Implementation status today).

### App Store

- **(a) Published, for a trader:** address, phone number and email address, on the App Store product page, in the EU only. Apple's *Manage European Union Digital Services Act trader requirements* (developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/manage-compliance-information/manage-european-union-digital-services-act-trader-requirements, read 2026-10-10, re-read 2026-11-06): "Articles 30 and 31 of the Digital Services Act (DSA) require Apple to verify and display trader contact information for all traders distributing apps on the App Store in the European Union (EU)"; for an organisation the address on its DUNS record "will automatically display" and a phone number and email address are entered "for display on your App Store product pages"; "Once verified, Apple will publish this information on your App Store product page when your app is distributed in any of the 27 territories of the EU." Apple's news post *Provide your trader status in App Store Connect* (2024-03-21) adds: "You'll need to let us know whether or not you're a trader to submit new apps to the App Store."
- **(b) Trader status is the lever.** The same page, on the non-trader choice: "This is not a trader account. You won't need to provide any contact information." and "If you're not a trader, consumers in the EU will be informed that consumer rights stemming from applicable consumer protection laws won't apply to contracts between you and them." So on the App Store the carried sentence holds: a non-trader account publishes no trader contact details, in the EU or anywhere; a trader account publishes address, phone and email on the EU product pages. The declaration is account-wide with a per-app override ("You'll then have the option to turn off or specify your trader status for each specific app that you distribute"), and it is required whether or not the app is distributed in the EU.
- **(b′) Who is not a trader, on Apple's page.** The same page, re-read 2026-11-06 (and in a copy the owner saved that day): "If you don't distribute apps on the App Store in the EU (for example you only distribute apps through alternative distribution, or TestFlight, or on the App Store only outside the EU), you're not acting as a trader on the App Store." Beyond that the classification is the developer's own: "Apple can't determine whether you're a trader." The page names "a range of non-exhaustive and non-exclusive factors" — revenue from the app (In-App Purchases, a paid or ad-sponsored app), commercial practices towards consumers such as advertising, registration for VAT, and developing the app in connection with a trade, business, craft or profession — and adds: "Regardless of whether you're an individual developer or organization, if you have a legal status associated with a business activity, that would suggest you may be a trader." This doc records the page's words, never how they apply to the organisation (§ The classification question).
- **(c) Declared: not a trader, on 2026-11-06.** Before that day the tree held a *decision* to declare non-trader (2026-08-22) and one later statement, in the legal-review packet, that the declaration "has already been made to both stores", which cited nothing; the enrollment record said the status was unconfirmed as of 2026-08-22. On 2026-11-06 the owner declared it in App Store Connect → Business → *Complete Compliance Requirements* → *Digital Services Act Compliance*, as § The classification question records they declare wherever a store offers the choice: the account-wide choice "I'm not a trader under the DSA or I don't plan to distribute in the EU", under which the form reads "You won't need to provide any contact information to be displayed on your App Store product page." (the console's wording of the choice (b) quotes from the help page as "This is not a trader account."). No per-app override was set. Read back by the owner the same day: the Business page says "You have completed all regulatory requirements at this time."; its Compliance table lists *Digital Services Act* — 27 Countries or Regions — Nov 6, 2026 — Active; its View link shows "I'm not a trader under the DSA or I don't plan to distribute in the EU". No App Store product page has been read back: nothing is world-visible on the App Store yet (§ Implementation status today).

### Microsoft Store

- **(a) Published, for a Company account:** a customer-contact phone number, physical address and email address, and the support email. Microsoft's *Company account verification requirements* (learn.microsoft.com/windows/apps/publish/store-business-verification-reqs, page dated 2025-02-27, updated 2026-03-09, read 2026-10-10): "Due to regulatory requirements and Microsoft's commitment to customer experience, a phone number, physical address and email are required." — Microsoft accepts a P.O. box or a registered agent's address, a voicemail number and an auto-reply alias. *Verify your company profile* (learn.microsoft.com/partner-center/account-settings/update-your-partner-profile, dated 2025-06-04, updated 2026-06-25, read 2026-10-10): "For companies enrolled in Windows and Xbox program in Partner Center, business verification information and customer contact details are mandatory as per European Union Digital Services Act" and "provide contact information which will be displayed in storefronts soon by expanding Contact Information for Customers section." The App Developer Agreement v8.10 (published 2025-09-12, effective 2025-10-14) § 2: "You agree that Microsoft may publicly display in the Store the email address that you provide in Partner Center for customer support purposes." Whether the storefront shows the customer-contact set today or still "soon" is **unknown** until the listing is read.
- **(b) There is no non-trader choice for a Company account.** The agreement's § 2: "If your intended distribution of Apps or In-App Products through the Microsoft Store is in relation to your business, trade, or profession, your Store Account must be a Company account type. By choosing an Individual or Company account type during the Store Account sign-up process, you thereby declare such status." The verification page categorises every such account as a trader: "a developer categorized as a 'Trader' must submit either their DUNS ID or one form of a recognized business verification document." So on the Microsoft Store the carried sentence is **contradicted**: the account type is the trader declaration, it was made at enrollment, and the contact set is mandatory and published regardless. No submission form asks a trader question to answer "non-trader" to.
- **(c) Declared:** a Company account, enrolled and verified 2026-08-22 ([`windows.md`](windows.md) § Store distribution (MSIX)) — which under § 2 above is the declaration. **Unknown:** whether *Contact Information for Customers* has been filled, with which phone, address and email, and whether the listing page shows them yet; read back by the owner (§ Implementation status today).

## The readback rule

A console form answer that changes public listing content is read back from the console after saving — and from the public listing or developer-profile page once it is live — and the reading is recorded here with its date. It is never assumed from a plan, a runbook step or a sibling store's behaviour. The three stores above differ on exactly the point everyone assumed was uniform; that is why this rule exists.

## The classification question

Whether a non-profit distributing a free, non-monetised app is a trader under the DSA is a legal classification, and the specification states none ([`../../README.md`](../../README.md) § Writing protocol → *State mechanisms, never law*): the project's earlier self-certification was cut from the shipped spec on 2026-08-30 and stays out under that rule — the organisation's own decision, no longer a wait on counsel (review by a lawyer is never a gate: [`../public-texts.md`](../public-texts.md) § Rulings, 20). **How the owner declares is the owner's decision, ruled 2026-10-18: the organisation declares itself not a trader wherever a store offers the choice** — on the App Store the account-wide DSA trader status (declared 2026-11-06), on Google Play the account-level trader status (declared 2026-11-05) — a declaration of how to answer the store's form, not a legal classification. This doc records what each store *does* and what *was declared*, as facts read back from the stores; it never states which status the organisation ought to hold.
