## Goal

One release **AAB** (Android App Bundle), built reproducibly from the same tree as every other app, signed via **Play App Signing**, and distributed through **Google Play as the primary channel**. F-Droid and a direct-download APK are follow-on channels once the source is public. The developer account is owned by the organization — never an individual — with a second board member as co-admin for continuity.

**Four shipping build types, one tree (the `foss` split ruled 2026-08-24; the `kids` flavor ratified 2026-09-25).** `release` is the Play AAB; `storeSafe` is `release` minus the gated-feature registry members ([`../dynamic-features.md`](../dynamic-features.md) § The App-Store escape hatch); **`foss` is `release` minus every proprietary Google Play client library** — the F-Droid / direct-download artifact. The only such libraries are the store-age arm's two IPC shims to the Play Store app (Play Integrity + Play Age Signals, [`../../behavior/family-safety.md`](../../behavior/family-safety.md) § The account age band): closed-source, forbidden by F-Droid's inclusion policy, and useless on a Play-less device. They are declared only on the Play-distributed build types, their glue lives in the per-build-type twin pair `src/storeAge/` (real) / `src/noStoreAge/` (inert — the `src/noPayments/` posture), and `just android-foss-check` is the witness: the `foss` dex names no `com.google.android.play` class. A `foss` admission carries no age claim (provenance `none`) — the honest partial coverage the age band accepts. **`kids` is `storeSafe` under the Fauna Kids identity** (shape and every decision owned by [`../../behavior/family-safety.md`](../../behavior/family-safety.md) § The account age band, the kids-app bullet): `initWith(getByName("storeSafe"))`, the application id `social.fauna.faunakids` (§ Store identity — a different package, never a suffix; set on the variant through `androidComponents.onVariants`, because a build type can only suffix the id), `buildConfigField("boolean", "KIDS", "true")` for the render conditions, the excised shell in a `src/noKids/` source set that the four other build types take, with an inert `src/kids/` twin of only what `src/main` still names (the feed and posting, discovery, every bridge, web publishing, monetization and third-party surfaces — Kotlin has no inline compile-time exclusion, the `src/noPayments/` / `src/noStoreAge/` posture, one polarity over), the `kids-floor` FFI feature compiled in, and **no `versionNameSuffix`** — it ships, on the train at the fleet version. The `android-kids` / `android-kids-check` recipes are its build and its two-column witness (the excised element ids absent from the kids dex and the excised UniFFI faces absent from its packaged library, all present in `release`; the kids identity on the kids manifest only — the floor is a compiled constant with no string to grep, so its presence is pinned at the recipe and Rust-test tiers), the witness deliberately not in the merge-gate check on `android-store-safe-check`'s cost grounds; tier_1 source pins carry the rot risk. *Build state (2026-10-05):* the build type, its identity, the `kids-safe,kids-floor` FFI flavor (`just android-ffi-kids`, staged under `src/kids/`), the shell split (`src/noKids/` plus the `src/kids/` twin, the `BuildConfig.KIDS` renders) and both recipes exist and are pinned; the kids artifact assembles and `android-kids-check` is green. Open: the witness's kind-string column, which waits on the shared-Rust gating of the feed, search and web wire senders ([`../dynamic-features.md`](../dynamic-features.md) § Implementation status today, the kids paragraph), the not-supervised surface, and the Families listing. Listed under Play's Families policy (target audience children); a `foss` twin of `kids` is a non-goal (F-Droid has no kids category and no store-age signal).

## Store identity

- **applicationId: `social.fauna.fauna`** — the permanent Play identity, on the organization's own domain. It can never change after the first artifact is uploaded to Play. **Changed from `social.fauna.android` 2026-08-22, before any Play upload** (the last moment it was free), under the ratified leaf rule owned by [`macos.md`](macos.md) § Identifier domain → *How the LEAF is spelled*: **one identifier per registry unit, rooted at `social.fauna.`, leaf = the PRODUCT NAME, lowercase, never a platform or generic word.** The same string is the Apple bundle id and the linux/Flathub app id — **convergent** uniformity, not manufactured: every registry independently accepts it, so no store's rules are being fought (windows is the one exception — Partner Center *mints* `FaunaSocial.FaunaSocial`). ⚠ This supersedes an earlier ruling recorded here the same day ("each platform's registry identity is independent — do NOT try to keep them equal"): its *reasoning* stands — per-store rules genuinely differ and equality must never be forced against them — but its conclusion does not follow once a single leaf satisfies all of them on their own terms. Note the **source** namespace `com.fauna.app` and the UniFFI Kotlin `package_name` `com.fauna.ffi` are deliberately decoupled from this and are NOT drift to fix.
- **The kids listing's applicationId: `social.fauna.faunakids`** (ratified 2026-09-25 — the Fauna Kids product under the same leaf rule: leaf = the product name, lowercase; a second registry unit, never a suffix of the main id, because a Families-policy listing is a different Play record). Freezes at its first upload, like the main id; the same string is the iOS kids target's bundle id.
- The Gradle **namespace stays `com.fauna.app`** (source packages, `R`, `BuildConfig`) — deliberately decoupled from the store identity: renaming every source file buys nothing, and the two are independent by design in AGP.
- The test APK derives `social.fauna.fauna.test`; every manifest authority derives from `${applicationId}` (documents provider, FileProvider, startup initializer) so none can drift from the store identity.

## Signing model

Android has no CA-issued developer certificate — both keys below are self-generated; the "certificate" a store relationship provides is the **developer account** plus **Play App Signing**.

- **App signing key: Google-generated and Google-held** (Play App Signing — mandatory for new Play apps). Devices verify installs against it; Google re-signs each AAB on delivery. Choosing Google-generated (rather than uploading our own) means the key that user devices trust can never leak from a dev machine.
- **Upload key: a locally generated keystore** that signs what we upload to Play. Held on the primary dev machine **outside the repository** (never committed, never in CI secrets until a release pipeline exists); passwords held by the user. An upload key is recoverable — Google support can register a replacement after identity re-verification — so local custody is acceptable where it never would be for the app signing key.
- **Release builds** read the keystore from an untracked, machine-local properties file; when it is absent the release build simply stays unsigned (dev machines and CI don't need the key to build). Concretely: a dedicated dev-fleet script generates the keystore (PKCS12, RSA-4096, alias `fauna-upload`, 25-year validity) into `~/.config/fauna/android/upload-keystore.jks` and writes the sibling `signing.properties` that `app/build.gradle.kts` reads; the password is generated (32 hex chars from the kernel CSPRNG; `--prompt` types your own instead), passed to `keytool` through the environment rather than argv, and never lands in a tracked file. Generated is the default because Gradle must open the keystore unattended — the password therefore sits in plaintext beside the `.jks` whoever picks it, so permissions rather than the password guard the key, and a typed one only adds the risk of forgetting it. The consequence: back up the whole directory, not just the `.jks`. Signing is therefore *opportunistic* — the `signingConfig` materialises only where that file exists, so every other machine still builds the identical R8/minify path and only the final upload artifact needs the key.
- **The upload artifact is an AAB, built by `just android-bundle`** — Play splits the bundle per device itself and will not take the APK `just android-release` produces. That recipe also greps the bundle's dex for the debug `TestAgent`'s own strings, which is [`../e2e-automation-surface-gating.md`](../e2e-automation-surface-gating.md) point 15's release-artifact check applied to android's shipping artifact.
- **Play's "automatic protection" (installer-source check) is DECLINED — 2026-08-22, surfaced live at app creation.** Play offers to inject a check into the shipped code that prompts users who obtained the app from any other source to re-download it from Play. Three ratified positions rule it out: § Goal makes **F-Droid and direct-download APK follow-on channels**, so the check would nag our own users for using our own channels; [`../../behavior/region-blocking.md`](../../behavior/region-blocking.md) § Region determination treats **sideloaded and self-built apps as a first-class case** with its own determination rule, not an anomaly; and § Goal requires the AAB be **built reproducibly from the same tree**, which a post-upload code injection defeats — the thing users could rebuild and compare against would no longer be the thing that ships. The toggle lives under Play Console → App integrity and takes effect per release, so it must be verified OFF before each upload, not once.

- **Direct-download APKs are a separate signing domain**: Play App Signing covers only Play-delivered artifacts, so a direct APK needs a key we hold and protect ourselves. That custody decision is deferred until the channel exists. F-Droid signs its own builds from public source (their standard flow), so it adds no key custody here.

## Developer account posture

- **Organization account** (not personal): verification rides the organization's DUNS number and registry documents, the account survives any individual, and organization accounts are exempt from Play's closed-testing gate for new personal accounts.
- Google Play publishes the developer's **legal name, legal address, developer email, and developer phone** on every listing for organization accounts — so every published field carries organization-level values (role mailbox on the org domain, the org's phone and registered address), never a person's.
- One-time registration fee (USD 25); no renewal.

## Versioning

`versionName` is the product version — the ONE fleet-wide string, sourced from the root `Cargo.toml` (owner: [`../product-version.md`](../product-version.md); the `version-lockstep-check` merge gate holds them equal) — and `versionCode` is **derived from it arithmetically**, never tracked by hand as a second number:

```
versionCode = major * 10000 + minor * 100 + patch
```

So `0.1.0` → `100`, `0.1.1` → `101`, `1.0.0` → `10000`. One number a human edits (the product version); the counter Play actually enforces follows mechanically. Bumping the product version can never *lower* the counter, which is the property Play requires — it rejects both reuse and regression of `versionCode`.

**A reship of unchanged product code bumps the patch version — fleet-wide.** Play demands a fresh `versionCode` for every upload, including a re-upload after a rejected or withdrawn build, and this scheme deliberately gives each product version exactly one code. The reship rule itself is now owned fleet-wide by [`../product-version.md`](../product-version.md) § Reships (it generalizes what this section first ratified for Play): the bump is a one-commit product-patch bump across every surface, and only the store that demanded the re-upload ships it.

**Why derived-and-committed rather than computed at build time.** A `versionCode` generated from git history (commit count, tag distance) is monotonic on this repository's linear `origin/main` and needs no human step — and it was rejected anyway, for two reasons:

- **F-Droid builds from source, not from this checkout.** It is a declared future channel (§ Release channels), and its builds run in F-Droid's own environment against a source tarball or tag, where a commit-count derivation is either unavailable or yields a different number than the Play upload claimed. A `versionCode` that cannot be reproduced from the source tree alone actively fights a channel this document already commits to.
- **It would be a second mechanism.** Windows hand-maintains its version in a committed file; a git-derived Android counter would make the two stores disagree about where a version comes from, for no gain (priority #1).

**Both numbers live in `apps/fauna-android/app/build.gradle.kts`**, and the file states the arithmetic beside them so the relationship survives a cold read.

`versionCode` is capped by Play at 2 100 000 000; this scheme reaches that only at major version 210 000, so the ceiling is not a practical constraint.
