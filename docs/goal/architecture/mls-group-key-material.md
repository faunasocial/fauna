### Audience: an MLS group at a specific epoch (a defined membership set that can change)

**Per-chunk file-sync key** — `libs/fauna-core/src/chunk_crypto.rs` (`derive_chunk_key` / `derive_chunk_nonce`; **live** for group-bound shared folders via the M2 per-set content key — owner-only unbound sets stay on `BackupKey`)

- **Root:** under the live M2 mechanism (below), the per-set **content key** the MLS group distributes + rotates — not the raw `epoch_secret`, which rotates on every commit and so cannot open pre-join content (the raw exporter output was the pre-M2 interim root; the primitive is identical either way since it only ever takes an opaque 32-byte root).
- **Derivation:** two-step BLAKE3 — `derive_key(context = "fauna.chunk.v1", key_material = root_secret)` for context separation, then `keyed_hash(intermediate, content_hash)` for the per-chunk salt (the chunk's BLAKE3 content hash, the same digest the content-addressed store keys it by). **Deterministic** per (root, content hash) — **convergent**. Matches the pattern used by `derive_post_key` (`subscription/crypto.rs`); `fauna-core` no longer pulls in `hkdf` for this path.
- **AEAD:** ChaCha20-Poly1305, deterministic nonce derived from the content hash (`content_hash[..12]`, the same convergent shape as `blob_crypto`). ✅ **Landed (Slice 0, 2026-06-27):** the per-chunk key *and* nonce now derive from the chunk **content hash**, not its file position. This is what makes the seal convergent: identical plaintext under one root → byte-identical ciphertext, so the content-addressed store dedups it; distinct plaintext → a distinct key *and* nonce, so a ChaCha20 keystream is never reused across two *distinct* chunks at one epoch. **That sentence needs one more clause to be true, because the hashed value and the encrypted value are different objects on this plane** (`compress` § Pipeline order: the nonce salt is the digest of the *unframed* chunk, the AEAD plaintext is its *framed* body — `0x00 ‖ P` or `0x01 ‖ zstd(P)`): the framing must be a **single fixed function of the chunk, applied by every writer**, or one (key, nonce) meets two plaintexts. Until 2026-09-03 that was an unenforced convention across a Rust crate and a Go binary, and it did not hold — the Go WebDAV MDA sealed the raw chunk through a bare `encrypt_chunk` export while the sync engine sealed the framed one, and the XOR of the two ciphertexts recovers the chunk with no key (a two-time pad; the Poly1305 one-time key reused as well). **Since 2026-09-03 the invariant is structural:** the AEAD primitive is `pub(crate)` in `fauna-core`, and the only seal door is `fauna_core::chunk_seal` (`seal_chunk_body` / `FramedChunk::frame`), which verifies the caller's hash against the plaintext and frames with the file-sync corpus's one framing; the engine, the daemon, the MDA (`fauna_ffi::webdav_seal_file`) and the e2e agent all seal through it, and there is no build-time switch on the framing any more (the `compress_chunks` cargo feature is retired — a feature-off build was itself a raw writer). A `chunk_seal` witness test keeps the XOR recovery as documentation of why. **History — the pairs already at rest, and why nothing audits them now (2026-09-09 → 2026-09-24).** The door stops *new* pairs only, so from 2026-09-09 the nest's GC walk audited the pairs a deployment of the time could still hold — a WebDAV-served set that overlapped a synced folder between 2026-07-08 and 2026-09-03, or an owner-only set the daemon and the app both wrote between 2026-07-13 and 2026-07-31 — findable with no key from `chunk_hashes` beside `stored_hashes` (one plaintext hash, two store keys, one root). The audit counted them on the admin dashboard (*Chunks awaiting re-seal* / *Chunks pinned by a snapshot*), served the owner's worklist on `fauna.sync.chunk_pairs.list`, and a device pass (`SyncEngine::reseal_paired_chunks`) re-recorded every referencing file through `chunk_seal` so GC could reclaim the raw twin; a pair whose raw twin a snapshot pinned was the one population no device could clear, and the Backups page named the pinning snapshot so its owner could delete it. **The whole programme was retired on 2026-09-24 by the compat-remnant sweep** ([`version-compatibility.md`](version-compatibility.md) § Dimension 2, the fourth ratified exception at its universal scope): the 2026-09-24 baseline reset established that no installation or data existed anywhere, so no chunk the retired raw writers sealed rests on any nest, and a mechanism that can only ever find nothing is a remnant. The audit, its tables, the kind, the device pass, its marker, the dashboard cards and the owner's pinned-pair surface left together; the structural single-door invariant above is what keeps a pair from ever forming again, and a raw twin is neither scanned for nor re-sealed. The earlier positional `chunk_index` scheme (which broke dedup and risked nonce reuse across distinct files at one epoch) is corrected per the design (tracked internally, ratified 2026-06-25) § Q2.

- **Audience justification (why this path will exist):** the cross-user-shared folder case admits join/leave, needs forward secrecy on member removal, and benefits from content-defined dedup across group members at the same epoch. MLS gives all three; deterministic **content-hash** per-chunk derivation is what makes the dedup work — identical chunks across users in the same group at the same epoch yield byte-identical ciphertext, so the blob store stores them once.

**M2 content-key mechanism (live).** The `chunk_crypto` root is **not** the raw `epoch_secret` (which rotates on *every* commit and so cannot open a chunk sealed before a new member joined) but a separate **per-set content key** the MLS group distributes — the group is a key-*distribution* channel. The mechanism is the direct analog of the subscriptions period-key custody (§ Audience: an opaque set of subscriber pubkeys; `SubscriptionsConfig`/`TierPeriodKeys`), differing only in the distribution channel (MLS group, not per-subscriber X25519 `KeyBlob` wraps):

- **Generations.** A shared set has a content-key history: `current` (new uploads seal under it) + an **uncapped** `prior` list (most-recent first). Each generation is `{ version (1 at bind, +1 per removal), key [u8;32] (the `chunk_crypto` root), rotated_at (µs, strictly increasing) }` — the same shape as `TierPeriod`. The pure custody core + transitions (genesis, rotate-on-removal, the version lookup, the bundle round-trip) is `libs/fauna-core/src/folder_keys.rs` (`FolderContentKeys` / `ContentKeyGeneration`). **Same-version candidates (fixed 2026-07-08):** versions are unique in the single-writer flow, but the no-generation-lost CRDT `merge` (two owner devices committing different-keyed rotations to one version; `migrate_set_identity`'s target-exists branch on a concurrent serve+share race) can retain **two distinct-keyed generations at one version** — and chunks can exist stamped with that version under *either* key, so no single version→key mapping can serve both. Every open path therefore resolves `keys_for(version)` — **all** candidates, current-first — and tries each (the AEAD tag disambiguates; an empty candidate set stays the fail-closed posture): the sync-engine chunk + sealed-manifest opens, and the Go WebDAV MDA via `webdav_content_keys_for`. **How a same-version pair settles (ruled and built 2026-10-03):** it is not undone — two generations of one version are a legitimate, permanent state of a set's history on every holder. **(1) One order everywhere.** `current` is the highest generation by `(version, rotated_at, key bytes)`; the owner's `merge` and the member's `from_generations` apply that same order, so every owner device and every member lands on the same `current` whatever order the custody writes or the envelope's bundle arrive in. New uploads seal under it; the other generation stays in `prior` as a read candidate. **(2) The member accepts the pair.** The envelope carries the owner's custody verbatim, both generations included, and a member's ingest reconstructs exactly that history. Until this ruling `from_generations` refused a bundle with a repeated version as malformed, which left the set dark for members after any concurrent rotation; the refusal guarded nothing — the bundle is authenticated by the envelope's seal and the owner's signature (`succession-cut.md` ruling (11)(b)), and the pick among same-version generations is the total order above, never arbitrary. Only an empty bundle is refused; a repeated identical generation folds away. **(3) No healing rotation.** The owner does not mint `version + 1` to make versions unique again: both generations were minted after the event that asked for the rotation (the unserve, the succession cut), so either one meets its purpose; the succession rotation's trigger compares the merged `current`'s `rotated_at` against the cut and is satisfied by either, so the collision does not repeat; and a heal would be one more unattended rotation open to the same race. **(4) Compatibility.** Nothing changes on the wire or at rest: the generation shape, the envelope and the version stamped on every sealed record are as before, and the nest's content-key floor compares versions, which both generations share. A member on a build from before this ruling refuses a pair-carrying envelope whole and keeps the custody it already holds — nothing is lost, and it reads the newer generations once it updates. Pinned by `folder_keys::tests::a_member_ingests_a_bundle_with_two_generations_of_one_version` (red first; a chunk sealed under each key opens from the member's reconstructed history) and `fauna_client_folders::custody::tests::two_devices_rotating_at_once_settle_and_the_member_ingests_the_envelope`. **Not covered:** the peer-serve re-seal legs in `fauna-sync-engine` (`serve_seal_roots`, `share_chunk_body`, the two manifest backfills, and `peer_share_store`'s twins) still re-derive under the first candidate only, so a body sealed under the losing generation is not served peer-to-peer and the puller falls back to the nest — a lost shortcut, never a failed read.
- **Multi-writer (ratified 2026-07-18 — design tracked internally; the same-nest write plane is BUILT 2026-07-19, mechanics owner `../behavior/file-sync.md` § Multi-writer shared sets).** A member the owner grants `access == 'writer'` (`../ui/folders.md` § Sharing owns the access model) seals uploads under the **current** generation from their **own** custody copy exactly as the owner does — the convergent seal is holder-agnostic by construction, so this changes **no crypto**: only the **owner** ever *generates* a generation, rotates, or publishes the envelope (the claimant gates are untouched). Two consequences ratified with it: **(a) member custody-ingest is a real leg** — a member's client fetches the envelope at join and on every rotation-commit receipt, opens it via the group epoch, and merges the generations into their own custody, the account-state plane kind `fauna.state.folder-keys` since its cut ([`config-dissolution.md`](config-dissolution.md) § The `__config` dissolution schedule → *The kinds*; `__config` when ratified) (the "received copy" this section already names). **A receipt that fails to open must leave the member RETRYING on its ordinary poll cadence until one succeeds** (`maybe_ingest_folder_custody` clears its per-channel "holds current custody" mark on any attempt that merges nothing, so the quiet-poll arm stops short-circuiting; added 2026-07-24). Treating the commit receipt as a one-shot trigger therefore strands that member on the pre-rotation generation until an app restart, with every later owner upload failing closed in `content_open_roots` — silently, since fail-closed is the correct local behavior. The retry is the mechanism, not a safety net. **This read leg is BUILT (Phase 0, 2026-07-18)** — `fauna_core::folder_keys` merge + `fauna_client_folders::custody::merge_received_keys`, driven by the conversations session (`FaunaMlsBackend::maybe_ingest_folder_custody` at the folder join + commit-poll), with Media's `download_file` resolving per-set content keys from that custody; build-status owner `../ui/folders.md` § Sharing → *Member custody-ingest read leg*; **(b) the owner-sole-writer premise of the structural-integrity argument below narrows** — provenance of a change becomes **nest-asserted** (the home nest stamps `author_actor_id` from the authenticated/gated writer; a compromised home nest can misattribute among key-holding members, never forge sealed content) — the cryptographic restoration is exactly the **per-manifest MLS group binding**, ruled 2026-09-27 with its compat analysis as the *Writer-signed change records* bullet directly below. A writer racing a rotation is bounded client-side by the drain's never-publish-prior-generation rule and server-side by a **version floor**: the envelope row gains an additive owner-stamped `current_version`, and a non-owner record stamped below it gets a typed `stale_content_key` refusal (owner devices exempt — their landed resume/drain machinery self-corrects). **The floor is BUILT (2026-07-19)** and **monotonic** by construction: `content_key.put` stores `MAX(stored, new)` and an absent stamp (an older owner client) never lowers or clears an established floor, so an owner downgrade cannot re-open the stale-writer window. An **absent** stamp on a *record* fails closed for the same reason — an old-client writer cannot prove freshness — with **one exemption, added 2026-08-21**: on a `public`-audience folder an **unstamped** member record is the ratified declassified shape (`../behavior/folders.md` § Target re-model owns the audience model; a member's engine takes the plaintext arm off the projection like the owner's), so it passes. The exemption is unstamped-only — a record *stamped below* the floor is refused there too — and it grants no access, because removal is enforced at the role gate (`resolve_writable_folder`), not here. Without it a writer member could never declassify: a plaintext record carries no generation, `>= floor` can never hold, and the heal this refusal promises (re-seal under the advanced generation) is unreachable for a record that must not be re-sealed while public — a permanent wedge wearing a retryable code. **The floor is readable by every holder (added 2026-09-27):** `fauna.folders.list` projects it on both arms as the row's additive `content_key_floor` (absent = no floor established, or an older nest, which asserts nothing), so a client that must hold itself to the floor — the capability host, which the owner exemption above does not cover (`../behavior/on-demand-files.md` § Shared sets on a capability host, decision 2) — reads it off the same row that carries the binding and the caller's access; a cross-nest member, which has no row here, reads it off the federated content-key read reply into its `ForeignFolder` record (`federation.md` § Cross-nest → *Recipient-side access discovery*; added 2026-09-28).

A new joiner (Welcome → current epoch secret) decrypts the envelope and reconstructs the history via `from_generations`. The bounded "since-last-rotation" alternative (option (b)) is rejected: universal shared-folder expectation is full history, and it adds no trust violation (the bytes a member can read are exactly the shared content — § *no new trust-property violation*). **⚠ Pre-bind gap (implementation, latent — verified 2026-07-06):** history-on-join distributes all content-key *generations*, but generation 1 exists only from `bind_set` onward, and `bind_set`/`share_set` do **no** re-seal of files that already existed (`libs/fauna-client-folders/src/orchestration.rs::bind_set` records the genesis key + publishes the envelope, nothing else). Closing it needs a one-time **owner-client re-seal migration** (re-chunk/seal existing files under the current generation, re-record, GC old chunks only after verify — no-user-data-loss), the natural home being the shared `FoldersAuthor` orchestration + a sync-engine re-seal pass. **The identical slice is wanted by the WebDAV-served-set design** (now ratified 2026-07-06 — `docs/goal/behavior/webdav-server.md` § Key model: flipping an existing `BackupKey` set to content-key-served needs the same re-seal), so **build it once, coordinated** — tracked internally (+ the WebDAV-side slice 2 tracked alongside it). - **Pre-bind re-seal migration (closes the history-on-join gap; shared with WebDAV serve-enable).** A one-time, **owner-client** pass that re-seals a set's files still on the pre-bind path (`content_key_version = None` — owner `BackupKey` or plaintext) under the set's **`current`** content-key generation, so a joiner (or the WebDAV MDA) decrypts the whole back-catalogue, not only post-share files. Three parts, sequenced so the correctness fix is **additive / no-data-loss by construction** and the destructive reclaim is separable:
  - **(A) Re-seal pass — additive, closes the read gap.** The owner's device (the only holder of the local plaintext) re-uploads each not-yet-`current` file through the ordinary seal path (read plaintext → `content_seal_root()` seals under `current`, ciphertext-hash-keyed → `record_change` stamps the new `content_key_version`). A higher-seq change supersedes the old one in the reader's latest-per-path fold, so the member/MDA immediately fetches + decrypts the re-sealed copy. **Idempotent** (convergent `chunk_crypto` ⇒ byte-identical ciphertext on re-run) and **deletes nothing** (old chunks linger as garbage), so an interrupted pass is simply re-runnable and no data is ever at risk.
  - **(B) Reclaim — destructive, review-gated (landed 2026-07-07, design + adversarial review).** The nest kind **`fauna.sync.changes.supersede`** `{folder, device_id, path, manifest_hash}` (owner-only via `owned_folder`, the `changes.record` device write-capability gate, ST-RES-1 not-found fold; custody-copy sets rejected — custody supersedes on upsert) marks the path's pre-head `sync_changes` rows superseded: an additive nullable `superseded_at` column (the table's first per-row mutation — rows are **marked, never deleted**, the `backup_custody`-tombstone precedent). Four safety properties, each pinned by a test: **(1) head-bound** — the caller names the manifest it **verified retrievable + decryptable end-to-end**, and the mark happens only if that equals the path's live head (`SupersedeOutcome::HeadMismatch` otherwise — typed, retryable), marking only rows *strictly below* the head, so the head is structurally unmarkable and every superseded row always has a strictly-later live manifest row (`webdav-server.md` § Architectural rules "deletes nothing until verified"); **(2) pin-by-absence** — GC's `sync_change_manifest_hashes(superseded_cutoff)` is `DISTINCT` over the remaining **live** rows, so a manifest shared by another path/set/actor stays pinned by that other row, and snapshot pins (`snapshot_files`) are independent and untouched (an old snapshot keeps its superseded manifest alive); **(3) grace-buffered** — a row superseded within the GC grace window stays pinned (keyed on `superseded_at`, since blob-`created_at` grace gives a long-existing chunk zero post-supersede buffer), so an in-flight reader mid-download isn't yanked; **(4) feed-hidden** — both `changes.list` queries exclude superseded rows, so a catching-up device (the eager per-row downloader in `pull_remote_changes`) never attempts a fetch of a reclaimable manifest. Reclaim itself stays the existing GC grace sweep — nothing deletes a blob directly. **Client leg (in `reseal_pending_under_current`):** after each re-sealed file, the owner engine verifies via `download_file_bytes_by_manifest` (real route, whole-file blake3) — a verify failure aborts the pass (fail-loud — the file's local row stays unstamped, so the next engine start retries it) — then calls supersede **best-effort**: reclaim is a storage optimization, so a failure (an older nest without the kind, a concurrently-moved head) logs and defers rather than failing the pass (I2: any client×nest within a major is supported). Version skew is fail-safe both ways: an older nest binary ignores the column and conservatively pins everything; an older client never calls the kind (garbage lingers — status quo). Legacy `folder_id IS NULL` rows are never matched (stay pinned — accepted).
  - **(C) Crash-safe + triggers — the pass is self-driving; no custody sentinel (retired 2026-09-25).** The triggers (`bind_set`/`share_set`, the WebDAV serve-enable flip, `paywall_set`) stage **nothing** beyond the genesis custody write (no pre-bind ledger rides it: the provenance ruling under (D) below that would have added one was superseded before build by writer-signed change records); the pass itself stays ungated. The **pass runs where the live engine is owned — the sync agent's always-resident engine, not `FoldersAuthor`** — because `SyncEngine` is `Send` but **not `Sync`** and is owned by exactly one per-set engine task, so it cannot be driven behind a shared reference on the transport-generic + wasm-light author: `bins/fauna-sync-agent/src/engine_driver.rs`'s `run_always_resident_root` calls `SyncEngine::reseal_pending_under_current` **ungated on every engine start**, after the initial converge and before the watch loop, on every agent-backed platform (the uniform agent shape, `apps/sync-agent-credentials.md`). Termination and crash-resume are the pass's own: a successful record stamps the local row's `content_key_version` at `current` (`commit_recorded_head`) and the pass skips rows already at the target generation, so a re-sealed file is never revisited; a failed or interrupted pass leaves its rows unstamped and the next start resumes there; an unbound set returns `0`. **The one carve-out — a WebDAV-served, group-less set (decided 2026-09-29):** such a set has no group, no member and possibly no agent anywhere (an iOS-, Android- or web-only owner), so no engine pass may ever reach its pre-serve files; there the **flipping client** runs an engine-less, nest-sourced walk right after the serve flip ([`../behavior/webdav-server.md`](../behavior/webdav-server.md) § Key model, the app-side seams bullet, (c), which owns it). It is not a second driver of this pass: it refuses a set bound to a group, it opens an unstamped head under the owner root only through (D)'s per-record enabler (the head verified as the owner's own signed record of this set), and its manifest byte move is this pass's own nest-bytes source (`fauna_core::nest_reseal::reseal_nest_copy_windowed`), so where an agent's pass runs too the two converge on the same store keys. There is no separate `resume` entry point and no "this set owes a pass" marker — the engine start *is* the resume path. **History:** until 2026-09-25 a `pending_reseal` custody sentinel (`FoldersConfig.pending_reseals`, staged by the triggers, read and cleared by the closure-injected `fauna_client_folders::run_pending_reseal` driver from the identity-holding app's in-process sync loop) gated the pass; the resident-drive retirement (`../behavior/sync-engine-deployments.md` § Implementation status) deleted that driver's only caller, leaving a write-only at-rest sentinel, so the sentinel, its custody helpers, the driver module, the `FoldersAuthor::stage_reseal` hook and the at-rest field + `FolderPendingReseal` type were removed outright — no reader, no key material, nothing a user could not recreate; `FoldersConfig` decodes without `deny_unknown_fields`, so a blob an older client wrote with the field still parses and drops it on the next rewrite. `drain_pending_uploads` is **not** reusable verbatim — `transfer_queue` carries no generation column and re-seals under whatever `current` is, not a pinned target generation.
  - **(D) An owner with no resident replica — decided 2026-09-26; mechanism built 2026-09-27, NOT ENABLED (security note below); enabling ruled 2026-09-27 (the pre-bind ledger, the sub-bullet below) and RE-RULED the same day — the enabler is the signature verify at the owner-root open, *Writer-signed change records* under *Multi-writer* above, ruling (5) — UNBUILT.** (A)–(C) assume the owner's device holds the file: the pass walks materialized rows and reads them from disk. An owner whose replicas are all on-demand — a phone, or a desktop location in on-demand mode — holds placeholders, so for that owner the pass moves nothing, a joiner never reads the back-catalogue, and the owner's own on-demand host stops opening its pre-bind files the moment the set is bound ([`../behavior/on-demand-files.md`](../behavior/on-demand-files.md) § Shared sets on a capability host, decision 4, which is what asks for this). Two changes close it. **The pass walks every live row and sources what is not on this disk from the nest** — the shared re-seal trio's nest-bytes source, which re-seals in memory and never hydrates — on any owner host that holds both roots, a capability host included; there it runs one file at a time in bounded memory (an iOS extension's memory cap rules out a whole-file buffer), after the host has observed the bind, resumable by the same per-row stamp. **And a bound reader selects the root by the record's stamp:** a stamped record opens under that generation and never under the owner root (the content-keyed precedence is unchanged — a stamped chunk is never owner-keyed); an **unstamped** record of a bound set opens under the owner root, and only for a holder that is the set's owner. An unstamped record of a bound set is pre-bind content by construction — a member's unstamped record is refused at the floor (*Multi-writer*, above), and a declassified record is a plaintext manifest that no key selection reaches — so the owner root is the only root that can have sealed it; a member's own `BackupKey` is never a candidate, since it sealed nothing in this set. The seal side does not move: a bound set never seals under the owner root. Without the read, the pass's own nest-sourced fetch could not open what it exists to move. **As built:** the holder's role is `FileDownloadKeys::set_owner`, which is read-only and never a seal input. `content_open_roots` consults it only for an unstamped record of a content-keyed set, bound-keyless included, and it reaches the owner's raw root plus its retired predecessors, bypassing `effective_owner_key` on purpose. An engine declares it with `SyncEngine::with_set_owner`. The pass (`SyncEngine::reseal_pending_under_current`) walks `Synced` rows everywhere and `Placeholder` rows on an owner's engine. Its nest-bytes source (`reseal_nest_copy_windowed`) (1) verifies the whole source in a bounded walk (`verify_file_by_manifest`) before any chunk is sealed under the new root, since a stored group-sealed chunk is readable by every member whether or not a manifest names it; (2) re-seals a `ManifestWalk` window at a time, keeping the source's chunk list, with each chunk's length checked against the size table; and (3) moves the recorded thumbnail rather than regenerating one. The post-re-seal verify is bounded too. The File Provider host drives the same rows one file at a time while the OS has no request waiting (`reseal_next_pending_under_current`, continuing past a row that will not open), armed at every build and every refresh. **Security note: why no production builder sets `set_owner` yet (refuting "pre-bind content by construction", 2026-09-27).** That claim rests on the nest. Change rows carry no binding to their set that a client can verify, path labels seal under the per-account owner root with no set in the derivation, and the caller's `role` is the nest's projection. So a malicious nest can point an "unstamped" row of a shared set at a manifest from another of the owner's sets. The owner root is per account, so that row opens. The pass would then re-seal it to the set's members. By spoofing `role`, the nest could do the same with a member's own private sets. The class predates this work: a nest that plants a row into an owner-only set before the share already gets it published by a resident owner's pass at bind. But enabling (D) would widen it to post-bind planting, to on-demand owners, and to members. The ruling that answers this — what lets a client tell a genuine pre-bind record of *this* set from a planted one, that it is the set's owner, and whether the pre-existing class closes — is the sub-bullet below.
    - **The provenance ruling — the pre-bind ledger (decided 2026-09-27, adversarially reviewed the same day; SUPERSEDED BEFORE BUILD the same day by *Writer-signed change records* (*Multi-writer* above, ruling (5)), which dominates it on every axis and closes the pre-act half it could not — kept verbatim as the record of the analysis, of the per-device guard and of the residual list the successor inherits).** The owner root opens a record of another set because it is per account, and the record's set is the nest's word — so no property of the record can tell a genuine pre-bind record from a planted one. What the client *can* own is **the set's contents as the owner saw them when the owner acted on the set**, and that is the ruling. **(1) What the ledger is.** At each **owner act that adds readers to a set** — `bind_set` (the first share, a served set's share included), each further `share_set`, `serve_enable`'s and `paywall_set`'s unshared branches (the three `custody::record_new_set` callers plus the admit path) — the identity-holding app folds the set's change log latest-per-path and records, **in the set's `FolderKeyCustody` entry, in the same atomic `__config` write as the custody change**, a **pre-bind ledger**: one digest per live (non-deleted) head whose `content_key_version` is `None`, `blake3::derive_key("fauna.prebind-ledger.v1", path_hash ‖ manifest_hash)` over the two hashes as **raw 32-byte values** (the record carries them hex; the encoding is pinned because two devices' ledgers merge), sorted. `Some(empty)` is a ledger (a set shared while empty); `None` is no ledger. **Every act unions a fresh fold into the entry's ledger** — never "keep the first": a serve/paywall off-window, or a second device's write racing the bind, leaves genuine unstamped heads the first fold never saw. The app also folds an **owned, content-keyed set whose entry carries no ledger** at its ordinary custody reconcile (the repair for a ledger a pre-ruling client's rewrite dropped). It is owner-only state — `BackupKey`-sealed, fleet-synced, loaded by every owner host through the custody load of [`../behavior/on-demand-files.md`](../behavior/on-demand-files.md) § Shared sets on a capability host, decision 1 (the File Provider host today; the agent once *One mechanism* converges it) — so no nest and no member can write one. A served set's ledger moves with its entry pseudo→real at share (`migrate_set_identity`); the `sets` merge arm joins ledgers by **set union, `None ∨ Some → Some`** (both are owner-authored views). **(2) What it gates — the owner-root OPEN, and only that.** The as-built `FileDownloadKeys::set_owner` role flag is **superseded**: `prebind_owner_roots` offers the owner root (and its retired predecessors) for an unstamped record of a content-keyed set **only when the ledger holds the record's digest**, decided from the record's own `path_hash` + `manifest_hash` at the open — the same rule for every key shape resolved from custody (`ResolvedFolderKeys` and its label and media twins carry the ledger beside `content_keys`). **The stamp's absence is never an admission** (a `public`-window member record is unstamped too — *Multi-writer* above); the ledger is the only one. The ledger does **not** select what the pass sources: the pass walks `Placeholder` rows on an engine whose entry carries a ledger, sources every unstamped head from the nest as built, and a head whose manifest is **plaintext** (a `public`-window record, re-sealed on the flip back to private by `converge_corpus_to_audience`) needs no root and re-seals regardless — gating the source would leave an on-demand owner's public-window files resting plaintext after the owner re-privatized. A ledger miss at the open is a **skipped row, warned, never an aborted pass** (the whole-pass drive today fails fast on the first row error; a planted row must not wedge it). A materialized (`Synced`) row re-seals from disk as today. **Ownership is the ledger's presence in the holder's own custody entry, never the nest's `role`** — but the boundary rests on the root, not on the ledger's absence elsewhere: the ledger admits a record to *the holder's own* owner root, and a member's root sealed nothing in the set, so a member's entry that came by a ledger (a nest-steered fold of a same-named set of the member's own — names are unique per owner only) opens nothing. Member custody-ingest merges generations, never ledgers. An entry without a ledger keeps today's refusal — the fail-safe direction on the security axis. **(3) What it closes.** Post-bind planting of an **owner-root-sealed** record, for on-demand owners, resident owners and members alike — a record the nest adds after the owner's last act is in no ledger, whatever set it was copied from, so the owner root never opens it (a resident owner's bound engine has no owner root for a pull either, `owner_open_roots` being empty on a bound set); role spoofing — the nest's `role` is not consulted, and a spoofed member has no root that opens the record; and replay of a record the owner deleted before the act — a deleted path has no live head, so no entry. The security statement, narrowed on review: **the set of owner-root-sealed bytes the pass can ever publish is fixed by the owner, from the owner's own view, at the owner's last act on the set; over those bytes the nest gains no power it does not already hold over stamped records.** What the ledger cannot see is a **plaintext** manifest (no `stored_hashes`): a bound engine pulls one under no root, and the pass re-seals it under the generation from the owner's device — nest-chosen plaintext, published as the owner's. That is the nest's existing integrity power over every set (it can write a plaintext record into any owner's folder today, and it held the bytes already), so it adds no confidentiality loss; it becomes an attribution escalation the day writer-signed change records land (the owner's device would sign nest-chosen bytes), which that design closes: the pass re-seals only rows that verify (*Writer-signed change records*, ruling (5)). **(4) What it does not close, deliberately.** The pre-existing class — a record the nest plants **before** the owner's act — is inside the owner's view by construction: the owner's knowledge of a set's contents is the nest-relayed fold plus this device's own edits, and no client-held check can separate a planted pre-act record from one the owner made on another device. It is bounded to the owner's own owner-root content — their other sets, their backups, and every predecessor identity's corpus (`predecessor_backup_keys`) — and it needs record-level provenance from creation to close: two end states this doc and its neighbours already name, neither this ruling's to build and neither blocked by it — **Phase 2's per-folder owner keys** (`../behavior/on-demand-files.md` § Who runs the hydration host → Phase 2) bind every manifest to its set cryptographically for content sealed after they land, and **writer-signed change records** — the per-manifest MLS group binding, ruled 2026-09-27 with its compat analysis (*Writer-signed change records* under *Multi-writer* above) — give the change log an owner-authored provenance and, being birth-shape, close this pre-act class outright. Two variants of the class are named because they lack even the visibility mitigation: the fold is **name-addressed** (`changes.list(folder = name)`, ownership enforced by the nest against the connection actor), so the nest chooses which log seeds a ledger, and a row it inserts between `fauna.folders.share` and the fold inside `bind_set` is pre-act to the fold — neither surfaces as a file the owner sees first. **Residual inside the ledger, regraded on review:** an entry the owner deletes or overwrites after the act and before the pass reaches it can be replayed by the nest as an unstamped head; the pass then **re-records that content as a new, stamped change from the owner's own device** — a durable, owner-authored resurrection that outlives the nest turning honest, which stamped-record replay (mere withholding) cannot produce. Two bounds: the re-record rides `is_resolution = true` with the device's own `derived_through`, so every reader whose path frontier passed the owner's later delete or head skips it as a stale resolution (`../behavior/conflicts.md` § Concurrent resolution & ancestor freshness) — what remains is a reader the nest kept from the later record, i.e. withholding; and the build adds a **per-device guard**: a device never nest-sources an unstamped head for a path it has itself recorded a stamped head or a delete for (a `SyncDb` tombstone), which closes it for the device that made the edit. The build may also **retire** a set's ledger once every entry is stamped in the app's own fold (a whole-ledger end state written by the custody writer, never a per-entry mutation from a custody loader); a nest lying "stamped" to hasten it only closes the read. **Availability, stated plainly:** a frozen ledger can exclude a genuine pre-bind file from the owner-root read — a second owner device's write racing the bind, a pre-ruling client's custody rewrite dropping the field, a nest omitting rows from the fold — and unlike a withheld blob the exclusion does not lift when the nest behaves; it lifts at the owner's next act on the set (the union re-fold) or at the no-ledger repair, and a resident owner device re-seals such a file from disk regardless. Decision 4's "every file stays openable on the owner's device" is therefore bounded by the ledger, and says so. **(5) Rejected candidates.** An owner-device signature over (set, path, manifest) on every record *is* the writer-signed change record above — ruled the same day as the end state for the change plane AND, under the 2026-09-24 baseline, the gate on this migration after all (the device key already rests in every host's principal slot, the wire field is additive, and the replay bound is the causal stamps'), which is what superseded this ledger. Per-set owner roots are Phase 2. Ownership from MLS state (the creator's leaf) is client-held but reaches no capability host (rule #7 keeps MLS state out of it) and answers only half the question. A ledger of manifest hashes alone would let the nest re-path an entry — harmless in content, but the digest over the pair costs nothing. **The strictly stronger candidate, rejected with its cost stated:** the identity app runs the nest-sourced re-seal itself at the act, over the same fold, and no host ever gets an owner-root read of a bound set — post-act planting closed by construction with no new custody field. Rejected because decision 4 degrades to "eventually" (pre-bind files unreadable on the owner's on-demand host until the app finishes), because on a phone the app is foreground-only and memory-capped while the extension is the process the OS keeps running, and because it re-creates a second driver of the pass one day after part (C) retired the resident drive to one home. (The rejection is for bound sets; the served, group-less set is (C)'s carve-out, where the app-run walk is the only one that can exist.) **Cost.** A whole-log, name-addressed `changes.list` fold from `FoldersAuthor` at every act (a nest heads projection is the build's option, not a requirement); one 32-byte digest per pre-bind file in the `__config` custody blob (a 10,000-file set adds ~320 KB until its ledger retires) — a set that outgrows the blob needs a separate owner-sealed ledger object, which is its own design, not a build-time knob; the field grows every custody-resolved key shape, not one struct. **Compat.** Additive at rest only (`FolderKeyCustody` gains an optional field; no wire change, no re-seal of any existing content); a nest of any version is unaffected; an older owner client neither reads nor writes the field and keeps today's refusal; its custody rewrite drops the field (`FoldersConfig` decodes without `deny_unknown_fields`), which closes the read until the repair above — never widens it.

- **Generation-stamping + selection on read.** Each snapshot stamps the `version` it was sealed under; the engine resolves `keys_for(version)` — all same-version candidates, current-first (see *Generations* above) — trying each on open, and **fails closed** if the holder lacks that generation entirely (the FS-BIND-5 posture — never fall through to a plaintext/owner-key read). `SyncEngine::chunk_root` becomes generation-aware (a version→key map / a current-version stamp on upload).
- **At-rest blob keying (FS-BIND-1/2 — ciphertext-hash).** A content-key-encrypted chunk is stored under its **ciphertext** hash, not the plaintext hash, so its AEAD body satisfies the nest chunk route's F9 anti-poisoning check (`blake3(body) == X-Content-Hash`) with no route change — the blob store stays a flat, content-addressed store that holds opaque bytes (the nest learns no group binding). The plaintext hash stays the AEAD key/nonce salt + integrity anchor; the per-chunk store key rides the manifest as `ChunkManifest.stored_hashes` (parallel to `chunk_hashes`). **Every** store-addressing site — the nest reachability/GC/restore sites, the forward + server-reassembly paths, **and the client-side sync-engine drain/resume worker** — must address the blob store by this ciphertext store key (`manifest.store_keys()` / the ciphertext hash), so a resumed content-key upload re-uploads its queued chunks under the same key the enqueue used rather than silently dropping them (see § Implementation status). The drain worker builds its match candidates under **every retained content-key generation** (current-first, `FolderContentKeys::generations`), not just the current one — a rotate-on-removal can land between the enqueue and the drain (the owner's own removal; the sole-writer model never bounded this), and an entry enqueued under a now-prior generation only reproduces its store key under that generation's root; store-key equality picks the right candidate, the drain twin of `keys_for`'s all-candidates open. A **prior-generation match is matched but never published**: the chunk never reached the destination before the rotation, the removed member holds that generation irrevocably, and chunk GET is unauthenticated by design — so uploading it post-rotation would hand the removed member content *first published after their removal*. Instead the drain **requeues the path under `current`**: the engine re-runs the shared re-seal trio (force upload sealed under the current generation → whole-file verify → best-effort supersede of the stale change record, whose never-uploaded chunks would otherwise dangle) — the change record IS re-stamped with the new generation — and completes the stale queue entries only after the re-seal lands (crash-safe: entries outlive the re-seal, so an interrupted requeue re-detects on the next drain; the re-upload is convergent + store-deduped). Candidate residency is bounded by the queued store-key set (sealed one generation at a time, only queued matches retained — never (generations × chunks) resident). Generation edge closed 2026-07-09; requeue-under-current + residency bound 2026-07-10 — pinned by `drain_after_rotation_requeues_prior_generation_chunks_under_current` + `map_retains_only_wanted_keys_and_marks_current_generation`. Manifest integrity is **structural** — group-gated change-log → content-addressed `manifest_hash` → content-addressed ciphertext → AEAD decrypt → plaintext-hash verify — so no separate manifest signature is needed under the owner-sole-writer model (the per-manifest group binding is ruled — *Writer-signed change records* under *Multi-writer*, 2026-09-27). The owner-only `backup_key` path is **reconciled with this route the same way** (FS-BIND FOLLOW-ON A — fixed + user-ratified 2026-07-07): it seals through the identical convergent `chunk_crypto` primitive under the domain-separated `BackupKey::convergent_chunk_root()` and keys the store by the ciphertext hash via `manifest.stored_hashes` (`version = None` — no generation history; **the manifest is self-describing: `stored_hashes` presence selects the convergent open, its absence means the stored chunks are plaintext — unconditionally, whatever keys the reader holds.** The former "absence + a key ⇒ legacy random-nonce framed open" reading is retired: the framed corpus provably never rested anywhere — framed uploads were rejected 400 by the F9 route, the very live-broken finding FOLLOW-ON A fixed — while the overload made a keyed reader treat genuinely-plaintext owner-only manifests as framed ciphertext, hard-failing every windows on-demand hydration of an owner-only file. A sealed manifest read by an engine with no key material fails closed, loudly — never a ciphertext passthrough). The ratified tradeoff — convergence leaks chunk-equality *within one owner* to the owner's own chosen destination, in exchange for dedup + idempotent retry — is recorded at [`owner-key-material.md`](owner-key-material.md) § Path A (Path A → *Backup chunks and manifests*); decision analysis tracked internally (decisive fact: the equality-leak is strictly dominated today by the unsealed manifest's plaintext `chunk_hashes` — the manifest plaintext-hash oracle hardening track at [`owner-key-material.md`](owner-key-material.md) § Path A). The engine also **fails loud** on the `upload_bytes` path when a destination rejects chunks (no manifest upload, no success report — the silent swallow that masked the pre-fix breakage is closed). - **Sealed manifest hashes (plaintext-hash oracle closed — read enablement landed 2026-07-08, the write flip 2026-10-02).** The `ChunkManifest` of a sealed-chunk upload (M2 bound sets AND convergent `BackupKey` backups) carries no plaintext `file_hash` or `chunk_hashes` beside the ciphertext `stored_hashes`: in the clear they are a candidate-plaintext **confirmation oracle** for the destination (it can hash a guessed file/chunk and test membership), strictly more signal than the ratified convergent-ciphertext equality (tracked internally, § 4). The manifest seals exactly those two fields and nothing else:
  - **Field split.** Sealed: `file_hash`, `chunk_hashes` — needed only by readers that already hold the set's chunk root (they are the AEAD key/nonce salts + post-decrypt anchors). Plaintext forever: `stored_hashes` (every destination site — GC, existence-check, restore staging, the forward path — addresses the blob store by it; the keyless-GC contract of `message-segment-store.md` § GC reference-walk needs nothing more), plus `total_size`/`chunk_sizes` (a size fingerprint the destination already reads off the stored ciphertext blobs themselves — ratified as accepted, inherent to chunk-granular content-addressed storage, not the manifest).
  - **Mechanism** (`fauna_core::manifest_crypto`): convergent ChaCha20-Poly1305, two-step BLAKE3 like `chunk_crypto` but domain-separated (context `"fauna.manifest.v1"` vs `"fauna.chunk.v1"`), sealing the canonical encoding of `{file_hash, chunk_hashes}` into the additive `ChunkManifest.sealed_hashes` field. The convergent salt is the **`stored_hashes` sequence** — the one identity of the sealed content a reader knows *before* opening (the plaintext `file_hash` cannot salt the seal: the reader would need it to derive the open key). Under a fixed root identical `stored_hashes` ⇒ identical payload, so deterministic nonce reuse only reproduces the identical blob (idempotent retry); the salt also *binds* the sealed blob to the store keys (a tampered `stored_hashes` list fails the tag check).
  - **Root per path = the root that already seals the set's chunks** — the M2 content-key generation stamped on the change record, or `BackupKey::convergent_chunk_root()` for the owner path. No new key category (§ Architectural rules #1); anyone who can open the chunks can open the manifest, nobody weaker can.
  - **Compat: one shape from the first public build (user-accepted 2026-10-01, the baseline survey's finding A1).** The write flip is a reader-compat break — a reader that predates the enablement cannot open a sealed-only manifest, since `chunk_hashes` is its pre-decrypt AEAD salt — and was deferred to a major bump for those readers alone; under the 2026-09-24 baseline reset none exists ([`version-compatibility.md`](version-compatibility.md) § Dimension 2, the fourth exception; the acceptance is recorded in [`compat-remnant-sweep.md`](compat-remnant-sweep.md)), so it landed in place. **Every writer** of a sealed-chunk manifest encodes `ChunkManifest::wire_form`: `seal_hashes` moves `file_hash`/`chunk_hashes` into `sealed_hashes` under the root that sealed the chunks, blanks them (`chunk_hashes` empty, `file_hash` the all-zero digest) and stamps `min_reader = 2` (`MANIFEST_READER_VERSION`, so a reader too old for the shape gets the honest typed "update this client" error instead of a hash-mismatch). The writers are `blob_seal::seal_reader` (the engine's batch upload, the custodian pull and store, the reseed, and the Go WebDAV MDA's PUT through `webdav_seal_file`), the engine's streaming upload, `nest_reseal`, and the `fauna-sync` daemon's `chunk_and_cache`; each keeps the plaintext view for its own use (progress, the held-body index) and uploads only the wire form. **Every reader refuses the other shape:** `ChunkManifest::check_hash_shape` — at the engine's `fetch_manifest` boundary, inside `unseal_hashes`, and in the daemon's `open_cached_manifest` — accepts a manifest carrying `stored_hashes` only with `sealed_hashes` and no plaintext hash, and one without `stored_hashes` only without a sealed blob; a sealed-chunk manifest naming its plaintext hashes is a permanent apply failure (`MANIFEST_SHAPE_REFUSED`), never tolerated. A reader that holds no root walks `store_keys()` alone: the nest (its `share_routes` server-reassembly refuses `stored_hashes` manifests; GC, check, restore and forward address blobs by store key) and the Go WebDAV MDA, which fetches by store key and opens through the shared walk (`webdav_open_file`). A public-audience folder's manifest carries no `stored_hashes` and stays plaintext — a live shape ([`encryption-at-rest.md`](encryption-at-rest.md)), not this one. From the 2026-10 baseline on, any further change to this shape is a major bump with a transition window.
- **Sealed names & paths (`fauna.path.v1` — mechanism ratified 2026-07-29; build-out status owned by `../behavior/path-sealing.md` § Sealed names & paths, not restated here).** User-chosen file/folder/set labels seal under **the same roots as this section's chunks** — a bound/served set's content-key generation, or `BackupKey::convergent_chunk_root()` for the owner path — via a two-step BLAKE3 derivation domain-separated from `fauna.chunk.v1`/`fauna.manifest.v1`, convergent only where the salt determines the plaintext (`path` salted by `path_hash`, set `name` by the floor's `name_hash = blake3::derive_key("fauna.set-name.v1", name)`), explicit-random-nonce for mutable labels. **No new key category** (rule #1): whoever opens a set's bytes renders its names — members via M2 custody, the MDA via the `WebdavKeysBlob` session keys, nobody weaker. Mechanism, envelope shape, hash companions, and the expand→migrate→contract staging are owned by `../behavior/path-sealing.md` § Sealed names & paths; the ruling + tightening set by `encryption-at-rest.md` § Carve-outs.
- **Admitting a member (share #2..N — design ratified + BUILT 2026-07-23; UI/behavior owner: `../ui/folders.md` § Sharing → *Adding the 2nd..Nth member*).** The owner stages an MLS Add on the set's **existing** group and distributes it exactly as a conversation add-participant does: on the gated plane through the device-owned-epoch commit rebase loop (`CommitGate::gated_add_member`'s discipline — each rebase round re-stages and re-mints the Welcome, so the delivered Welcome always matches the landed commit); on the ungated fallback with **Rule-1 ordering** (stage → persist the local pending → send on the set's claimed channel → merge only on an accepted send, clear on a failed one — `devices.md` § Cross-device MLS group-state sync), so a merged-but-undistributed Add is unrepresentable and existing members are never stranded an epoch behind. **No rotation on admit** (history-on-join, above: the newcomer receives the full generation bundle) and **no custody sentinel**: the removal sentinel exists because a rotation's staged fresh key is irrecoverable once distributed (§ *Custody* above); an add mints no key material, so nothing needs pre-staging. The one add-specific crash artifact is the **Welcome** — MLS mints a Welcome only inside the Add commit itself, so a fresh Welcome for an already-added leaf is *not constructible* — and its loss is made **visible and gesture-recoverable** rather than resume-driven: the newcomer reaches the owner-visible roster only at delivery (same-nest `actor_channels` at `welcome.deliver`; cross-nest `channel_foreign_members` at the federation relay), so every post-merge crash leaves them *in the group but off the roster*, rendered truthfully as not-yet-shared, and the owner's **re-share heals**: on-roster → no-op access refresh; in-group + off-roster → evict the ghost leaf through the ordinary rotate-on-removal machine (its rotation is unnecessary when the Welcome provably never landed, but harmless — reusing the proven crash-safe machine beats a bespoke rotation-less evict), then admit fresh with a new KeyPackage. For the discriminator to stay sound, a recipient **decline** (and a Blocked-sharer suppression) must drop the roster row via the existing self-scoped `fauna.folders.leave` (relayed cross-nest) — behavior owner: `../ui/folders.md` § Sharing. **Envelope ordering:** after the Add merges, the owner re-publishes the envelope (same generations — the `current_version` floor is unchanged, and the nest-side monotonic MAX makes the re-put safe) **before** delivering the Welcome, so the joiner's join-time custody ingest (`maybe_ingest_folder_custody(channel, true)`) opens it at their post-Add epoch instead of failing closed against a stale-epoch seal with no timely retrigger; the distributed Add commit is itself the existing members' re-ingest trigger (`applied >= 1` on the folder commit poll). `bind_set` is **not** re-run on an add (genesis + the pre-bind re-seal are first-share-only; every post-bind upload is already sealed under a generation the joiner receives). **The nest enforces the binding itself (added 2026-07-24).** "Never a fresh group, never a re-bind" is a property of the *stored* `folders.mls_group_id`, and the nest — not the client — is the authority for that name→group mapping, so `share_core` refuses a `fauna.folders.share` whose `group_id` differs from a set's existing binding with the typed `already_bound`. Client-side discipline alone was insufficient: the roster and the read gate both project over the set's **current** derived ChannelId, so accepting a differing id silently drops every earlier member — and full bidirectional client↔nest compatibility within a major version (`version-compatibility.md`) means a pre-fix client, whose `share_set` unconditionally minted, would keep doing exactly that against fixed nests for the whole 1.x window. A **loud** refusal on the old client is the deliberate choice over a silent revocation on the new one. Only a *differing* id is refused: a first share (`NULL` binding) is the ordinary first-binder claim, and re-sending the set's own id is the add path's idempotent claimant re-bind (below). The refusal lands **before** the namespace claim, so a rejected gesture leaves no stray claim or roster row on the fresh channel. There is deliberately no re-bind-after-unshare gesture — nothing in production unbinds a set — and if one is ever wanted it rides an explicit unbind step, never a silent UPDATE.

**Vs a staged removal on the same channel: resume-then-add** — the add branch first drives any staged `pending_removals` sentinel on the channel to completion; if the removal defers (gate not engaged / unconverged / poisoned-deferral), the add refuses with a typed retryable error — it never builds a commit over an unmerged pending and never clears another machine's sentinel; both machines serialize on the backend's per-channel lock. A set bound to a group this device's engine does not hold (a fresh device before its replica restore) likewise refuses the add retryably.

- **The same crash window in CHAT `add_participant`, and its heal (BUILT 2026-07-29).** `libs/fauna-conversations/src/backends/fauna_mls.rs::add_participant` distributes an Add exactly as the folder path above does — gated rebase loop or ungated Rule-1 — and so inherits the identical artifact: the Welcome lives only in a local variable until `fauna.conversations.welcome.deliver` returns, so a fault after the merge leaves a **phantom leaf** (a genuine MLS member with no way to join). The heal is the folder one, and for the same reason: a Welcome is mintable only inside the Add commit that mints it, so the ghost leaf is **evicted through the ordinary removal machine and re-admitted with a fresh KeyPackage** — never "re-deliver the Welcome", which is not constructible. **The discriminator is the nest's routing roster** `actor_channels`, read by the member-scoped `fauna.conversations.channel.actors` (the chat twin of the folder owner's `actor_members_list`): the nest writes the row at Welcome delivery, so *seated in MLS but off the roster* is exactly "the Welcome never landed". **Local MLS state alone is NOT a discriminator** — a healthy member and a phantom are byte-identical to `find_leaf_by_identity` — so the three arms are: on-roster → **idempotent no-op** (chat has no access row to refresh, and re-admitting a healthy member would cut them off and re-invite them for nothing; the add-participant overlay does not filter existing members, so this gesture is reachable); off-roster → evict + re-admit; **roster unreadable → refuse and surface a specific, actionable error**, never a guess. (The roster write at delivery is itself best-effort — `register_actor_channel_gated` logs rather than fails the Welcome — so a lost row degrades to an *unnecessary but safe* evict-and-re-admit of a healthy member, never to a member left broken.) That last arm is what keeps the heal sound where the roster cannot answer — an **older nest** without the kind, on either side of the relay. A **foreign-homed channel** (the caller joined from a cross-nest Welcome, so the authoritative roster lives on the channel's home nest) is no longer refused: `roster_holds` passes the recorded home URL through the seam, which rides the **distinct kind** `fauna.conversations.channel.actors_remote` → the `fauna.federation.channel.actors` relay to the home's union (2026-07-29; the kind's contract — gate, disclosure, no-auto-register, and why it is never an additive field — is owned by `federation.md` § Cross-nest, the *Channel roster read* row), degrading to the refuse arm whenever any hop cannot answer. On the channel's **home** nest the roster answers for everyone (2026-07-29): `channel.actors` is the union of `actor_channels` and `channel_foreign_members` — the same substrate rows the Welcome relay already writes for every channel kind — so cross-nest members heal exactly as same-nest ones there, and a healthy cross-nest member is an idempotent no-op, never an evict + re-invite. The **local identity is refused up front**, before the discriminator: the owner's own leaf answers `find_leaf_by_identity` like anyone's, and an owner who has not yet posted is not on the roster either, so an unguarded heal would classify the caller as a phantom (openmls's `CannotRemoveSelf` is a backstop, not a design). One channel-lock acquisition spans evict + re-admit so the background inbound poll cannot interleave mid-repair. The composition's own half-done state — evict landed, re-admission did not — is deliberately **not** a new recovery problem: it is strictly better than the phantom it replaced (the group is internally consistent, the member is simply absent) and a plain retry takes the ordinary fresh-add path, which is what the error says.
- **Rotate-on-removal (the forward-secrecy property).** Owner commits the MLS Remove (epoch advances; the removed member loses future epoch secrets) → **distributes the Remove commit to the remaining members** (5d(d), landed 2026-07-08: `FoldersAuthor::drive_removal` posts the commit bytes to the set's claimed channel as a `ChannelEnvelope::Commit` over the ordinary `fauna.conversations.channel.send` — the same plane conversation membership commits ride; the claimant-owner passes the claim/roster gates, and the send push-nudges the roster. The commit obeys the ratified merge-ordering rule (`devices.md` § Cross-device MLS group-state sync, Rule 1: the merge is the **point of no return** — MLS cannot re-produce a commit for an already-merged transition and remaining members cannot skip epochs, so a merged-but-lost commit strands every remaining member with no detector and no repair) by one of two routes. **Gated** (the leg passed the session backend to `FoldersAuthor::with_removal_gate` and the multi-device plane injected a `CommitGate`): `drive_removal` routes the Remove through the device-owned-epoch commit rebase loop (`MlsStateSync::send_commit_gated` — stage → CAS-put the provider replica, a durable identity-stamped staged pending → gate-send under `expect_no_commit_since` → merge on accept), after an entry pass that walks the set's channel log to head (healing a sent crash-window commit via the shared own-leaf resync arm) and defers whenever the log still carries a commit the engine has not incorporated; on this route the sentinel carries no commit bytes and the send happens inside the loop (tier_3 proof over the real gated plane: `conformance_shared_folders::gated_removal_rotates_and_excludes_removed_member_real_nest`; the byted and byte-less resume paths are pinned by its `byted_resume_…`/`gated_resume_…` siblings). **Ungated fallback** (no gate injected): `drive_removal` stages the Remove (`remove_member_staged` — the epoch does *not* advance), **persists the staged pending locally** (`MlsEngine::save_state`, so a restarted engine can still merge exactly this commit), records the bytes on the `FolderPendingRemoval` sentinel, and only then `merge_pending_commit`s, persists the merged state, and sends. On resume, durable sentinel bytes are **preferred and never rebuilt over**: the resumed drive merges the restored pending if one is staged and re-sends the identical bytes (a harmless duplicate append, quiet-skipped as past-epoch); a fresh commit is built only when nothing can have been distributed — and **byte-less alone is not that proof**. Bytes land before the merge (which precedes the send) on the **ungated** route only; the **gated** route distributes its commit *inside* the rebase loop and records **no bytes** on the sentinel at all. So a byte-less sentinel **loaded from custody** may carry an already-distributed commit, and on a plane whose gate is wired but never engaged (`GatedRemoval::GateNotEngaged` — still restoring, or a permanently failed load, e.g. an older nest with no `fauna.mls` plane) there is no walk-to-head to settle which. The safe fresh-build condition is therefore whether an **engaged gated attempt ever started for the sentinel** — recorded durably on it as `FolderPendingRemoval::gated_attempted`: staged `false` at creation, and stamped `true` by `drive_removal`'s gated leg strictly *before* the gate runs (the stamp precedes anything that can distribute; engagement is a one-way latch, and a not-engaged probe never runs the gate, so it never stamps). A byte-less sentinel still stamped `false` is **provably undistributed** and builds ungated — the single-device / no-plane fallback that must keep working, since on a nest whose `fauna.mls` plane never engages an unconditional defer would leave the removal permanently incompletable (the member never evicted, the content key never rotated — a client-causable unrecoverable state). A byte-less sentinel stamped `true` **defers retryably and stays staged** for a later, wired launch, rather than rebuild blind and fork the group — the same posture `recover_poisoned_removal` takes. (The stamp proves nothing about a **peer device's** attempts beyond what config sync has delivered, and the ungated leg's freshness check is a *local* group read — that staleness window is the accepted cost of the single-device fallback, closed only by the gate's walk-to-head; the stamp is a per-sentinel proof, not a cross-device one. The merge folds it as the lattice join `None < Some(false) < Some(true)`, so a stamp from any device survives a custody union.) The guard covers the **foreground** path too, not just the launch resume: `remove_member` *resumes* an already-staged sentinel for its `(channel, member)` rather than re-staging, so a user clicking Remove again after an interrupted gated attempt would otherwise reach the identical blind rebuild with no crash or launch involved. **The restored pending must be *this* sentinel's own commit before it may merge**: the engine stamps each staged commit's identity (`blake3` of the wire bytes) durably beside the pending in its provider KV (`MlsEngine::pending_commit_hash`), so a byted resume compares that against `blake3(sentinel.commit)` and **refuses to merge a foreign pending** — a sibling drive's gate-staged Remove that happens to sit in the shared engine (reachable because the durable-bytes rule below routes a byted sentinel through the ungated merge leg on the gated plane too). Merging a foreign pending while broadcasting the sentinel's own bytes would fork the group and clear the wrong sentinel; on a mismatch the resume fails loud and leaves the foreign pending untouched (it may be the sibling's only merge source). Sentinel bytes with *no* recoverable pending and the member still present is a **poisoned** sentinel — reachable not only as a legacy pre-durability remnant but in two ordinary ways: a **web/wasm tab reload** between the sentinel write and the send (`persist_group_state` is a no-op on wasm), and a **cross-device union** of a byted sentinel onto a peer device that never staged the pending. A plain resume cannot merge it (no pending) or safely rebuild it (the bytes may already have been distributed → fork) or seal it (that would go under the pre-removal epoch), so it fails loud with the typed `PoisonedRemovalSentinel` — but it is **not a permanent brick**: `resume_pending_removals` isolates per-entry errors (one poisoned sentinel never `?`-aborts the loop and starves the healthy removals selected after it under byted-first ordering — the exact *client-causable unrecoverable nest state* `principles.md` § Client-recoverable nest state forbids, since `pending_removals` lives in nest-persisted account state — the `fauna.state.folder-keys` kind's removal rows, [`config-dissolution.md`](config-dissolution.md) § The `__config` dissolution schedule → *The kinds*), and it auto-recovers via `FoldersAuthor::recover_poisoned_removal`, which routes the removal through the commit gate's **walk-to-head**: the walk heals a distributed commit (→ member off the leaf → finish the rotation the poisoned drive never published) or, on a clean walk with the member still present (proving the commit was never distributed), safely re-stages a fresh Remove — fork-free either way. Because every real plane (native/linux/web) wires the gate, the launch-time resume discharges a poisoned sentinel with no user step; only a client with no gate engaged yet defers it retryably to the next launch. **The launch-time resume is driven at the restore-completion point on each of the three planes** — `libs/fauna-ffi/src/mls_sync_launch.rs::resume_folder_removals` (which the session factory injects into *every* native session, so apple/android/windows inherit it with no per-app glue), linux's `conv_backend.rs::resume_folder_removals`, and web's `conversations.ts` post-`restoreMlsState` call. That placement is load-bearing, not incidental: the removal gate **is** the session backend, and the replica restore is what injects its `CommitGate`, so a resume driven any earlier would see `NoGate` and defer every gated sentinel to the next launch. **The resume has a second edge, and runs at whichever is later (2026-10-01).** The staged removals rest in the folder-key custody, which rests in the seat's account runtime ([`config-dissolution.md`](config-dissolution.md), the `fauna.state.folder-keys` entry), and on every seat that runtime assembles on its own task with no ordering against the conversations launch — so at the restore-completion point custody may not be readable yet. Readable: the pass runs in line, before the first poll, as before. Not readable: the pass is detached — it waits the assembly out and runs then, beside the receive loop, and the trigger returns at once. Two other shapes were refused. Awaiting the runtime in line puts the receive loop (on web, the manager every page waits for) behind the account store's assembly, for the full write wait of about 30 s when the runtime never comes up. Keeping the plain read answers "not running", which the trigger logs and swallows, so the removed member stays keyed until a launch that happens to win the race — what every launch of the web folders journeys did. Running beside the receive loop is no new posture: a foreground `remove_member` resumes a staged sentinel mid-session under the same guards. The resume reads custody as a write does (`FolderKeyStore::load_for_write`), which is what the detached leg waits on; the native legs share one body (`fauna_client_folders::FolderRemovalResume`, holding the backend weakly across the wait so a torn-down session ends it), and web decides on the start promise it already holds (`$lib/launch-pass`: in line when the runtime has started, else once the start settles and the build is still the tab's; a start that failed skips the pass and says so). The same pass re-runs the foreign-home seed, which the restore runs over the same custody and so in the same race; the seed fills only holes, so the second run is a no-op wherever the first could read. Pinned by `orchestration::tests::resume_before_the_runtime_assembles_waits_rather_than_skipping_the_launch`, `launch_resume::tests` and web's `launch-pass.test.ts`. The gated plane itself is pinned end-to-end by `conformance_shared_folders::launch_recipe_resumes_staged_removal_over_backend_engine_real_nest`, which builds the author exactly as the launch triggers do (group-crypto seam from `backend.engine()`, gate from the same backend). *(Wired 2026-07-12. Until then the three exports existed and were fully tested but **no client called any of them** — the recovery machinery was green at tier_1/tier_3 and entirely dark in production, so a crash-staged removal was never re-driven and this paragraph's guarantee did not hold. The tier_3 pin above exists so that cannot silently recur.)* A *legacy* pre-5d(d) sentinel carrying no bytes still has nothing to distribute. Durable sentinel bytes **outrank the gate**: a byted sentinel always resumes through the ungated leg *without consulting the gate* — on the ungated route the merge precedes the send, so the bytes may describe a **merged-but-undistributed** commit (a plain failed send reaches this state, no crash needed), and the gate would classify that merged engine as "member already absent" and silently discard the bytes, stranding the remaining members. So a merged-but-undistributed commit *is* representable on the ungated route; what is unrepresentable — on both routes — is a merged-but-**unrecoverable** one: a merged commit always leaves durable re-sendable bytes (the sentinel) or a durable identity-stamped replica pending (the gated loop), and, since the merge was made durable, a resumed rebuild racing an already-distributed commit is unrepresentable too. Two serializations protect the restored-pending merge source: a fresh ungated build defers while a **byted sibling sentinel** exists on the channel (and `resume_pending_removals` drives byted sentinels first), so the sibling's restored staged pending is never cleared or staged over; and the whole ungated commit section holds the backend's per-channel lock when a backend is wired (`RemovalCommitGate::ungated_channel_lock`), so the background folder poll cannot merge a foreign commit mid-window) → generates a fresh generation (`rotate`) → re-publishes the envelope under the **new** epoch → new uploads seal under the new generation. Each **remaining** member applies the commit through the folder commit poll (`poll_inbound_folder`, driven by `ConversationsSession`'s receive loop over an engine-derived, restart-durable channel set, using the same typed `process_commit` dispatch as the chat rail), advancing its epoch so the re-published envelope opens — the epoch-advance **liveness** half (tier_3 proof: `conformance_shared_folders::remaining_member_advances_epoch_via_distributed_remove_commit_real_nest`); the removed member's *exclusion* never depended on it. **A remaining member's custody reaching their record is only half of them still being able to read the set; the other half is the running engine re-keying:** a bound set's content keys are **final at engine build time**, so a running engine keeps the generation it was built with until it is rebuilt. The desktop sync agent rebuilds it at its own edges — the ingest's custody write fans out the custody nudge the agent re-resolves on (a `config.put` and its `__config` nudge until 2026-09-30, the `state-fleet` nudge since), and its engine stamp (which keys on the current generation) does the restart; the owner's own rotation reaches the owner's agents the same way ([`../behavior/on-demand-files.md`](../behavior/on-demand-files.md) § Shared sets on a capability host → *One mechanism* owns the edges; proven by `bins/fauna-sync-agent/tests/agent_custody_rekey.rs`). The ingest also raises the optional `FolderCustodyObserver` on `NestFolderCustodySink`, fired **only** when the merge genuinely advanced custody (never on a no-change re-ingest) — until 2026-09-27 the apps' hop to re-push a content-key blob, which the agent no longer needs. Without a re-key a member's engine would stay on the pre-rotation generation and every subsequent owner upload fail closed in `content_open_roots`: **fail-closed and logged, not data loss**, but the member would silently stop receiving new content. Tier_3 proof of the edge (both the positive edge and the must-not-fire negative): `conformance_shared_folders::member_custody_ingest_notifies_the_observer_only_when_custody_actually_advances_real_nest`. Pre-rotation content stays readable to prior holders (they kept those generations — acceptable, they could already read it); the removed member can read **neither** the re-sealed envelope (new epoch) **nor** post-removal content (new generation). **This is the crypto-layer basis the "removed member can't read post-removal content" guarantee rests on — never the nest roster.** Rotate-on-removal MUST *also* evict the removed member from `actor_channels` (a metadata-confidentiality fix, since the un-evicted roster still admits the removed member to discovery-metadata reads). The eviction holds only because the `group_id → ChannelId` namespace is **claimed** (first-binder-wins, the `folder_channel_claims` table — see § Implementation status): without the claim a removed member could re-insert themselves back onto the roster, so the claim guard is the co-requisite that makes the one-time `members.evict` durable. The claim guard gates **every** roster-add path onto a claimed channel, across three families that would otherwise let a removed member re-insert themselves directly: (1) the `share`-rebind; (2) the three raw `actor_channels` auto-register paths of the shared **same-nest** conversation rail (`channel.send` / `channel.fetch` / a self-addressed `welcome.deliver` — `bins/fauna-nest/src/conversations_handlers.rs::folder_channel_claim`: on a claimed folder channel only the claimant may drive an auto-register (an unresolvable claim read gates the same way as a real claimant would); unclaimed DM/group/scheduling channels are untouched, since their delivery depends on that auto-register); and (3) the **cross-nest** RECEIVING twin `fauna.federation.welcome.deliver` (`bins/fauna-nest/src/federation_handlers.rs`). The cross-nest federation welcome has **no local claimant** to compare (its caller is a peer nest, not an authenticated actor), so — consistent with the ratified **cross-nest-always-knocks** design — for a claimed folder channel it does **not** pre-register the recipient on knock-delivery **at all**: a knock leaves the roster untouched, and the recipient joins only on explicit accept (`join_folder_welcome`, the sole cross-nest roster-add). Unclaimed DM/group/scheduling federation welcomes still auto-register (their delivery depends on it), and the guard consults this nest's authoritative `folder_channel_claims` record rather than the peer-supplied `channel_type`, which is untrusted. And (4) the cross-nest **RELAYING** twin — this nest's own `fauna.conversations.welcome.deliver` when the caller names a peer `nest_url` (`conversations_handlers::welcome_deliver_core`'s relay branch), which grants the recipient's home nest a `channel_foreign_members` row via `db::channels::register_foreign_channel_member`. That row is the other half of the one channel roster (`federation.md` § Cross-nest shared folders + channel append) and is an authorization grant in its own right: `require_foreign_member` serves the peer `channel.fetch` (the channel's application-message ciphertext) and `channel.actors` (its roster in the clear) off it, and the push fan-out dials every home nest listed in it. Here the caller IS a locally authenticated actor, so the gate is the ordinary `claim_permits` one, on the same claim read the function's other two roster consumers share: on a claimed folder channel only the claimant may relay a grant, `Unclaimed` stays permissive **for the first grant** (a first cross-nest DM Welcome's caller is on no roster yet, and its delivery depends on the grant) but not for a **rebind** of one that already exists, which additionally requires standing on the channel — the claimant, or a current roster member — because `home_nest_id` is the authorization itself and `federation.md`'s ratified TOFU premise charges its last-writer-wins residual to *the inviter's* power, not to knowledge of the channel id (which an MLS-removed ex-member keeps, removal advancing the epoch and not the id), an unresolvable read refuses. So eviction is durable against the share-rebind, the same-nest raw self-insert, the cross-nest federation self-insert **and** the relay-side foreign-member grant; content confidentiality never depended on the roster (rotate-on-removal above), the roster gate is a metadata-disclosure hardening. The conversation-reuse case (a folder sharing a conversation's MLS group) is **refused at the claim, for anyone** (2026-07-29): `claim_folder_channel` denies every claim on a roster-populated channel — same-nest members in `actor_channels` **or** cross-nest members in `channel_foreign_members`, the union `list_channel_actors_union` reads — member or not. Admitting a roster member — the previous rule — let any member of an ordinary group chat take a **permanent** claim on their own group's channel (INSERT and SELECT are `folder_channel_claims`' only operations tree-wide: no DELETE, no cascade, no admin surface), which then froze the group, because the claim gate of that era refused every non-claimant Commit (the owner could no longer add, remove or rotate) and the claim-read gate silently suppressed the owner's roster writes on later Welcomes. (The nest Commit gate has since been re-ratified to roster-membership admission — 2026-08-24, `federation.md` § Cross-nest shared folders + channel append, which owns the rule — but the claim-refusal stands on the claim-read gate's suppression and the permanence alone.) With no release path, a member could do that and abandon the account, leaving the group unrepairable by any client — the `nest/common.md` § Client-state recoverability invariant. Refusing costs no shipped flow: a first share mints a fresh group (empty roster), and the 2nd..Nth share's idempotent claimant re-bind is answered before the roster is consulted. A stranger who planted a roster row on that same fresh, still-unclaimed channel before the real share completed would deny the legitimate claim exactly as permanently; the acceptance rests on the channel id being cryptographically unpredictable, not merely on the roster's brief emptiness — `ChannelId::from_group_id` (`libs/fauna-mls/src/types.rs`) BLAKE3-derives it from the MLS group id `MlsEngine::create_group` has openmls mint with no caller-supplied value (`libs/fauna-mls/src/engine.rs`), so a stranger has no id to target in that window. **When a real conversation-binding flow is built it must make the claim-read gate (`folder_channel_claim`/`claim_permits`) conv-reuse-aware (it must not suppress the conversation owner), add a claim-release path, and decide explicitly what happens to any pre-existing `channel_foreign_members` rows on the channel it binds (inherit them deliberately, or refuse to bind a channel carrying foreign grants the claimant never issued) — never re-open the claim branch alone.** (The Commit gate's half of that old obligation is discharged: since 2026-08-24 it admits any rostered member's Commit, so co-members were already never refused.) See the design (tracked internally, ratified 2026-06-25) § slices/3.
- **Voluntary member leave (`folder-leave-button`) is NOT rotate-on-removal.** When a *member* voluntarily leaves a set shared with them (the recipient-side `folder-leave-button`, `docs/goal/ui/folders.md` § Sharing), the owner does **not** rotate the content key: the leaver keeps the generations they already held — "forward secrecy from yourself is not a meaningful threat" (§ *BackupKey* threat model above). Leaving is two self-scoped, idempotent halves: the nest `fauna.folders.leave` drops the **leaver's own** `actor_channels` row (so off the roster their `content_key.get` is denied — they receive no *future* rotations), and the client locally forgets the MLS group (`MlsEngine::forget_group`, dropping the set from the `has_group`-filtered member-visible list). No `ownerSecret` and no owner action are required; the leaver's ghost MLS leaf, if any, is cleaned by the owner's ordinary rotate-on-removal later (now signalled by the roster drop). Rotate-on-removal above stays the owner's tool for *involuntary* removal, where forward secrecy against the removed member is the requirement. **Landed 2026-07-02** (shared Rust + native FFI; the wasm face + per-app render are the `*-folders-ui` follow-on).

**Per-content per-group seal — `derive_post_key`** — `libs/fauna-core/src/subscription/crypto.rs`

- **Root:** the MLS group's `epoch_secret` (when `Post.gated.key_access = Mls { group_id, epoch }`).
- **Derivation:** two-step BLAKE3 — `derive_key(context = "fauna.gated.v1", key_material = epoch_secret)` for context separation, then `keyed_hash(intermediate, post_id)` for the per-post salt.
- **AEAD:** ChaCha20-Poly1305 with a random 12-byte nonce per call (random because one per-post key seals the post body and N attachments together — per-blob nonces must not collide; `docs/goal/ui/media.md` § Encryption at rest).
- **Protects:** group-restricted posts' `encrypted_ref` body (`docs/goal/behavior/restricted-posts.md` § Encryption at rest, "Group-restricted" class) and any media attached to those posts (the same per-post key seals body + attachments together with random nonces per blob, per `docs/goal/ui/media.md` § Encryption at rest).

**Per-content per-group seal — `derive_blob_key`** — `libs/fauna-mls/src/blob_crypto.rs`

- **Root:** the MLS group's `epoch_secret`.
- **Derivation:** `BLAKE3::derive_key(context = "fauna.blob.v1", key_material = epoch_secret)`.
- **AEAD:** ChaCha20-Poly1305 with a deterministic 12-byte nonce derived as `blake3::keyed_hash(nonce_key, plaintext)[..12]`, the nonce key epoch-derived under the domain-separated context `"fauna.blob.nonce.v1"` — a content-addressed blob store dedups identical plaintext under the same epoch by construction; convergent encryption is the feature. **The nonce is a stored value, not a re-derived one** — it rests in the clear as the sealed blob's first 12 bytes (readers take it from the prefix; the write-side derivation is free to change with no migration), which is why it must be **keyed**: an unkeyed `blake3::hash(plaintext)[..12]` prefix was a keyless ~96-bit candidate-plaintext confirmation oracle and a key-independent cross-group/cross-owner correlator for anyone holding the sealed bytes — the nest by construction, or a backup thief — contradicting [`owner-key-material.md`](owner-key-material.md)'s ratified no-confirmation/no-correlation property, and out of reach of § *Sealed manifest hashes* (that remedy seals two manifest fields; this carrier is the blob's own at-rest layout). Keyed 2026-08-31 with no major bump: readers never re-derive the prefix, so every pre-keying blob still opens. **Residual:** blobs sealed before the keying keep their unkeyed plaintext-derived prefixes at rest until re-sealed under a fresh write — for that back-catalogue the oracle stands; no re-seal pass is scheduled (the blob store is content-addressed and messages reference blobs by `sealed_cid`, so a rewrite is a coordinated re-reference, not a cheap rewrite-in-place).
- **Protects:** conversation **attachment** blobs — media/files attached to a conversation message, sealed client-side via `libs/fauna-mls::blob_crypto::encrypt_blob` (`libs/fauna-mls/src/engine.rs::seal_conversation_blob`), stored in the content-addressed blob store and referenced by `epoch` from the message — and group-shared MLS-attached blob content generally (including media-page items attached to a conversation, per `docs/goal/ui/media.md` § Encryption at rest). **NOT the conversation message *body* itself:** the body is sealed by the **MLS application-message layer** (`create_message`/`process_message`, per-sender ratchets — `libs/fauna-mls/src/engine.rs`, the `ChannelEnvelope::Application` seal), stored opaque in the per-channel segment store (storage shape: `message-segment-store.md`); the nest never opens either the body or the attachment, ever (no identity signing key — rule #7 / MLS-2). *(The earlier "Protects: group conversation messages" text conflated the message body with its attachments — corrected 2026-07-03; conformance: `docs/goal/architecture/encryption-at-rest.md` § Per-content-kind conformance row `Conversation messages`; mechanism: `docs/goal/architecture/message-segment-store.md`; UI authority: `docs/goal/behavior/conversations-at-rest.md` § Encryption at rest.)*
- **Why two functions, not one:** the KDF (BLAKE3) and AEAD (ChaCha20-Poly1305) are now harmonized between the two primitives — the choice was ratified 2026-05-15, picking the modern stack already used by `BackupKey`, `chunk_crypto`, FTS index, and the wrapped-blob HPKE wraps (this codebase's dominant pattern). The two functions remain distinct because they take *different inputs* — `derive_post_key` adds a per-post salt (the `post_id`), `derive_blob_key` doesn't (epoch alone is sufficient since the blob hash already provides per-blob discriminator) — and because they use *different nonce strategies* per the bullets above (random for posts, deterministic for blobs).
