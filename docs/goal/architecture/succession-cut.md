## The succession cut

  - **(11) The succession cut — the set nonce re-minted by the successor, and the take-over that follows (ruled 2026-10-02, design pass; adversarially reviewed the same day in three passes — seven blocking findings re-ruled, then five more the corrections opened, then two one-clause ones in the re-rule, every correction folded in; refutable downstream; PARTLY BUILT, (a)–(c) and (g) — § Implementation status today).** Residual (vii) of (5): a succession ends a retired identity's access and not its power to sign for the sets it owned or wrote, so whoever holds the retired seed, with a nest that lies, can plant a row there that every reader admits as the successor's own ((8)(b)) and opens under the retired root ((8)(c)). Ruling (9)(e) left the form of the cut open and named seven findings as this pass's brief; each is answered below by its number. **The design turns on one fact the code already states: a reader admits nothing it cannot verify, every signature covers the set's nonce (`SignedChange::set_nonce`, the statement's first field), and a reader holding no nonce for a row holds a row that is not a record.** So a cut carried *by the nonce itself* is the one cut a nest cannot withhold from a reader without withholding, from the same reader, every row the successor writes after it — the withholding power the plane already accepts as residual (ii), and nothing more. A cut carried by anything else — a per-set closure the successor signs, a field on custody, a field on the envelope — is served or withheld on its own, and finding (i) stands against it: a reader served the old envelope and every later row never learns of it. The forms weighed and refused are in (k).
    - **(a) The cut is a re-mint, and it is an invariant of the owner's custody, driven by custody and never by the nest's listing.** *An owned set's live nonce is one the current identity minted.* `FolderKeyCustody` gains `minted_by: Option<ActorId>` (the identity whose device minted the entry's nonce; the plane's set row carries the same field) and `replaces: Option<[u8; 32]>` (the nonce this entry was re-minted over — the **lineage** that tells a re-mint's retirement from a delete's, (b)); both join as `None ∨ Some → Some`, two `Some`s to the lexicographic minimum (deterministic, like every custody field — "write-once" in effect, since one device mints one entry); the create helper (custody (d)) writes `minted_by` and no `replaces`. The owner's custody reconcile (custody (g)) gains one more arm, run first and **over custody, not over the nest's listing**: for every LIVE **owned** entry — one that carries a `name` (a member's received copy carries none, custody (a)) — whatever the nest lists (a set the nest hides from `files.list`, or tags `member`, is cut all the same, and a nest that then refuses the push has stopped the set visibly, at every launch), whose `minted_by` is not the current identity, a predecessor or `None` alike (so a successor that proves its predecessors by the statement walk alone, with no attested seed, re-mints too; and every set that predates this build is re-minted once at the first launch after it, on accounts that never succeeded too — a one-time cost of one update per set, accepted), it **re-mints**: first it writes the device-local **adoption marker** of (d) for the set (its own store, not custody — naming the nonce it is about to replace); then, in one custody update (load, join, write — the plane's door joins, there is no CAS, and two owner devices re-minting at once leave two live entries that custody (c)'s pick settles, the loser retired as a *sibling* in the lineage, (b)) it retires the live entry (`retired_at = now`, keeping its nonce, keys and name as (e) does — **a re-mint's retirement is never lifted**: `lifted_at` applies to a refused delete only) and adds a live entry under 32 fresh bytes with the same `channel_id`, `keys` and `name`, `created_at = now`, `minted_by = the current identity`, `replaces = the old nonce`; then it pushes the pick to the nest (custody (f), `FolderUpdateRequest::set_nonce`); and **on a bound set it re-publishes the envelope** ((b); today nothing publishes one after a re-mint — a build requirement, not a description) — the push and the re-publish are ordered again whenever the pick changes, a race loser's retirement included. Order matters and is stated: the marker, then custody, the nest's copy, the envelope, records last — a record signed under the new nonce before the nest holds it is refused `signature_invalid` at ingest and retried, never a planted row. **Where it runs:** in the post-store-ready pass of the succession aftermath (`run_ledger_aftermath`, which holds the account store the custody lives in — `run_succession_aftermath` does not), and at every launch of an identity-holding app as part of the reconcile, so a device that missed the aftermath cuts at its next launch; the arm needs the current identity as an input the reconcile does not take today. Its order against the corpus re-seal is immaterial: the re-seal runs in the sync agent at engine start, and rows the current identity records under the old nonce stay current by (c)(3) and are moved onto the new one by the re-record leg's first arm. The arm is idempotent and crash-safe by what custody already is: a crash after the write leaves the reconcile to push and re-publish on its next pass; a crash before it leaves the set un-cut and the arm to run again, the marker standing. No ceremony step, no new kind, no clock. The window between the ceremony and the arm is residual (j)(2).
    - **(b) What a reader holds: the set's nonce lineage, each nonce with its minter — carried to members in an owner-signed envelope that only moves forward.** `ReaderBinding` gains the set's retired nonces beside the live one, each paired with its `minted_by` (`None` where none was recorded), **and the owner's chain in order** — built from succession statement links, nearest predecessor first (`proven_predecessors` already returns that order; the registry's attested set and the roster's map do not carry one), since (c) asks which of two identities is the earlier — on every reader: the engine, the Media and version-history projections, the peer leg. **The retired list is the lineage, computed by nonce, not a timestamp filter:** the `replaces` walk from the live entry back, **plus every entry connected to it by `replaces` edges** — the connected component around the walk, so a re-mint race's loser and an entry re-minted over that loser are in it alike (members may hold either, and the first re-record arm must still read them) — **plus this incarnation's create-race losers**, which are the same-name entries custody (c)'s pick retired (`FolderKeyCustody` gains `retired_by_pick: Option<[u8; 32]>`, the winner's nonce, stamped by the reconcile's retire; a timestamp comparison across two devices' clocks would let a fast clock put a deleted earlier incarnation into the lineage). A second succession keeps the first cut's nonce (the `retired_at ≥ live_created_at` filter alone would drop it), and a deleted earlier incarnation of the name, which no edge reaches and no pick retired, stays out, exactly as custody (e) and ruling (2) need. `migrate_set_identity` keeps nonces across the pseudo→real channel move, so the lineage is walked by nonce across entries, never by channel. *The owner's hosts* read the lineage off custody (`FolderEngineKeys` carries the live and retired nonces today; it gains the minters and the siblings; the ordered chain reaches the engine on the agent's own `predecessor_actor_ids`, nearest first, which the owner's binding takes as its chain). *A member* reads it off the envelope: the payload gains `minted_by` beside `set_nonce` and `retired_set_nonces`, the lineage with its siblings as (nonce, minter) pairs, newest first — additive on the payload — and `merge_received_keys` writes them into the member's entries, so a member that joins after a cut still verifies every row written before it, a member's retired list is no longer empty by construction, and the foreign binding's empty retired list is retired with it. **Two rules the carrier needs, both new, and both unconditional.** *Every envelope is owner-signed:* the sealed payload is an AEAD under the group epoch's export secret, which every member at that epoch holds, so a member with a nest's help could seal a payload naming a nonce of its choosing — and a signature required only on a payload that carries the new fields would be stripped with them. So the blob the nest stores becomes signature-plus-ciphertext: `FoldersAuthor`, which holds the identity key and runs every publish (`publish_envelope`, `finish_rotation`, `republish_envelope`), signs the ciphertext under a `fauna.folders.envelope.owner-sig.v1` tag in the `sig_domain` registry, and a member ingests **no** envelope without a signature that verifies — there is no legacy payload to accept before the first public release, and after it the baseline rule applies. *What a signer may move:* a payload that changes the member's live nonce, or extends its lineage, is accepted only when signed by the **current owner as the member's MLS-recorded folder-owner marker names it** (`ReaderBinding::owner` — the marker a witnessed succession re-points inside commit processing, which no served roster can roll back; never the nest-served roster row) with a `minted_by` inside the owner's chain, `None` included (an equality with the signer would add no security under that signature and would stall a member added, or a set created, before the arm has run); a payload signed by a proven predecessor of the owner may confirm what the member already holds and nothing more, so a stolen predecessor seed with a colluding member seals nothing that moves a nonce. A member whose marker has not yet been re-pointed refuses the successor's envelope — fail closed, keys and nonce discarded together since the payload is ingested as one unit — and retries on its next poll, which is why a refusal must count as a non-merge; and because a member re-fetches the envelope today only when the epoch advances, **a member re-fetches it also when a row of the set is refused `signature_invalid` or when the set's pushed nonce changes, and the owner re-publishes whenever the published epoch is behind the group's** (a retried sweep can advance it after the arm's publish) — build requirements, both. *A member's nonce moves forward only:* `record_received_set_nonce` replaces today whatever the opened envelope names; it refuses a payload whose live nonce the member already holds as a retired one of this channel, or whose lineage, siblings included, does not cover the member's own, so a stale envelope cannot un-cut a member and a race loser's envelope does not lock a member out of the winner's. A legitimate re-create of a deleted set is unaffected: it is a fresh MLS group, so a fresh channel, with no member history to move forward from. **The judge verifies under the live nonce first, and only a row that fails there is tried under each retired nonce** — one extra signature check per retired nonce per failing row.
    - **(c) The reader rule — three verdicts where there were two.** Let the *owner's chain* be the set's owner together with every proven predecessor of the owner, in order ((8)(b): on the owner's own hosts the statement walk over the account's recorded successions, the attested set alone carrying no order; on a member's, the roster's owner-row statements **under the owner the reader's binding names — the channel's MLS-recorded marker of (b), which the roster's owner row never moves, forward or back**: a link is signed by its successor alone, so an owner read off the row that carries the link would be whoever claimed the last one, and (8)(b)'s precedence protects the owner's rows only while the owner is not read off that row; a host that holds no marker reads the owner off the roster's owner row — ruling (3)'s foreign rule, on every set — and never off the list row's `owner_actor_id`; the peer leg's cached roster, which must store each predecessor *beside the writer it belongs to* — (8)(e)'s text, which the code flattens to a writer row today, (i)). **Membership in the chain is decided by the statements alone, before (8)(b)'s precedence**: a roster that also lists a predecessor as a `writer` in its own right does not lift it out of the chain — arms (1) and (2) below fire first, and (8)(b)'s *A as a writer itself wins* orders only the rows (3) admits. For a row that verifies cryptographically under nonce N with minter M, signed as A: **(1) N is the live nonce and A is a strict predecessor of the current owner:** if M is a *strict successor* of A the row is **refused** as history-era, never current — the nonce was minted after A retired, so A never legitimately signed under it, and the row is a plant by construction (the nest holds every nonce in plaintext, so this arm is load-bearing: without it the thief signs under the new nonce and (8)(b) admits the row); if M is A itself, or earlier than A in the chain, the row is **current** — an inheritance not yet cut: A signed under a nonce that was live in A's own time, which is exactly the state ruling (8) admits until the arm of (a) runs. A minter the reader cannot place in the chain — a `minted_by` that names an identity the roster's statements do not reach — **holds every row signed as a strict predecessor** (the current owner's own rows are never held by it) and the reader re-reads the roster, as (8)(e) has it for a writer it cannot yet attribute. **(2) N is a retired nonce and A is a strict predecessor of the current owner:** the row is **history**. **(3) A is the current owner, or outside the owner's chain** — a member writer, or a member writer's own predecessor by (8)(b): the row is **current** under the live nonce and under a retired one alike. The current owner's own rows are current under every nonce of the lineage because the thief cannot sign as the current identity, and making them history would cost what the review found: a stale successor device re-recording a file the successor deleted in the window, and a resolution stamp over rows the device never read. A cut touches nothing a member signed. A `minted_by` of `None` reads as the earliest identity in the owner's chain — earlier than, or equal to, every predecessor — so under arm (1) an un-cut set admits every predecessor's rows exactly as ruling (8) has it until the arm cuts it. **What history is:** a row a reader lists among the path's versions, rendered under the identity it was *signed as*, and nothing else — never a head, never a delete, never input to the corpus re-seal or any re-record except the take-over of (d), never a conflict candidate except through ruling (10)(b)'s restore branch, never this device's own echo; **and a history head the fold passes is noted as a skip** (`note_permanent_skip`), so the honest anchor of every later stamp on that path accounts for it and no device claims to derive from a row it never applied — with one consequence stated: the head re-judge of (8)(f) folds **current** verdicts only, never a history one, so the skip note a history *delete* leaves is never the licence (8)(f) reads to fold that delete. The verdict carries the same `writer` and `signed_as` as a verified row, so attribution and the root bound of (8)(c) are unchanged for a history row a restore later opens. The head re-judge treats a nonce arriving as a gain (a `nonce:` token beside the `signer:` and `root:` ones): a reader that learns the cut re-reads its passed heads once; a history verdict on a head it had folded is a loss, which (f) never acts on — (e) says what happens to that entry.
    - **(d) The take-over — what the successor records in place of what the cut made history.** One pass per set, **on the set's owner's own hosts only** (a member device holding the content keys could otherwise re-sign owner-chain history as itself and (c)(3) would admit it), run on every owner device when its reader's binding gains a nonce and at engine start: **what:** every path whose newest non-retention row is history under (c)(2). **From where — the device vouches for what it holds, and the nest's word is adopted once (finding (ii) and the review's fifth):** a device holding local state for the set (any `sync_entries` row — synced, placeholder, or awaiting hydration) re-records **only paths in that state, at the manifest that state holds**; **nest-sourced history is adopted only under the adoption marker of (a)** — a durable, device-local, per-set marker the re-mint arm writes before its custody write (the cut runs in the identity app and the take-over in the sync agent's engine, two processes; the marker crosses that seam in the device's own store and rides `FolderEngineKeys` to the engine), consumed by the engine on that device — the one that comes up for the set, or the one already running when the re-pushed keys arrive — and **the spend is recorded durably on the engine's side, keyed by the nonce the marker names, after the adoption completes**, since the app re-pushes `FolderEngineKeys` at every agent start and the marker would otherwise be spent again; *holds local state* is judged on the state that existed before the marker arrived, so an adoption that skipped every head does not read as a fresh device next time, and a crash part-way leaves the spend unrecorded and the remainder adopted on the next start; a crash between the marker and the custody write leaves the marker standing for the arm's next pass. A device whose re-mint lost custody's pick holds a marker too, since it performed a cut. A set for which no engine ever runs on the cutting device is never adopted: its owner-chain rows are history until a restore (f), and the ruling says plainly that where the marker is consumed the take-over is the nest's word at that moment ((j)(3)). No other device, however fresh, ever adopts history — **the arm `rerecord_predecessor_heads` loses its nest source: it reads local state under this rule, and the nest only under the marker.** **How — no re-record skips the open (finding (iv)), and who signed a head is durable state:** each head's label and bytes are opened under the roots (8)(c) allows its *signer* (`RecordSigner::Predecessor(A)`); the signed-as identity of every entry's head is **persisted per local entry** at the fold — today it lives in an in-memory map that reads as the current identity after a restart, so a planted placeholder naming a manifest the successor sealed in another set would open under the current root and be re-sealed here, the copied-record escape through the very arm meant to close it — an entry whose signer is unknown opens under no owner root (fail closed). Where the bytes rest under an owner root they are **re-sealed** under the current root and recorded as the corpus re-seal records (`reseal_predecessor_sealed` is this arm for owner-root sets, and it already reads local entries); where they rest under a content key (a bound set, a served set's generations, `retired_content_keys`) or in plaintext (a public set) they are **re-signed over the same manifest** once the label and the manifest have opened under that key — admitted for stamped heads now because nothing in it reaches an owner root, and since ruling (10)(c)'s bound (the stamp binds the root) is built — and the plaintext path the record door needs is the opened label. A head that opens under none of the roots its signer is allowed is **skipped and noted**, never re-signed (the `SIGNER_BOUND` note). **As what:** the current identity, the live nonce, a resolution stamp over the path's honest anchor (never an edit stamp — the device authored nothing; under the marker that anchor sits below the head being re-recorded, so a peer that folded that head stale-skips the re-signed row — harmless, since it is the same manifest), **and no `changes.supersede`**: that door marks every older row of the path with a manifest, whatever its manifest, and the version listing excludes what it marks, so a supersede after a re-sign would erase the path's whole history, members' versions included; the history row stays as the version it is, and quota is (g)'s. **What it skips:** a path whose live-nonce head already carries the manifest this device would record (another device took it first; the nest's exactly-once does not cover this, since actor and signature differ). **Deletes (finding (iii)):** on an owner-only set no delete is re-recorded — every row is the owner's chain's, a history tombstone and an absent path read the same, and the tombstone stays in the version history for `undelete`; on a shared set the cut itself makes a predecessor's admitted delete history, so a member's older row it deleted resurfaces as the head on every reader until the take-over re-records that delete as the current identity's — and that record lands, since the nest door lands a signed delete over a tombstone signed under a retired nonce (built 2026-10-02); a predecessor's history *edit* over a member's older row is re-recorded as the current identity's in the same pass, and a reader that had not folded the predecessor's edit shows the member's row as head only until that record lands.
    - **(e) What a reader already folded (finding (v)).** A cut is a loss of admission, and (8)(f) never folds on a loss; nothing on disk is unlinked. An owner device holding local state is, by (d), the device that re-records those very entries, so what it folded before the cut becomes the current head under the live nonce in the same pass. The one entry that stays behind is a head the device cannot open under its signer's roots: it stays on disk as noted history — recorded nowhere, listed as a version where the projection lists versions, and **never offered to the nest as new local content** — a build requirement, since the scanner's new-file path reads no note today: a path carrying the `SIGNER_BOUND` note is held, not a file with no head, because re-uploading it would launder exactly the row the note refused.
    - **(f) Version restore (finding (vi)).** A predecessor's version stays listable and restorable after the cut: the version history lists history rows as versions under the identity they were signed as, and a restore takes (8)(d)'s branch as amended — its bytes and label opened under a root (8)(c) allows the version's signer, re-sealed under the current root where they rest under an owner root, recorded under the live nonce as the current identity. The restore is the user's own vouch for one named version, which is what makes it the right door for content the take-over left as history; the sync agent's `restore_file_version` refusal of an inherited version stands until it opens, and the Media restore's opening form is the one both share (ruling (10)(b)).
    - **(g) Quota (finding (vii)) — the charge is transferred, never skipped (BUILT 2026-10-03 — § Implementation status today).** A re-sign over the same manifest adds no bytes, so it adds no charge: **the charge follows the (path, manifest) pair across the path's listable rows, held by exactly one of them**: a record whose `manifest_hash` equals the path head's takes the head's charge — the meter is owner-pays (`vet_row_charge_in_conn` keys on the owner, plus the recorder's member cap where the recorder is a member), so the owner's total moves by nothing and the check is on the *net* charge, never refused at a full quota for a move — **and "a record" here is every door that mints a charged row, the two conflict doors included (ruled and built 2026-10-03; the reading and its reasons are [`../behavior/file-versions.md`](../behavior/file-versions.md) § Retention (4)'s)**; the former head is marked `charged = false` (a per-row flag, `true` on every row that exists today) and its member half is released through the version accounting, which a retired or departed recorder already makes a no-op. **When a charged row leaves the listable set** — the supersede door, the soft-prune, the retention purge — **and an uncharged listable row of the same path and manifest remains, the flag moves to the newest such row instead of crediting**; a release credits `size_bytes` only where the leaving row is the charged one and no such row remains; and **`undelete` of an uncharged row charges it iff no listable row of its path and manifest is charged**. So a head whose bytes outlive a pruned sibling is never left charged to no one — the review's reverse case, a same-manifest re-record followed by a new head and a soft-prune of the re-record, holds the charge on the surviving row — and nothing is released twice. A charge that is merely skipped, and a transfer that frees the former head for good — the first two shapes — were each refused on review: in the first the former head, pruned by retention, credited its bytes back while the live head held them free; in the second the re-record, pruned, credited them back while the former head still listed them. The re-seal arm moves real bytes and is charged as the corpus re-seal is today, with the reclaim of part (B) releasing the predecessor-sealed chunks behind it; a successor whose quota cannot hold one re-sealed file is the corpus re-seal's standing shape, not this ruling's.
    - **(h) Where the cut rests, and who can write it.** The cut is custody state the live owner writes through the account plane and the owner-only `folders.update`, under the current identity's keys, and reaches a member only in an envelope the current owner signed (b); the retired identity is refused at every authenticated door (`refuse_if_superseded`), the nest's echo selects nothing (custody (c)), the plane joins only — `retired_at` is never cleared on a re-mint's retirement, `minted_by` and `replaces` join to one value — and a member's nonce moves forward only, so no stale copy, no served blob and no member-sealed or predecessor-signed envelope can un-cut a set or re-mint one under a predecessor's name. *A closure signed by an identity that has itself since retired* is therefore moot: there is no closure statement to sign; a later succession re-mints again, and the earlier cut stands as the retired entry it already is, kept in the lineage. *Two writers of one set who both succeeded* each hold their own chain; only the owner's cuts. *A row signed by an earlier predecessor* (P0 → P → W) is in the owner's chain and is cut with the rest.
    - **(i) The other doors.** *The peer leg* (`judge_peer_row`) runs the same judge over the same binding, so it needs the lineage and the owner's ordered chain in its cached roster and nothing of its own — and that cache must change shape: today it stores every proven predecessor as a writer row, under which a cut predecessor would win (8)(b)'s precedence and be admitted; it stores the chain beside the writer, as (8)(e) states (a build row of this ruling; (8)(e)'s status entry is corrected with it). *Choose-winner and the review list* are bound by ruling (10): a history row's manifest named as a candidate is an admitted *version*, takes the restore branch and opens before anything is signed. *A served set's pseudo-device rows* are unsigned by class, nonce-free and exempt under (1)(i) as amended by (8)(d); a cut leaves them exactly as they were (residual (vi)), and the owner's adoption of (7)(b) signs them under whatever nonce is live. *The Media and version-history projections* judge every listing whole with the same verdicts: a history row is listed as a version and never as the current item. *The re-record leg's first arm* (`rerecord_under_live_nonce`, this device's own heads under a retired nonce, its deletes included) keeps its race-loser purpose — the lineage's siblings and create-race losers are exactly its input — and after a cut it also moves the rows the current identity recorded under the old nonce onto the new one; those rows were current throughout by (c)(3), so the move is a convenience, and under (g) it transfers each charge rather than doubling it.
    - **(j) What it closes, and the residuals.** Closed: on every reader that holds the live nonce — the owner's hosts, every member that has ingested the owner-signed envelope published at the cut, every peer that has read the roster — no row signed as a predecessor of the set's owner is ever live state again, under a retired nonce (history) or the live one (refused). Residuals, stated: **(1)** withholding the cut is withholding every later row — residual (ii), unchanged in power and now visible as a set that stops moving; a nest that hides a set from the listing no longer escapes it, since the arm runs over custody; **(2)** between the ceremony and the arm, the set stands as ruling (8) has it; a row planted in that window and folded by an owner device before that device's binding gained the new nonce is local state on that device, and (d)'s local-state rule **launders it** on that device — every owner device that folded a window plant does, not only one under the marker — bounded by the window's length on each device and by (8)(c)'s reach; **(3)** the one pass that adopts the nest's word — a cut under the adoption marker on a device holding no local state for the set, every former device lost — launders whatever the nest serves under the retired nonce at that moment (two devices whose re-mints raced each hold a marker, so two such passes can run; and the one-time re-mint at this build's first launch writes markers on accounts whose succession is long past, where what it adopts was already admitted under (8)); what the thief can plant is content sealed under the retired root or earlier ((8)(c)), **or, on a bound set, under a content-key generation the retired seed's custody holds** — the sweep removes the thief's seat and advances the MLS epoch, and since 2026-10-03 the successor then rotates each inherited bound set's content key ([`../behavior/succession-aftermath.md`](../behavior/succession-aftermath.md) § Re-key scope, the MLS groups row, which owns the rule and its limits), so this reach is the generations minted before that rotation and no later one; **(4)** a predecessor's genuine delete the nest withheld from a device is resurrected when that device re-records the file it still holds — residual (i)'s shape on the successor's side, and permanent for that path, since the delete is history once served; **(5)** a member writer's own succession is not cut: the member's predecessor rows stay admitted as (8)(b) has them, which is residual (iii)'s power (the nest names who writes) and no more — and (iii) reaches the owner's chain on a *member's* reader by the same door: a nest that lists the retired owner as a `writer` and withholds the owner row's succession statements has that member admit the retired identity's rows as that identity's own, since the reader cannot place it in a chain it was never shown; the row then reads as a retired identity's, never as the successor's, which is what (iii) always allowed and what (8)(c) already bounds; **(6)** rows no surviving device held, and sets for which no engine runs on the cutting device, are history until the user restores them, one version at a time — an availability cost, recoverable, never a loss; **(7)** a current member of a bound set can still seal a *keys* payload's ciphertext (the AEAD is group-held), but no member can publish one — every envelope a member ingests is signed by the owner as its roster states — so the remaining power is the pre-existing one over the keys a member already holds. **The WATCH:** a planted row reported from a deployment whose cut ran on a device with state re-opens (2) and (3); a deployment where (6) bites re-opens the question of a bulk restore gesture, and the ceremony UI offering to defer the cut to a device with state is the lever for (3) — neither is ruled now, since both add a user gesture to an emergency flow for a case no deployment has met.
    - **(k) Compat, and rejected.** At rest additive under the 2026-09-24 baseline: `minted_by` and `replaces` on `FolderKeyCustody` and the plane's set row (`#[serde(default)]`; `None` reads as (c) says); the envelope payload's `minted_by` and `retired_set_nonces`; the owner signature beside the ciphertext in the stored blob, which the nest keeps opaque as today; the engine's per-entry persisted signer and the device-local adoption marker; the `charged` flag on the nest's row. Wire additive: `FolderEngineKeys` (the identity app → agent push, a wire type) gains the minters, the siblings and the marker (the ordered chain rides the agent's `predecessor_actor_ids`, nearest first — a second copy on every entry would carry nothing new); `ReaderBinding` is in-process; `FolderUpdateRequest::set_nonce` exists; the record request is unchanged; the content-key put carries the same opaque blob. Birth-shape, by the baseline: no envelope exists that lacks the signature and no reader exists that lacks the three verdicts before the first public release (an envelope stored before the build is unreadable to the new opener until the owner's one-time re-mint re-publishes it, which the same rule covers); the nest parses none of the blob (`content_key_put_core` checks a non-empty hex under the channel claimant, re-keyed at a succession), so the signed blob is no change at its door; after the release the baseline rule applies in full. **Rejected.** *A per-set closure the successor signs, in custody or on the envelope* (the first draft, (9)(e)): finding (i) — served or withheld on its own, so it narrows (vii) to the readers that happen to hold it. *A cut by time* — the predecessor's rows live for a window after the succession statement's `created_at` by the reader's clock: the statement's stamp is the client's and unchecked, the row's `created_at` is the nest's, so the window measures when a reader reads and nothing about when a row was written; it hides what no device took over inside the window and launders what any device took over after it. *The re-mint with the reader rule as it stands* — one live nonce, everything else refused: it hides every member's rows and the whole version history under the old nonce from every reader, and a departed member's or a lost device's heads vanish for good; (c)'s three verdicts are what makes the re-mint affordable. *Making the current identity's own pre-cut rows history*: no thief can sign as the current identity, and the review showed the cost — a stale device resurrecting a deleted file and a resolution stamp over rows never read. *Every device re-recording every head the nest serves, or any later fresh device adopting history*: finding (ii) and its sequel — the device vouches for what it holds, and the nest's word is adopted once, under the marker, or never. *"The pass that performs the cut" with no marker*: the cut and the take-over run in different processes, and a crash between them would lose the one adoption. *A fresh device deferring its cut until a device with state has run, or until the nest's device list says none exists*: the device list and the live heads are the nest's word, so a lying nest defeats the deferral, and the honest case only loses availability. *The reconcile's arm over the nest's listing*: the nest would choose which sets are cut. *An owner signature required only on a payload that carries the new fields*: stripped with them. *A predecessor's signature moving a member's nonce*: the stolen seed with a colluding member. *`minted_by` equal to the envelope's signer as a condition*: no security under the current owner's signature, and a stall for a member added or a set created before the arm ran. *The nest-served roster as the signer anchor*: a rolled-back roster naming the predecessor as owner; the MLS-recorded marker is what no served row moves. *The lineage as the bare `replaces` walk*: it drops a re-mint race's loser, locking members onto it, and the create-race losers the first re-record arm reads. *A supersede after the same-manifest re-sign*: the door erases the path's versions. *A skipped charge on a same-manifest record*: (g). *Re-signing without the open*: finding (iv), the escape (8)(d) closes. *A nest door that marks a signer's rows history*: the nest's word, and a mutation door on a log whose integrity is that it has none ((8)(h)). *A cutoff by `seq`*: (8)(g). *Cutting a member's predecessor on the member's re-grant*: the owner's re-mint cannot reach it under (c)(3), and a rule that reached it would rest on the roster — the nest's word — and buy nothing (iii) does not already concede.
