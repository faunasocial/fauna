# Deployment threat model — a compromised nest, and what it can reach — target state

Owns: deployment-threat-model, vulnerability-disclosure

Status: ratified 2026-10-10 (the two concerns and their split are the user's framing of 2026-10-10; path 5's project cut re-drawn the same day around the acceptance nest of [`org-nests.md`](org-nests.md); refutable by the user); Concern 2 widened 2026-10-17 from "reaching a dev machine" to "reaching beyond itself" with path 6, the lateral-movement map between the organisation's nests, on the owner's ask of that day, and path 5 re-cut the same day on the owner's word — four projects, cut by who holds each token, superseding the 2026-10-10 two-project cut; amended 2026-10-17 — § Disclosure (the owner's ruling on the security policy's promises)

Authority: owns the **threat enumeration over a deployed nest** — who can attack a running box, with what, and what a box that has fallen can reach next — and the **mitigation chosen per path**. Two concerns, kept apart because they have different attackers and different victims: **(1) remote compromise of a nest**, by (a) anyone on the internet or (b) an authenticated user of that nest; and **(2) a compromised nest reaching beyond itself** — a dev machine, the build infrastructure, the release path, production DNS, a sibling nest or the cloud project and zone the nests share (lateral movement). This doc owns no mechanism: what a port *reveals* → [`nest/network-exposure.md`](nest/network-exposure.md); runtime crypto and transport trust → [`security.md`](security.md); the sealed-at-rest property and the grant primitive → [`encryption-at-rest.md`](encryption-at-rest.md); the loopback trust between co-resident processes → [`security.md`](security.md) § Co-resident process trust boundary; third-party code on the nest → [`third-party.md`](third-party.md); the trust tiers of the ship pipeline and the supply chain → [`release-integrity.md`](release-integrity.md) § Trust tiers; the firewall and verify-job mechanics → [`image-pipeline.md`](image-pipeline.md) § Image tags & channels.

## Goal

A nest is the one Fauna machine that talks to strangers: once a deployment serves real users its ports are open to the whole internet, its source is public, and a strong model reading that source will find bugs faster than any review did. The posture therefore never rests on "the attacker will not find the bug". It rests on three things, each owned elsewhere and assembled here: **what a stranger reaches is small and memory-safe, and the native parsers that remain are isolated**; **what a box holds is sealed, so a fallen box yields metadata and keys of its own, not user content**; and **trust flows one way — from the dev fleet toward the boxes, never back** — so a fallen box cannot climb to the machines that build what every other box runs.

## Concern 1 — remote compromise of a nest

### 1a — anyone on the internet

**The surface.** With no cloud firewall in front of it (the organisation's acceptance nest, which runs open by design — [`org-nests.md`](org-nests.md) § fauna.build — and any box once its firewall is lifted) a nest listens on: tcp 443 (the SNI router: WS-RPC and HTTP to the nest, CalDAV to the mail bridge by SNI), tcp 25 / 465 / 587 (SMTP: the Go mail bridge as MTA and submission), tcp 993 (IMAP: the Go mail bridge as MDA), udp 7842 (the P2P relay's address discovery), tcp 80 only while a certificate is obtained over HTTP-01 — and **nothing on tcp 22 (user-ruled 2026-10-11): a deployed box runs no sshd and holds no SSH key, the organisation's nests included.** **A hand-built box is bound as a target, not as a precondition (ruled 2026-10-17):** the no-sshd posture is a property of every box Fauna provisions and of the organisation's boxes, which the installer achieves; a box its admin built by hand reaches it once the box is claimed — Fauna's own install needs no shell after the claim either — so the guide for a hand-built box tells the admin to disable sshd, or at least to close 22/tcp to the world, after claiming, and says why; the nest cannot enforce it, and an sshd an admin keeps open is that admin's own exposure, outside this model's guarantees and never a reason for the nest to behave differently. No app needs a shell on the box — the only humans in a deployment configure through the apps, and every recovery is the admin's app's ([`nest/box-recovery.md`](nest/box-recovery.md)) — so the provisioning cloud-init disables sshd and allows no 22/tcp, the app passes no provider key at create and writes none into a box unless its admin chose the SSH door (below), and the provider's own fallback for a keyless server (a generated root password, mailed to the account holder, with password login left on) is thereby closed too. The fleet observes a box only from the outside: the health endpoint carries what the gates compare (the build commit, the build id, and the sha256 of every binary the image ships — not the image digest, which no image can carry about itself), and the loopback-only ports are asserted by an external probe that finds them closed. There is no debug key and no env-gated exception — a box that needs its own logs read is reached through the provider's out-of-band console by the user, or re-created ([`testing.md`](testing.md) § Gap 3) — and the lasting answer is a product affordance, not a shell: **the nest serves its recent service logs to its admin in the app, over the authenticated connection**, on every nest — built (`fauna.admin.logs` and the `admin-logs` page on all seven apps, [`apps/observability.md`](apps/observability.md) § 3 Surfaces), with the co-resident services' signals reaching it through that doc's sidecar log plane. **One exception, chosen by a box's admin — the SSH door (owner-ruled 2026-10-20, superseding the hand-applied `dev.fauna.build` exception of 2026-10-14):** a box's admin may, in the provisioning wizard and **default off**, give their box a **key-only** sshd (password and keyboard-interactive login off, root by key only) that **admits the one public address the admin's app claims the box from**, and nothing else — widening it is the admin's own act over the door, before their address changes; the private key is minted on the admin's device for that box alone and written to that device's `~/.ssh` (or handed to the admin once, on a phone or web), the app keeps no copy and the nest never holds it, the health endpoint never advertises the door, and Fauna never needs it (no install, update, recovery or configuration path uses a shell; changes made over it are unsupported). The mechanism — the cloud-init drop-in, the claim-time one-address whitelist through the nest→host maintenance channel and the root coordinator's one-shot `ufw` rule, where the key is written — is [`installers/vps.md`](installers/vps.md) § The SSH door; the wizard's toggle and its warning, [`../behavior/onboarding-provisioning.md`](../behavior/onboarding-provisioning.md) § 5. **What it costs this model:** a box with the door open carries one more pre-auth listener — OpenSSH's, key-only, reachable from one address — and the wizard says in plain words that this reduces the box's security; a nest compromised in the claim window may choose that one address (the reachability of a key-only sshd from one place, not a shell), after which the channel is inert. **What it does not change:** every box whose admin leaves the toggle off is the keyless, sshd-less box this section describes; a channel-keyed or env-gated sshd stays refused (the Dev channel is one any admin may subscribe to); no throwaway e2e box ever opens the door. **The organisation's nests:** `dev.fauna.build`'s admin opens it — today the box carries the hand-applied, console-typed sshd of 2026-10-14 (key-only, one box-only key on Linux, 22/tcp from Linux's address in `ufw` and the cloud firewall), which the owner's 2026-10-20 console run showed unworkable to repeat (the provider's VNC console has no paste and a US key map) and which the door replaces on the box's next re-creation, the kept-box drive opening it through the app as any admin would; `test.fauna.build` and `fauna.build` keep it shut, and `deploy-verify` asserts `:22` closed on them. Behind them, by implementation language: the nest itself and every wire parser it owns (HTTP, WebSocket, DAG-CBOR, the signed-envelope recipe of [`security.md`](security.md)) are Rust; the mail bridge is Go; and two **native C/C++ parsers** read every inbound mail before the bridge does — the ClamAV and rspamd sidecars of the compose bundle ([`release-integrity.md`](release-integrity.md) § Third-party container images). The sidecars are the largest body of memory-unsafe code a stranger can feed, and they are fed by anyone who can send mail.

**What an anonymous caller can do before any identity.** The pre-identity WS surface (`node.info`, `handle.available`, `node.resolve`, `actor.by_handle`, `claim_admin`, invite verification) is throttled per source and, for the claim path, globally; what each answer may reveal is [`nest/network-exposure.md`](nest/network-exposure.md)'s. Federation inbound (nest↔nest, ActivityPub, the ATProto relay crawl) is gated by relationship and signature checks owned by the bridge docs. The outbound fetches a stranger can induce (an ActivityPub fetch of their actor, a federation dial) go through the SSRF-safe resolver that refuses every non-global destination; the accepted residue is request forging against *public* hosts.

**If the box falls.** The attacker holds the box: its TLS private keys, its DKIM key, the bridge enrollment keys, the mail bridge's plaintext of mail **in transit** (an MTA sees inbound mail before the nest seals it, and submission sees outbound), every metadata plane, and whatever derived views a materialization grant left readable. The attacker does **not** hold user content at rest: canonical planes are sealed to keys the nest never has ([`encryption-at-rest.md`](encryption-at-rest.md)); a box compromise is a metadata and in-transit-mail incident, not a content breach. Watchtower, which holds the Docker socket (root-equivalent on the box), is reachable only from loopback and only if its API is enabled; a stranger reaches it only through the nest or a sidecar first.

**Mitigations (ruled).**
1. **Memory safety is the first line, and the native residue is named**: ClamAV and rspamd parse stranger input in their own containers; the ruling is that a sidecar holds **no secret and no socket** — no registry credential, no Docker socket, no volume the nest seals into — so a sidecar RCE is contained to mail triage until it also crosses a container boundary. The nest's own native residue is ffmpeg (§ 1b below).
2. **Update latency is a security control.** A bug found by reading the public source is fixed in the public source and reaches every box through its update channel; the time from fix to deploy is the window the box is exposed for. The updater's registry check interval is that window on the compose bundle — a constant per channel, a minute on Dev and Test and an hour on Stable, where it adds at most an hour to a promote that is itself a human act measured in hours ([`nest/update-channel.md`](nest/update-channel.md) § The updater → *The check interval*) — and the sidecar images move on the monthly currency cycle ([`release-integrity.md`](release-integrity.md) § Third-party container images).
3. **Detection from outside.** The serving gate reads the box's build id and the binary gate reads the container's binary hashes; a box serving a build the pipeline never produced is red ([`image-pipeline.md`](image-pipeline.md) § Verifying a deploy carried the binaries). This is how a quietly replaced nest is noticed; it is not prevention.

**The pre-auth surface, reviewed 2026-10-17 before the acceptance nest's firewall lift (refutable by the user).** The owner's concern — "we should be extremely afraid of remote code execution style attacks against our not-at-all tested 24/7 open ports" — framed the review: every port the acceptance nest would expose, enumerated from the image and the code rather than from memory, each pre-auth parser judged for RCE and memory-safety exposure, ranked by what a stranger can do in one packet, after a handshake, or never without an account, with a verdict per port. Dependency source stayed unread by rule; third-party code is judged by advisory history, scanner output and its isolation. **The RCE judgment.** The fear is not borne out by the enumeration: every parser in our own code that a stranger reaches is memory-safe Rust or Go, with no `unsafe` on any parse path (the Go side's `unsafe` is the generated UniFFI glue; the sandbox and fleet binaries' `unsafe` is syscall glue a stranger never feeds), and the dependency scanners are green where it matters — `cargo vet` succeeded, govulncheck is clean over the bridge and the fork, npm is clean, and cargo-audit's one red is the three `zip` advisories (SUID bits and symlinks on extraction to disk; the nest only ever reads one zip entry in memory, on the DMARC-report path, and the bump is queued as need 11). The native code a stranger can feed is exactly: the ClamAV and rspamd sidecars (C, fed by anyone who can send mail — the one place a parser RCE is realistic, and the one contained by construction: no secret, no socket, no volume), `ring` and `rustls` on every TLS handshake and Ed25519 verify (high-assurance, continuously fuzzed upstream), `libsqlite3` behind our own parameterised statements, and `libsecp256k1` for Nostr Schnorr verifies. ffmpeg, the image codecs, HTML and XML parsing all sit behind a bearer. So a stranger's realistic win is **denial of service and abuse of standing**, not code execution on the nest; and the sidecar win is bounded by what a sidecar container can reach, which is the subject of the lateral paragraph below. Two measurements were owed rather than assumed, because "memory-safe" is a property of the code, not of the bounds around it. The first is done: property-based tests (`proptest`) now cover each stranger-facing pure parser in our own code — panics and unbounded allocations being the residual class — as in-crate `proptests` modules that run in the `workspace-test-check` and nest-lib heavy gates (the Existing-witnesses column names each); they found no panic. Coverage-guided fuzzing (`cargo-fuzz` libFuzzer targets over the same functions) is queued as a dependency need for the owner's next bump round. The second is still owed: one tier_4 suite witnessing every listener's pre-auth refusals on the real image.

**Ranked findings (reachability × impact; H = fix before the lift, M = the same window, L = disclosure or low).**
1. **H1 (tcp 443, one connection): the federation channel admits any fresh keypair, and `keypackage.fetch` drains any local user's MLS key packages.** The hello is verified against the key the peer itself names; no pairing or relationship gate sits at the handshake (the code's own comment calls this "inert today only because federation is not live in alpha, which is not a property to depend on" — the acceptance nest runs federation on, open). On that channel `fauna.federation.keypackage.fetch` consumes one-time key packages for any `target_actor_id` down to the last-resort package; `welcome.deliver` is held by the registered-recipient check and the reach floor; a peer-asserted owner domain still triggers a discovery dial (SSRF-guarded, 32-concurrent cap). **Fixed 2026-10-17, by a per-target budget instead of the relationship gate this review first proposed:** a stranger's first conversation starts with exactly this fetch and the wire names no requester, so a relationship gate would close the open federation [`federation.md`](federation.md) § Trust model rules — instead at most 10 one-time packages per hour leave any one user's pool, summed over every peer and source, and every fetch past that is served the last-resort package without consuming it (owner: [`federation.md`](federation.md) § Key packages — privacy & exhaustion); and the hello is metered per source IP before its verify ([`federation.md`](federation.md) § Security → *Rate limiting is the primary DoS defense*). **The same fix audited every other kind a stranger reaches without pairing (2026-10-17):** most are gated by a binding this nest wrote (foreign membership, a recorded invitation, a pairing capability, a follow homed at the caller) or are public reads; four still consume without a per-target bound — `post.forward` stores any self-signed post under the default `Open` submission policy with no hosted-author check, `follow.subscribe` queues unbounded fresh-keypair requests against a hosted author, `abuse_report.deliver` stores an attacker-chosen `report_ref` per call and rings the admin, and `reports.exchange`/`trends.exchange` cap rows per self-minted peer id only. All are memory-safe storage/attention floods, not key-material loss; **each is bounded per target since 2026-10-17** (§ Implementation status today).
2. **H2 (tcp 443, one connection): `fauna.auth.challenge` is unthrottled and fills an unbounded in-memory store**, one entry per call for any valid Ed25519 key with a 300 s TTL, and the WS dispatcher has no per-connection frame budget, so the unthrottled handshake kinds (`auth.verify`, the device/custody handshakes, `auth.nest_handshake` — one signature each), the federation hello and NIP-42 AUTH frames are unmetered CPU. Fix: throttle and bound the store; a per-connection pre-identity frame budget. Built 2026-10-17 (§ Implementation status today).
3. **M1 (tcp 443): the ActivityPub inbox fetches the actor the unverified body names and upserts it before the signature is checked**, under a limiter keyed on the attacker-chosen domain with no sweeper — unbounded actor rows, a stranger-steerable request forger against public hosts (the link-preview residue was accepted for an authenticated, per-actor-limited user; here the trigger is anonymous), and the exact version in the outbound `User-Agent`. **Fixed 2026-10-17:** every key-free check runs before any dial, a known actor verifies against its cached key, an unknown actor is fetched only under a per-source budget and stored only once the signature verifies, the inbox limiter is keyed on the PROXY-resolved source with a sweeper, the body cap refuses before buffering, and the User-Agent carries no version (owner: [`activitypub.md`](../behavior/activitypub.md) § Security posture).
4. **M2 (tcp 80) — built 2026-10-17: the HTTP-01 listener was a bare `axum::serve`** in the nest process — no connection cap, header timeout, governor or body limit — so a slow-loris on :80 spends the nest's file descriptors, and its fallback reflects the `Host` header into a `308`, an open redirect on every box. Now `acme_http01::start_http01_listener` serves it through `serve_plain` (the one admitted accept loop) behind a per-source governor (`HTTP01_REQUESTS_PER_SECOND_PER_IP`, 10), the shared router refuses any request body (`413`) and redirects only to `domain` or a host `MultiDomainCertResolver::serves_host` names (a per-domain cert's domain, a DNS or IP SAN of the default or IP bridge cert); every other `Host` is `404`. Pinned by `bins/fauna-nest/tests/http01_listener_admission.rs` (the connection past the ceiling shed unanswered, the token answered, a foreign `Host` given no `Location`) and the router's and resolver's unit tests.
5. **M3 (tcp 25): no overall memory budget** — each DATA is read whole (5–25 GB across the 100-connection cap), the MIME parse and the DNS-heavy verify (a fresh resolver per message) run before the 64-scan cap with no Go-side timeout, every RCPT costs a nest RPC, and two limiter maps are never swept. **L4:** the DMARC aggregate-report unpack inflates a stranger's attachment up to 500 MB in memory. Memory-safe, so a DoS lever against the fleet's otherwise model perimeter. **Built 2026-10-17:** a 256 MiB in-flight DATA byte budget (`451 4.3.1` past it), a ten-refusal unknown-recipient cap per session (`421 4.7.0`), a 30 s verification deadline over one shared resolver, sweeps of both limiter maps, and the DMARC unpack lowered to TLSRPT's 16 MiB / 64 MiB — the bounds live in [`smtp-server.md`](../behavior/smtp-server.md) § Connection-time limits and § Error / tempfail strategy, the inflate bound in [`dmarc-reporting.md`](../behavior/dmarc-reporting.md). **Hardened 2026-10-18:** one source trickling DATA could hold the whole budget, so the budget now carries a per-source share and a whole-DATA deadline, bounds in the same `smtp-server.md` table.
6. **M4 (relay, tcp 443 + udp 7842): every relay connect cost one `fauna.relay.admit` RPC** — **the tcp 443 half is built (2026-10-17):** verdicts are cached (admit 60 s, refusal 5 s), uncached keys spend one global budget, and a key the relay admitted within 7 days is re-asked from a reserved budget fresh keys cannot reach, so a sustained fresh-key flood locks no recent member out (2026-10-18; [`../behavior/p2p.md`](../behavior/p2p.md) § The relay); whether the relay dependency throttles is unread, and the udp 7842 handshake reply size is still unmeasured.
7. **M5 (tcp 465/587 and DAV): built (2026-10-17).** Submission AUTH now runs the dummy KDF for unknown users and no-blob credentials, closing ruling F3 of [`nest/network-exposure.md`](nest/network-exposure.md) on this port, and every credential Argon2id passes one process-wide in-flight gate (4 at once, 2 s wait bound), so the aggregate pre-auth cost the lockouts admit (300 failures per minute per IP per surface, 64 MiB each; DAV re-runs it on every request) no longer reaches the box's memory or cores unbounded. The gate is fair across sources (2026-10-18): a freed slot goes to the queued source holding the fewest slots, a source being an IPv4 address or an IPv6 /64, so one source — however many addresses of its /64 it rotates through — cannot hold every slot while another waits, and a lone source still uses all four. An attempt that finds no slot within the wait bound checked no credential, so it is a temporary failure, never a wrong password: SMTP `454 4.7.0`, IMAP `NO [UNAVAILABLE]`, DAV `503` with `Retry-After`, with no lockout failure booked and no `fail` audit row written — and the unknown-user dummy KDF answers the same, so a saturated gate is no account-existence oracle (pinned by `internal/mailfauna/kdf_gate_test.go` and the busy-gate tests beside each surface's lockout tests).
8. **L1 (tcp 443): `/internal/router-status` had no loopback gate — built 2026-10-17.** It served the exact version and the user counts to any internet client and every sidecar, against the "no counts to anonymous" posture [`nest/network-exposure.md`](nest/network-exposure.md) owns. Ruled and built 2026-10-17: loopback-only and fail-closed through the shared internal gate, no exception for a remote-backend router ([`nest/worker.md`](nest/worker.md) § Router Status Endpoint owns the mechanism; its § Implementation status today names the pinning tests and the outside-in path probe).
9. **L2 (tcp 443, accepted):** the payment webhook stamps a provider "rejected" on an unsigned request before verification — by the monetization design (evidence-based status), so a stranger can only flip the admin's status indicator; noted, not queued.
10. **The sidecar-version finding (tcp 25 → the sidecars): the sidecars' upstream versions are unrecorded and their pins have drifted.** ClamAV fixed a batch of parser out-of-bounds writes in 1.5.3 (CVE-2026-20213…20217, -20243/-20244, July 2026; denial of service stated) and a PDF overflow with possible code execution in CVE-2025-20260; the vetted-images table records each pin's digest and vet date (2026-08-27) but not the version inside it, and `--check-image-currency` reports both sidecar pins drifted. No rspamd advisory of 2025–2026 was found. Fix: record the version per pin with a floor, re-vet through the bump queue. **Floors recorded (2026-10-17):** `scripts/vetted_images.py` carries a floor per scanner pin — ClamAV 1.5.3; rspamd 3.14.1, a currency floor (no advisory) — the installer suite refuses a recorded version below it, and the currency report flags an unread one; the real-clamd tier_4 test reads clamd's `VERSION` reply and asserts the floor. The version inside each current pin is unread (no label or version tag names it; reading the daemon's reply runs the image on a dev machine, which waits on the owner's approval of that image), and the re-vet of both pins is one queued bump need.

**What a full RCE in each listener's process reaches (the container-hardening half).** The nest holds the metadata planes, its TLS and identity keys and the DKIM key, and nothing sealed (§ *If the box falls*). The MTA and MDA, under Landlock, hold their enrollment key and in-transit mail; the sealed store is kernel-denied to them. The ATProto bridge holds its PDS signing key and the relay its relay key, each under its own uid but without a Landlock domain. A sidecar holds nothing, and it shares the compose network with the nest container — so **the image binds every listener nothing outside the container dials on container loopback (ruled and built 2026-10-17):** the nest's own `:3000` (the entrypoint's bind default and an every-boot reconcile of `nest.toml`'s `listen`; every co-resident dialer and the HEALTHCHECK use loopback, and clients arrive through the SNI router's `:443`) and the MDA's plaintext STARTTLS `:143`, which no bundle publishes; the CalDAV, relay, PDS and metrics listeners already bound loopback. What stays on every interface is what faces the internet anyway — `:443`, the published mail ports, udp 7842 — so a fallen sidecar reaches exactly a stranger's surface, under the router's caps and with the router's PROXY attribution. That supersedes the 2026-06-23 decline of a loopback `:3000`, which rested on tier_4 tests dialing `:3000` across containers: the docker-mode harness and every tier_4 docker test now reach the nest through `:443`, the production path. Witness: `test_sidecar_lateral_reach_docker.py` connects from the clamd sidecar's own network namespace and finds `:3000` and `:143` refused while `:443` and `:993` connect and `:143` greets on loopback inside the nest container; `test_mail_scan_real_clamd.py` repeats it from the real clamd. Nothing on the box reaches the Docker socket but the updater, which has no port. The accepted SSRF residues were re-judged for an open box: the link-preview and federation-loopback dials stay accepted (both are authenticated, per-actor-limited, and unchanged by the lift); the bearer-less media proxy memo is superseded (both media routes require a bearer today); the anonymous throttle keyed on the PROXY-resolved peer holds (the router secret is provisioned on every image), and the admin-claim cap's "firewall while claiming" advice is moot for a box claimed before its lift.

### 1b — a user of that nest

**The attacker** is an authenticated principal: a user, an admin, or a third-party principal (a remote app, a device app, a nest-hosted plugin — [`third-party.md`](third-party.md)). It reaches everything in 1a plus the authenticated API.

**What it attacks.**
1. **Authorization between accounts** — the main surface. Every handler that names another account's data is an authz decision; the signed-envelope recipe binds each request to its signer, and the sealed-at-rest property makes a bypass yield ciphertext for every plane the attacker holds no grant to. The reachable prize is metadata and the attacker's own grants' scope, never another user's content.
2. **Server-side processing of its own uploads.** The nest transcodes user video by spawning `ffmpeg` in its own container (`bins/fauna-nest/src/video/transcode.rs`) — a native parser over attacker-chosen bytes, running next to the nest's keys. **Ruled: ffmpeg runs isolated from the nest's secrets** — a separate process identity or sandbox with no access to the data directory beyond its input and output files, resource-limited. The mechanism is a sandbox, not an identity, because the nest is itself an unprivileged uid with no other uid to drop to: ffmpeg runs under `fauna-sandbox`'s `transcode` profile, whose Landlock domain grants one per-job directory read-write and the system trees read-only, so the sealed store is kernel-denied though ffmpeg shares the nest's uid (§ Implementation status today). The ruling covers every ffmpeg the image runs, under the one `transcode` profile: the ATProto bridge's mp4 remux runs ffmpeg as the bridge's own uid, next to its PDS signing key, over nest-produced MPEG-TS, and is wrapped the same way. Hosted third-party code runs under the runner contract: WASM components with fuel and memory caps and no host I/O they were not granted, or curated containers ([`third-party.md`](third-party.md) § Execution forms).
3. **Outbound fetch primitives** — link previews and federation dials a user can aim. Both go through the SSRF-safe resolver; the residues accepted on review are request forging against public hosts (any port, plaintext allowed, bounded by per-actor rate limits) and a loopback dial that can only complete a Fauna handshake against the box's own loopback services. Accepted, not pursued; the rationale lives in the code comments and the reviews they cite.
4. **Abuse of the box's standing** — spam from a legitimate submission account, storage exhaustion, rate exhaustion. Submission is authenticated per user; quotas and per-actor limits are the mail and storage owners'.
5. **The admin.** An admin's power is configuration (bridges, DKIM, TLS, federation peers, grants) through the app UI, audited; it is not content — the admin holds no user's keys. A compromised admin identity is a configuration incident, recoverable through the same UI.

## Concern 2 — a compromised nest reaching beyond itself

A dev machine is Tier 2 of the ship pipeline ([`release-integrity.md`](release-integrity.md) § Trust tiers): it proposes source that every box eventually runs. A box is **Tier 3**, below it — it runs stranger input and holds no authority over anything but itself. **The invariant: trust flows from Tier 2 toward Tier 3 only.** Nothing a dev machine does with a box may let the box act on the dev machine — and, widened 2026-10-17 on the owner's ask, nothing a fallen box holds may let it act on a sibling nest, on the cloud project or on the zone the organisation's nests share (lateral movement, path 6 and § The lateral-movement map). Six paths, each with today's exposure and the chosen mitigation:

1. **Text from the box entering an agent session's context.** Health JSON, API error strings, nest and sidecar logs read over SSH, a test failure quoting a server reply, a mail body a live mail test read over IMAP, a DNS answer. Sessions run with broad permissions, so an instruction a hostile nest plants in any of these is a prompt-injection path to code execution on a dev machine, and the dependency-log ruling already names the shape ([`release-integrity.md`](release-integrity.md) § Dep-authored text in build and CI logs: an execution engine for the text it reads). **Ruled: box-sourced text is untrusted data, never instructions — the same stance, the same detection-signal response.** The test for "box-sourced" is provenance: it arrived over the wire from a deployed box, whatever tool displayed it. A session consumes a verification by its gate's verdict lines (the serving gate, the binary gate, the firewall gate all summarize), reads a box's logs only for a named question and never pages them, and treats an imperative in any of it as a detection signal to stop on and flag. The operational copy is the session operating rules' dependency-source bullet; this paragraph is the authority.
2. **Client-side exploitation of the apps and the test harness.** In live mode the apps on a dev machine — and the browser engines the web app and the harness drive, the platform WebViews, the native media players, the Android emulator on the host — are clients of the box. The Rust parsers they share are memory-safe; the residue is the browser and WebView engines and the native media decoders, which are third-party native code fed by the box. **Ruled:** live-mode runs target only boxes this fleet deployed and whose build the gates verified, and never the acceptance nest, which a dev machine connects to beyond a probe only once per re-creation ([`org-nests.md`](org-nests.md) § fauna.build); the engines stay current through the dependency currency cycle; and the residue is accepted as a detectable Tier-2 compromise, not a prevented one — the box's own deviation is what the gates in § 1a catch first.
3. **Credentials flowing toward the box.** Audited 2026-10-10: the box receives **no** dev-fleet credential. No SSH reaches a box at all (§ 1a, the 2026-10-11 ruling; before it, a box-only key); the admin identity in the per-box identity file authenticates by signature, so its seed never leaves the machine; the cloud API token is spoken only to the provider's API, never to a box; the DNS-provider credential rests on the box sealed to the admin's keys ([`behavior/dns-management.md`](../behavior/dns-management.md) § Where the credential lives); live mail tests use accounts the run provisions on that box, worthless elsewhere. **Ruled, to hold:** nothing on a dev machine opens a shell on a box — no sshd to reach, no key to offer, no debug exception (re-ruled 2026-10-11; the earlier "no agent, box-only key, pinned host key" rule is subsumed) — **save a box whose admin opened the SSH door (§ Concern 1a, ruled 2026-10-20) — of the organisation's nests `dev.fauna.build` alone, its key box-only and kept on Linux**, whose key is box-only and lives on Linux alone, so a fallen `dev.fauna.build` still holds nothing that opens any other machine; **a box holds no registry credential** — the image package is public and pulls anonymously, and with no shell nothing can write one after creation, so a box created before that ruling is re-created rather than cleaned; and nothing new may hand a box a secret that is valid anywhere but on that box.
4. **How a box gets compromised, today and after the opening.** Today every box sits behind its own cloud firewall admitting its serving ports from the dev host's egress only, plus tcp 25 from the mail relay the fleet uses to test inbound mail; so today's ways in are the image supply chain (a write to the package the box pulls — a Tier-1 or Tier-2 event, upstream of the box, not a new path), the cloud project credential (the machines that hold it), **inbound mail through the relay — the one stranger-originated input a box parses today, through the sidecars and the bridge**, and an authenticated user (§ 1b). The per-run admission of the verify job's runner ([`image-pipeline.md`](image-pipeline.md) § Image tags & channels, the two pipelines, part 2) adds the runner's address for the minutes of its run, on the four verify ports; a stranger sharing that address in that window gains what every stranger gains the day the firewall is lifted — § 1a, bounded. Once a box serves users, § 1a applies in full, and the nest's own bugs become reachable from anywhere; nothing in this concern changes then, because none of its mitigations depend on the firewall. **The acceptance nest becomes that box the day the owner lifts its firewall** ([`org-nests.md`](org-nests.md) § fauna.build — firewalled until then, ruled 2026-10-17): the product as shipped, reviewer and tester accounts only; what it can reach is bounded by path 3 (no credential valid elsewhere) and path 5 (its project holds nothing but the box and its firewall, its zone nothing but the three names), and its fall costs one re-creation — which happens weekly, at every UAT entry and before every complex dev-machine connection regardless ([`org-nests.md`](org-nests.md) § fauna.build).
5. **The cloud credential's blast radius.** The provider scopes a token to a **project** — every server, firewall and DNS zone in it, read or read+write, nothing finer — and the one project in use holds every box and the whole apex zone, mail records included; so one token rebuilds any box, rewrites production DNS, and with it mints certificates and redirects mail. **Ruled: trust tiers get their own projects, cut by who holds each token (re-drawn 2026-10-10 around the acceptance nest; re-cut 2026-10-17 on the owner's word, on their lateral-movement ask) — four projects:** **`zone`** holds the organisation's own zone `fauna.build` (a registered domain, not a subzone of the apex: the provider's zones are registrable domains only) and nothing else: its token is the credential every organisation nest's admin app holds for managed DNS and DNS-01 renewal, and the DNS half of the provisioning drive; **`uat`** holds the dev and test nests with their firewalls and the live suite's throwaway boxes ([`org-nests.md`](org-nests.md) § One zone, one project): its token is what the two verify jobs hold (`verify-nest` Environments — both pipelines verify the firewalled nests only, the private one the dev nest, the public one the test nest, and nothing promotes with a cloud call), what the firewall gate, the firewall copy and the per-run admission use against those boxes, and the VPS half of the drive; **`acceptance`** holds the fauna.build box and its firewall — behind it until the owner lifts it ([`org-nests.md`](org-nests.md) § fauna.build, ruled 2026-10-17): its token lives on the dev host alone, beside the production token, never in CI, and CI observes the acceptance nest with no token at all; **`production`** holds the fauna.social apex zone (the organisation's mail records and the front door's names) and the front-door box: its token lives on the dev host alone, used by hand for the front door and the apex, and is **never placed in CI**. A token is minted **per consumer** and revocable alone. **The one token a nest's admin holds is a consumer too (added 2026-10-11):** managed DNS puts a provider credential in the admin app of each organisation nest ([`behavior/dns-management.md`](../behavior/dns-management.md) § Where the credential lives), and on the acceptance nest that admin runs the open box; so that credential is a `zone`-project token minted for that nest's admin alone — never the fleet scripts' token file — revocable by itself the day an admin device or the admin's identity file is in doubt. What each closes: a leaked verify token rebuilds dev or test and rewrites their firewalls but cannot touch fauna.build, its firewall or any DNS record; a leaked admin DNS credential redirects the three names, mints certificates for them by DNS-01 and redirects their mail — the zone's own reach, unavoidable while one zone carries all three names, and accepted — but rebuilds no box and opens no firewall; the acceptance token alone lifts fauna.build's firewall or rebuilds the box, and is held where the production token is held; nothing but the production token touches the apex zone, the organisation's mail or the front door, and that token lives nowhere a box or CI reaches. What it costs: two console steps (create the two projects; re-create the zone in `zone`, a delete-and-create since a zone no more moves between projects than a server does — its nameservers are the provider's same three, so the registrar is untouched, and the zone holds nothing to lose today); two token files; the box→project map growing a purpose axis (a host's *cloud* token and its *dns* token are different projects' — `scripts/_cloud_project.py`); the provisioning drive typing two tokens where it types one (`tests/e2e-unified/tests/live/test_staging_box_provision.py`, whose DNS and VPS credential forms are already distinct); the verify workflows' refuse-list naming fauna.build; and the dev-setup console steps re-written. **How the cut got here.** The 2026-10-10 ruling was two projects — *uat* holding the zone and all three nests, *production* as now — on the reasoning that the acceptance nest is disposable by construction, so a token that can rebuild it does what the organisation does on demand, and that the admin's DNS credential's whole-project reach was bounded enough by that cut. Four things had changed by 2026-10-17, and the owner re-cut on them the same day. **(i)** The provider's separate DNS console and its DNS-only tokens are gone (read-only from 2026-05-20, shut down May 2026; `libs/fauna-provisioning/src/dns/hetzner.rs` already speaks the Cloud API): a zone is a resource of a Cloud project, a token reaches every resource of its project, so a DNS-only *credential* exists only as the token of a project that holds a zone and nothing else. **(ii)** Nothing of the uat layout existed yet but the zone with its NS and SOA records (measured 2026-10-17: no server, no firewall, no network, no key in the uat project), so the re-cut costs console steps this week and a migration any week after a box lands. **(iii)** The admin's DNS credential is not a box asset: it rests on the account plane sealed to the admin's keys and nest-opaque ([`behavior/dns-management.md`](../behavior/dns-management.md) § Where the credential lives), so a fallen box yields ciphertext, and the credential's holders are the admin's devices — which the per-consumer rule already names. **(iv)** A token's power over a *firewall* is a ruling bypass: the owner's "firewalled until further notice" ([`org-nests.md`](org-nests.md) § fauna.build) is one rule-set write away for any holder of a token that reaches fauna.build's project — the two repositories' verify tokens included, the day they exist. Declined, with reasons: **a three-project cut** (`zone` split off, fauna.build left in `uat`) saves one token file and leaves fauna.build's firewall at the verify tokens' mercy, which is (iv); **per-box projects for dev and test** (five) would separate the two repositories' tokens from each other — a genuine further cut, declined by the owner 2026-10-17 because both verify jobs observe the same staging pair by design, the dev nest is re-created from the dev host on demand, and the throwaway boxes need one home either way; **the provider's firewall-only or DNS-only token scope** does not exist (confirmed 2026-10-17, as in 2026-10-10); **the credential-free shapes** — a fixed-egress tunnel on the runner is a dependency add for what one API call does, and verifying from the dev host or from the fleet's self-hosted runner (both already admitted by egress) puts a release gate's observation on Tier 2 or on a machine the public repository must not run workflows on ([`release-integrity.md`](release-integrity.md) § The enforcement point).
6. **Lateral movement between the organisation's nests.** A fallen box reaches a sibling only through what the siblings share: their public addresses (nothing else — **they share no provider private network, by ruling**, since the provider's firewalls do not filter private-network traffic at all), the cloud project and the zone (path 5), the dev host and CI (paths 1–4). **Ruled: no organisation nest ever admits a sibling's address in any firewall rule, and no organisation nest joins a provider private network** — two invariants the firewall gate asserts and the firewall copy refuses to violate; written down because the provider's rules are allow-only (a deny is not expressible), so the posture rests on the allowlist never naming a sibling rather than on a rule that drops one. Nothing further is ruled in — the owner confirmed 2026-10-17 that the two invariants are the whole of the inter-box rule: egress filtering per box is declined — the product needs any-host egress (federation, outbound mail, link previews, ACME, the registry), the provider's outbound rules are an allowlist that cannot say "everything but the siblings and the dev host", and nothing at those addresses accepts a connection from a box anyway; an explicit box↔box drop on the lifted fauna.build is declined — once it admits the internet a sibling is one stranger among all, and § Concern 1a is the posture. The map itself is § The lateral-movement map.

## What a fallen box cannot reach — the standing invariants

- **No dev-fleet credential exists on a box**, and no credential a box holds is valid on another box or at the provider (concern 2, path 3).
- **Every observer of a box consumes verdicts, never raw text as instructions** (concern 2, path 1).
- **The gate that judges a box runs off the box**, on a machine the box cannot reach (concern 2, path 5; [`release-integrity.md`](release-integrity.md) § The enforcement point).
- **No organisation nest admits a sibling's address, and the nests share no private network** (concern 2, path 6).
- **User content at rest is not the box's to give** (concern 1; [`encryption-at-rest.md`](encryption-at-rest.md)).

## Disclosure — what the organisation promises a reporter (ruled 2026-10-17)

**Ruled 2026-10-17 (the owner, answering the first consistency review's question on the security policy's promises): the organisation stands behind the three promises its public security policy makes, and this section owns them.** **(1) Acknowledgement within a few days.** A report through the public repository's private channel (Security → Report a vulnerability) is acknowledged within a few days, and the reporter is kept informed as the finding is investigated and fixed. **(2) Coordinated disclosure.** The organisation asks the reporter for a reasonable window to release a fix before any public disclosure, and discloses the finding itself once the fix is out. **(3) Credit on request.** A reporter who wishes to be named is credited.

**Scope follows this doc's two concerns.** A compromised nest or hosting provider is *in* scope — the design's promise is that a box cannot read what the user did not share with it, beyond the mail and the messages it passes to and from email and other networks, the address of a link standing on a line of its own in a post or a message, which the nest of each person who reads it fetches for its preview, and the text of a message or a post only some people can open that someone attaches to a report (§ Concern 1; [`encryption-at-rest.md`](encryption-at-rest.md); the bridged messages are the transport-only class, which the bridge inside the nest reads by construction — [`../behavior/conversation-rooms.md`](../behavior/conversation-rooms.md) § The three classes; the mail and the bridged messages worded so by the owner 2026-10-18, kept verbatim; the link-preview fetch — [`render-model.md`](render-model.md) § D4 — and the report excerpt — [`../behavior/moderation.md`](../behavior/moderation.md) § User-initiated reporting → What a report carries — added 2026-10-21 in the guides' words) — and so is an authenticated user of a nest (§ 1b). Out of scope: a finding that needs the user's own device compromised, and a finding in a third-party dependency, which goes upstream (with a note to the organisation if Fauna's use of the dependency is what matters). There is no "operator" to compromise ([`../principles.md`](../principles.md) § One configuration surface), so no scope line names one. The policy text (`SECURITY.md` in the public repository; inventory entry `repo-security`) states these promises and this scope and nothing more, and changes only through the public-texts request ([`public-texts.md`](public-texts.md) § The rule).
