## Capability tiering (the trust surface; formerly "by storage mode")

The trust property has a system-wide corollary: **a content operation — serving, searching, or scoring user content — runs at a capability position, and which positions exist for a given box is its grant set.** Every feature is available everywhere; only its *execution position* varies: content serving is the AUTH'd MDA session opening records per-record ([`../behavior/imap-server.md`](../behavior/imap-server.md) § Content path); search over sealed slices is the session, the client, or an index-granted holder (§ Search and indexing); scoring is the perimeter, the client, or a granted content-processor (§ Content scoring and classification). A box holding broad standing grants does all of this server-side — the old plaintext mode's capability, now data the branch checks. A grant may also be **keyless** — conveying sync-custody authority with no read reach at all: the account plane's custodian replicas admit on exactly such a grant ([`account-replica-posture.md`](account-replica-posture.md) § Replica posture owns that concept). The one non-tiering case, per [`content-scoring.md`](content-scoring.md): a *deployment-wide* pre-classifier that needs no per-user secret runs at the **mail-bridge perimeter on every nest** (the uniform, branch-free shape) — the perimeter holds inbound plaintext regardless of any grant.

**The tier ladder, stated once (2026-08-10):** a grant conveys, in increasing reach — **keyless custody** (sync-custody authority, zero read reach), **scoped read** (open the named scopes at capability positions; artifacts kept are sealed-or-metadata per the purpose test), and **materialization** (scoped read *plus* the right to keep readable derived views of the named own-actor scopes at rest — § Readable classes class 4; nest-only holder in v1). For account-plane class-2 kinds, the grantable universe is bounded one level below the ladder: only **delegable-rung** kinds are mintable at all — fleet-only-rung kinds (operational secrets) sit on a key branch the grant machinery structurally cannot reach, at any tier ([`account-data-taxonomy.md`](account-data-taxonomy.md) § The audience ladder owns the rung model; R13, 2026-08-11). A materialization grant is standing/auto-renewed by nature (a view that dies weekly is churn, not a feature), its concrete scope and holder shape is ruled below (*The materialization grant*, designed 2026-11-08 — additive grant-plane vocabulary, no new crypto), and its revocation obligates the holder to delete the views — the delete is logged against the grant, and its enforcement bound is the honest one stated in class 4.

**The materialization grant — ratified design (designed 2026-11-08; refutable until first build, after which each part is amended here, never re-derived).** This paragraph and its parts (a)–(g) own the grant's concrete shape; class 4 (§ Readable classes) owns the at-rest property and its seven bounds, every part below keeps all seven, and the opt-in surface is [`../ui/nests.md`](../ui/nests.md) § Trust facet — grants → *The materialization option*. Register row T17 of [`account-data-plane.md`](account-data-plane.md) is answered in full by (a), (b), (c), (e) and (f).

- **(a) Shape — grant-plane vocabulary, no sync-plane family tag.** A materialization grant is an ordinary `GrantBlob` whose declared scope is the key-bearing `content.read{kind}` tuple of each chosen own-actor kind in the **master-key regime** (the standing wrap; never the bounded per-epoch form — class 4's retroactivity bound needs the keys that open the already-held history, and a view that must be rebuilt as a window slides is churn) plus one **keyless** tuple of the new class **`materialize`** per kind (`ScopeTuple::CLASS_MATERIALIZE`, `kind` the kind, `set`/`tier` mirroring the read tuple's qualifiers where the kind has them; keyless like `custody`, `content.label-write`, `identity.op` and `deposit` — `derive_scope_payload` answers `None`). The `materialize` tuple is what makes the tier auditable in the declared scope the Now lens reads, and what lets one revoke end both the keys and the views: the two tuples ride one grant and die together. It is never bundled with `content.label-write` (a materializer is not a filter). v1 kind: `mail`; calendar and card joined 2026-11-08, each under its own key and each its own grant ([`encryption-at-rest.md`](encryption-at-rest.md) § Implementation status today, the 2026-10-23 split); a public post is plaintext already, a restricted own post's period key is shared with its subscribers and a folder is a shared plane, so none of those is offered without a class-4 reading of its own. **No `fauna_protocol::scope` family tag is minted:** that grammar names the planes a replica syncs; what a nest may keep readable is grant data, and the sync plane never sees posture (R7). The class stays reserved to device apps and nest-hosted code (`ScopeTuple::reserved_for_device_or_hosted` answers `true`), and the nest's mint and renew doors additionally refuse a `materialize` tuple to any holder but this nest's own materializer and refuse a `materialize{mail}` blob whose mail wrap is bounded — the nest-only holder and the master-key regime are enforced at the door, not trusted to the minting app.
- **(b) Holder — the nest's own in-process materializer.** The nest binary's second in-process capability holder, on the web-serve holder's template (`bins/fauna-nest/src/web_content/holder.rs`): its own seed file in the data dir (`materializer_holder.key`, 0600, not factory-reset-preserved — a reset wipes the grants anyway, so the apps re-mint to the fresh key), an X25519 and ML-KEM pair derived under their own context strings, a self-approved `bridge_service_users` row (role `content-processor`, `bridge_id = "materializer"`, `in_process`), a `fauna_capability_holder::Registry` whose fetch seam is the direct `capability_grants` read, the standard holder contract (a transient fetch keeps the cached set; an authoritative absence drops the grant and zeroizes; revoke bites at use). The User-class `list_service_users` view already admits an in-process content-processor holder, so every member's mint picker discovers it; an admin's revocation of the row is respected — never resurrected at boot — and then every member's views are deleted at the next reconcile (an admin can always stop a component of their box; the consequence is honest). One holder per purpose: the materializer is not the web-serve holder (its roster row must read "keeps your mail searchable", never "serves paywalled posts") and not a third-party principal (no consent ceremony, no client id — it is the box itself, which class 4 names as the holder). The spam baseline's `!in_process` rule is that class's, whose artifact is nest-opaque by design; this class is nest-readable by design, as the web paywall's is, so the nest's own key is its seal target exactly as web-serve's is.
- **(c) The per-actor area and the materializer.** Views rest in `<data_dir>/__materialized/<actor_hex>/<kind>/` — one directory per granted kind, each bound to the grant that declared it — **not a segment kind**: absent from [`message-segment-store.md`](message-segment-store.md)'s kind table, never opened by the `SegmentManager`, under no snapshot, compaction or backup arm; the hourly hot-copy and the daily logical dump copy `nest.db` alone, so the area is outside every self-backup by construction, which is what makes deletion at revoke real (class 4's third bound). Per kind one readable `fauna-index` working index — the unsealed Tantivy directory a client's own working index already is ([`../behavior/content-index.md`](../behavior/content-index.md) § Encryption posture → *On a client*), with **no stored source text** (that doc's Don't stands: a snippet is rendered at query time from the sealed record opened under the held key) — and a small `state.db` holding the grant id the view is bound to, the per-scope catch-up cursor and the format version. The doc shape is the client builder's (`IndexedDoc`; for mail the content id is the RFC `Message-ID` and `secondary_id` the nest message id), the tokenizer the shared `fauna-mail` one, so the nest's hits and a device's own hits are the same documents. **Cadence — event-driven with a resumable catch-up, every constant a Rust constant:** *arm* — when the registry first holds a materialization grant for an actor (a mint, or boot with one standing) the materializer creates the area, binds it to the grant id and starts the **catch-up walk** over the actor's sealed history from the cursor, oldest first, batched, yielding between batches, resumable across restarts — retroactive by construction, over everything the held keys open; *trickle* — a post-append observer at the mail, calendar and card segment append seams hands every new record of a granted actor to the materializer, which opens it under the held key, tokenizes and adds it, flushing on a debounce; a record the held wraps cannot open (a lineage no wrap covers yet, (e)) is skipped and counted, and the next catch-up retries it; *retire* — a record's tombstone (a delete, an expunge, a compaction rewrite) deletes its doc (a calendar or card row is replaced in place, so each walk of those kinds diffs the corpus by `uid_hash`); *revoke and lapse* — the revoke handler deletes the area before it replies, and the registry's refresh treats a lapsed window exactly as a vanished grant (the window is the user's stated bound; a one-off duration is allowed and ends the view at lapse); *boot reconcile* — before serving, every `__materialized/<actor>/<kind>/` view whose bound grant no longer stands (revoked, lapsed, the holder row revoked, the actor deleted) is deleted, so no crash leaves a view behind ([`nest/common.md`](nest/common.md) § Client-state recoverability). Account deletion purges the directory with the actor's tables. A scope dehydrated under R10 materializes only what the nest still holds — stated, not hidden. The area is metered against no user quota in v1 (it is the nest's own derived state, bounded by the corpus it derives from); a per-actor ceiling is a later constant if one is ever needed.
- **(d) The first consumers — what search and the nest-hosted shell do with it.** `fauna.search.query` on a nest whose materializer holds the **authenticated actor's** materialization grant additionally queries that actor's readable views — each kind their grants keep — and merges the hits into the one reply (for mail `content_type: "mail"`, `content_id` the RFC `Message-ID` — the client's local arm's id, so the shared snapshot dedups a native app's own hit against the nest's by `(kind, content_id)`; the nest message id rides the additive `extra` map under the local doc's `secondary_id` key; the snippet is rendered at query time, bounded by the page size; for an event or a card `content_type` `calendar` / `contact` — `ContentKind`'s spelling, never `card` — and `content_id` the hex `uid_hash` the client builder keys its own doc by, with no `extra`; a `content_type` filter naming one kind answers from that kind's view alone). It never answers for another actor: the connection actor is the ACL (class 4's sixth bound), and the per-user index isolation rule is untouched. This is the ratified fast path of § Search and indexing made concrete — the one shape that gives the thin web SPA and a phone-only user full mail search with no local engine, with no change to any app. **IMAP `SEARCH` keeps the MDA's own position in v1** (its session opener and the synced `__index` slices); letting the MDA read the nest's view through a bridge-plane kind is a later lift, not this design. **The nest-hosted cli shell keeps no view of its own** ([`apps/cli.md`](apps/cli.md) § Don't do these): its search tool calls `fauna.search.query` through the principal chokepoint as the bound account, and the nest answers from the owner's readable index only for the kinds that binding's consent twin grants read on — so served search over sealed mail is the user's two consents composed (the materialization grant to their nest, which keeps the view; the shell's mail-read twin, which may ask), and without the first the shell answers from the floor corpus like every caller; its body reads stay stateless per-request opens under its own twin.
- **(e) T17's two constraints, stated honestly.** *The continuing per-generation key vehicle.* A standing grant must keep opening every key lineage a covered scope mints after the grant — MSEK generations for mail today; a folder's content-key generations; R14 generation ids once a content scope seals under generations — yet generation keys distribute only to enrolled devices and to escrow. The grant plane's own vehicle is the **renew top-up**: `fauna.capabilities.renew` appending a `WrappedScopeKey` indexed by the lineage (`epoch` is already a per-kind lineage index — the sealing epoch for mail, the generation version for a folder), which is exactly the bounded-mail rotation heal and the folder read twin's renew-on-rotation. So the owner's app, at every event that mints a new lineage for a covered scope (an MSEK rotation; a folder rotation; a generation mint, when that day comes), runs an equal-end renew of every standing materialization grant carrying the new lineage's wrap — the rotation flow's heal (`drive_rotation_loop`, today over outstanding *bounded* grants) generalized to every grant covering the rotated scope; the first build adds the master-key mail arm. Until a device has run the top-up the materializer is **dark on the new lineage** and says so: the trust row's status reads *catching up* (the paused family of [`../ui/nests.md`](../ui/nests.md) § Expiry / renewal; a renew heals it), nothing bounces and nothing is lost — canon is sealed regardless and the view catches up at the next top-up. Grant keys are never fed from the escrow plane: the nest stores escrow wraps it cannot open, and escrow is a recovery artifact, not a capability position. *The severance asymmetry — two axes, deliberately.* **Fleet membership** (R14): removing a device mints a new generation that excludes it, severing the removed device's *future* reach; it touches no grant. **Granted positions**: a standing grant to the nest's materializer continues across that mint — any remaining device tops it up — so the user's search on their own nest survives a device removal, and a removal is never read as "revoke the nest"; conversely revoking the grant severs the nest's future reach and deletes its views and changes no fleet membership. Conflating the axes would either break R9 at every mint (a removal that severed grants would kill the nest's view at every rotation) or quietly make the nest a fleet member (a grant fed generation keys the way a device bundle is — the materializer never holds a device bundle, an identity key or anything of the fleet plane; rule #7 of [`key-material-hierarchy.md`](key-material-hierarchy.md)). A custodian device as holder (T16, when it comes) inherits both statements unchanged.

The per-page detail is [`../ui/nests.md`](../ui/nests.md)'s.

**The fallback is always present, so server-side is an optimization.** For a per-user operation the client holds the keys (post-decrypt), and native apps keep a full local index replica so search works with no server-side help (§ Search and indexing → Where queries run); the AUTH'd MDA session is the bridge-side fallback during an active IMAP/CalDAV session. **The general extension — background re-processing under a user-minted capability (BUILT — Phase 2, 2026-07-06; design spec).** The three server-side positions above (serve / search / score) are all *reception-time or serve-time* — a key-holder reads content it already holds. The one operation that reads *content at rest later* — re-scoring on a model change, re-indexing on a tokenizer bump — is the target of the **content-processing-bridge + scoped-capability** pattern (design ratified 2026-07-04; tracked internally); it generalizes the MDA/FTS/DKIM prior art into one primitive and is the substrate under [`content-scoring.md`](content-scoring.md)'s compute abstraction. **Load-bearing honest bound (that spec's § Phase 2 Step 1, resolved 2026-07-04): a capability's "time limit" is NOT cryptographically enforcing on _future_ content until the target kind is sealed under a rotating _content-sealing epoch_ — and no content kind is today** (every at-rest base key is standing, rotating only on membership events — MSEK hard-revoke, subscriber removal, MLS commit). So on an untrusted box a background grant is a **standing "trust-until-revoke" grant, not a crypto-time-boxed one**, until the per-kind re-key that introduces such an epoch lands (mail's landed 2026-07-18/19 — § Content-sealing epochs below; not part of Phase 3 itself, which sealed mail/calendar to one standing key). Scope and un-forgeability *are* crypto-self-enforcing; expiry's teeth are at off-box key-acquisition and future-only, and even then only over an epoch-sealed kind. The primitive is now **BUILT** (Phase 2 — the nest re-score drain plane + the Go MDA drain worker, proven end-to-end tier_3 on 2026-07-06; [`content-scoring.md`](content-scoring.md) § The re-score drain plane). The honest bound above governed every kind through the write flip (2026-07-19); mail is now the sole exception (§ Content-sealing epochs below) — a post-flip bounded mail grant's window is a genuine cryptographic bound. Every other kind (calendar, conversations, restricted posts, …) still has no content-sealing epoch, so a background grant over them remains standing trust-until-revoke. § The sealed posture remains authoritative for reception/serve-time content handling; this primitive is the additional *at-rest re-processing* path (re-score on a model bump, re-index on a tokenizer bump). **The full wire shape is built** (that spec's § Phase 2 Step 2): the `GrantBlob` CBOR shape, the one new `for_capability` HPKE-AAD arm (no other net-new crypto) — a scope tuple's two optional qualifiers, `set` (the folder scope, 2026-07-12) and `factor` (the per-labeler license: `labeler:<id>` confines a `content.read` wrap to computing that one labeler's score and a `content.label-write` tuple to writing that one factor; absent = the built-in perimeter factors; 2026-09-27), join that AAD only when present, so every older grant's bytes and binding are untouched and a store can never re-label a wrap as licensing a different set or labeler, the owner-minted / holder-fetched `fauna.capabilities.{mint,fetch,renew,revoke}` RPCs plus the drain plane's `{rescore_worklist,submit_scores}`, and the re-score obligation reusing `content-index.md`'s per-user `tokenizer_version` watermark against the `model_versions` bus. **Content-sealing epochs (mail first) — DESIGNED 2026-07-18; B1–B5 BUILT 2026-07-18; write flip THROWN 2026-07-19.** The per-kind re-key that upgrades the honest bound above from advisory to cryptographic is now funded, designed, and **built** for **mail** (launch consumer, user-ratified 2026-07-18: a standing background scorer/indexer on an untrusted box; design spec tracked internally, built as five slices — shared-Rust derivation, client mint/renew, pubkey schedule, gated ingest cutover, holder/drain honoring — the last proven end-to-end on a real nest: a windowed grant cannot open content sealed one epoch past its held set even holding every key it ever fetched). Mechanism in brief — the design spec is the frozen rationale, this paragraph is the owner-doc summary: the mail sealing epoch is `floor(unix_seconds / MAIL_SEALING_EPOCH_SECS)` (weekly; a hard-coded per-kind Rust constant — no config surface); per-epoch keypairs derive independently from MSEK (client-side root, forward-bounded by construction — no hash chain); the owner's client pre-publishes a ~26-epoch pubkey schedule so the MTA can seal with no client online, with a never-bounce degradation (stale schedule → newest published epoch key → standing key; mail ingest never bounces on epoch machinery); the nest bounds that schedule table nest-side (hardening 2026-07-19): a provision's epoch indices must sit within ±2× the publish horizon of the current epoch and each actor's stored schedule is capped at 64 rows with prune-oldest (`MAX_ACTOR_EPOCH_SEAL_KEY_ROWS`) — provision-write-path guards only, ingest untouched; the `MailRecordEnvelope` byte-shape is unchanged (openers select keys by record timestamp + a bounded trial chain); a mail grant is either **bounded** (per-epoch wraps only) or **master-key** (standing wrap) — never both in one grant, or the window would be theater (the nest's renew handler enforces it over the merged wrap set, `wall_clock_epoch_regime_conflict`); and a bounded grant's window is never wider than its wraps: a renew that moves a per-epoch wall-clock scope's end across a sealing-epoch boundary must carry a wrap for every epoch it newly covers, or the nest refuses it (`uncovered_bounded_extension`, added 2026-09-27 — the extension epochs are `epoch_of(old_end) + 1 ..= epoch_of(new_end)`; an equal-end renew, the rotation heal's shape, extends nothing), so a keyless bump can never advertise a window whose tail no key opens. **Wrap retention — the window SLIDES, it never grows (ratified 2026-09-28; BUILT the same day):** a renewal re-centres the window on the renewal instant — the end moves to `now + L` and the start to `max(old_start, now − L)`, L the grant's mint-time length (`grant_log::renewal_window`, the ONE computation the manual renew and the auto-renew loop share) — so a renewed grant keeps one mint-length of history behind the renewal and one ahead, and its window is never wider than 2L (at most 27 weekly epochs for the standard ~90-day grant). The renew carries the new start (`RenewGrantRequest.new_epoch_start`, additive; absent keeps the stored start, which is also the folder kind's shape — its wraps are generation-indexed, not calendar epochs); the nest refuses a start that moves backward (widening into the past would need wraps the renew cannot prove) or past the end, and on EVERY renew prunes every per-epoch wall-clock wrap below `epoch_of(start)` from the merged set (`fauna_mls::wrapped_blob::prune_wraps_below_window_start`), so the held epoch set is exactly the window's epochs; the rotation heal re-wraps the *recorded* window as it stands (an equal-window renew), so both client drivers and the nest agree on the retained set. Why a ruling was needed: without retention every renewal appended one wrap per newly covered week for ever — a wrap is ~2.5 KB to a classical holder and ~3.6 KB to a post-quantum one (the 2432-byte per-epoch secret plus KEM ciphertext and framing, k × 2432 at a rotation-boundary epoch), so a weekly-renewed grant crossed the nest's then-64 KiB blob cap within months of renewals and the silent auto-renew loop went dark with only the liveness pill saying so. The cap is now 256 KiB (`MAX_CAPABILITY_GRANT_BYTES`, sized from the 2L bound with rotation headroom — a nest constant, never a knob). Rejected: keeping full history and scaling the cap (unbounded growth, ~220 KB at 60 epochs and rising for ever), and re-minting at a threshold (a fresh mint covers no history at all and churns the log twin). The trade the ruling accepts: a holder can no longer open content sealed more than one mint-length before its latest renewal — a scorer or indexer has processed it by then, and its derived views persist under the materialization tier; the user-facing duration stays the trust's *lifetime*, not its lookback (`ui/nests.md` § Expiry / renewal → *Duration and blessing*). Proven by the nest handler test that renews a post-quantum bounded grant weekly through 60 epochs and stays accepted with exactly the slid window's epochs held. **MSEK-rotation heal for bounded grants (amendment 2026-07-19):** a grant carries at most ONE wrap per `(scope, epoch)` — universal for wall-clock kinds, mint-side and renew-side alike (the builder refuses duplicate per-epoch wraps; the nest's renew replaces a differing-bytes wrap in place) — and cross-generation coverage rides *inside* the one wrap: an epoch that more than one MSEK generation could have sealed (a rotation-boundary epoch, plus its one-epoch propagation tail) gets a payload concatenating each such generation's 2432-byte per-epoch secret, newest first, chunk-trialed at the single shared length-dispatch site with AEAD arbitrating (the same try-each shape as the folder kind's two-keys-per-version CRDT precedent). Which generations qualify is computed from **recorded rotation instants** (`MailConfig.prior_msek_retirements`, additive, keyed by the prior-MSEK value): each generation covers epochs from its activation to its retirement + 1; every rotation records the instant in the same write that retains the generation, so a retained prior always carries one (the pre-amendment reading — a prior with no recorded instant is provably pre-flip and skipped — was retired 2026-09-24 by the compat-remnant sweep ([`compat-remnant-sweep.md`](compat-remnant-sweep.md) § Program 4); an unrecorded prior is now an inconsistent config and still contributes no epochs, since its interval is unknowable). This makes the renew replace-heal lossless across rotation boundaries and lets a post-rotation bounded mint cover pre-rotation in-window content; the old generation's epoch-*root* is never wrapped into a grant (it would reach old-lineage epochs beyond the window). `WrappedScopeKey.epoch` is a per-kind key-lineage index: for `folder` it is the content-key **generation version** (landed); for `mail` (and later `calendar`) it is the wall-clock sealing epoch; the AAD's kind binding keeps them cryptographically disjoint. **Multi-nest.** A user may run several nests holding different grant sets at once (§ Goal); each location does what its grants permit, and a client holding the content locally self-serves regardless. The refinement of **preferring a reachable broadly-granted nest's server-side path and falling back to local/bridge work when none is reachable** — a cross-nest capability negotiation — is the intended direction but is **not yet specified or built**: today's connect-time capability negotiation ([`version-compatibility.md`](version-compatibility.md) § Dimension 3) carries feature-family tokens, and no doc yet defines routing server-side work to a granted peer. It matters only for a **thin client** (the fauna-web SPA, which cannot run the index engine — § Search and indexing); every native app already self-serves locally, so the negotiation is an efficiency refinement, not a correctness requirement.

**Cross-user operations have no client fallback → they require every contributor's grant, or they are not offered.** An operation that must read *several users'* content — the deployment spam-baseline aggregation ([`../behavior/mail-spam.md`](../behavior/mail-spam.md), `publish_spam_baseline`: an additive merge of opt-in users' per-user models) — cannot fall back to any single user's client, which holds only that user's keys. With no plaintext mode to gate on, such an operation runs only at a position holding a reaching capability from **each opt-in contributor** (opting in = granting read of the contributed artifact); a deployment where contributors have not granted simply does not offer the baseline. The feature degrades with the granted trust, exactly as it degraded with the mode. A secure-aggregation scheme removing the grant requirement is a possible future addition, not a current requirement.
