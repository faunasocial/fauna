## Delegable-scope reclamation

- **Delegable-scope reclamation (measured and ruled 2026-10-01; parts (1) to (4) built 2026-10-02 — § Implementation status today).** The delegable scope (`state`) carries six kinds: the four preference records, the seen-set and the read markers ([`account-data-taxonomy.md`](account-data-taxonomy.md) § The audience ladder). All are generation 0 under the account's one schedule, so every row of a logical item sits at one blinded item key, whichever device wrote it. A predecessor identity's rows sit at the keys its own schedule derived, and [`../behavior/succession-aftermath.md`](../behavior/succession-aftermath.md) § Re-key scope → *The predecessor's own row is retired behind the carry* owns those. The nest keeps one live row per `(item, writer)`, collapses only a writer's own earlier rows, and counts every pair against the same cap of 4,096 as on the fleet scope ([`account-data-taxonomy.md`](account-data-taxonomy.md) § The generation machinery → *Fleet-scope reclamation* states the cap and, in clause (1), the retire kind). Before this ruling nothing retired a row here: the reclamation pass and the sign-out's severance are handed the fleet plane alone.

  **Measured 2026-10-01**, on real runtimes over the real nest handlers (the counts in full: § Implementation status today). *(a) A removed device.* Two devices over 20 channels and one preference record held 16 live rows each. A sibling removed one of them. Its fleet-scope rows went from 4 to 1 in the next pass, and its 16 delegable rows were all live six passes later. Five of those rows were the only rows of their items: the markers that device alone had raised. Retired by hand, as a rule keyed on membership would retire them, all 16 retires were accepted; the two devices that remained each still held all 20 markers in merged state and published nothing in four more rounds; and the next fresh device read 15. *(b) Sign-out and sign-in.* A sign-out erases the store and the writer key, so each sign-in on the same machine is a new writer. One machine that signed out and in three times, advancing the same five markers and one preference record in each life, left 11, 17, 23 and 29 live rows under 1, 2, 3 and 4 writers. Every earlier life's rows stay, and each life adds a row for every item it changes. *(c) Live devices.* Three devices over five channels. The first raised the markers: 5 rows. The second raised them further: 15 rows, because each of the other two devices, walking the newer value over an older one it held, re-authored the joined value as a row of its own. The third had raised nothing and now held five rows. So the scope's ordinary shape is one row per item per device that has held two values of the item, not per device that changed it. A device that meets one value only (a fourth, walking in afterwards) writes nothing. *(d) A second nest.* While its writer is a member, a row that alone carries a preference record reaches a second nest through a sibling's publish diff. Once the writer is removed the diff no longer vouches for the row ([`account-sync-plane.md`](account-sync-plane.md) § The bind leg, ruling 1(a)): after three settled passes the second nest held no row of the scope, the surviving device still held the record, and a fresh device walking that nest read nothing. *Who writes here.* The put door admits the account's own session and no other caller class (a custodian, a bridge and a third-party principal are refused), and it does not check the writer id it is handed. In the measured flows every writer of a delegable row was a device's fleet id.

  **The rule: one row carries an item, and every other row of it goes.** For a logical item X, the **cover** at a nest is a live row at X's item key, written by this device or by a verified member, that opens to this replica's merged entry for X and is published as the pass saw it (`account-sync-plane.md` § The bind leg, ruling 1(d)). A row is **below** another row of the same item when it opens to an entry that lost the merge to the other's, or when it opens to the same entry and sits earlier in that nest's serve order. A row a nest does not list has no place in its serve order: at that nest's publish diff it is below a listed cover whose entry covers it, an equal one included, since pushed it would carry nothing the cover does not and, landing last, displace the cover it duplicates (measured 2026-10-02: a successor's diff pushed its predecessor's unsent copy of the row it had just re-authored, and the cover step then retired the successor's own row behind it). The rule has four parts.

  (1) **A walk that takes a row whole writes none.** When a served seen-set or read-marker row joins with the held entry to exactly the served value, the walk adopts the row at its own coordinate, as it adopts a latest-wins row that wins. It re-authors only a join that equals neither side, which is a value no row carries yet. This alone ends (c)'s growth: a device holds a row of its own for an item only when it added to the value. Built 2026-10-02 (§ Implementation status today).

  (2) **A put names the rows it covers.** `fauna.account.state.put` gains `replaces`: rows of the put's own scope, each named by its cleartext coordinates (item key, writer, writer seq). Inside the put's transaction, once the put has passed its own sequence checks, the nest marks superseded each named row that is live at exactly those coordinates, skips one that is not, and only then counts the cap. A put that is refused supersedes nothing. The reply says how many rows went (`replaced`), and its presence tells the device that the nest read the list. On every put of an item on this scope a device names each row of the item that it has opened and merged and that is below the row it is sending: the rows its relay plane holds at the item's key under other writers and, for a carried row, the predecessor's row it was carried from. Its merged entry is the join of everything it has walked, so the row it sends covers each of them by construction. When the reply carries `replaced` the device forgets its relay copy of every row it named.

  (3) **The hand-over.** In a full pass, after the scope's reconcile and publish diff and after the fleet walk (so the member view is this pass's), a device that holds a merged entry for X and has no unsent row of its own for X re-authors the merged entry as its own row in two cases: it finds no cover; or it finds one, and a listed row it opened that no verified member wrote is not below it (that row opens to the same entry and sits later in the serve order, so it can neither be the cover nor be retired behind one). The row is the merged entry's value, merge metadata and tombstone marker verbatim, sent through the ordinary writer door, and it names every listed row of X the device opened; being the latest row of the item it is above them all. This is clause (3)(g)'s hand-over arm without a hander. Any member's pass does it, and the first put to land is the cover that stops the next device. Two devices that pass at once write two equal rows, and part (4) retires the earlier. It hands over only a merged entry that covers every listed row of X it opened, the join the rule presumes: a replica whose merged entry trails a row it opened has a walk still to merge it, and its own put of the trailing entry would collapse its own newer row at the nest.

  (4) **What is left over is retired.** In the same step the pass retires, with the existing retire kind, each listed row of X that it opened and that is below the cover. Any runtime that opened both rows may ask, whoever wrote the row: a departed device, a live sibling, itself. The nest's retention gate applies as to any retire, the pass withholds against the gate's watermark as the fleet pass does, and the device forgets its relay copy when the nest answers retired or gone. The publish diff never pushes a row that is below a listed cover (`account-sync-plane.md` § The bind leg, ruling 1(b)).

  **Why no row is lost.** Every replacement and every retire names one row and cites a row above it, and what is above is decided by two things no replica's view can change: what the two rows open to, and the nest's own serve order. So the citations cannot form a cycle, and the highest row of an item is never named, whatever each device believes about who is a member. A device's view decides one thing only, whether a row may serve as the cover. A device cites no row it has not opened, keeps every row it cannot open (clause (3)), and names exact coordinates, so a newer row of the same writer that it has not walked is untouched.

  **What retires a departed device's rows: the cover, and neither membership nor a removal.** Measurement (a) is why. A device that left by removal, by sign-out or by loss may have written the only row of an item, and the nest cannot tell such a row from one a sibling also carries. A removal says who left and nothing about what else carries the item, so clause (3)(d)'s licence does not carry over. The let-go arm's does not either: that arm retires rows that describe the departed device, and these describe the account. Under this rule a departed writer's row goes as any other row goes, once a member's row carries its item. Where a member already carries the item, parts (2) and (4) take the row. Where none does, part (3) writes the member's row first. **What must precede the retire is a member's row, and measurement (d) is why it must be a member's:** the bind leg carries to another nest only rows it can vouch for, so an item whose one row was written by a departed device is absent today from every second or rebuilt nest, and the hand-over is what closes that. A writer this replica has not verified yet (its enrollment row not merged here) is treated the same way. That costs one redundant put for each item it alone carries and loses nothing: its entry is unchanged, its row is replaced by an equal one, and its own copy leaves by the diff's refused put (ruling 1(c)). **No writer on this scope is anything but a device of the account** (measured above), so the rule needs no second principal class. **Tombstones** are entries: a tombstoned item's cover is the tombstone row, and a hand-over re-puts it as a tombstone.

  **A full scope is owed a put that needs no room.** The succession measurement (`succession-aftermath.md`, the paragraph named above) showed that a scope with no free pair cannot begin, because every carry is a new pair. With part (2) a carry names the predecessor's row and a hand-over names the departed writer's, so each is count-neutral and lands at 4,096 live rows as it does at 40. A write of an item another device has a row for names that row and is count-neutral too. The one put a full scope still refuses is the first row of an item no row carries. **A replaced row waits on no retention gate.** The gate makes a retire safe against a walker that has not walked what supersedes the retired row (clause (1)). A replaced row leaves in the transaction that inserts the row covering it, and the feed serves that row to every walker that has not walked it. A writer's own re-put collapses its earlier row with no gate for the same reason. A plain retire stays gated, because the nest cannot see what covers it. So a device that has stopped walking holds back part (4)'s retires above its mark until the user removes it, as on the fleet scope, and it holds back no put. **The fleet scope takes no `replaces`.** The nest refuses a list there `invalid_request`. That scope's retires carry orderings the gate enforces (clause (3)(d); `account-sync-plane.md` § The bind leg, ruling 7), and a replacement would walk round them. Whether a fleet-scope put may ever name a row is *Fleet-scope reclamation*'s to rule. **At a linked nest** the leg does what the pass does, on that nest's own listing (`account-sync-plane.md` § The bind leg, ruling 4): its reconcile opens that nest's rows, its diff pushes the cover and names the rows that nest lists below it, and the leg retires what that nest still lists below a cover it lists. The evidence is that nest's listing, so this needs no retire record.

  **The bound that stands: one pair per item.** The items are the four preference records, one seen-set entry per content scope the account has observed, and one read marker per channel it has read ([`../behavior/conversation-read-state.md`](../behavior/conversation-read-state.md) § The read-marker record). With one row per item the cap is met at 4,092 scopes and channels together, however many devices the account has had. Four of those scopes are the own-actor ones (mail, calendar, card, post), so that is 2,044 conversations where each has a marker and a seen-set entry. A conversation here is one chat channel, a direct conversation or a group; a scheduling channel and a shared folder's group carry neither item. Today's shape meets it at 4,096 divided by the devices and signed-out lives that touched each item: three devices over the markers of 1,366 channels. What stands above one row per item, stated: a row waiting on a gated retire, the equal row of two hand-overs that raced, and a row an older client re-authored, each until part (2) or part (4) takes it. **What a scope is owed once every pair is a different live item** is the next paragraph's: until it is built the put such a scope refuses is a new item's first row, the refused row holds back every later row of that device's journal, and nothing takes an item's row away. Predecessor rows that no successor device can open are another class (`succession-aftermath.md` § Re-key scope → *Declared residuals of the carry*, (c)(i)): nothing opens them, so nothing can cover them, and they take whatever exit clause (3)(i)'s bound (β) is given.

  **A scope full of live items: the publish goes on past the refused row, and a left conversation's rows leave the nest (measured and ruled 2026-10-01; part (5) built 2026-10-02, parts (6) and (7) built 2026-10-03 — § Implementation status today).** *Measured*, on real runtimes over the real nest handlers (the flow in full: § Implementation status today). One device filled the scope to 4,096 items of one row each: 1,069,075 B sealed, 261 B a row. *(a)* It then raised a marker on one more channel. Each of its next three passes sent that one put, the nest refused it `scope_full`, and the scope did not change. *(b)* The same device rewrote the moderation record, an item the nest holds a row of its own for, and raised a marker the nest already holds. Neither row was sent in the passes that followed: the publish stops at the first refused row. *(c)* A second device walked in, read 4,095 markers and the record, raised a marker on another new channel and rewrote the record. Its first put was refused and its second was never sent. *(d)* At the end the first device held the record's second value, the second device its third, and a fresh device read its first and neither new channel's marker. So today the first conversation a device reads at a full scope ends that device's publish on this scope, preference records included. *What a full scope costs when nothing is wrong:* every full pass's reconcile was served all 4,096 rows and took 2.7 to 3.3 s (a debug build, in-process transport), against 4 ms for a scope of one row. The rule gains three parts.

  (5) **A row refused for room is parked, and the publish goes on.** [`account-replica-posture.md`](account-replica-posture.md) § The store device principal, refinement 11 → *A row refused for room is parked* owns the law: how the row is remembered, retried and healed, and why this scope may break the order and the fleet scope may not. On this scope a full scope then costs the new item and nothing else. A device's re-put of an item it has a row for, a put that names a row (part (2)) and a tombstone need no room, and they land. The publish diff follows ([`account-sync-plane.md`](account-sync-plane.md) § The bind leg, ruling 1(d)).

  (6) **A departed scope's rows leave the nest, and the entries stay on the devices.** A *member scope* is a content scope the account holds by membership, which today is a conversation channel. Its items on this scope are its seen-set entry and its read marker. A replica that concludes a scope's departure keeps the scope in a device-local list until an affirmative membership answer names it again ([`account-replica-posture.md`](account-replica-posture.md) § The replica boundary, T2 transition (3) owns the conclusion and the list). At its first affirmative pass under this rule it also lists every member scope it wrote a row for and the answer does not name: it wrote there, so it was in the scope, and the answer says it no longer is. In every full pass, in the step that runs parts (3) and (4), the replica retires each listed row of a departed scope's item that it opened and that was written by itself or by a writer that is not a verified member. It never retires a member sibling's row on this licence. That device retires its own row when it concludes the departure, and until then the account is in the conversation as far as it can tell. The retire is the existing kind behind the existing gate, and the device forgets its relay copy on the nest's answer. The replica also sends no row of such an item: an unsent or parked one leaves the publish, and the diff pushes none. Its merged entry stays in the store, where nothing reads it while the scope is not held. At a linked nest the leg does the same on that nest's listing.

  (7) **A member scope's item is handed over only by a device that is in the scope.** Part (3) applies to a member scope's item only on a pass whose membership answer is affirmative and names the scope. A device that cannot tell (no answer this pass, or a host that wires no source) hands over nothing for member scopes. Preference records and the own-actor scopes' seen-set entries are handed over as part (3) says. This is what keeps a retired row retired: every other device still holds the entry, and without this part the first of them to pass would write it back. It is also what brings an entry back. A device that holds the entry and is in the scope again finds no cover and re-authors it, so a re-joined conversation resumes at the old position wherever a device kept it.

  **Why two devices never undo each other.** A device acts on its own rows and on rows no member wrote, so a disagreement about a scope costs at most one put. A device that has not yet learned of the departure may hand the item over once more; its row stays until that device concludes the departure and retires it. A device whose membership answer is wrong the other way (its channel state lost while the account is still in the conversations) retires its own rows, a member device re-authors each item once, and nothing is lost, because every device keeps its entries.

  **The bound, stated.** With parts (5) to (7) the scope holds the eight items that never leave and two for each conversation the account is in: 2,044 conversations at once, where before it was 2,044 over the life of the account. Past it the first row of a new item parks. That conversation's read position and observation record stay on the device that wrote them, reach a sibling only over the peer leg (which serves a parked row like any other), and land at the nest when a conversation is left. A preference record written for the first time at a full scope parks the same way, and parked preference rows are retried first. The apps show nothing for it: a notice would be a new UI element, and this ruling adds none. **What stays, stated.** (α) A conversation left before the rule is built, whose rows were written by devices since removed and which no present device wrote to: no device remembers the departure, so nothing retires its two rows. (β) A member device's row for a scope that device never held, which only today's re-authoring walk writes (part (1) ends it): it goes with part (4) while a cover stands, and stays if the cover's writer retires the cover first. (γ) A conversation left on an account's only device, which then signs out before its next pass: two rows. None of the three grows once every device runs the rule.

  **Compatibility.** `replaces` and `replaced` are additive wire fields, and every nest reads the list: a put that lands has superseded each named row or found it not live, so the device forgets its relay copy of every row it named. (A device-side arm that kept the copies when the reply carried no `replaced`, for a nest older than the field, was built 2026-10-02 and removed 2026-10-04 with the compat-remnant sweep — [`compat-remnant-sweep.md`](compat-remnant-sweep.md), the 2026-10-04 paragraphs: no such nest exists.) Parts (1), (3) and (4) are client-side: an existing put, an existing retire, nothing new at rest. Parts (5) to (7) are client-side too: an existing put, an existing retire, and two device-local lists (the parked rows and the departed scopes).

  **Refused.** *Retiring a departed writer's rows on membership or on removal evidence:* measured, it took five of twenty markers off the nest and no device put them back. *One designated hander,* as clause (3)(g) names one per generation: there the choice rests on reach, which this scope does not have, and a designated device that has stopped walking would hold every hand-over. *A retire ahead of its cover, with the cover journaled on the device:* between the two the nest serves the item to no one, a device lost in that window takes the only copy with it, and at a full scope another writer's waiting row can take the pair the retire freed. The rung is recreatable (§ The audience ladder), but a muted-word list is recreated by the user retyping it, and the put that needs no room makes the window unnecessary. *The nest collapsing per item at every put, with no list:* it cannot tell a row the putter merged from one the putter never saw, so a multi-master item would converge on whoever wrote last. *One row per device as redundancy:* a nest is rebuilt from relay planes, which hold verified siblings' rows beside a device's own. *Raising the cap:* it moves the wall, and every live row is served again to every device at every full pass: measured, 261 B a row and 1 MB a pass at the cap, which a debug build walked in about three seconds. *Refusing a new item at the writer door when the scope is full:* the device's count is a pass old, so a row can be journaled and refused all the same and the publish has to survive that anyway; and a refused write is lost, where a parked one lands by itself when room appears. *Retiring a left conversation's rows only once the cap is met:* by then the device that concluded the departure has usually been replaced, and the conclusion cannot be made again. At the departure the device is there, and the feed stays as large as the conversations the account is in. *Concluding a departure from a membership answer that omits the scope:* a conversation never opened on a device is missing from that device's answer while the account is in it. *A tombstone, or a record of shed items, on the plane:* a tombstone is a live row and frees nothing, and a record that lists shed items is one more item that only grows. *Dropping the entry from the devices:* nothing needs it gone, and keeping it is what lets a re-join resume. *Retiring a member sibling's row for a scope this device has left:* two devices that disagree would retire and re-author one item at every pass. *Packing the markers of many channels into one row:* every read would seal a row of up to 64 KiB where it seals 261 B, and the kind's value shape is frozen.

## Implementation status today

- **Measured and ruled 2026-10-01; part (5) BUILT 2026-10-02, parts (6) and (7) BUILT 2026-10-03 — § Delegable-scope reclamation, parts (5) to (7), a scope full of live items.** *Part (5), built (shared Rust, every app's runtime).* On the bound delegable plane `AccountStatePlane::publish_pending` parks a row the nest refuses `scope_full` and goes on (`libs/fauna-account-plane/src/account_state_plane.rs`): the row's `writer_seq` is recorded durably in the store's parked list (`AccountStore::park`, one `store_meta` key per scope and writer, `parked/<scope>/<writer>`, so every backend holds it and it goes with a wiped store) before the slot moves past it. Every publish first retries the parked rows (`retry_parked`): a preference record's row before any other (`preference_put::is_preference_kind`), the rest oldest first, no further once one is refused for room again; a row the entry has moved past leaves the list unsent, and a retry refused `stale_writer_seq` (the typed `CoordinateRefused`) stays parked with its relay row retired as before and does not stop the rest. The walk's own-echo arm unparks the row it meets. `succession_tail::tail_reauthor_pass` re-journals a predecessor's parked rows with its tail, compacts them with a burnt predecessor's carried rows, and empties its list. `PumpReport::parked` counts the delegable plane's parked rows after each pass. The `ScopeFull` refusal now names its scope and says on this one that the row is parked and lands when the scope has room. Because the carry's re-authored row now parks, the walk's carry arms no longer meet a `scope_full` refusal on this scope; `WalkReport::carry_scope_full` stays as the fleet scope's view. The fleet plane still stops at `scope_full`.
