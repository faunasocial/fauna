## Goal

The user can see every entity participating in their deployment — their own client devices and their nests — and, per entity, **what it is trusted to read** and **what work it is delegated to run**. Heavy background work (content re-scoring on a model change, full-text indexing, backup uploads) runs on a **capable, always-on** participant — a nest, or a plugged-in desktop — and stays **off battery/metered mobile devices**, by default and without configuration.

"Participant" is the **docs/internal umbrella term only** — it names the shared model, never a page or a user-facing string (§ Naming). The user-facing surfaces are the **Devices** and **Nests** pages plus a small cross-participant **Task delegation** surface.

## The participant model (shared Rust)

One shared row shape backs both sibling pages (priority #2/#3 — one model, two renderers). A **participant** is:

- **Kind + identity** — client device (actor id, device id) or nest (nest actor id, URL). Special-cased per kind at render, shared underneath.
- **Class** (for delegation eligibility) — `always-on nest` / `plugged-in desktop` / `battery mobile`. A nest is always `always-on`; a desktop app reports plugged-in/on-AC state; iOS/Android are always `battery mobile`.
- **Trust facet** (nests only, v1) — the content-processing grants the participant holds: scope + `lasts-until`, revoke control, and the **Now / History** lens over the client-authoritative signed grant-event log (per-app frontier: [`../ui/nests.md`](../ui/nests.md) § Implementation status today; mechanism: [`../architecture/encryption-at-rest.md`](../architecture/encryption-at-rest.md) § Capability tiering).
- **Roles** — durable relationships the participant has, rendered as badges: **backup destination** (Q-F; derived from the destination list matching the nest's actor pubkey), sync device, home nest / linked nest, **escrow holder** (ratified 2026-08-15 with the custody facet — a participant holding the account's R14 (account-data-plane.md § The ratified decisions) generation-key escrow, nest by default; the badge is what makes the participant pages the one "who holds what for me" surface), … Additive; each role's behavior stays owned by its own doc.
- **Delegated tasks** — which heavy task kinds this participant currently runs (§ Task delegation), shown per-row and assignable on the Task delegation surface.
- **Quota display** (nests only) — the user's usage/allowance on that nest, read-only (Q-D). Read over the user's authenticated connection to *that* nest (the same quota read the Settings status display uses against the home nest).
- **Custody facet** (ratified 2026-08-15 with the W8 (account-data-plane.md § Workstreams) opening; BUILT ON TUI, per-app trickle-down in progress — § Implementation status today owns current status). The replica-posture axis rendered per participant: a **custodian** participant holds sealed copies it cannot read ([`../architecture/account-replica-posture.md`](../architecture/account-replica-posture.md) § Replica posture owns the mechanism; § The custody grant + ceremony the grant). Two row families: (a) **custodian rows on the owner's pages** — custodian *nests* on Settings → Nests, custodian *devices* on Settings → Devices, including the one participant kind this facet adds to the model: the **cross-account custodian device** (a friend's device holding for this account — identity = the counterpart account + the pinned device-principal key from the custody ceremony); an own relay-only enrolled device is an ordinary device row wearing a keyless-posture marker. (b) **held-for-others rows on the host's Devices page** — what this account's devices hold for other accounts, with the consent surface for incoming custody offers and the per-custody budget control. Per-page UX: [`../ui/nests.md`](../ui/nests.md) / [`../ui/devices.md`](../ui/devices.md); element IDs arrive at build via ui.yaml rule-A approval. Nest-side held-for-others (custodial accounts on a box) stays the admin quota model's business ([`admin.md`](admin.md)), not this facet's.

- **Third-party principal (participant kind added 2026-09-05 with the third-party integration chain; unbuilt — [`../architecture/third-party.md`](../architecture/third-party.md) § The principal model rule 5).** An approved external app, device app or nest-hosted plugin is a participant beside device, nest and cross-account custodian device, so the trust facet renders its grants, the history lens its audit log (the oracle's `GrantEvent`s included), and revoke is the same control. Identity = the principal row (the document URL, the publisher domain, the holder key). Its **class** for delegation is `always-on nest` when hosted, `battery mobile` / `plugged-in desktop` when a device app reports it, and *n/a* when remote — a remote principal runs no delegated task kinds. The mechanism asymmetry below holds: a principal is an *untrusted holder* of scope-limited wrapped material, never a trusted mint. The page that lists principals is [`../ui/connected-apps.md`](../ui/connected-apps.md); this doc owns the row's place in the model.

**Mechanism asymmetry (load-bearing — never paper over).** Clients and nests are asymmetric content-processing participants: a **client is the trusted mint** — it holds the content root, processes with its own material, and needs no grant ("delegating to a client" = that client runs the background loop); a **nest is an untrusted holder** — it receives a scope-limited, HPKE-wrapped capability grant (the v1 mechanism). One user-facing concept, two mechanisms dispatched by participant kind. **Identity is never delegated either way** ([`../architecture/key-material-hierarchy.md`](../architecture/key-material-hierarchy.md) rule #7) — a maximally-trusted participant reads content; it never impersonates.

## Task delegation (Q-B/Q-C)

### Concepts

- **Task kind** — a heavy, resumable background job over the user's content. Initial kinds: **backup uploads** (nest-run since the segment-backup slice-5 flip — the source nest is the writer, [`backup-restore.md`](backup-restore.md) § Background Tasks), **content re-scoring** on a model/rules change (the capability design's drain obligation), **full-text indexing** ([`content-index.md`](content-index.md)). The set is extensible; each kind declares what it needs (content access, storage, compute).
- **Participant class** — `always-on nest` / `plugged-in desktop` / `battery mobile` (§ The participant model).
- **Assignment** — per task kind: **automatic** (default) or **pinned** to one participant.
- **Policy order (the automatic default)** — a task kind runs on: (1) a nest holding a sufficient grant for it, else (2) a plugged-in desktop, else (3) it waits. **Battery-mobile participants never run heavy task kinds** — not even as a last resort; the work queues until an eligible participant appears. (Mobile still does its own foreground/incremental work — this policy governs only the heavy background kinds.)
- **Dispatch by kind** — assigning a task to a **nest** means minting/holding the capability grant its processing needs (the v1 mechanism; revoking the grant unassigns it). Assigning to a **client** means that client runs the loop with its own keys — no grant involved.

### Coordination primitive (Q-C — ratified + built)

- **The assignment record lives in the account-state plane kind `fauna.state.delegation`** (client-authoritative, sealed + synced across the user's devices; [`../architecture/config-dissolution.md`](../architecture/config-dissolution.md) § The `__config` dissolution schedule → *The kinds* — it rode the `__config` blob until the rail retired on 2026-10-02, [`../architecture/config-dissolution.md`](../architecture/config-dissolution.md) § The `__config` dissolution schedule → *The closure order*, step (6)) — so every one of the user's clients sees the same assignments, and the surface is client-revocable per the product invariants.
- **A running participant re-reads the assignments; there is no change push.** The lease loop gets no change push, so a seat that read the delegation record once at login would honour a pin only until the next app start — the surface would name a device the user had already assigned away from, and that device would go on running the kind. Every lease loop therefore refreshes its copy of the pins on its own heartbeat cadence and re-decides at once on a change (`fauna_client_conversations::index_lease::refresh_pins` → `LeaseCoordinator::set_config` + the loop's wake). The cadence is the lease loop's because the pin is an input to the decision that loop makes every cycle; the page is unaffected either way, since a visit re-reads the config itself.
- **The running participant holds a heartbeat lease** per task kind. Other participants observe the lease; when it goes stale (runner offline, unplugged, uninstalled), the **next eligible participant per the policy order takes over**. Exactly-one-runner is advisory, not transactional — task kinds must be idempotent/resumable (they already are: segment upload is manifest-keyed; re-score is obligation-drain; index builds converge via the stage-time content-id guard plus `(kind, content_id)` query-time dedup — [`content-index-ingest.md`](content-index-ingest.md) § Ingest triggers, v1).
- **The lease rendezvous** is naturally the home nest (a small advisory lease record all the user's participants can read/heartbeat). **Wire ratified (slice 2, 2026-07-07; tracked internally):** two `fauna.delegation.*` RPCs — `.heartbeat` (a participant claims/renews; the nest writes it **last-writer-wins**) and `.observe` (read the current per-kind lease snapshot) — plus a best-effort `fauna.delegation.lease_changed` push (a "re-observe" nudge; the observe poll is the backstop). **The nest is a dumb, per-actor, in-memory (never persisted) last-writer-wins blackboard; it does no CAS/epoch/transaction** — it serves the record plus a nest-computed `age_ms` freshness, and **all decisions are client-side.** Convergence to a single runner comes from a pure client rule (`decide`): a fresh co-tier holder is left alone (**sticky**, no churn), a holder that is not in the current candidate set is **preempted** (this yields tier-1-nest-over-tier-2-desktop handover for free, since `current_candidates` returns only the winning tier), and a free/stale lease is claimed by any eligible participant (races converge via the sticky rule, needing no cross-device ranking). Heartbeat/stale constants are hard-coded Rust (not config). **The blackboard admits only the shared kind list** (`fauna_core::delegation::LIVE_TASK_KINDS`, keyed per actor): a heartbeat naming any other kind is refused as malformed (Rejected-class — a newer client naming a kind this nest predates classifies it per [`../architecture/version-compatibility.md`](../architecture/version-compatibility.md)) and writes nothing, and an observe simply answers no lease for it — so an actor holds at most one slot per listed kind, and the in-memory map cannot be grown by a caller.
- The web SPA's MLS tab-election is the intra-browser seed of this pattern (last-writer-wins + staleness, no CAS — the model slice 2 mirrors); the cross-device MLS state work (`docs/goal/behavior/devices.md` § Cross-device MLS group-state sync) is adjacent prior art but deliberately **not** mirrored (its hash-CAS + epoch guard is for irrecoverable state, wrong for a disposable lease).
- **The nest as runner (slice 6 — ratified + built).** For a nest-run kind (`content-rescore` and `backup-upload` — **not** `index`, which is client-built; § *Kinds no client can run today* below), the home nest participates **in-process** — it heartbeats its own blackboard directly (no wire kind, no self-connection), claiming an owner's `(kind)` lease as `AlwaysOnNest` **only while the box holds what that kind needs to run**. Sufficiency is per-kind: `content-rescore` needs an approved enrolled holder with a live `content.read{mail}` + `content.label-write` grant (the pair the client mint issues); `backup-upload` needs the owner's granted `NestBackupKey`, ≥1 registered destination, **and** passes that are not stalled. The first two are the set the nest's backup worker sweeps, so the lease cannot claim work the worker isn't doing. The third is what keeps the predicate honest in the *failure* direction (ratified 2026-07-30): capability alone is configuration-shaped, so a nest whose every pass fails — destination unreachable, its nest-writer grant revoked destination-side, TLS, quota — would keep satisfying it and heartbeat forever, while every app stands down on seeing a fresh `AlwaysOnNest` holder; the owner's segments would then be backed up by nobody, with the Task-delegation row still naming the nest as runner. So a run of consecutive **wholly-failed** sweeps (every `(destination, kind)` tuple erroring; any success resets) makes the owner insufficient and the nest releases the lease, and a client takes over. **The worker keeps retrying a released owner** — releasing is not standing down, and it is what lets a recovered nest re-claim; a nest and a client both uploading is the safe overlap (uploads are content-addressed and idempotent), a kind nobody uploads is not. The failure memory is in-process like the blackboard itself: a restart re-claims and one more failed sweep re-establishes the truth. Holding the grant IS the assignment, so a revoke **releases the lease immediately** (nest-internal release + `lease_changed` push; a client's lease can only go stale, the nest's own can be dropped). Because the user's pins live in the sealed `fauna.state.delegation` record the nest can never read, the nest runs no `current_candidates`/`decide`: its lease policy is deliberately minimal — renew its own, claim free/stale, and **never preempt a fresh foreign holder**.
- **Tier-1 handover *into* the nest is client-side, and the client's evidence is the observed holder's own class.** An eligible client that sees a **fresh** `AlwaysOnNest` holder treats it as tier-1 and stands down; the nest keeps the lease. The evidence is deliberately the lease record rather than the client's grant-event log: `backup-upload`'s `NestBackupKey` row is a live nest read kept **outside** the signed log ([`../ui/nests.md`](../ui/nests.md) § Trust facet — backup rows), and a nest heartbeats a kind only when its own sufficiency scan passed, so the claim already carries the fact. This grants the rendezvous nest no new trust — it also supplies the holder, class and age a client's `decide` already consumes — and the "claims but never works" case is caught by the source-untrusted backup audit loop instead ([`backup-restore.md`](backup-restore.md)), which is the right detector for it. **Freshness is required, not incidental:** a stale nest must drop out of the candidate set, or an eligible client would be gated out permanently rather than for the staleness window.
- **The work loop's enforcement half is per-kind.** `content-rescore` is gated at the nest's admission chokepoint (the drain worklist serves an owner only while the nest may claim — or holds — that owner's lease), the nest-side analogue of the client `with_lease_gate`; in-flight write-backs are never interrupted, matching the client gate's per-pass semantics. `backup-upload` needs no such gate: its sufficiency predicate *is* the backup worker's sweep set, so a second gate would restate the same condition.
- **The `index` kind under the lease (RULED 2026-08-03).** A client index builder **heartbeats the advisory `index` lease for the lifetime of its attachment** — from the moment a launched builder is registered at login until logout, not merely while actively staging — contending through the ordinary `current_candidates`/`decide` cycle like any other kind. Two things follow. (1) `RunnerStatus` becomes truthful: the row names the standing builder-of-record, and `Waiting` returns to meaning what it says — *no builder device is live right now* — which is exactly the phones-only lagging-index state [`content-index.md`](content-index.md) § Where the index is built (*Build vs. query*) describes, made user-visible on the Task-delegation page instead of left implicit. (2) A pin stops being inert: the pinned desktop claims, and every other builder's `decide` yields for the work the lease governs. **What the lease *gates* is deliberately narrower than what heartbeats, and that split is owned by [`content-index.md`](content-index.md) § Where the index is built:** stand-down binds only *re-presenting* build work — the launch catch-up backlog and future re-index passes — never the receive-path trickle, which is foreground/incremental work outside this surface's scope exactly as § Concepts already scopes the policy to the heavy background kinds. The MDA bridge is **not** a lease participant (same owner section). Ruled 2026-08-03; **built the same day on the direct-Rust seats (tui + linux), still unwired on the UniFFI ones** — § Implementation status today.

### Data shape (ratified + landed — `fauna-core/src/data.rs` + `delegation.rs`)

```rust
// fauna.state.delegation — client-authoritative, an account-state plane kind
struct DelegationConfig { assignments: Vec<TaskAssignment> }   // Default = empty ⇒ pure policy defaults

struct TaskAssignment {
    task_kind: String,                  // "backup-upload" | "content-rescore" | "index" | …
    pinned_to: Option<ParticipantRef>,  // None ⇒ automatic (policy order)
}
// ParticipantRef identifies a device (device id) or nest (actor pubkey) — the shared model's key.
```

`DelegationConfig::default()` is empty: zero configuration yields the pure policy order (works out-of-the-box). The lease is **not** config — it is live advisory state (the rendezvous record above), never persisted in the account's synced state.

### The assignment picker (the Task-delegation surface's one rule)

A pin collapses the candidate set to exactly the pinned participant, which runs **iff currently eligible** — so pinning a participant that can *never* be eligible makes the task kind wait **forever**. The picker is therefore a correctness surface, and its rule is stated once, in shared Rust, for **all 7 apps** (priority #2): **a client offers as a pin target only a participant that can run the kind.**

**That rule is per-(client, kind), and since 2026-08-03 the code says so.** The decision has two halves and a self-pin needs both: `TaskKindSpec::client_runnable` — can *any* client run this kind — and `HeavyTaskCapability` — which kinds does *this build* ship a runner for (`HeavyTaskCapability::runner_for([…])`; a client that runs none, the former `ViewerOnly`, is never a pin target for anything). Q-B's *never on battery* is enforced at the point of choice, not merely at the point of running: the mobiles and the web SPA run nothing, and web additionally cannot ever build the content index (tantivy in a browser — [`content-index.md`](content-index.md) § Where queries run).

*(Corrected 2026-08-03. The two halves were previously one `Runner`/`ViewerOnly` bit per client, which was a faithful encoding **only while every lease-driving client shipped every client-runnable kind's runner**. That stopped being true and the bit then made the picker lie in both directions: **linux** reported `Runner` after its in-app upload driver was deleted 2026-07-29, so the picker offered a `backup-upload` self-pin it could only ever wait on — a live stranding of exactly the kind this section forbids; and **tui**, which does run the content-index builder, was `ViewerOnly` and so unofferable for a kind it genuinely runs. Both are fixed by the split, and the general lesson is worth keeping: whenever a per-app runner set diverges, the picker's honesty depends on the per-kind half existing.)*

Assigning a **nest-run** kind to the nest is not a picker action at all: it is minting the trust grant on the Nests page (§ Dispatch by kind), and revoking it un-assigns.

**`content-rescore` is the one `client_runnable = false` kind, and it is nest-run** — permanently this major, so it offers "This device" on **no** client, desktop included. **`index` flipped to `client_runnable = true` on 2026-08-03**, when the client builder began running end-to-end (linux and tui resume a builder at login and publish). It was never nest-run: the content index is built at **client capability positions, never the nest**, and no index grant is minted this major (owner: [`content-index.md`](content-index.md) § Where the index is built; the nest cannot derive an index key at all under [`../architecture/key-material-hierarchy.md`](../architecture/key-material-hierarchy.md) rule #7, so a nest runner is key-impossible, not merely unbuilt) — its `false` was purely transitional, exactly as promised. **The flip was safe with only two of the seven apps building precisely because the capability is per-kind:** windows/macos drove the lease loop for `backup-upload` and correctly withheld the `index` self-pin until they wired the builder. **They wired it 2026-08-03** — the UniFFI `conversations_session` factory now injects the content-index builder on its *desktop* targets, so windows/macos declare `index` too, while iOS/Android keep withholding it because they deliberately query without building ([`content-index.md`](content-index.md) § Where queries run — *Build vs. query is not the same question*). Both halves read one build-time constant, so a client cannot offer a pin its build never honours. *(Earlier correction, 2026-08-02: slice 6 pattern-matched `index` onto `content-rescore`'s nest-run template on the same day the nest-side ingest build was deleted; that restatement outlived the mechanism it restated.)*

**`backup-upload`'s transitional exception is OVER as of 2026-08-16 — it is now `client_runnable = false`, a nest-run kind on both halves of the rule.** The paragraph below records why the exception existed and how it was discharged; it is history, not a live carve-out. The last in-app upload driver (windows') was deleted 2026-08-16, and the flag flipped in the same commit, together with the retirement of `FfiHeavyTaskCapability::Runner` — the only arm that ever declared the kind — so neither half of the picker rule can offer a `backup-upload` self-pin any more. Build detail + the per-arm ledger: [`backup-restore.md`](backup-restore.md) § Background Tasks → *Flip status (slice 5)*.

**How the exception worked while it lasted.** It became nest-run at the segment-backup slice-5 flip, but stayed `client_runnable = true` until the last in-app upload driver was deleted — **windows was the last to remain**; linux's went 2026-07-29 (and linux correspondingly stopped *declaring* the kind, which is how the per-kind half retires a client from a kind without touching the kind's own flag), android's went 2026-08-15 (android always shipped `HeavyTaskCapability::ViewerOnly` and never declared the kind, so its deletion retires a driver, not a declaration), and **apple's went 2026-08-15 — the case that needed BOTH moves**: macOS shipped a driver *and* declared the kind (through `FfiHeavyTaskCapability::Runner`), so deleting the driver alone would have left precisely linux's four-day stranding in place. The FFI enum grew an `IndexOnly` arm for it (a desktop that runs the content-index builder but drives no upload loop) rather than widening `Runner`; **windows moved onto that same arm on 2026-08-16, `Runner` lost its last caller, and the variant was retired with the flag flip.** Flipping the kind's flag early would have stranded a user's *existing, still-legitimate* self-pin to one of the devices that still shipped the driver — the exact failure the flag exists to prevent. That is why the flag flipped to `false` only as the final step, once no client shipped the driver; while the exception stood, both a pinned client driver and the nest were legal runners, and the lease's never-preempt + stand-down rules kept exactly one of them working at a time.

Two corollaries keep the surface honest rather than merely safe: an assignment the user already holds is always **rendered** (so a pin made from another client — or a stale one predating this rule — is visible and escapable), even where it would not be *offered*; and **Automatic** is always offered, first, on every app. Owner of the shape: `fauna_core::delegation::{PinOption, delegation_rows, resolve_pin}`; the async surface is `fauna_client_delegation::TaskDelegationView`.

## Don't do these

- Don't build a merged "Participants" page — deliberately not ratified (Q-A); a merge is a fresh user decision.
- Don't put "capability" (or "grant") in any user-facing string (Q-E).
- Don't scope-creep the Devices page — participant facets arrive through this doc + ui.yaml approval only.
- Don't rebuild the backups destination surface — the role badge *derives from* the destination list; the operational home stays the Backups page (Q-F).
- Don't let a heavy task kind run on a battery-mobile participant, even as a fallback — it queues instead (Q-B).
- Don't offer a pin target that can never run the kind (a battery mobile, the web SPA) — the pin would strand the task forever (§ The assignment picker). Don't re-derive that rule per client either; it lives in `delegation_rows`/`resolve_pin`.
- Don't make the lease transactional or nest-authoritative — it is advisory liveness over idempotent tasks; assignments are client-authoritative in the `fauna.state.delegation` record (Q-C).
- Don't delegate identity — trust means *read*, never *impersonate* (rule #7).
