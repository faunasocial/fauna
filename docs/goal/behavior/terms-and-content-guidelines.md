## Why this doc exists

Two stores demand a published document for any product with user-generated content, and Fauna had none — no page on fauna.social, no link in any app.

- **Microsoft Store Policies 7.20 § 11.12 User Generated Content** (learn.microsoft.com/windows/apps/publish/store-policies, page dated 2026-09-14; read 2026-10-14, re-read in full 2026-10-16): *"User Generated Content (UGC) is content that users contribute to a product and which can be viewed or accessed by other users in an online state. If your product contains UGC, you must:"* — *"Publish and make available to users a product terms of service, code of conduct, and/or content guidelines for User Generated Content that are accessible from within the product and on your website."*; *"Provide a means for users to report inappropriate, illegal, or harmful UGC within the product to the developer for review and removal or disablement if found to violate applicable content guidelines, and/or implement a method for proactive detection of inappropriate or harmful UGC."*; *"Respect user safety, parental, and content privilege settings, and gracefully handle scenarios in which a user does not have access to UGC or related online functionality due to restricted privileges."*; *"Remove or disable UGC when requested by Microsoft."* (A fifth bullet binds only products integrating a third-party mod or UGC platform, which Fauna is not.) The 2026-10-14 read had dropped the report bullet's closing *"and/or … proactive detection"* clause; it is restored here because it is the one alternative the policy offers to a report path.
- **Google Play's User Generated Content policy** (support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9876937; the page carries no revision date; read 2026-10-14, re-read in full 2026-10-16): an app that contains or features UGC *"must implement robust, effective, and ongoing UGC moderation that:"* — *"Requires users accept the app's terms of use and/or user policy before users can create or upload UGC;"* *"Defines objectionable content and behaviors (in a way that complies with Google Play Developer Program Policies), and prohibits them in the app's terms of use or user policies;"* *"Conducts UGC moderation, as is reasonable and consistent with the type of UGC hosted by the app. This includes providing an in-app system for reporting and blocking objectionable UGC and users, and taking action against UGC or users where appropriate."*; and among its worked examples *"Apps that provide access to publicly accessible UGC, such as social networking apps and blogger apps, must implement in-app functionality to report users and content, and to block users."* and *"UGC features that enable 1:1 user interaction with specific users (for example, direct messaging, tagging, mentioning, etc.) must provide an in-app functionality for blocking users."*; plus *"Provides safeguards to prevent in-app monetization from encouraging objectionable user behavior."* The policy's incidental-sexual-content conditions (hidden by default behind a two-action filter, children barred by an age screen) bind the content-label and family-safety planes, not the report mechanism.
- **Apple's App Review Guideline 1.2 User-Generated Content** (developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines, page *"Last Updated: October 16, 2026"*; read 2026-10-16): apps that let users contribute content *"must include:"* — *"A method for filtering objectionable material from being posted to the app"*, *"A mechanism to report offensive content and timely responses to concerns"*, *"The ability to block abusive users from the service"*, *"Published contact information so users can easily reach you"* — and, two paragraphs on, *"It is your responsibility to remove content that violates this guideline, your terms of service, or your community standards. Developers who fail to implement appropriate safeguards or fail to remediate identified violations after notice may be considered in violation of the Developer Code of Conduct. If we find such content, we will ask you to remove it, and provide a plan to improve your compliance with this guideline."* It asks for no terms document (it names *"your terms of service"* as something the developer may have, not must); the four bullets are owned by [`moderation.md`](moderation.md) § User-initiated reporting → *Why this exists*, and the removal responsibility is mapped below. The 2026-10-14 reading that Apple asks for *"reporting, blocking, filtering and contact details, not removal"* was wrong on the last point: the responsibility-to-remove paragraph is in the guideline.

The reporting, blocking and filtering halves are built on all 7 apps; this doc owns the document half, and § The stores' requirements against the admin's levers the mapping of the whole requirement onto what an admin can do. The gap gates the first Microsoft Store submission and Play's and the App Store's first public releases, never internal testing.

## The federated premise — whose terms

The organisation publishes software and runs no user's nest. Content lives on the nest each user chose; that nest's admin is the party who admits users, holds the levers (suspension, the legal-takedown withholding), receives reports, and under the EU Digital Services Act is the hosting provider owing notice-and-action. The organisation holds none of a user's content and no key to it, and cannot act on a nest it does not run — the same position a mail app's publisher is in toward the user's mail server. The one nest it does run, `fauna.build`, exists for store reviewers and its own testers ([`../architecture/org-nests.md`](../architecture/org-nests.md) § fauna.build — the acceptance nest), and there the organisation *is* the admin.

So a single "terms of service" in the hosted-platform sense — one operator, one set of rules, one removal desk — does not exist and must not be faked. What can truthfully be published is layered: the organisation's terms for *using the app*, including a baseline of content guidelines every nest starts from, and each nest's own rules on top. The fediverse's apps converged on the same split (an app's own terms, plus the server rules a user sees and accepts when joining a server), which is also what makes it legible to a store reviewer.

## The decisions (proposed 2026-10-14; ratified by the owner 2026-10-20 as recommended — decision 5 ruled 2026-10-17)

1. **Layered terms: one organisation document, plus per-nest rules.** The organisation publishes one page, *Terms and content guidelines* — what Fauna is, who runs a nest and what an admin can and cannot do, the content guidelines a user agrees to, how reporting works, and what the organisation itself can and cannot do. Per-nest rules (§ Per-nest rules) are the product-correct second layer and the one the fediverse precedent shows, but no store requires them; they are target state, built later, and `fauna.build`'s own rules publish through that mechanism, never through the site. **Recommended:** both layers, the organisation's page first.
2. **The content guidelines are the report-reason vocabulary, spelled out.** The eight reasons a user can report under — `spam · harassment · hate · violence · sexual · illegal · impersonation · other` ([`moderation.md`](moderation.md) § User-initiated reporting → *What a report carries*) — are the guidelines, one plain-language rule each, with *other* read as "the rules your nest adds". A guideline nobody can report under is a dead letter, and a report reason that no guideline names is a surprise; one vocabulary, owned once in shared Rust, keeps the page and the sheet from drifting apart. A new reason therefore lands in the enum and on the page in the same change. **Recommended** as stated. *Note (owner-confirmed 2026-10-15 in the review, superseded 2026-10-16):* on 2026-10-15 the page was to list the seven rules while per-nest rules are unbuilt, with *other* named as the catch-all report reason and the eighth guideline returning when the rules ship; on 2026-10-16, in the request's review with the owner present, the eighth guideline returned to the page as *Your nest's rules*, stated as planned ("once nests can publish rules, which is planned"), and *other* is named below the list as the catch-all report reason.
3. **Acceptance is a by-continuing notice at every gesture that puts the user on a nest, with nothing persisted** (amended 2026-10-19 — *every gesture*, not *the two steps*: round 1 of the public-texts review found two account-making paths the first wording missed, and the terms page and the certification paragraph promise the notice "at the step that joins or claims a nest" on every path). Play requires acceptance before a user can create UGC. An account on a nest is made three ways ([`login.md`](login.md) § Direct Auth, the *Registration is always required* bullet: the registration ceremony, the admin claim, an admin admitting a user), and a user reaches one through five gestures on four wizard pages; the notice — one sentence above the page's bottom row, *"By continuing you agree to the Fauna terms and content guidelines"*, with the link — renders on every one of them: **(i)** `invite_request`, the invite-request submit and the invite-code redeem (joining a nest; an approved request is admitted by the admin later, so the submit is the user's gesture) — always rendered; **(ii)** `claim_code`, the claim submit (claiming one's own nest by a typed code or claim URI, the factory-reset re-claim included — the admin accepts as a user too) — always rendered; **(iii)** `nest_provisioning`, *Buy and set up* (`provisioning-start-button`), the gesture that builds a rented box **and claims it**: the claim is automatic and the user never sees a code ([`onboarding-provisioning.md`](onboarding-provisioning.md) § 6 *Provisioning = build + claim*), so the commitment to the build is the acceptance gesture — rendered above the bottom row, below the price recap, before the charge; the deferred-DNS path's later claim from the "Almost ready" surface and a resumed run claim under that same gesture and show nothing more; **(iv)** `handle_entry`, Continue on an `AlreadyOnNest` outcome — the first sign-in of a user an admin admitted directly ([`admin.md`](admin.md) § Section 3 — Admit), whose account the admin made and who never sees `invite_request`. That page cannot tell an admitted user's first sign-in from a returning user's sign-in on a new device, and the notice does not try: it renders whenever Continue signs in — a sign-in reaffirms, as the sign-in line of any app does — and never on the other outcomes, whose Continue leads to a page carrying its own notice. The rule is shared Rust, not seven comparisons: an additive `terms_notice_visible: bool` on `handle_check_snapshot()`, true iff `outcome` is `AlreadyOnNest`; the apps paint the notice from the field. The nest's rules, where the nest publishes any, render above the notice on (i) and (iv) — the two joining gestures ([`nest-rules.md`](nest-rules.md) § Acceptance at joining); on (ii) and (iii) there is no admin yet and nothing to show. No other path makes an account: the silent challenge at launch signs in an identity already on the nest, a third-party protocol client never registers, and the `cli` app's onboarding is a coaching prompt, never a tool ([`../architecture/apps/cli.md`](../architecture/apps/cli.md) § Sovereign acts). No checkbox (the fediverse's apps and the stores' own reading accept a by-continuing line), no record of acceptance in nest state (nothing to configure, nothing to migrate — the one-configuration-surface invariant's bucket 1 has nothing to hold), and no retroactive gate for accounts that predate the notice: nothing was released anywhere before the 0.1.x window closes, so no such account exists outside the fleet's own testers. **Recommended** as stated; the alternative — a checkbox with a persisted acceptance — buys nothing a store asks for and adds nest state; and for (iv) the alternative — a nest-stated first-sign-in fact on the handle check's verify reply, so that a returning sign-in shows no line — buys a wire field and a nest-side has-signed-in mark to spare a returning user one sentence they may as well read. (That fact now exists for a weightier act on the same Continue — the one-tap trust offer, [`onboarding.md`](onboarding.md) § 3b-ter's `AlreadyOnNest` ruling of 2026-10-19, `login.md`'s `first_sign_in` — and the notice still does not read it: its reason was never the cost, a sign-in reaffirms.)
4. **Surfaces.** (a) **The site page** `fauna.social/terms`, copy at `docs/sites/fauna-social/terms.md`, wrapped exactly as `/privacy` is (`sites/fauna-social/src/pages/privacy.astro`): no nav entry, in the sitemap, the footer unchanged — the privacy precedent of 2026-09-05, brought back for the Microsoft Store submission. (b) **The in-app link** `settings-terms-link` in the Settings About row beside `settings-app-version` on every app ([`../architecture/product-version.md`](../architecture/product-version.md) § Implementation status today, the About-row bullet, owns where each app paints that row): a button that opens the URL in the browser, or on a terminal shows and copies it — the `admin-mail-health-delist-link` precedent. (c) **The onboarding notice** `onboarding-terms-notice` (text) with `onboarding-terms-link` (button) on the four pages decision 3 names — `invite_request`, `claim_code`, `nest_provisioning`, and `handle_entry` on a sign-in Continue (amended 2026-10-19; two pages before). (d) **The URL is a shared-Rust constant**, `fauna_core::TERMS_URL = "https://fauna.social/terms"` — bucket 1, nobody chooses it — read by every app through the existing shared faces; the sentences are `en.yaml` strings rendered as `LocalizedText`; no app hard-codes either. tui leads, the six follow in batched trickle-down. **Recommended** as stated; the three IDs are proposed under rule A below.
5. **"Report to the developer" and "remove when requested".** The certification notes (§ Certification notes wording; the one owning text of the review-access notes points here) say that reporting is in-product on every post, message and profile and reaches the admin of the reporter's nest and the author's nest, who hold the removal tools; that the publisher runs no user's nest; and that on `fauna.build`, where the reviewer signs in, the admin is the publisher, so reports there reach the developer directly. A store's request to remove content **on `fauna.build`** is carried out by the organisation as that nest's admin through [`moderation.md`](moderation.md) § Legal takedown, with the store's request as the citation the console requires — a contractual compulsion is a compulsion, and no second mechanism is minted. A request about content **on any other nest** cannot be met by the organisation, which holds no custody; the answer names the nest's admin path (the report mechanism) and says so. **Recommended** as stated; the owner confirms the legal-takedown reading. **Ruled 2026-10-17 (the owner): either lever, by the method the store uses to ask** — a request to *remove* goes through the legal takedown (withheld from every viewer, retracted off-box where the protocol allows, the request as the citation); a request to *disable* goes through the block (withheld from everyone but the author, who keeps their own copy and presumably has the content elsewhere), filed on the admin's own report under the reason that fits, with the store's request recorded in the report's note as the reference — the request is the recorded reference either way. **Re-weighed 2026-10-16, after the owner ratified the admin block of a reported item** ([`moderation.md`](moderation.md) § Admin block of a reported item): a second path now exists on `fauna.build` — the organisation's admin files a report themselves (the block's own design lets an admin report an unreported item) and Blocks it. *(Superseded by the ruling of 2026-10-17 above — a request to remove goes to the takedown, a request to disable to the block; kept as the weighing's record.)* **The recommendation was: the takedown, with the store's request as the citation, for a request the store makes *as the store*** — Microsoft's *"when requested by Microsoft"*, Apple's *"we will ask you to remove it"*. The ground is the deciding difference: a takedown's ground is a compulsion external to the admin's judgment, which is exactly what a store's request is, while a Block's notice names the admin's own finding on a report under one of the eight reasons, which would misstate who found the violation; and a store's request says *remove*, which the takedown does for every viewer, the author included, retracting off-box where it can, while a Block leaves the author their view and retracts nothing. The author's queue label (*Removed under legal obligation*) is accurate under the contractual-compulsion reading above; on `fauna.build` the authors are reviewer accounts and the organisation's own testers. The complementary case decides itself and needs no word: a reviewer account that *reports* an item through the app lands the report on the organisation's queue, where it is handled as any report — Blocked if it violates the guidelines, dismissed if not — which is the report path a reviewer is testing. The owner's ruling above (2026-10-17) answered this with both paths, chosen by the verb the store uses.
6. **Counsel.** The page has legal weight, and the counsel round that would review it has waited since 2026-08-30. **Recommended:** ship the plain-language page now — the privacy page shipped the same way on 2026-09-05 — and add it to the counsel packet as a further item, so counsel's answer amends a live page rather than gating the Store submission. **Ruled 2026-10-20 (owner):** the page ships now; and review by an actual lawyer is postponed indefinitely and never blocks any other action or row in the project ([`../architecture/public-texts.md`](../architecture/public-texts.md) § Rulings, the counsel ruling).

## The stores' requirements against the admin's levers (mapped 2026-10-16; decision 5 ruled 2026-10-17)

**The question (the owner's, 2026-10-16, in the terms page's review).** Does what a nest admin can do satisfy the "report content" feature the three stores require — the owner's guess was *no*. The texts are the three quoted in § Why this doc exists, each read in full on 2026-10-16. Each store's words are read for three distinct things: **(a)** a report path with a human response; **(b)** action on the *user*; **(c)** removal or disablement of the reported *item*. The admin's levers, each owned elsewhere and only cited here: **suspension** and **eviction** ([`admin.md`](admin.md) § Cutting a user off — eviction and suspension); the **cited legal takedown** ([`moderation.md`](moderation.md) § Legal takedown); the **admin block of a reported item**, ratified by the owner on 2026-10-16, **built on the nest and painted on tui, linux, web and android, 2026-10-17; macOS, iOS and windows follow** ([`moderation.md`](moderation.md) § Admin block of a reported item, with its build rows); and the report mechanism itself ([`moderation.md`](moderation.md) § User-initiated reporting, built on all 7 apps). A report is evidence and removes nothing; the reporter's own hide and block are their personal filter.

**Microsoft Store Policies 7.20 § 11.12** (required — "you must"):

- *terms, code of conduct and/or content guidelines accessible in-product and on the website* — a document requirement, not a report one: **not met in-product**; the page is approved and wrapped, undeployed, and the in-app link and notice are unbuilt (§ Implementation status today) — this doc's decisions 1–4 are the answer.
- *a means for users to report … within the product to the developer for review* (and/or proactive detection) — **(a), met**: the report verb on every post, message and profile on all 7 apps, landing on the admin's queue with a doorbell, the reporter told *acted* or *dismissed*. "To the developer" holds on `fauna.build`, where the developer is the admin, and is read for every other nest as "to the party who can act", the nest's admin — which the certification notes state. The policy's alternative, proactive detection, is met too by the spam/phishing classifier and the user-side filters, but the report path is what a reviewer tests.
- *… and removal or disablement if found to violate applicable content guidelines* — **(c), required, met by design and in the built tree on the nest and the lead app since 2026-10-17 (painted on tui, linux, web and android; the windows and apple paints follow in the trickle-down)**: the admin block of a reported item is exactly this ("found to violate" after a report → the item withheld from the nest's audience, with notice, appeal and record); until it is built the only removal is the takedown, which needs a cited compulsion, so a guideline violation found on review could not be disabled. Reading "disablement" as suspension of the author (the row's candidate (iii)) is rejected on the text: the bullet speaks of the UGC, and a suspension leaves the item visible.
- *Remove or disable UGC when requested by Microsoft* — **(c) on an external request, met on `fauna.build`** (decision 5, ruled 2026-10-17: the takedown on a request to remove, the block on a request to disable, the request as the recorded reference either way — both built on the nest) and **structurally unmeetable on any other nest**, which the organisation neither runs nor holds custody of — the mail-app position the notes explain. Named as the one expectation risk: a reviewer may read "when requested" as reaching the product wherever it runs; the honest answer is the federated premise, and nothing can be built to change it.
- *Respect user safety, parental, and content privilege settings* — not a report requirement; [`family-safety.md`](family-safety.md) owns it. *Third-party mod/UGC platform* — not applicable.

**Google Play's User Generated Content policy** (required — "must implement robust, effective, and ongoing UGC moderation that"):

- *requires users accept the app's terms of use and/or user policy before users can create or upload UGC* and *defines objectionable content and behaviors … and prohibits them* — document requirements: **not met**; decisions 2 and 3 are the answer — the guidelines are on the approved page, the notice is unbuilt (§ Implementation status today).
- *an in-app system for reporting and blocking objectionable UGC and users* — **(a) and (b)'s user-side half, met**: the report verb; `fauna.knocks.block` with the block-also-hides corollary. The worked examples — public UGC apps must report users and content and block users; 1:1 features must block — are met by the same two mechanisms.
- *taking action against UGC or users where appropriate* — **(b), met** (suspension now, eviction after a warning); **(c), an expectation** ("where appropriate", a capability the moderation must have rather than a procedure a reviewer tests): **met by design and on the nest and the lead app since 2026-10-17** — the admin block, painted on tui, linux, web and android; the windows and apple paints follow in the trickle-down.
- *safeguards to prevent in-app monetization from encouraging objectionable user behavior* — not applicable: the apps carry no in-app monetization. The incidental-sexual-content conditions bind the content-label (`nsfw`) and family-safety planes, owned there.

**Apple's App Review Guideline 1.2** (required — "must include"; the removal paragraph a stated responsibility):

- *a method for filtering objectionable material* — **met**: [`moderation.md`](moderation.md) § Categories & enforcement item 1's shared render-verdict engine plus muted keywords.
- *a mechanism to report offensive content and timely responses to concerns* — **(a), met**: the report verb and queue; "timely" is the admin's practice, on `fauna.build` the organisation's own.
- *the ability to block abusive users* — **(b)'s user-side half, met**: `fauna.knocks.block`.
- *published contact information* — a listing task, owned by [`../architecture/installers/store-listing-contact.md`](../architecture/installers/store-listing-contact.md).
- *It is your responsibility to remove content that violates this guideline, your terms of service, or your community standards … If we find such content, we will ask you to remove it* — **(c), stated as the developer's responsibility; met by design and on the nest and the lead app since 2026-10-17** for a violation the developer finds (the admin block, painted on tui, linux, web and android; the windows and apple paints follow in the trickle-down); **met today** for Apple's own request on `fauna.build` (decision 5, the takedown with the request as citation); unmeetable by the organisation on any other nest, as for Microsoft. The 2026-10-14 reading that Apple asks for no removal is corrected in § Why this doc exists.

**What the mapping says, in one place.** (a) and (b) are met on every store by what is built. (c) is stated by all three — Microsoft as a requirement, Apple as the developer's responsibility, Play as an expectation — and the built tree meets it only under a cited compulsion, so the owner's guess was right for the tree as built; the admin block of a reported item, ratified the same day the question was asked, is what closes it, and this mapping cites that section rather than designing a lever a second time. The row's candidate (ii), a `fauna.build`-only posture, is not needed: every nest's admin holds the same four levers and `fauna.build` differs only in who the admin is, so one posture serves the reviewer's nest and every other. **What it binds on the submissions:** the first Microsoft Store submission and the first public App Store and Play releases waited for the admin block to be **built on the nest and the lead app** (and, since the same day, painted on linux, web and android) — the surface the organisation's admin acts from on `fauna.build` — which it now is; the trickle-down of the admin console gates each console's entry (ruled by the owner 2026-10-17 — § Certification notes wording, the italic note): the reviewer's app needs only the report path, which is built, but a note is entered only once the submitted app paints the block's notice the note states — android does since 2026-10-17, so the Play Console entry waits only on decision 4's terms link and notice; the Microsoft and App Store Connect entries wait on the windows and apple paints. The reason is the certification-notes rule above (§ Certification notes wording, the 2026-10-15 note): the notes, as the public-texts request rewrites them, state the block, and a note is entered into no console before the build carries what it states. Internal testing, and the first external TestFlight build, stay gated on the report mechanism alone — the 2026-10-07 sequencing ruling in [`moderation.md`](moderation.md) § User-initiated reporting, unchanged here. Every public text that states the admin's levers — the terms page, the certification notes, the tours — changes only through that request, never from this section.

**Decision 5 is ruled (the owner, 2026-10-17): either lever, by the store's own verb — remove → the takedown, disable → the block (above).**

## Certification notes wording (one paragraph, every console)

> Fauna is federated software: the app runs on the user's devices and connects to a nest — companion software on a server the user rents or a community runs — and the publisher hosts no server or nest for users, the way a mail app does not run the user's mail server. User-generated content is held on the user's devices and on their nest. The app has in-product reporting on every post, message and profile, and blocking of any account; a report reaches the admin of the reporter's nest and, when the author lives elsewhere, that nest's admin. An admin can suspend an account, remove an account from the nest after a warning period, and must remove content under a legal obligation, naming it, with a visible notice, a permanent record and, for a post, an appeal; an admin can also block a single reported item on their nest, naming the report's reason, with the same visible notice, permanent record and, for a post, appeal; no removal is silent. On the nest the reviewer account signs in to, fauna.build, the admin is the publisher, so reports there reach the developer directly. The terms and content guidelines are at https://fauna.social/terms, linked from the app's Settings and shown in the app at the step that joins or claims a nest. A request from the store to remove or disable content on fauna.build is carried out by the publisher as that nest's admin through the matching lever — removal, or a block that leaves the author their own copy — with the request as the recorded reference.

## Per-nest rules — the nest's published blocking rules (owned elsewhere)

**A nest's rules are blocking rules like a region's and a guardian's (the owner's direction 2026-10-16, ratified the same day):** a content-policy document the admin publishes in the app, carried on the nest's self-description, which the member's own device applies to what reaches them through that nest — visibly, with the rule named — rules only, never a picked item, never loosenable by a member; joining the nest is accepting them, so `invite_request` shows them above the by-continuing notice (decision 4's placement stands) and the eighth guideline, "your nest's rules", is that document. Owner: [`nest-rules.md`](nest-rules.md) — the document, its carriage, what it binds, the acceptance and change notices, and the one rule editor admin, guardian and authority share. The plain-language house rules this section once specified (`NestInfoReply.moderation.rules: Vec<String>`) are **retired, unbuilt**; the `ModerationInfo` slot carries the signed document instead. The separate manual block of a reported item is [`moderation.md`](moderation.md) § Admin block of a reported item's; a block is never a rule. `fauna.build` publishes its own rules through this and nothing else. The terms page states a nest's rules as they work, with no "planned" qualifier (the owner's amendment in public-texts round 1, 2026-10-19: "no rules is also rules"). That is true while no nest publishes any, so the page claims no surface the build lacks: it never says the app shows a nest's rules when you join.
