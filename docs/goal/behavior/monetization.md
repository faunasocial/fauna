## Goal

One creator, one mental model: **define tiers, decide who is entitled, and content unlocks for them** — regardless of *how* the entitled person reads it (a Fauna app or a web browser) or *how* they paid (manual grant, free follow, or an external payment provider).

The free, always-present default tier is the creator's **followers** (`restricted-posts.md` § Encryption at rest: "the always-present default tier is the user's followers"). Following *is* subscribing to the free tier; paid tiers are additional ranks above it. Follow and subscribe are therefore **one coherent relationship model**, surfaced together on the creator's identity surface (the profile) — not two separate features. This is why the subscribe affordance is a **profile** concern, not a **contacts** concern (see § Why profile, not contacts).

## The unifying model — tier as entitlement

A **tier** (`fauna.subscriptions.tiers.*`; `libs/fauna-core::subscription`) is the single entitlement primitive. It has a name, a `rank` (higher ranks subsume lower), an optional `price_hint` (a human string — fauna never parses or charges it), an optional `payment_url` (where the creator collects money, off-platform), and an `auto_approve` flag. **Rank `0` is reserved for the free `followers` tier** — the always-present default that "follow" subscribes to; author-created paid tiers are `rank >= 1` *above* it, so the auto-approve cascade (`rank <= R`) enrolls every paid subscriber as a follower too. `tiers.create` rejects `rank < 1`, keeping the rank-0 slot system-owned; the nest **auto-provisions** the `followers` tier on the first follow of an author it hosts — a follow naming an author with no account on this nest answers `tier_not_found` and writes nothing, so a caller cannot mint tiers or pending requests for arbitrary ids (`fauna_core::subscription::{FOLLOWERS_TIER, FOLLOWERS_TIER_RANK}`; nest `subscription_handlers::ensure_followers_tier`). **One relaxation, ruled 2026-09-06 (archive-import slice 3, refutable advisory):** `tiers.create` additionally accepts the reserved name `followers` at exactly rank 0 — `auto_approve` forced true, free, no designation — as the idempotent client-side twin of `ensure_followers_tier`, so the author's client can provision the tier *and its birth `KeyBlob`* before the first follower exists; the archive import needs it to gate friends-only history to followers on an account nobody follows yet (`archive-import.md` § Audience mapping), where until then the compose leg refused with "no period key for tier". A second create of the existing row answers `created: false` and stores the birth blob only when the tier has none. Any other name at `rank < 1` stays refused, and a nest that accepts the relaxation advertises `hidden-tiers` (below). **The name `room` is refused at any rank (2026-09-10):** it is the constant `GatedInfo.tier` every room-restricted post carries (`fauna_core::subscription::ROOM_POST_TIER`), whose readers are a room's floor members and never a subscription ([`restricted-posts.md`](restricted-posts.md) § Encryption at rest → *Room-restricted — the ruling*, ruling 3), so no author's tier may share it. **A rank at or above `OWNER_ONLY_TIER_RANK` is refused for every name but the reserved `only-me`:** `tiers.create` previously bounded rank only from below, so an ordinary tier could be minted at the reserved owner-only tier's own rank — see § Per-post pay-to-unlock's boundary rule (e) for why that matters and :21 below for what it enforces.

**A tier may be `hidden` (additive, minted 2026-09-06 for the archive import — the flag's owner is this section).** `TierCreateRequest.hidden` / `TierItem.hidden` (`#[serde(default)]`, so every pre-existing tier and every older client's create is offered as before) + the `subscription_tiers.hidden` column (`NOT NULL DEFAULT 0`, reconciler-added). A hidden tier is **never offered**: `offers.list`, the unauthenticated `GET /api/v1/subscriptions/tiers/{author_id}` read and the compose gate picker (`FeedManager::refresh_own_tiers`) all omit it; only the author's own `tiers.list` carries it, flag set. Create-time immutable like `unlocks_post` (no `hidden` field on `tiers.update`). The nest enforces exactly the flag's meaning and nothing about *which* tier is hidden. Its one use today is the **reserved owner-only tier** — `fauna_core::subscription::{OWNER_ONLY_TIER, OWNER_ONLY_TIER_RANK}` (`"only-me"`, rank `u32::MAX`) — which the archive-import machine mints once, on first need, for imported content whose original audience was custom, only-me or unknown (`archive-import.md` § What each category becomes → *Audience mapping*): at the top of the rank range, held there by two checks rather than arithmetic alone — `tiers.create` refuses any other name at or above `OWNER_ONLY_TIER_RANK` (above), and every rank-cascade door (the grant-time fan-out and its creation-time and boot-time reconcile twins) additionally excludes `hidden` tiers outright regardless of rank (§ Per-post pay-to-unlock rule (e)) — so its period key stays with the author, who already decrypts own gated posts with their own period key. The reserved name and rank are client conventions defined once in `fauna-core`, exactly as `FOLLOWERS_TIER` is. **Ruled 2026-09-06 (archive-import slice 3, refutable advisory): hidden means *not offered and not subscribable*.** `fauna.subscriptions.subscribe` to a hidden tier answers `tier_not_found` — fail-closed and indistinguishable from a tier that does not exist, so a stranger who guesses the reserved name learns nothing and can never land a pending request the author might approve by accident; and even a request that reaches the queue anyway — an older nest, or a future regression at an enqueue door — is refused at approve time too: `fauna.subscriptions.requests.approve` answers the same `tier_not_found` ahead of its plaintext/encrypted split, and the author's client independently refuses the same request directly (`SubscriptionsAuthor::approve_subscriber`) and skips it in the auto-approve drain; the owner-only mint additionally passes `auto_approve: false` (belt and braces: the birth `KeyBlob`'s roster is empty and the nest holds no key, so nothing could be granted anyway). A nest that enforces this — and accepts the reserved-name followers create above — advertises the `hidden-tiers` capability (`fauna_protocol::discovery::capability::HIDDEN_TIERS`, `version-compatibility.md` § I4's negotiation surface); an older nest stores the flag in `extra` and offers the tier, which is why the machine checks the token before minting and never creates the reserved tier against a nest that would offer it.

An actor's **entitlement** to a tier can be granted three ways:

1. **Author approval** — the creator (their client) approves a pending request. *Built (Pillar 1).*
2. **Auto-approve** — `auto_approve = true` grants with no creator *judgment*: the subscribe still enqueues like any other (the nest holds no period key, so only the creator's client can mint the KeyBlob), and the creator's client approves it on its next reconcile tick without asking — see Pillar 1.
3. **Verified payment signal** — a trusted message from the creator's configured payment provider ("actor X paid for tier Y until Z") drives the grant. *Built nest-side + shared Rust (Pillar 3); app UI legs pending — see § Implementation status today.*

Once entitled, the content unlocks over one or both **delivery rails**:

- **Rail A — E2E Fauna-app subscription (Pillar 1).** The content is sealed; the creator's client mints a `KeyBlob` wrapping the tier's period key to each entitled subscriber's pubkey; the subscriber's Fauna app decrypts locally. The **nest never mints, holds or wraps a period key** — every tier is client-minted (§ Pillar 1). This is the privacy-preserving rail for Fauna-native readers.
- **Rail B — nest-served web paywall (Pillar 2).** The content is a web-published post served as HTML from the nest (`web-content-hosting.md`). A browser visitor has no Fauna keypair, so the **nest enforces the paywall** server-side: the creator's client grants the nest's web-serve holder a standing scoped capability to read exactly the paywalled class, and the gate is a short-lived signed capability URL per visitor (§ Pillar 2). The creator opts that specific granted class out of nest-blindness in exchange for browser reach — nothing else on the box becomes readable.

The two rails share the *entitlement* layer (tiers + grants) but diverge sharply at the *enforcement* layer (client-side crypto vs server-side gate). Keeping the entitlement model unified while the rails differ is the central design constraint of this feature.

**One model, many mechanisms, two targets (ratified 2026-07-22, user-directed).** Paywalled content and **paid nest access** are both *payments between Fauna users*, and the entitlement model is **completely independent of the payment mechanism**. Every mechanism — a provider webhook (§ Pillar 3), a manually-minted claim code, a NIP-57 zap receipt (a companion adapter track) — reduces to the same normalized `PaymentEntitlement` naming a *(payee, tier)*; nothing downstream of that waist may special-case any mechanism. The **target axis lives on the tier, never on the entitlement**: whether an entitlement to a tier unlocks content keys (Pillars 1–2, Rails A/B), nest membership (§ Pillar 4, Rail C), or a single post (§ Per-post pay-to-unlock — a degenerate tier designated to one post) is a property of the *tier's designation*, so the waist stays mechanism-blind **and** target-blind, and a payee adding a payment mechanism automatically monetizes every surface they gate with it.

## Tips — per-post payments with no entitlement consequence (ratified 2026-07-22, user-approved)

*(Promoted from a paragraph of § The unifying model to its own section 2026-08-03, no content change: the registry routes the `tips` concept here and five references in this file cite "§ Tips", none of which resolved to a heading.)*

The Nostr norm for per-post payments is the *tip* (zap): an appreciation payment attached to one public post, unlocking nothing. Tips are first-class and **mechanism-independent** like everything else here, but they deliberately do **not** pass the entitlement waist — a tip names a *(payee, post)* and its consequence is attribution/display/notification, never a grant, so it carries no tier and no validity window. Zap receipts are the Nostr-native tip mechanism (**trust model ratified 2026-07-29 — § Zap receipts, below**; the adapter's tip↔purchase consequence split rides the zap-adapter track; zap-receipt parsing/storage specifics → [`../ui/nostr.md`](../ui/nostr.md) § The relay event store); other mechanisms may express tips later without any model change. Per-post *purchases* (pay-to-unlock a single post) are a different feature — ratified 2026-07-22 as degenerate single-post tiers through the unchanged entitlement waist (§ Per-post pay-to-unlock); a zap receipt targeting a post distinguishes the two by whether a tier genuinely *sells* that post and its asking price is met (sold + met → purchase; not sold, price not met, or no asking price set → tip; the sold-post binding is owned by § Per-post pay-to-unlock → *The designation is corroboration*, and the threshold's home, unit, and comparison rule by § The asking price).

## The asking price — the machine-comparable threshold (ratified 2026-08-02)

The "threshold met ⇒ purchase" sentences in § Tips and § Per-post pay-to-unlock need a number to
compare against; this section owns it. **A tier MAY carry an `asking_price` — an optional,
unit-tagged, machine-comparable amount:** `{ value: u64, unit: string }`, the first ratified unit
`"msat"`. It lives **on the tier**, beside `price_hint` (additive wire field on
`TierCreateRequest` / `TierUpdateRequest` / `TierItem` + an additive `subscription_tiers` column),
and it is a **user choice** ⇒ set from the tier form, app UI + nest state on all 7 apps (§ Product
invariants). The app cost is deliberately one optional input: the author types the price in
**sats** and one shared parse/format pair converts to the msat wire value (`value-formatting.md`
gains its section when the input ships) — display surfaces keep rendering `price_hint`, which
remains the human string and the zero-integration floor (§ Pillar 3 *Deliberate generality
bounds*); the two fields are independent, and fauna still parses, charges, and moves nothing.

- **Absent on an update means KEEP, never clear — unsetting needs its own verb.** `fauna.subscriptions.tiers.update` reads a missing `asking_price` (as it does a missing `description`/`price_hint`/`payment_url`) as *"leave the stored value alone"*, so a client that does not send the field cannot silently wipe a price. Clearing one back to unset is therefore a **separate kind**, `fauna.subscriptions.tiers.clear_field`, naming the field explicitly. Two consequences worth stating because they are easy to get backwards: an **older or reduced client** editing a tier it cannot fully author preserves what it did not send (this is what lets the asking-price input be *excised* on a store-safe build with no carry-through code — [`../architecture/dynamic-features.md`](../architecture/dynamic-features.md) § Platform-family surface excision), and a **UI that means to clear** must call the clear verb rather than submitting an empty field and expecting it to take.
- **The comparison rule (the waist's one new rule, mechanism-blind).** An amount-bearing verified
  payment event **without explicit tier intent** meets a tier's asking price **iff the event's
  amount unit equals the asking price's unit AND its value ≥ the asking price's value**. Unit
  mismatch — including a unit this build does not know — is *not met*, fail-closed. **Each event
  compares alone**: no cross-event accumulation ledger (rejected below). Over-payment is simply
  met; the surplus is appreciation, not credit.
- **Which mechanisms consult it: only intent-inferring, amount-bearing ones.** Today that is
  exactly the zap adapter's tip↔purchase split on a post whose tier carries `unlocks_post`
  (§ Zap receipts; § Per-post pay-to-unlock). **Explicit-intent mechanisms never consult it** — a
  provider webhook's `TierMapping` and a claim code already name the tier, and the
  provider/creator enforced the commercial terms before the event was ever minted.
- **Under-threshold — and a designated post with NO asking price — the receipt stays a tip.**
  This ratifies the shipped fail-closed behavior: a zap below the price (or on a tier that set no
  machine price) is recorded and attributed as an ordinary § Tips tip and grants nothing. A tier
  with only `price_hint` therefore *cannot* be bought by zap — inferred sale is opt-in via the
  asking price, and no free-text parsing ever infers a number. **Rejected:** *refusing* the
  under-threshold receipt (a zap is irrevocable — refusal returns no sats, it only destroys the
  attribution the sender is owed); a *partial-credit / accumulation ledger* (new secret-free nest
  state with refund semantics, gameable across arbitrary time windows, and the waist consumes
  single events by design).
- **The mechanism-independence tension: resolved by PRESERVING it.** `unit` is a **denomination**
  tag, never a mechanism tag: `"msat"` is a currency unit that a zap, an eCash note, or a
  Lightning-invoice webhook could all carry; nothing downstream matches on "zap", and the
  comparison rule above names no mechanism. A future fiat-denominated mechanism adds a unit arm
  additively (e.g. cents + an ISO currency). **Cross-unit comparison is undefined by design** —
  answering it would need an exchange-rate oracle, an off-box network dependency and a
  configuration surface this model refuses; a creator who wants both rails priced sets the units
  their mechanisms actually deliver. The rejected alternative — a bare `price_msats` field — is
  the Lightning special-case § *One model, many mechanisms, two targets* forbids, and it buys
  nothing the unit tag does not.
- **Editability.** `asking_price` is mutable via `tiers.update` (unlike `unlocks_post`): a price
  edit binds **future** events only; entitlements already granted persist unchanged, so no
  rug-pull semantics arise. Comparison in shared Rust, one site, consumed at every
  intent-inferring ingress — **built 2026-08-03 in the wasm32-clean `fauna-payments` crate**
  (`asking_price::AskingPrice::is_met_by`), as this section anticipated. A price can also be
  **unset** via the dedicated `fauna.subscriptions.tiers.clear_field` kind (`bins/fauna-nest/src/
  subscription_handlers.rs`) — `tiers.update` merges `None` as "keep current" for every optional
  field, so it has no way to express "wipe this back to unset", the same gap
  `description`/`price_hint`/`payment_url` share and this one kind clears for all four
  (`TierClearableField`, `fauna-protocol`; § Implementation status).

## Pillar 1 — Fauna-app subscriptions (built)

The end-to-end-encrypted paywall for Fauna-native readers. **Server, protocol, and shared FFI are complete; the ratified profile-centric UX is live on all 7 apps** — tui, last to arrive, built the subscriber offers/follow leg (M5 slice 2, 2026-07-15), closed the SELF author-management sections 2026-08-01 and the consumer `subscription-settings` page 2026-08-02, so all three UX surfaces below are now built everywhere (per-app state: § Implementation status today).

- **What it is.** A creator gates a post to a tier (`Post.gated = Some(GatedInfo { key_access: Broadcast { key_blob_ref }, tier, tier_rank, .. })`, `restricted-posts.md` § Encryption at rest). The body is sealed; the `KeyBlob` at `key_blob_ref` carries the period key individually X25519-wrapped to each current subscriber. Only an approved subscriber's client can decrypt.
- **Tier-key mint moves to the creator's client.** The nest never mints, holds or wraps a subscription period key, so KeyBlob minting/rotation happens on the creator's client (`mint_key_blob` in `libs/fauna-core::subscription::crypto`). Subscribe/unsubscribe **enqueue** intents; the creator's client picks them up, mints a roster-covering KeyBlob, and uploads it. Client-minted tiers are **broadcast-only** (no MLS group, no server-side period key). The nest-held period-key plane that once served pre-Phase-4 tiers was removed by the compat-remnant sweep on 2026-09-27 ([`../architecture/version-compatibility.md`](../architecture/version-compatibility.md) § Dimension 2, the fourth ratified exception).
- **Wire.** Subscription management rides the `fauna.subscriptions.*` WS-RPC kind family (registry: `register_subscription_handlers` — a counted list re-rots; the registration block is authoritative) (`libs/fauna-protocol/src/subscriptions.rs`; handlers `bins/fauna-nest/src/subscription_handlers.rs`). Three carry an `encrypted_upload: { key_blob, signer_auth }` envelope (`tiers.create`, `requests.approve`, `subscribers.remove`); two always enqueue intents for the author's client (`subscribe`, `unsubscribe` — whatever the tier's `auto_approve`). `requests.approve` and `subscribers.remove` refuse a call without the envelope (`fauna.subscriptions.missing_upload`). The nest verifies a four-step auth chain + three state checks before storing the blob (design ratified 2026-05-15; tracked internally). The mutating HTTP twins were **removed** in the WS-RPC-everywhere cutover; only public, **unauthenticated** reads (`GET /api/v1/subscriptions/tiers/{author_id}`, `nest/info`, `delegate/{author_id}`) stay HTTP — i.e. the per-author tiers HTTP read serves only genuinely-unauthenticated / external (web-paywall, federation) consumers; **every authenticated Fauna app reads over WS-RPC** (the ws-rpc-everywhere directive — see `api-layers.md`). `tiers.list` (added 2026-06-18) is the **authenticated own-read** of the caller's tier definitions — the SELF "My tiers" source; its reply additionally carries `auto_approve` so the edit form round-trips it. `offers.list` (added 2026-06-19) is the **authenticated another-author read** — the target author's public tier offerings (request-supplied `author_id`, reusing the `TiersListReply` shape), the WS-RPC successor to the public HTTP read for the in-app subscriber-browse on someone else's profile (`profile.md` § Layout & flow → *Another's profile*; powers `subscription-offers-section`). `mine.list` (added 2026-06-18) is the **caller-scoped consumer enumeration** — the caller's subscriptions across *all* creators (active subscriber rows + pending subscribe requests, deduped), each carrying the creator's nest-resolved handle (or `None` → the app renders the hex actor id); it powers the `subscription-settings` page (distinct from the per-creator `status.get`). **`tiers.create` REQUIRES the birth envelope.** `TierCreateRequest.encrypted_upload` is a required field: a create without it fails to decode, and the nest verifies the envelope — the same four-step auth chain, plus the tier-name match and the empty-roster check — **before** it inserts the tier row, so a refused envelope leaves no tier behind — except on the reserved `followers` slot (rank 0), whose row is the nest's own shape: the nest provisions it (`ensure_followers_tier`, the same call a first follow makes) *before* checking the envelope, so a refused birth blob there leaves the nest-provisioned keyless followers row that a first follow would have created anyway, never a client-shaped tier. The envelope-less create that alpha clients sent before the field existed (2026-07-13), once accepted under a 2026-08-02 wire-compat ruling, was removed by the compat-remnant sweep ([`../architecture/version-compatibility.md`](../architecture/version-compatibility.md) § Dimension 2, the fourth ratified exception), which supersedes that ruling. **Every upload door refuses a tier with no stored `KeyBlob`** (`fauna.subscriptions.key_blob_not_found`) — with ONE exception, `requests.approve` on the lazily provisioned `followers` tier: a first follow of an author who never created that tier provisions the row alone (`ensure_followers_tier` — the nest cannot mint), so the tier's first blob rides the first follow approval and is stored at version 1, minted under the followers period key the author's client generates on demand. Every other tier gets its birth blob at version 1 from `tiers.create`, so its first approve lands at version 2. The general no-prior-blob arm that served tiers stored without a birth blob was removed with the nest-held period-key plane ([`../architecture/version-compatibility.md`](../architecture/version-compatibility.md) § Dimension 2, the fourth ratified exception). A required envelope still cannot make a keyless "orphan" tier unrepresentable — the period key is client-custodied and invisible to the nest, and a birth blob's empty roster wraps no key, so a raw protocol client that mints a blob under a key it then discards still creates a tier no client can later mint for. That state stays recoverable, not a brick: `tiers.delete` and `requests.reject` take no key material (`nest/common.md` § Client-state recoverability is satisfied). Orphan *prevention* lives client-side, where the custody fact is visible: the shared orchestration persists the period key before it mints the birth blob, and the raw e2e seeding helper (`tests/e2e-unified/actions/api_actor.py::subscription_create_tier`, whose envelope comes from the same shared mint via `fauna_ffi.build_tier_birth_upload`) documents the orphan hazard in its docstring — that render-only seeding shape stays sanctioned. Pinned by `tiers_create_refuses_a_create_without_the_birth_envelope` and `tiers_create_refuses_a_foreign_envelope_before_inserting_the_tier`; `tiers_create_never_mints_a_period_key_or_mls_group` pins that even an enveloped create mints no nest key. **A third-party principal follows and unfollows through the sixth Fauna-family arm, `fauna:follow:write` (ruled 2026-10-02 — [`authorization-server.md`](authorization-server.md) § Scope grammar → *The planes ruled 2026-10-02*, which owns the arm):** principal twins of `subscribe`, `unsubscribe` and `status.get` for the `followers` tier only, the subscriber's ML-KEM key read from the per-account registry entry that arm's build adds, a missing entry refusing the follow.
- **Every tier is backed by the broadcast `KeyBlob`.** No writer creates an MLS-backed subscription tier any more: the nest-side MLS tier path went with the nest-held period-key plane, so the `KeyAccess::Mls` arm is authored only for room-restricted posts (`restricted-posts.md` § Encryption at rest; `key-material-hierarchy.md`); the `epoch_secret.get` / `archival_blob.get` read kinds and the `mls_in_encrypted_mode` refusal were retired with that plane on 2026-09-27 (`feed.md` room ruling 8, lifted), and the writer-less `tier_mls_groups` table left the schema with the plane's retire step (schema 91).
### Pillar 1 — UX (ratified 2026-06-14; IA approved, lead app = linux)

**IA: profile-centric.** Subscription is an *asymmetric* creator↔audience relationship, so it lives on the creator's identity surface (the **profile**), not on contacts (§ Why profile, not contacts). ui.yaml owns the IDs (`profile` page + `subscription-settings` page + 6 components); `profile.md` owns the profile-shell layout. Three surfaces:

1. **Author management — profile Tiers tab, own profile** (`is_self`):
   - **§1 My tiers** (`subscription-tiers-section`, component `subscription-tier-list`, form `subscription-tier-form`) — create/edit/delete tiers. Mirrors the admin quota-tier list shape (priority #3); distinct from `admin-settings-tier-*` quota tiers.
   - **§2 Pending requests** (`subscription-requests-section`, component `subscription-request-list`) — approve/reject pending **subscribe** requests, with a `subscription-request-kind` badge. **Flows.**
- **Subscribe:** `subscription-offer-subscribe-button` → `fauna.subscriptions.subscribe`; this always returns `Queued` for a caller not yet subscribed → `subscription-offer-status` flips to "pending" (an `auto_approve` tier included: its author's client grants it on the next tick). The per-tier `subscription-offer-status` badge is derived in shared Rust — `fauna_core::format::offer_status(tier_name, status_tier, pending) -> OfferStatus` (precedence Active>Pending>None) + `offer_status_label` (`subscriptions.offer_status_*`); `pending` is the transient post-`Queued` overlay (`status.get` carries no pending discriminant). Natives consume the `OfferStatus` enum (badge + Subscribe-button state); web the composite label. See `profile.md` § Where logic lives → Tiers tab (the canonical statement).
- **Approve / Remove — the transparent crypto:** `subscription-request-approve-button` (or `subscription-subscriber-remove-button`) → the client orchestrates *list post-mutation roster → `mint_key_blob` → `requests.approve` / `subscribers.remove` with the `encrypted_upload` envelope*; `subscription-request-busy` shows during the mint; nest errors (`roster_mismatch`, `stale_rotation`, `missing_upload`) surface to `error-message`.
- **Tier CRUD:** `subscription-tier-create-button` → `subscription-tier-form` → `fauna.subscriptions.tiers.create` (the nest never mints a period key or MLS group for a new tier — client-minted by design).
- **Unsubscribe:** `subscription-mine-unsubscribe-button` → `fauna.subscriptions.unsubscribe`; this returns `Queued` (the subscriber stays until the author's client commits the removal). **The commit is automatic — a leave is not the author's to refuse (ratified 2026-08-01):** the author pump's drain routes every `kind='unsubscribe'` row into the removal rotation (`remove_subscriber` + queue-row clear), unconditionally and before any same-pass approve-mint; the consumer row honestly persists until that commit (an app that optimistically drops it renders a leave as done while the KeyBlob still covers the leaver). Approve-minting an unsubscribe row is refused at both the shared seam (`AuthorError::WrongRequestKind`) and the wire (`fauna.subscriptions.wrong_request_kind`) — the approve upload's KeyBlob covers the roster *including* the leaver, so accepting it would silently cancel the leave (which is exactly what every app's §2 Approve button did until 2026-08-01).

**Where the logic lives — the load-bearing shared-Rust requirement (priority #2).** The approve/remove **mint+upload orchestration** (read the post-mutation roster → `mint_key_blob` → build `EncryptedKeyBlobUpload` → call the kind → retry on a nest `roster_mismatch`/`stale_rotation` if the roster changed under it; a `stale_rotation` re-reads the period key first — [`succession-aftermath.md`](succession-aftermath.md) § Re-key scope's period-keys row owns why) MUST live in **shared Rust** (a client subscriptions client/state machine), exposed via UniFFI + WASM, so all 7 apps dispatch a single high-level "approve subscriber" / "remove subscriber" call and render a snapshot — never re-implement the crypto dance per app. Today `FfiSubscriptionsClient.requests_approve` / `subscribers_remove` take a **pre-built** `encrypted_upload` envelope (the caller must mint), so the lead-app track's **first deliverable is adding that orchestration to the shared client crate** (the standing shared-Rust cleanup job — clean up the consumption surface). Tier CRUD / subscribe / unsubscribe / list reads are thin pass-throughs to the existing kinds. Snapshot shape (proposed): a `SubscriptionsSnapshot` carrying the author's tiers, pending requests (with `kind`), the selected tier's roster, an in-flight/busy flag for the mint, and `LocalizedText` errors; the subscriber views carry offered tiers + status, and the consumer view carries this user's subscriptions. Observer-driven rendering, no client-side caching (same rules as `feed.md` § Architectural rules). The thin per-kind wrappers exist in `libs/fauna-client-subscriptions` (`SubscriptionsClient<R>` — every kind, incl. the authenticated `tiers_list` + `mine_list` own-reads; `requests_approve`/`subscribers_remove` take a **pre-built** `encrypted_upload`), and the high-level orchestration that mints that envelope **landed 2026-06-15** as `SubscriptionsAuthor` in the same crate (`orchestration` module) — it bundled the thin client with a `ConfigClient` (period-key custody + the crash-staged removal sentinel + the identity keypair the mint signs with); since the rail retired 2026-10-02 it holds the `ActorKeypair` and the custody rests in the account-state plane kind `fauna.state.subscriptions` ([`../architecture/config-dissolution.md`](../architecture/config-dissolution.md) § The `__config` dissolution schedule → *The kinds*). The remaining per-app work is exposing it over UniFFI (Apple/Android/Windows) + WASM (web); linux consumes the Rust crate directly.

**The author pump's *cadence and tick body* are shared policy too (added 2026-08-01).** Each app owns only the *scheduler* — a tokio task, a Swift `Task`, a C# `Task.Delay` loop, a Kotlin coroutine, a JS `setTimeout` — because that is genuinely platform-specific. Everything else is `fauna-client-subscriptions`: **`SubscriptionsAuthor::reconcile_once()`** is the whole tick (resume crash-staged removals, *then* drain auto-approvals; each half best-effort, reported in a `ReconcilePass`, neither able to abort the other), and **`author_poll_interval()`** is the backstop cadence (the 30 s constant plus the `FAUNA_SUBS_POLL_SECS` e2e override; native-only env probe, so a browser gets the constant and supplies its own e2e cadence). Natives reach both over UniFFI as `subscriptions_reconcile_once` / `subscriptions_author_poll_secs`. **An app MUST NOT re-derive either.** Two properties are load-bearing and were being re-asserted in six independent hand-written loops until this landed: (1) **resume runs before drain** — a staged-but-uncommitted removal must be driven out before the drain mints over the roster, or the fresh `KeyBlob` re-covers the very subscriber being removed, leaving them read access for a full period; (2) **both halves run on every tick** — hoisting resume to once-per-connection looks equivalent and is not, because a removal staged *during* a session (a transient upload failure, or a peer device's staging arriving via config sync) then heals only at the next connect. Calling `reconcile_once` makes both mistakes unrepresentable; that is the point of the seam. The tick also carries a third half that runs **once per connection** rather than per tick — the stale-keyed-blob pass (`republish_stale_keyed_blobs`); what it heals and when it is owed is owned by `succession-aftermath.md` § Re-key scope (the period-keys row), and its latch rides the connection object the natives and the SPA already hold, so no app wires it.

**The headless-author corollary + the canonical e2e seeding shape (ratified 2026-08-01).** An inline `approved` on subscribe is reachable only for a caller who is already an active subscriber of the tier (the idempotent re-subscribe); for anyone else — on every tier, `auto_approve` or not — `subscribe` answers `Queued`, and the grant materializes only when the author's **client** next runs a reconcile tick. `auto_approve` removes creator *judgment* (no manual approve click), never creator *presence* (the KeyBlob mint needs the tier's period key, which only the author's client holds). So a headless author can never produce an `approved` subscription, and a test or fixture that needs "an author-granted subscription exists" seeds it as: **the author's client creates the tier** (a raw WS-RPC `tiers.create` whose birth envelope is minted under a throwaway key — the e2e seeding helper's shape — mints an **orphan tier**: its period key lands in no custody, so no client can ever mint a later KeyBlob for it; `approve_subscriber` deliberately auto-heals a missing key only for the reserved `followers` tier — § Pillar 1 Wire bullet) → subscribe (assert `Queued`) → the author's client grants — either its reconcile tick drains an `auto_approve`/`payment_entitled` request (the connect-time drain reliably beats a manual UI approve — the 2026-08-01 `test_sell_post_buyer_redeems_claim_and_unseals` finding), or the author drives the UI approve for a judgment tier — → deadline-poll the subscriber's `status.get` until active (a named generous budget, never a settle-sleep — `../architecture/e2e-latency-independent-assertions.md` § point 14) → proceed as the subscriber. **The mirror holds for leaving:** a queued unsubscribe is committed only by the author's client (the pump's unsubscribe-commit leg, § Flows → Unsubscribe), so its canonical e2e shape is unsubscribe (assert `Queued`, row persists) → author's client online → deadline-poll `status.get` until the tier is gone. **No instant-grant nest test-hook exists, deliberately:** such a hook would either fabricate a nest-minted period key — a state a post-Phase-4 nest cannot produce (the nest never mints one) — or mint an `approved` row with no KeyBlob behind it, which no production path produces either; both would run every downstream assertion against an unreachable state. Template: `tests/e2e-unified/tests/test_subscriptions.py` — `test_follow_auto_grants_in_encrypted_mode` proves the drain mechanism itself; `test_subscription_settings_lists_active_subscription_and_unsubscribes` consumes the shape as precondition seeding.

**Resolved dependency — client-minted period-key custody.** `mint_key_blob` takes the tier's **period key** as input, but the nest mints and holds none for a tier created since the storage-mode axis retired (`tiers.create` never mints one, by design), so the **author's client** generates it at tier-create and retains it as owner-private key material. **Custody home:** the account plane's `fauna.state.subscriptions` rows, fleet-only and generation-tip-sealed (plane-only since 2026-09-30) — per tier the `current` period key plus the full uncapped `prior` history (archival backfill needs every period). Generate-at-tier-create / retrieve-at-mint / rotate-on-removal live in `libs/fauna-client-subscriptions` (the standing shared-Rust home, mirroring `fauna-client-mail-settings`'s MSEK lifecycle). Full design + the storage-shape rationale: `key-material-hierarchy.md` § Audience: an opaque set of subscriber pubkeys → *Client-minted at-rest custody (author's client)*. This was the one piece of new *crypto-adjacent* client state the feature needs; everything else is glue over existing primitives. **Second client-state piece the mint needs:** a self-signed `DeviceAuthorization` granting `ManageSubscribers` (the single-device delegation the auth chain checks — 2026-05-15 spec § Auth chain step 3) — **built**, `fauna_core::subscription::crypto::build_manage_subscribers_self_delegation`, called from the `SubscriptionsAuthor` mint orchestration above (`libs/fauna-client-subscriptions/src/orchestration.rs`).

**Implementation slicing (lead = linux).** Slice A (author-first, the novel/hard part): the shared-Rust mint orchestration + the profile Tiers-tab SELF sections (tiers CRUD, pending-approve-with-mint, roster-remove-with-mint). Slice B: the subscriber side (offers + subscribe + status) and the `subscription-settings` consumer page. Then lift to the other 5 apps (the existing per-app `TODO-{client}-subscriptions-ws-rpc-1.md`, re-scoped from "migration" to "lift"). i18n strings (`S.subscriptions.*`, tier/request/status labels) are added to `en.yaml` by the lead at implementation.

## Pillar 2 — nest-served web paywall (design ratified 2026-07-12; not yet built)

Gating **web-published** content for browser visitors who reach the creator's site (`web-content-hosting.md`: apex / `<handle>.<domain>` subdomain / custom domain). A web visitor is a browser with no Fauna keypair, so Rail A's client-side decryption does not apply — the **nest enforces the paywall on the serving path**. The ratified shape (user greenlight 2026-07-12; resolutions recorded at § Open design questions Q1–Q3):

- **Content class (Q1).** A web-paywalled post is a member of the **web-paywall capability-granted readable class** (`../architecture/encryption-at-rest.md` § Readable classes, item 2): the creator's client mints the nest's web-serve holder a **standing scoped capability grant** via the shipped grant plane (`fauna.capabilities.{mint,renew,revoke,fetch}`; capability-mediated content-processing design, ratified 2026-07-04, tracked internally). What is paywalled on the web is the **intersection of two existing per-post opt-ins** — `web_published` AND gated-to-a-tier — so no new compose-time flag exists; publishing a gated post to the web *is* the act of choosing public-but-gated. That is strictly **more** private than today's ungated web posts, and no other content on the box becomes readable.
- **Holder identity.** The nest's web-serve component enrolls its **own service-user holder identity** (its own X25519 keypair via the existing bridge service-user enrollment path, content-processor role family); grants are sealed to that identity, never to the nest's storage layer — `key-material-hierarchy.md` rule #4 is preserved in substance (the unwrapped key exists only in the serve component's holder registry under the standard holder contract: fetch-refresh, revoke-bites-at-use, zeroize-on-revoke). Rationale: serving/routing/TLS already live in the nest (`web-content-hosting.md` rule 9), and co-resident process separation buys no operator-proofing — the security boundary is the off-box mint authority. The grant appears in the user's capability audit/revoke surface like any other holder (e.g. the mail MDA's).
- **Scope is crypto-enforced, per-tier and per-folder.** The grant's wrapped payload is exactly the paywall tier's `period_key` (the `content.read{post:tier}` scope class) and/or the specific `web`-mode folder's keys. The serve component structurally cannot read any other tier, folder, or content kind — web exposure is precisely the set of scopes the creator granted, and revoking a grant darkens exactly that slice.
- **Paywalled `web` folders (the folder half — design elaborated, this section's concrete mechanism; refutable by the security review).** Granularity is **per-set**: the creator paywalls a whole website-enabled folder to one subscription tier (finer slicing = a second web set) — a per-set choice persisted in nest state (`folders.web_paywall_tier`, set from the app, gated on the folder's website toggle since the folders re-model phase 4 — formerly web-type-gated — and refused on a `public`-audience folder, whose content is world-readable by ratified design; `folders.md` § Target re-model). A paywalled set is **content-key-sealed** — the M2 mechanism the WebDAV-served-set model already ratified (`mls-group-key-material.md` § M2: generations, genesis-at-flip or the one-time re-seal migration, serve pseudo-channel custody for unshared sets) — and the web-serve holder receives **the set's content keys via the grant plane** (the `content.read{folder:set}` scope class; wire + payload contract owned by `mls-group-key-material.md` § M2 → *distribution channels*). Serving: a sealed static file serves only through the same capability-URL token gate as post pages (the existing `(owner, path)` token scope covers file paths unchanged); no/invalid token → the **set-level** paywall teaser (tier + price + payment link, served **HTTP 402** — a set has no per-path pre-rendered teaser the way a post does); a sealed file with **no live grant fails closed to that teaser, and one with no paywall tier is not served at all — ciphertext is never served**. The tier on the set is the entitlement seam Pillar 3's engine consults (`is_subscriber(tier)` → the same nest-side mint). **v1 bounds:** render-input files (`.html.hbs`, `_site.json`) inside a paywalled set are not rendered — templates live in a public web set (the paywalled set carries static assets/downloads); tokens are per-resource, so full-subtree browsing waits for the named cookie-session increment below.
- **At rest.** Paywalled rendered output rests **sealed** (`web_rendered`); the holder unseals **on demand at serve time** and serves pre-rendered static bytes (the sanctioned serve-time-unseal pattern). Public ungated web content is unchanged — plaintext-by-design readable class.
- **Gate (Q2).** After a visitor proves entitlement (paid via Pillar 3, or redeemed a claim code), the nest mints a **short-lived signed capability URL** scoped to (grant, resource); on fetch it verifies the signature **statelessly** and serves the pre-rendered bytes. The check is nest-owned code in the same category as the serving layer's host-resolution/reserved-path checks — `web-content-hosting.md` rule 1 (no per-request *user-code* execution) is preserved.
- **Visitor identity (Q3).** The capability token is a **bearer credential presented explicitly per request — never a cookie**, so it is non-ambient: a hostile sibling subdomain cannot ride it, and `web-content-hosting.md` invariant 2 holds on apex, subdomains, and custom domains alike. Token-leak risk (referrer/history/sharing) is bounded by short TTL + (grant, resource) scope + free re-mint; a leaked token reads one rendered post, never account control.
- **No token → teaser.** The gated URL without a valid token serves a **pre-rendered public teaser/paywall page** (title/preview + tier price + the payment link / claim-code entry) — itself static output of the normal render pipeline.
- **Creator comp-link surface (app UX ratified 2026-08-02; IDs user-approved same day; shared layer built 2026-08-02, app UI owed — status owned by `web-content-hosting.md` § Published-post management).** The creator-facing face of `fauna.web.paywall.mint_token`: a *Copy paywall link* affordance on the published-post management surfaces (the feed ⋯ overflow + the `web-settings` published-posts section — structure owned by `web-content-hosting.md` § Published-post management). The minted link is a **preview/instant-share link**: it carries the standard short-TTL token (~10 min, freely re-mintable), and the UI copy states that validity. **Durable comping stays the § Pillar 3 claim code** — a creator who wants to grant lasting free access mints a claim code, never a long-lived token; no token-TTL knob is added (the short TTL is the ratified leak-risk bound above, and lengthening it for comps would re-open exactly that). The sibling *Copy web link* (the tokenless public URL, teaser + payment link) is the selling link.
- **Named later increment (deferred, not in the first cut):** full-site cookie-session gating for whole-site browsing, **custom domains only** (a separate registrable domain is origin- and site-isolated — `web-content-hosting.md`'s own gold standard). Revocation carries the capability plane's standard honest-box bound: no content-sealing epoch exists yet, so revoke reliably stops future serving but is not retroactively box-proof (same bound as mail).

## Pillar 3 — pluggable third-party payment-provider API (design ratified 2026-07-12; not yet built)

A native integration seam so a creator can connect their own payment provider and have "this person paid for this tier" flow into a Fauna **entitlement** — with **no Fauna-operated payment rail and no provider lock-in**. The creator stays in charge of the commercial relationship; Fauna provides the plug. The ratified shape (user greenlight 2026-07-12; Q4 resolution recorded at § Open design questions):

- **Adapter seam.** A `PaymentProvider` adapter trait in shared Rust (`libs/fauna-*`, per-provider modules) with three capabilities: **verify** an inbound provider event's authenticity (webhook signature per the provider's scheme), **map** the payment to a tier, and **parse** a validity window. All provider-specific knowledge is quarantined inside the adapter; nothing downstream knows Stripe from BTCPay.
- **Normalized event — the narrow waist.** Every verified payment becomes `PaymentEntitlement { provider, buyer, tier, valid_until, external_ref }`, where `buyer` is a Fauna `actor_id` (bound at checkout) **or** an unbound claim (redeemable later). One provider-agnostic entitlement engine — Pillar 1's tier machinery — consumes it and drives both rails: Rail A (the grant enqueues; the creator's client mints the KeyBlob on next sync, the same queue subscribe uses) and/or Rail B (the web gate mints capability URLs against it). A creator adding a provider automatically monetizes both surfaces.
- **Buyer↔actor binding (Q4).** Primary: **signed-in checkout carrying `client_reference_id = actor_id`** (Stripe checkout reference / Ko-fi custom field / provider metadata) — deterministic, no manual step. Universal fallback: a **post-payment claim code**, redeemed either in the buyer's Fauna app (binds their actor → full entitlement on both rails) or directly on the Pillar-2 paywall page (an anonymous browser-only buyer → Rail B only, no actor to bind). Claim codes also cover no-API providers (bank transfer, cash) — the creator mints them manually. Provider-email ↔ handle mapping is rejected as a primary binding (unverified, ambiguous, privacy-leaky).
- **Configuration.** The creator configures a provider from their app: provider kind + webhook-verification secret + tier mapping — app-set nest config per the product invariant, never CLI/env. The nest holds **only** the webhook-verification secret, a credential that can verify notifications but can neither move money nor read payout accounts; payout credentials stay with the provider and the creator.
- **Provider status — evidence-based, no ping (design ratified 2026-07-16; refutable advisory).** The §4 provider row's status contract (`configured | verified | error` — ui.yaml `subscription-provider-status`, values user-approved 2026-07-12) is derived from **webhook evidence**, never an active probe: a webhook-verification secret is only provable by a real signed delivery (no mainstream provider exposes a "validate my webhook secret" call), so no ping kind exists — by design, not omission. The nest stamps two nullable fields on the provider row at ingress: `last_verified_at` (a delivery whose signature verified against this row) and `last_rejected_at` (a signature failure against this row — the HTTP response stays non-2xx; an unknown provider never creates a row). `providers.list` reply rows carry both (additive wire evolution, plain optional fields). Status is one shared decision in Rust — both null → `configured` (no evidence yet); `last_rejected_at >= last_verified_at` → `error` (most-recent evidence wins; ties resolve conservative); else `verified` — exposed as a label face over UniFFI + wasm exactly like `claim_status_label`, so no app re-derives the branch. **Honest bound (closing the security-review finding):** the webhook endpoint is public and its two path parts are discoverable (the creator's actor id + a provider kind from the small known set), so `error` asserts exactly *"at least one delivery-shaped POST failed signature verification against this row"* — a state any unauthenticated third party can induce, and hold with periodic POSTs. A red badge means "worth re-checking the secret and the provider dashboard — unless real deliveries are still verifying," never proof of creator misconfiguration; the opposite direction stays trustworthy, since `verified` is provable only with the row's secret, so a false *healthy* cannot be induced. Corroboration heuristics were considered and **rejected**: an N-rejections bar is attacker-clearable (N POSTs cost the same as one), a recent-`verified` grace horizon re-opens a false-healthy window on a genuinely rotated secret (the exact direction this design closes) and only protects rows with frequent deliveries, and filtering `MissingSignature` out of the rejected stamp would hide the real misconfig class where the wrong provider kind is registered (its header names don't match, so no signature is found). App copy for the `error` state (tooltip/guide wording, when the per-app render flip lands) follows this framing. Self-healing like `valid_until`: a provider-side secret rotation surfaces as `error` on the next delivery and clears once a delivery verifies after the creator re-enters the secret.
- **Webhook ingress.** Payment providers cannot speak WS-RPC; a webhook is an external unauthenticated-caller surface — the same class as the surviving public HTTP reads (§ Pillar 1 Wire; `../architecture/api-layers.md`) and web visitors. It lands on the nest's public HTTP surface under a reserved `/api/v1/…` path (never shadowable by user web content — `web-content-hosting.md` invariant 3). ⚠ The handler must return **non-2xx on unknown provider or bad signature**: the serving layer's catch-all fallback answers unmatched paths with a `200` HTML info page, which a provider's delivery system would record as success.
- **Expiry & revocation — self-healing via `valid_until`.** A renewal webhook extends the window; a refund/dispute event shortens or voids it; a lapsed entitlement simply stops being renewed. No revocation list.
- **Deliberate generality bounds.** The entitlement quantum is **tier membership** (time-boxed or perpetual): metered/usage-based pricing has no home, and every provider reduces to "machine-verifiable event carrying a reference" or "human-mediated claim code". **Per-post pay-to-unlock is a declared goal (user-directed 2026-07-22) and its design round is RESOLVED (Q9, same day): a single post is sold as a degenerate auto-minted single-post tier** — see § Per-post pay-to-unlock; the quantum itself is unchanged, which is the point of that resolution. Per-post *tips* bypass the entitlement model entirely (§ Tips, above). `price_hint` / `payment_url` on the tier remain the zero-integration floor that works today (hand-linked checkout + manual approval); Pillar 3 automates the grant.

Sibling of the §1–§3 tier/request/roster sections (ui.yaml owns the IDs).

## Pillar 4 — paid nest access: membership tiers (design ratified 2026-07-22; build steps (1)–(4) of 5 landed — the waist + designation 2026-07-22, the admission arms + lapse reconcile 2026-07-23; step (5), the app storefront, not yet built, and a narrower nest-side gap still blocks the OOB-code registration-time admission leg)

The nest-access leg of the unified model (§ *One model, many mechanisms, two targets*): an admin charges the users of their nest — membership tiers — using exactly the machinery Pillars 1–3 built. Nothing here mints a second entitlement system; Pillar 4 is a *designation* plus three enforcement seams.

- **The unified waist, made real (BUILT 2026-07-22).** `PaymentEntitlement { provider, payee, buyer, tier, valid_until_secs, external_ref }` (`libs/fauna-payments/src/lib.rs`) is the single normalized value every payment mechanism produces, and the **one value the nest engine consumes**: `payment_core::apply_payment` / `apply_refund` take it and nothing else, so a new mechanism plugs in as an adapter without touching the engine. A mechanism's whole job is to produce one — webhook ingress via `PaymentEntitlement::from_verified_event` (which also owns the buyer-binding normalization: an absent or unparseable `buyer_reference` falls back to `Buyer::Unbound`, never a dropped payment), claim redemption from the stored claim row. The entitlement names a *(payee actor, tier name)* — the payee is carried **in** the value, since each mechanism resolves it differently (webhook: the URL path segment; redemption: the claim's author) and the engine must never infer it; the tier's designation decides what it unlocks. Claim minting for an unbound payment is **engine** behavior, not any one mechanism's: it lives behind the waist, so every mechanism inherits the same idempotency (per `(payee, provider, external_ref)`) and the same audit trail.

- **A membership tier is an ordinary subscription tier plus a nest-state designation.** The admin is a user with an extra role, and as a payee they use the creator machinery verbatim: their own `subscription_tiers` rows (name, rank, `price_hint`, `payment_url`), their own per-payee provider config (`payment_providers` rows are already keyed by payee actor — the § Pillar 3 configuration model generalizes from "per-creator" to "per-payee" with **no schema change**), their own claim codes, their own §4/§5 profile-Tiers-tab management UX. What makes a tier a *membership* tier is an **Admin-class designation persisted in nest state** (set from the app, § Product invariants — never a config file): a `membership_tiers` link `(admin actor, subscription tier) → { admin_tier, lapse_tier }`, where `admin_tier` is the quota tier admitted members are assigned (`tiers` table — the tier *is* the quota, [`admin.md`](admin.md) § 2 Users) and `lapse_tier` is the quota tier a lapsed member degrades to (default `free`). The two pre-existing tier systems — the entitlement object (`subscription_tiers`) and the resource policy (`tiers`) — stay distinct concepts joined by this explicit link, never merged. Multiple membership tiers compose naturally (bronze/silver/gold, each linked to its own quota tier). Because a membership tier **is** a subscription tier, the admin can also gate posts to it (Rail A) — a nest membership that carries members-only content needs no extra design.

- **Rail C — what a membership entitlement gates.** Three lifecycle seams, each riding an existing mechanism:
  1. **Admission.** A **paid claim code doubles as an invite code**: the onboarding `invite_request` page's out-of-band code row ([`onboarding.md`](onboarding.md) § 3) accepts a payment claim code minted for a membership tier; redemption admits the actor at the linked `admin_tier`, records the membership subscription (a `subscribers` row under the admin's tier, carrying `valid_until`), and marks the claim redeemed — mechanically, `register_core`'s invite-code branch consults the payment-claim store when the invite-code lookup misses (the two code families already share one mint alphabet). Paid admission is self-service, so it **rides the registration mode exactly like invite codes** ([`../architecture/nest/public-mode.md`](../architecture/nest/public-mode.md) § Registration Modes): honored under `open` and `invite_required`, refused under `closed` — `closed` means *no self-service admission at all*; the always-available admin path in every mode is approving a pending request. A **pending invite request backed by a verified payment** auto-approves without admin judgment — the `payment_entitled` marker pattern `drain_auto_approvals` already implements for content tiers, applied at the `fauna.admin.invite_requests.approve` seam. Signed-in checkout (`client_reference_id = actor_id`, § Pillar 3 Q4) covers the already-has-an-account cases: an existing actor buying membership, and renewals. Paid/coded admissions are not free-tier accounts, so they bypass `max_free_users` the same way invite-code admissions at a paid tier do today.
  2. **Quota assignment + renewal.** Admission at membership tier M assigns `users.tier = link(M).admin_tier`. A renewal payment extends the membership row's `valid_until` (the existing `set_subscriber_valid_until` path) and, if the member had lapsed, restores `users.tier` to the linked `admin_tier`.
  3. **Lapse = quota downgrade, nothing else.** When `valid_until` passes, the member degrades to `lapse_tier` — automatically, reversibly, with **no new account state and no new dispatch gate**: quota enforcement already refuses over-cap *new* writes while every read, every existing byte, and export stay fully available, which is exactly the required degrade shape (write-limited, never data-destroying). Lapse **never** suspends (suspension denies reads and is deliberately axis-free — [`admin.md`](admin.md) § Cutting a user off — far too total for a lapsed payment, and reusing it would violate *user always controls their data*), **never** auto-evicts, **never** deletes; eviction remains an explicit admin action on the existing ladder. Enforcement is a **reconcile** of expired membership `valid_until` → `users.tier` (at webhook ingress for that buyer, at boot, and on a periodic sweep whose cadence is a hard-coded constant — bucket 1 of the one-configuration-surface invariant), scoped so it only downgrades a member whose current tier still equals the `admin_tier` **their admission ran under** (an admin's manual tier change is never clobbered). **Lapse policy freezes at admission, not at sweep time (ratified 2026-07-29).** Each membership `subscribers` row carries the `{admin_tier, lapse_tier}` pair frozen onto it when that membership was admitted or last renewed, and the reconcile reads *only* that stamp — it never re-reads the live `membership_tiers` link. This is what makes the admin's own later edit safe: re-pointing a designation, or clearing it outright, cannot strand members admitted under the old pair above `lapse_tier` forever (deriving from the live link did exactly that — a silent, unbounded quota leak with no surface that showed it). A **renewal re-stamps**, so the designation governs what a member buys *now*, and only an already-bought window is held to the terms it was bought under. The frozen stamp is also what *identifies* a membership row: an ordinary content subscription carries none and is invisible to the sweep. Being a frozen historical value, the stamp deliberately carries **no foreign key** to `tiers` (one would let a stale stamp block an admin from ever deleting a quota tier); `users.tier` does have one, so a stamp naming a since-deleted quota tier resolves to `free` at sweep time rather than failing the write — the member still lapses, which is the invariant, and one bad stamp can never abort the sweep for everyone else. Over-`lapse_tier`-cap data persists untouched — the no-user-data-loss invariant holds by construction.

- **Prospective-member storefront.** The admin manages membership tiers where every payee manages tiers — their own profile Tiers tab (§ Pillar 1 UX; §4 providers, §5 claim codes) — plus the designation control on the admin surface (one new section; surface owner [`admin.md`](admin.md) § 2 Users, IDs proposed at implementation per UI rule A). A prospective member with no account yet sees the offering on the onboarding `invite_request` page via the public per-author tiers read (`GET /api/v1/subscriptions/tiers/{author_id}` — the sanctioned genuinely-unauthenticated consumer class, § Pillar 1 Wire).

- **Out-of-the-box + compat.** No membership designation ⇒ nothing changes: registration modes, invite codes, and the free cap behave exactly as today. The designation, the claim-code admission arm, the `payment_entitled` approval marker for requests, and the reconcile are all additive nest state / additive wire evolution (`version-compatibility.md` I4). Deliberate bounds are unchanged from Pillar 3: the entitlement quantum stays **time-boxed tier membership**; per-item and metered pricing stay out; every mechanism reduces to "machine-verifiable event carrying a reference" or "human-mediated claim code".

## Per-post pay-to-unlock — degenerate single-post tiers (design ratified 2026-07-22; nest + shared-Rust client orchestration built 2026-07-29, lead-app UI built 2026-07-29, rank delivery built 2026-07-29, gap (2b) lifted on all 7 apps as of 2026-08-03 — windows was last; gap (2c) lifted on all 7 apps as of 2026-08-27 — android was last)

Selling access to a **single post** reuses the entitlement machinery whole: **a post-unlock tier is an ordinary subscription tier plus a creator-set designation naming the post it unlocks** — the third instance of the ratified "ordinary tier + designation" pattern (§ Pillar 4 membership tiers are the second; the target axis stays on the tier, § *One model, many mechanisms, two targets*). The resolution of the design round § Deliberate generality bounds declared open (user-directed 2026-07-22); recorded as Q9 below. Refutable by the security review or at build time.

- **The shape.** "Sell this post" on the author's app — **at compose time, and only there** (narrowed from "compose or post detail" 2026-07-29, at the lead-app build: an already-published public post's plaintext body has already travelled and rests unsealed, so there is nothing left to gate; selling is a property of how a post is *published*, not an action on a published one. A sold post is therefore authored as a gated post from the start, which is exactly what `prepare_sell_post` reads the composer for) — auto-mints an ordinary client-minted subscription tier carrying an additive, **create-time-immutable** designation `unlocks_post` (the post's id; additive wire field on `TierCreateRequest`/`TierItem` + additive `subscription_tiers` column, I4), then gates the post to that tier — ordinary `GatedInfo { key_access: Broadcast, tier, tier_rank, seal_id }` under the tier's fresh period key, birth `KeyBlob` from `tiers.create` as for any client-minted tier. A purchase is an ordinary entitlement to that tier through the **unchanged waist**: `PaymentEntitlement` still names *(payee, tier)*; the engine, claims, providers, expiry, and both rails are untouched. The buyer's unlock follows Pillar 3's paid-grant path verbatim (a `payment_entitled` request `drain_auto_approvals` mints on the author's client next online — the accepted mint-latency bound) and renders through the existing gated-post detail-open unlock (`restricted-posts.md` § Encryption at rest).
- **Every mechanism expresses "buy this post" with no new arm.** A claim code minted for the unlock tier (`claims.mint` today), a provider webhook whose `TierMapping` names it, and a zap receipt targeting the post (the zap adapter resolves the target post's `unlocks_post` tier and compares the receipt's msat amount against that tier's **asking price** — § The asking price owns the field, unit, and comparison rule; a receipt on a post with **no** unlock designation, or below the asking price, or on a tier with no asking price set, stays a tip, § Tips) all reduce to the same *(payee, tier)*.
- **Rank is the "do subscribers get it free?" knob — no new mechanism.** The at-or-below-rank cascade (a rank-R subscriber is enrolled in every tier ≤ R, delivered by the grant-time fan-out enqueue below — `CacheDb::enqueue_unlock_fanout`) already composes: an unlock tier minted at **rank 1** is included in every paid subscription (subscribers see it free; buyers of it also become followers, the ratified rank-0 cascade); one minted **above the author's highest regular tier** is pure pay-per-view. The app offers the choice as a simple toggle and computes the rank; the model needs nothing new. (Cost, stated honestly: each new subscriber's approve-mint fans out wraps for every included unlock tier — bounded, author-client-side, the same queue.) **Delivery ruled 2026-07-29 (after the cascade was proven unreachable for client-minted tiers): the fan-out is enqueued at grant time** — wherever a rank-R entitlement lands (request approve, or the payment-grant path), the nest additionally enqueues the author-client mint for every tier the cascade includes at rank R **that the author's client can mint**, and the author's client drains them through the existing `payment_entitled` queue. The nest holds no period key and cannot mint a KeyBlob, so the enqueue is the only rank delivery mechanism, not an optimization (the nest-side cascade that once minted directly for legacy nest-held tiers was removed with that plane on 2026-09-27). **Five boundary rules the enqueue carries:**

  - **(a) the only blocked edge is designated→designated.** An undesignated grant fans out to every tier at rank ≤ R (that is the cascade: "a rank-R subscriber is enrolled in every tier ≤ R"); a *designated* grant fans out only to **undesignated** tiers, so a buyer of a sold post becomes a follower per the rank-0 sentence above, while one cheap post still never unlocks every sold post. **Corrected 2026-07-30:** the original rule fired only for undesignated grants — one axis too wide — which made both the `:19` cascade sentence and this section's rank-0 sentence false at code level for the *default* author type, since a designated grant returned early and delivered nothing at all.
  - **(b) the fanned-in enrolment is perpetual** (`valid_until = NULL`), mirroring the *Perpetual by default* degenerate-tier rule below (and the retired nest-side cascade, which stamped the paid window on the requested tier only) — the subscription that covered the post covered it like a purchase. The granted tier itself is therefore **excluded** from its own fan-out pass: the caller has already written its request carrying the real paid window, and re-selecting it would blank that window and turn a time-boxed purchase perpetual.
  - **(c) a designated tier also enqueues for the author's existing unexpired eligible subscribers at its own creation** (the sale-time half of "every paid subscription", plus an idempotent boot-time reconcile as the standing self-heal, whose predicates mirror the grant-time twin's).
  - **(d) suppression is keyed on READABILITY, not on the `subscribers` row** (added 2026-07-30 as that correction's ruled residual). A `subscribers` row is the **social edge** — following is an immediate follow, and the row must land at once — while a delivered KeyBlob wrap is what makes the tier *readable*; the two are different facts. The enqueue exists to deliver the wrap, so it stands down only when **both** hold: an unexpired entitlement *and* an entry for that subscriber in the tier's current blob. Keying on the row alone let the (since-retired) legacy nest-side cascade defeat the fix that covers it — that cascade enrolled into a keyless undesignated tier and then silently skipped the blob it held no key to mint, so its own row suppressed the enqueue and the subscriber stayed **enrolled-but-unreadable** permanently. Readability is asked **per subscriber** (a tier having *a* blob says nothing about who is in it) and answered by decoding that blob, because it is not SQL-expressible: the wrap set lives inside `current_key_blobs.blob_data`. This is the structural half of the rule — it heals every provenance of a wrap-less row, **including rows already written by deployed nests**, which is what no source-side fix could reach. "Cannot prove readability" (no blob, undecodable blob) counts as unreadable and enqueues: a redundant request costs one drain, the opposite default re-strands the reader.
  - **(e) a `hidden` tier is never a cascade target, at any door**. Ruling 4 (§ The unifying model, above) is "not offered and not subscribable" — `subscribe` enforces the front door, but the grant-time enqueue here and its creation-time and boot-time reconcile twins (plus, until 2026-09-27, the retired legacy nest-held cascade) are back doors `subscribe` cannot intercept, and none of them read `hidden` before this fix. Unlike rules (a)–(d), this is a property of the target tier itself, not a consequence of rank, designation, or key custody — a hidden tier can sit at any rank, so it cannot be derived from the other four and each door checks it directly. The one hidden tier in use today, the reserved owner-only tier, sits at the very top of the rank range (`:21`, above) purely as belt-and-braces: rule (e) alone already makes it unreachable at every rank. `tiers.create` additionally refuses `hidden` combined with `unlocks_post`, closing the one path a designated tier could otherwise reach this same fan-out hidden.

  Build status + the fail-closed rank-derivation rider: § Implementation status today.
- **Degenerate-tier rules the designation carries** (display/lifecycle rules, not new machinery):
  - **Hidden from generic tier surfaces.** A designated tier never appears in `offers.list` consumers, the §1 My-tiers management list, the compose gate picker (`own_tiers`), or as the reported `status.get` subscription tier (the status pick skips designated tiers — a machine-named single-post tier is a purchase, never the subscription relationship that read reports, and without the skip the rank fan-out would make every paid subscriber's status collapse to a `post-unlock-*` name) — the unlock affordance renders **on the post** (teaser card/detail + the Pillar-2 web teaser page), and the author audits sales where claim codes already live (§5). Same special-casing family as the reserved rank-0 `followers` tier.
  - **The buyer's price read is post-addressed (ruled 2026-07-29 — closes the gap-(2c) question).** A prospective buyer's client resolves the unlock tier's **public purchase fields** (tier name, `price_hint`, `payment_url`) through a USER-class point read keyed by `(author_id, post_id)` — `fauna.subscriptions.post_unlock.get` — answering **iff** one of the author's tiers genuinely *sells* the post (both directions of the binding below, so a price is never quoted for a post the quoting tier does not gate). Post-addressed, never name-addressed: `post_id = blake3(body)` is unguessable without having seen the post, so possession of the id is evidence of legitimate teaser access and the offer-surface filters' anti-enumeration property survives — whereas a by-name read would re-open probing the author's *regular* tier namespace the §1 filters exist to hide. Authenticated only (ordinary USER-class dispatch limits; no bespoke throttle — an indexed point read): the anonymous case needs **no read at all**, because the Pillar-2 web teaser is nest-rendered server-side with the price embedded (§ Implementation status, Pillar 2 (b)), and the anonymous *purchase* leg rides Pillar 2/3's visitor entitlement legs (gap (4)) unchanged. An older nest answers `unknown_kind`; the client renders the teaser without a price and claim-code redemption remains the fallback purchase path — additive-everywhere.
  - **The author is not a prospective buyer of their own post (ruled 2026-08-25, at build time).** Both halves of the affordance — the post-addressed price read above and the buy mutation it paints — are scoped to a *prospective buyer's* client and **refuse when the local actor IS the post's author** (`FeedManager::{resolve_post_unlock_offer, buy_unlock_offer}`, one shared `is_local_actor` predicate; every app renders no affordance for the unresolved offer, so no per-app work follows). The author's access to their own sold body is **custody**, never a purchase — the `is_author` branch of `FeedManager::unlock_gated_post` reads the period key straight from their own tier custody — so an author-side purchase is not merely redundant: it is a state the model has no place for. Left unguarded it is actively destructive, because it *succeeds*: the author's click subscribes them to their own unlock tier, that seat is the one the §2 roster then shows, and the real buyer — whose own purchase never landed — is answered `not_subscribed` at `key_blob.get` and can never unseal what they paid for. Found exactly that way on macOS/iOS, where a stale pre-actor-switch snapshot left the seller's own buy affordance on screen for the incoming buyer's click; the guard is in shared Rust rather than the apps because the affordance is shared, and a count-only e2e assertion (`wait_for_subscriber(1)`) is satisfied by the wrong actor — the roster and pending-request identity assertions in `test_sell_post.py` exist to close that.
  - **A completed buy must not be re-offered — and only the buyer's own client can know that (ruled 2026-08-27, at fix time).** The price read above is post-addressed and carries **no caller axis**, deliberately: it answers iff a tier genuinely sells the post, and § *No rotation* below requires it to keep answering for a buyer whose entitlement lapsed or whose request the author rejected. So the nest must not suppress a re-offer on caller state — doing so would strand exactly those two buyers. But a successful buy leaves the actor **`Queued`, not subscribed** (a client-minted unlock tier is never `auto_approve`), so nothing in the post's own snapshot records it either, and `buy_unlock_offer`'s only completion signal is that it clears `unlock_offer` — a field whose emptiness is precisely what re-arms the resolve. Every app's render trigger re-runs the resolve on the `notify()` the buy itself emits, so the teaser reappears and the completion signal never durably holds. The memory therefore lives in shared Rust, one place for all 7 apps: `FeedManager::unlock_purchase_requested`, an actor-scoped in-memory set (a manager is built per actor) that `resolve_post_unlock_offer` checks first, written **before** the clear+notify. Same reasoning as the `is_local_actor` guard above — the affordance is shared, so its guard is too. It is not cleared by `reload` (re-offering a bought post after a refresh is the defect) but does not survive a restart, which is correct: by then the author may have approved, and the offer resolves against live tier state. Pinned by `FeedManager`'s `a_bought_posts_offer_is_not_re_offered_by_a_second_resolve`, red-verified against the unguarded tree.
  - **No rotation.** Rotate-on-removal protects *future* content; an unlock tier's content is fixed at creation, so refund/expiry un-entitles at the serving gates (`is_subscriber`, Rail B token mint) but never re-seals — exactly Pillar 3's honest-box bound, and no roster-removal mint burden.
  - **Perpetual by default.** A purchase maps to `valid_until = None` (the `claims_mint(tier, None)` precedent); a mechanism may still supply a window (a rental is expressible, not the default).
  - **Immutability + deletion.** `tiers.update` refuses to change `unlocks_post` (re-pointing a sold unlock is a rug-pull). Deleting the post removes the affordance for everyone (the author's right); the tier row and its claim audit trail persist. The designation promises *at-least-this-post*, not *only-this-post* — the picker exclusion keeps the 1:1 shape in practice; extra content gated to it by a determined author is harmless bonus.
  - **The designation is corroboration; the post's own gate is the authority (ratified 2026-08-04).** **A tier sells a post iff the post is gated to that tier AND that tier designates that post** — both directions, checked wherever a sale is inferred or a price quoted (one resolver, `CacheDb::get_tier_selling_post`). `unlocks_post` alone cannot be authority: it is a one-way client claim the nest *cannot* verify when it is made (the post does not exist yet — that is why create-time validation is format-only, above), and it is not unique, so a tier may name a post it has nothing to do with. `content_meta.gated_tier` is projected from `Post.gated.tier` inside the body the post id content-addresses, so claiming it after the fact means forging the id. Consequences, all ordinary rather than exceptional: a zap on an **ungated** post is always a tip however a tier was designated; a post gated to an ordinary subscription tier is **subscription content, not an individual sale** (that tier designates no post — this is the *at-least-this-post* rule read the other way); and a second tier claiming an already-sold post sells nothing. **This is why the rule is a hard binding rather than a tie-break:** misclassifying a tip as a sale does not merely mis-grant, it *removes* the receipt from the post's tip surface (§ Tips), which is the same attribution destruction § The asking price refuses when it declines to refuse under-threshold receipts.
- **Rejected — a post-scoped entitlement variant** (the entitlement/`KeyAccess` gains a post target): (1) a new `KeyAccess` arm in the decoded `Post` record is a **within-major wire break** — an old reader fails to decode the record entirely, where the chosen shape renders as an ordinary gated post (teaser + badge) on every shipped client, purchasable via claim redemption with zero client update; (2) it forks the waist §34 forbids forking — claims, tier mapping, expiry, the Rail B token scope, and the §4/§5 surfaces would each grow a post-scoped arm; (3) its supposed cost saving is illusory — per-post sale intrinsically needs per-post key material wrapped per buyer, so the variant pays the identical wrap cost in a bespoke untested structure instead of the KeyBlob. **Also rejected — a reserved tier-*name* scheme** (`post:<id>`) in place of the explicit field: tier names are user-facing and per-post families collide; the `FOLLOWERS_TIER` reserved name works because it is one global constant.

## Zap receipts — the trust model (ratified 2026-07-29)

The NIP-57 zap adapter is the Nostr-native payment mechanism feeding both § Tips and, where the target post carries an `unlocks_post` designation, the unchanged entitlement waist (§ Per-post pay-to-unlock). § Tips above delegates the adapter's **trust model** here; this section is that ratification. Refutable by the security review or at build time. Zap-receipt *parsing and storage* specifics stay owned by [`../ui/nostr.md`](../ui/nostr.md) § The relay event store.

- **The problem the mechanism has and a webhook does not.** A Pillar-3 webhook authenticates with a secret only the payee and their provider hold, so "who sent this" is answerable from the event alone. A **kind-9735 zap receipt is not**: it is signed by the *recipient's* LNURL/wallet server's nostr key — not the sender's, and not any key Fauna knows a priori — and it is plain signed JSON that anyone may mint naming any recipient, whose `bolt11` tag no part of this system checks against a real Lightning payment. **A zap receipt's own valid signature therefore proves nothing about payment and must buy it nothing.**
- **The trust root is a payee-designated signer list.** Each payee designates which signer pubkey(s) may speak for their money — their LNURL provider's `nostrPubkey`, and any second provider they use. Which wallet/LNURL provider a payee trusts is a genuine user choice, so it is app UI + nest state per § Product invariants, never a config file. **A payee who has designated nobody believes nobody**: the empty designation makes every receipt inert, which is the correct out-of-the-box default — no zap is silently believed.
- **What a designated signer still may not do.** A receipt is believed only when, in addition to the signer being designated: (i) its `p` tag names *this* payee; (ii) the kind-9734 zap request embedded in its `description` tag names the same recipient as the receipt; and (iii) **when it names a zapped event (its `e` tag), that event is one this box holds authored by the payee.** Conjunct (ii) closes a *recipient* forgery — stapling a genuine zap request onto a different recipient. Conjunct (iii) closes the distinct *subject* forgery (ratified 2026-07-31): the first two conjuncts are entirely about the `p` tag (who may speak for this payee's money) and say nothing about the `e` tag (what event they may speak *about*), so without (iii) a signer a payee designated to report their *own* income could attribute a zap — of any amount, since `bolt11` is checked against nothing — to any *other* user's post. The subject's stored author is compared to the payee at ingest; an event this box does not hold is refused (a total for an unseen event is unrenderable, so nothing observable is lost, and accepting-but-marking would defer the check to every reader — which the *at ingest, never at read* discipline exists to prevent). A profile zap (no `e` tag) names no subject, so (iii) does not apply. A receipt carrying no parseable request is not rejected on the (ii) ground alone (`description` is optional in the wild and the signer is already designated); only an actual disagreement is fatal.
- **The gate is applied at ingest, at every ingress, never at read.** Filtering untrusted rows at read time would leave forged rows resting on the box and would have to be re-applied by every future reader (the tip surface, the purchase leg, any display). Refusing at ingest means every downstream consumer inherits the guarantee structurally — the same permanent-discipline shape as the kind-1059 recipient gate.
- **Rejected — deriving trust from the payee's LNURL metadata.** The nest could fetch the payee's `lud16` LNURL-pay endpoint and trust the `nostrPubkey` it advertises. This moves the trust root off-box onto an outbound fetch to an attacker-influenceable URL (a new SSRF surface) and makes "who may speak for my money" a network result rather than a user act. Auto-discovery remains available later as a **pre-fill convenience for the same designation**, which changes no part of this model.
- **Rejected — implementing the `PaymentProvider` trait.** That trait's `verify(secret, headers, body, now_secs)` is webhook-shaped; a zap has no shared secret and no HTTP headers, so satisfying it would mean feeding a fake secret and empty headers into the one seam whose job is honesty about authenticity. The adapter instead produces a `VerifiedPaymentEvent` and goes through `PaymentEntitlement::from_verified_event` — the actual transport-agnostic waist entry point — which is what keeps the waist untouched as Q9 requires and keeps NIP-57 knowledge quarantined (§ Pillar 3, adapter seam).
- **Where it lives.** The verdict is pure shared Rust in `libs/fauna-bridge-nostr` (`nip57::classify_zap_receipt`), *not* `libs/fauna-payments`: the payments crate is wasm32-clean and consumed by the web wasm build, while the nostr crate pulls `tokio`, so the dependency runs nostr → payments and never the reverse.

## Why profile, not contacts

Profile and Contacts are **list → detail over one "user" concept**, not two homes for it (`profile.md` § Relationship to Contacts): the **Profile** is the per-user *detail* surface (the home for every per-user action), and **Contacts** (`contacts.md`) is a *list* you own — your symmetric mutual edges (knock / accept / block / confirm) + knock inbox — that links *into* profiles. A subscription is an **asymmetric creator↔audience** action: you can subscribe to a creator you have no mutual relationship with (a public creator with thousands of subscribers), which the contacts edge model structurally can't express. So, exactly like follow (the free "followers" tier) and every other per-user action, subscription lives on the **Profile detail surface**, not on the Contacts list. Contacts may show a subscription-status badge as a convenience cross-link, but it is not the canonical home. ## Open design questions (all resolved)

These were genuine architecture/product decisions, not drift; each blocked its pillar until resolved. Resolutions are recorded here as one-liners; the ratified prose lives in the pillar sections.

- **Q1 — Web paywall vs "nest is blind." ✅ RESOLVED 2026-07-12 (user greenlight):** web-paywalled content is a **capability-granted readable class** — the creator's client mints the nest's web-serve holder a standing scoped grant (§ Pillar 2). The rejected options: client-uploaded sealed HTML under a nest-held serving key (the nest reads plaintext at serve time anyway — adds key custody for nothing), and the mode-conditional variant (obsolete — the storage-mode axis is retired; `../architecture/nest/storage-modes.md`).
- **Q2 — Dynamic gate vs "static delivery only." ✅ RESOLVED 2026-07-12:** short-lived signed capability URLs, verified statelessly by nest-owned code; content stays pre-rendered static bytes; the narrow gate check is not user-code execution (§ Pillar 2).
- **Q3 — Identifying a paying web visitor vs "no auth cookie." ✅ RESOLVED 2026-07-12:** explicit bearer capability tokens, **never a cookie** — non-ambient, so `web-content-hosting.md` invariant 2 holds on every surface; the earlier "first cut narrows to custom domains" concern was too pessimistic for per-resource access. Full-site cookie-session browsing is a named later increment, custom domains only (§ Pillar 2).
- **Q4 — Buyer↔actor binding. ✅ RESOLVED 2026-07-12:** signed-in checkout carrying `client_reference_id = actor_id` primary; post-payment claim code as the universal fallback (client-redeemed → both rails; paywall-page-redeemed → Rail B only); email↔handle mapping rejected as primary (§ Pillar 3).
- **Q5 — IA placement (Pillar 1 UX).** ✅ **RESOLVED 2026-06-14 (user-approved):** profile-centric (author mgmt on own profile's Tiers tab; subscriber browse on others'; consumer "my subscriptions" on the `subscription-settings` page). Lead app = **linux**. See § Pillar 1 — UX. **Profile-shell IA ratified 2026-06-17 (user-approved):** the Profile is the canonical per-user *detail* surface (list→detail with Contacts), reached via a top-level `profile-tab`; follow = subscribe to the "followers" tier; the one net-new shared-Rust piece is a `fauna.profile.get` kind + `fauna-client-profile` crate. See `profile.md` + § Why profile, not contacts.
- **Q6 — Does nest access reuse the tier-as-entitlement primitive? ✅ RESOLVED 2026-07-22 (design pass, per the user-directed unification):** yes — a membership tier is an ordinary admin-owned subscription tier plus a nest-state designation linking it to a quota tier; the two tier systems stay distinct concepts joined by an explicit link, never merged (§ Pillar 4). The waist's target axis lives on the *tier's designation*, so `PaymentEntitlement` itself is unchanged — mechanism-blind and target-blind.
- **Q7 — Lapse semantics. ✅ RESOLVED 2026-07-22 (design pass):** lapse = automatic, reversible **quota-tier downgrade** to the link's `lapse_tier` — no new account state, no read/write dispatch axis, no suspension, no auto-eviction; existing quota enforcement supplies exactly the required degrade (new writes capped, reads + export + all existing data intact). Rejected: reusing `suspended` (denies reads — violates *user always controls their data* for a mere lapse, and `admin.md` § Cutting a user off deliberately kept suspension axis-free); a new read-only dispatch state (re-introduces the read/write axis across ~45 handler families that suspension's design explicitly avoided, for no gain over the quota mechanism the tier system already enforces).
- **Q8 — Paid admission vs registration modes. ✅ RESOLVED 2026-07-22 (design pass):** paid admission is self-service, so it rides the mode exactly like invite codes — honored under `open`/`invite_required`, refused under `closed`. `closed` retains its meaning (*no self-service admission at all*; admins can still approve requests in every mode) — anything else would collapse `closed` into `invite_required`, defeating the mode enum's own unrepresentability rationale (`public-mode.md` § Registration Modes).
- **Q9 — Per-post pay-to-unlock quantum shape. ✅ RESOLVED 2026-07-22 (design pass, per the user-directed round):** degenerate **auto-minted single-post tiers** — an ordinary subscription tier plus a create-time-immutable `unlocks_post` designation; waist, engine, mechanisms, and both rails unchanged. Rejected: a post-scoped entitlement/`KeyAccess` variant (within-major wire break for old readers; forks the waist; pays the identical per-buyer wrap cost in a bespoke structure) and a reserved tier-name scheme (user-facing, collision-prone). See § Per-post pay-to-unlock.
- **Q10 — Where the zap msat→tier threshold lives, and in what unit. ✅ RESOLVED 2026-08-02:** an optional **unit-tagged `asking_price` `{value, unit}` on the tier** (`"msat"` the first unit), consulted **only** by intent-inferring amount-bearing mechanisms; comparison is unit-equality + `≥`, fail-closed on mismatch/unknown/absent — under-threshold (and no-price) stays a **tip**, ratifying the shipped behavior. Rejected: a bare `price_msats` field (the Lightning special-case the model's first rule forbids); parsing `price_hint` free text; refusing under-threshold receipts (zaps are irrevocable — refusal only destroys owed attribution); a partial-credit accumulation ledger (new nest state, refund semantics, gameable); an exchange-rate oracle for cross-unit comparison (off-box dependency + a config surface). See § The asking price.
