# The conversations oracle — how a device principal reads and sends in rooms — target state

Owns: conversations-oracle
Status: ratified 2026-10-17 (the cli's one open design pass; refutable until the first build, which is enumerated in § Implementation status today) — target state ahead of code; built in part (2026-10-18, § Implementation status today)

> **Audience:** whoever builds the cli's conversation tools, the oracle door in the first-party apps, and the nest's record kind; every session that reasons about what an LLM may read of a user's rooms.
> **Purpose:** the answer to *"can an LLM read my conversations and reply in them without ever holding a key that could impersonate me — and what exactly does it hold while it may?"*

## Goal

A user asks their LLM "what did my sister write about the trip" or "tell the family room I'll be late", and the LLM answers from, or sends into, the user's own rooms — end-to-end, community or bridged alike — through a grant the user minted from their own app, revocable in one row, audited per operation, while **no process but the user's own signed-in first-party app ever holds the MLS leaf, the group state or any room key.**

## The ruling — the oracle, not an export and not a membership

[`../architecture/apps/cli.md`](../architecture/apps/cli.md) § Open decisions (2) named three candidates. One is chosen, two are rejected, and the reasons are the mechanism's own constraints:

1. **A per-room export the user's device re-encrypts to the principal — REJECTED.** It hands the principal a readable copy of a co-authored log that then rests under the principal's own key, on the LLM host's disk: the one shape the materialization tier refuses for member scopes ([`../architecture/encryption-at-rest.md`](../architecture/encryption-at-rest.md) § Readable classes, class 4, *own-actor scopes only*), carried onto a weaker holder. Its revoke bound is the worst of the three — a copy already exported is beyond every key — and it buys only that reads work while no first-party app runs, a state the device shell already declares degraded. A derived view the device keeps for itself is not forbidden by this; a derived view handed to a non-member is.
2. **A bridge-class membership under TP8 — REJECTED.** A bridge member makes a room transport-only by derivation ([`conversation-rooms.md`](conversation-rooms.md) § The three classes), so the principal could read only rooms re-created around it — never the user's existing end-to-end rooms, which TP12 keeps it out of as a theorem — and it would label every such room as read by a far network, which the principal is not. The one true sentence in the candidate survives in § The class does not move, below: a member set names *key holders*, and the principal holds none.
3. **The co-resident first-party app as a local oracle — CHOSEN.** The signed-in first-party app that hosts the account's conversations engine on the same device is a **custodian** in the oracle's exact sense ([`../architecture/key-material-hierarchy.md`](../architecture/key-material-hierarchy.md) § *The oracle*): the principal, holding a keyless `identity.op` grant, asks it to perform named operation classes — read a room, send into a room — and never holds the key. It is the oracle's shape with the custodian moved to where the key is: MLS material rests only on the user's devices ([`conversations-at-rest.md`](conversations-at-rest.md) § Encryption at rest, the group-state bullet), so the custodian of a conversation class can only be a device. It is also exactly the evolution [`../architecture/account-runtime-roles.md`](../architecture/account-runtime-roles.md) § Multi-instance concurrency already declares for the engine role — a non-holder reaching the holder over IPC — with the cli as the first non-holder. The constraint it must keep, *needs the app running*, is accepted and declared (§ What each shell serves).

**Is this a superset?** Of what matters, yes: every room class the user's app can render, the same affordances the page offers, gated the same way, through the same shared manager — and the one property the other two cannot give, that nothing conversation-shaped ever rests under a key the LLM's process holds.

## The custodian and the door

**The custodian is a signed-in first-party app on the same device that hosts a conversations engine for the account** — tui, linux, macOS or windows; android and iOS host engines too but no LLM host launches a process beside them, so the door is a desktop and tui surface; web has no local seam and is never a custodian; the sync agent holds no MLS state and is never a custodian ([`../architecture/encryption-at-rest.md`](../architecture/encryption-at-rest.md) § Readable classes names it an MLS-less host). Different apps on one device are different MLS devices with their own engines ([`../architecture/account-runtime-roles.md`](../architecture/account-runtime-roles.md) § Multi-instance concurrency, the role lock's keying), and any of them may serve; **one serves at a time** — the door's endpoint is taken under a kernel-arbitrated exclusive lock of the agent's single-instance shape, per (OS user, account), first-come, released when that app signs out, exits or loses its engine role (`ServedElsewhere`), so a second signed-in app takes it over on its next tick. What the custodian serves is its own device's view of the account's rooms, which the `__mls` state replica converges across the fleet ([`devices.md`](devices.md) § Cross-device MLS group-state sync).

**The door is a second `fauna-ipc` endpoint, the agent's shape with the sides mirrored** — the app serves, the principal connects: a `0600` unix socket in the agent socket's `0700` per-user dir on linux and macOS, a per-SID named pipe on windows, each name carrying a 16-hex-character **fingerprint of the account's actor id** (the full id would overrun `sun_path`'s 104-byte budget on macOS — [`../architecture/apps/sync-agent.md`](../architecture/apps/sync-agent.md) § Control plane split, *the unix socket path has a hard length budget*), the handshake carrying the full id and refusing a mismatch. The frames are the `fauna-ipc` length-prefixed canonical dag-cbor frame, one enforcer of `MAX_FRAME_SIZE`; on windows the connecting principal runs the same server-identity check the sync-pipe client runs before its first frame, because the pipe namespace is machine-wide. **The seam carries only what is structurally local**, the test [`../architecture/apps/sync-agent.md`](../architecture/apps/sync-agent.md) § Control plane split states: the key the operation needs lives in this process and nowhere reachable, which is the strongest form of local there is. No policy rides it — the grant, the roster row and the ceiling are the account's state, read by the custodian, never told to it by the caller.

**The caller is authenticated as the principal, not as a process of the same OS user.** The same-user boundary the socket's mode and the pipe's DACL already draw is the agent's whole trust model, and it is not enough here: a same-user process that is not the consented principal must get nothing. So the handshake is a **possession proof of the principal's attested holder key**: the caller names its `principal_id` — told to it by the token answer ([`../architecture/third-party.md`](../architecture/third-party.md) § The principal model); the custodian reads that row's `holder_x25519` from the roster (`fauna.principals.list`) — the principal id is minted by the nest at the token endpoint, after the grant, so the owner's grant log never names it and its frozen event shape cannot grow it; the roster is the id → key map and nothing more, because what admits an operation is the owner's log holding a live grant to that key, and a roster that cannot be read refuses the operation rather than falling back to a cached row — HPKE-seals a fresh 32-byte nonce to it, and admits the connection only when the caller returns the nonce opened. The proof binds the connection to one principal of **this custodian's own account**; a principal of another account on the same device reaches that account's door, never this one. The DPoP key and the OAuth token play no part — the door is not the nest and verifies no token; the attested holder key is the principal's identity at every key-side door ([`../architecture/third-party.md`](../architecture/third-party.md) § The principal model, rule 2).

## The two classes

Two `IdentityOpClass` variants under the second scope arm `fauna:identity:op:<class>` ([`authorization-server.md`](authorization-server.md) § Scope grammar), one custodian, `Custodian::DeviceConversationsEngine` — the first custodian that is a device and not the nest or a bridge. Both are keyless, both are minted by the user's device at consent exactly as every `identity.op` tuple is, both are refused to a **remote** form at PAR (TP4 reserves conversations for device apps and nest-hosted code, and a remote process has no device door to reach; the `wasm` form is admitted by the arm and served nothing by § What each shell serves). Each row below is the policy the custodian enforces before it performs — the class table in [`../architecture/key-material-hierarchy.md`](../architecture/key-material-hierarchy.md) § *The oracle* carries the same two rows and points here.

| Class | What it performs | Policy the custodian enforces | What crosses the door |
|---|---|---|---|
| `conversation.read` | list the account's rooms; read one room's messages by position, one page per operation; search the device's own conversation index and the community room's position-only door ([`community-rooms.md`](community-rooms.md) § The three classes → *The door answers where*) | the room is one the custodian's own account is a member of and its page can render; every verdict the page applies applies here — the family gate's `guardian_state`, a disconnected bridge, a position the member holds no wrap for; the result is the manager's projection (`ThreadSummary`, `ThreadDetail`, a page of `MessageSnapshot`s), carried as the door's own records — a room, a page of messages by position, a hit — so the versioned wire never freezes the manager's render model, **never** an envelope, a key, the group state, the `__mls` slice or a member's key package | in: the room id, a position and a page size, or a query; out: the projection, as data |
| `conversation.send` | send a text message as the account into a room | the room is one the account can send in under the page's own capability vector (`ThreadCapabilities`, the role gate, the guardian gate's outbound rule) — the custodian performs the page's own send: the MLS application message the leaf signs, the bridged family's seal to the bridge key, mail's submission, whichever the rail is; the body is shown to the custodian, never opaque; attachments are **not** in v1 (a declared refinement — the attachment store's budget and the privacy strip are the page's, and a second ingress for bytes earns its own ruling); never a membership operation — invite, remove, rename, policy, transfer stay the page's (§ Don't do these) | in: the room id and the body; out: the message's position, or the typed refusal |

**Why sends are an oracle class and reads are too.** A send is identity-bearing by construction: an MLS application message is signed by the member's leaf and bound to it at every reader (MLS-1/MLS-2, [`message-reactions.md`](message-reactions.md) § Reactions & message delete → *Sender authentication* and *Leaf-credential binding*) — so a principal can send only by asking the leaf's holder, which is the oracle's definition. A read is not identity-bearing, but it needs the same holder for the same reason (`process_message` on a full member), so it takes the same keyless shape rather than a `content.read` tuple that would have to carry MLS material: the class table's rule that a class "against a named key" is the custodian's to perform holds for reading under that key as for signing with it.

**The ceiling and the page.** The oracle's hard constant applies — `OPS_PER_WINDOW` per principal, the `RateWindow` kept on the custodian's own per-principal row in the app's account-scoped store, so it binds across restarts and across the apps that take turns serving. One page of messages is one operation; the page size is a Rust constant chosen so a room's recent history is a handful of operations, and a principal that pages a whole community log hits the ceiling and waits — the bound is the point.

## What the principal's process holds, and the revoke bound

While the grant lives the LLM's process holds **exactly the tool results it was served, in memory, as data** — a projection of messages, never bytes it could open later. The device shell keeps no conversation index and no conversation store ([`../architecture/apps/cli.md`](../architecture/apps/cli.md) § The device shell's local content work covers the principal's *granted scope keys*, of which a conversation grant conveys none), so there is nothing conversation-shaped on the host's disk to delete at revoke. What the LLM host itself keeps of a tool result — its own transcript, its provider's logs — is outside Fauna's boundary and said so on the consent card.

**Revoke bites at the next operation.** The custodian re-resolves the grant per request against the owner's own grant log — it *is* an owner app, so the client-authoritative record is its local read, with the nest's reconcile sweep as built ([`../ui/nests.md`](../ui/nests.md) § Trust facet — grants → *Reconcile*) — and a revoke from any app reaches it by the log's own sync; the roster's one verb (delete the row) ends the grant family the same way. The honest bound is therefore the same as every oracle class's and tighter than every content grant's: nothing to key-rotate, nothing to delete on the holder, one window of at most the ceiling's operations between the revoke and the refusal.

## The class does not move

**A room's confidentiality class names who holds a key to its log; the oracle gives the principal none, so a room read through it stays exactly the class it is** — end-to-end stays end-to-end, with the same member set on every seat's roster. This is the position of a screen reader, the clipboard, or tui's external media handoff: a member's device renders the log to whatever the member runs on that device, and nothing in the room model ever promised co-authors otherwise — the model binds key holders, visibly, and nothing else. What the oracle adds over those positions is that this reader is **named, grant-bound, rate-limited, audited and revocable from the member's own app**, which no clipboard is. Two consequences, stated so nobody re-derives them: a co-author is not asked and not told, because the roster shows key holders and the principal is not one — a design that painted "Alice's assistant" on the roster would claim a membership TP12 forbids presuming; and **a room is never re-classed, re-created or re-keyed to admit the principal** — the one thing that would move the class is giving it a key, which is the rejected candidate 2.

The nest-hosted shell has no device and no custodian, and the one key-holding position on a nest — the community room's home nest — serves a closed list of purposes amended only in [`community-rooms.md`](community-rooms.md) § The three classes → *Forbidden, stated once*; serving a room's text to a principal is not on it and is not added here. A possible future shape for that shell — a *reader principal* seated on a community room's floor under the recipient-set scheme, visible on the roster as what it is — is a room-model change for [`conversation-rooms.md`](conversation-rooms.md)'s owner, **declared and not taken**.

## The per-operation record

Every operation is one row of the roster's oracle record — class, the room's id as its detail (a plaintext-floor fact already; never a body, a sender or a query string), time — the audit split's custodian half ([`../architecture/key-material-hierarchy.md`](../architecture/key-material-hierarchy.md) § *The oracle* → *The audit split*). The custodian here is a device, so the record reaches the other apps through the nest: the custodian submits each row over a User-class, self-scoped kind, `fauna.principals.ops.record` (the account's own principal only; the nest keeps the newest 256 per principal in the shape of `nostr_oracle_ops`), and `fauna.principals.ops` merges it into the row as a second custodian's record, exactly as the roster already merges custodians. The ceiling's window is the custodian's too, so each submission carries the custodian's position in it — the window's start and its count, as read when the batch leaves — and the nest keeps the newest position any batch reported (by start, then count), which the roster's count in window reads; the nest refuses a window opening beyond the record's clock skew or holding more than the ceiling admits, and the position is a display, never a ceiling the nest enforces. A row that fails to submit is retried from the custodian's own store and never blocks the operation; the operation's admission is the grant and the ceiling, not the record.

## Architectural rules

1. **No MLS material ever crosses the door, in either direction, in any form** — not a leaf, a key package, the group state, an epoch secret, a wrap, a sealed envelope or the `__mls` slice. A projection goes out; a body comes in.
2. **The custodian is a signed-in first-party app hosting the account's engine, on the same device.** Never the nest, never the sync agent, never the principal itself, never a remote process.
3. **The caller is the principal, proven by its holder key, of this custodian's own account.** Same-OS-user is necessary, never sufficient.
4. **The policy is the page's.** What the oracle serves of a room is what that app's page would show its user; what it sends is what that page would send. A verdict the page applies and the oracle skips is a hole.
5. **The grant is re-resolved per operation, the ceiling binds per principal, every operation is recorded** — the oracle's three properties, with no exemption for a "trusted" first-party caller.
6. **The class does not move.** No re-classing, re-creation or re-keying of a room to admit a principal; no principal on any roster.

## Don't do these

- **Don't export, re-encrypt or cache a room's log for the principal** — not under its holder key, not under a derived key, not "just the last week". Candidate 1 is rejected for every room class.
- **Don't seat the principal on a room** as a bridge, a member or a reader; don't paint it on a roster. Candidate 2 is rejected, and the reader-principal shape for community rooms is a room-model decision this doc does not make.
- **Don't host the conversations engine in the cli**, and don't add an "LLM mode" to any app that holds the engine. The custodian is a first-party app a human signed in to.
- **Don't widen the seam to policy.** The grant, the roster row, the ceiling and the family gate are read by the custodian from the account's state, never carried by the caller.
- **Don't serve membership operations** — invite, remove, leave, rename, set policy, transfer — through `conversation.send` or any class. They are the page's gestures; the grant plane and the room's shape stay the human's ([`../architecture/apps/cli.md`](../architecture/apps/cli.md) § The sovereign-act boundary names the grant plane; a room's membership is the same class of act one level down).
- **Don't let the oracle write into the user's compose draft.** The send verb the custodian performs is headless — it never touches the thread's `ComposeState`, so a half-written draft under the user's cursor survives an LLM send.
- **Don't serve a room on the nest-hosted shell through the home nest's read.** The purposes of that read are closed, and this is not one of them.
