> **Audience:** the nest's subscription, feed, federation, outbox and succession planes; `libs/fauna-client-subscriptions` and `libs/fauna-feed`; every app's profile follow leg.
> **Purpose:** a reader on nest A follows an author on nest B and sees B's author's posts in their own feed, from their own nest, with the same key leg a same-nest follower has.

## The ruling this doc builds on

A follow reaches across nests (owner, 2026-10-16). A reader on nest A who follows an author homed on nest B has that author's post **envelopes** — public and gated alike — delivered to A, which stores them in its own index and lists them to **that follower** through the ordinary read model; a stranger on A never meets them because a neighbour follows the author (a per-reader source, never a nest-wide one). What crosses is the envelope: the sealed body and every attachment stay on B and are fetched by the reader's client by content address from B's public blob route; bulk bytes never ride the nest↔nest channel. Following *is* subscribing to the author's free `followers` tier ([`monetization.md`](monetization.md) § Pillar 1), so the subscription rides a signed federation twin of `fauna.subscriptions.subscribe` and the author's client wraps the period key to the remote follower exactly as to a local one — nest-blind, roster-covering, no nest holding key material. A seat on a room still buys no delivery ([`restricted-posts.md`](restricted-posts.md) § Encryption at rest, ruling 1). Everything below is the design the ruling left open.

## Decision 1 — Push, over the channel, through the outbox

**The author's nest pushes.** On every post a local author stores (`routes::ingest_post_core` — the one door every `fauna.posts.create` passes), the nest enqueues one delivery per **distinct follower home nest** — the set of `home_nest_id` over that author's `subscribers` rows that carry one (§ Decision 2), whatever the tier: a paid remote subscriber receives the same envelope, only the key leg differs — and the outbox worker delivers each as the new channel kind `fauna.federation.follow.deliver` (payload: the stored `EmbedAsBytes` wire split into `post_bytes` + `post_envelope` + the optional `signer_auth` cert, exactly `fauna.federation.post.forward`'s shape — `federation_handlers.rs::FedPostForwardRequest`; one decode helper, two kinds). It is a new kind and not a second use of `post.forward`, because the two deliveries differ in every gate and every consequence: a forward is the author's own nest-of-record storing their post under the pairing's `post_forward` capability and fanning it out to bridges; a follow delivery is a foreign index copy that is marked, never bridged, never re-forwarded, and admitted on the receiver's own follow record (§ Decision 3). The `inbox.deliver` `Post` shape is not reused either: that kind routes one payload into one recipient's inbox with a push notification (`routes::deliver_inbox_payload_core`), and a follow delivery is one index row per nest, not one inbox entry per follower.

**Why push and not a signed subscriber pull.** The pull alternative — the follower's nest polling each remote author's nest through a subscriber-authenticated arm of the author's index — must start from what `fauna.federation.feed.query` actually is today: an unauthenticated read served with `FeedAudience::OnBox` (`feed_routes.rs::remote_query_feed_core`, the author arm over `db::feeds::query_feed_for_authors`), which lists a gated post's **id and floor metadata** (never its bytes — the body is a separate `post.get`, and a gated body is sealed in any case) to any peer. Nothing a subscriber could prove changes what that arm may return, so a pull would be a new polled kind with its own cursor state per (author, follower nest), a poll schedule that costs every follower nest a round trip per author per interval whether or not the author posted, minutes of latency, and a second mechanism for tombstones. Push costs one delivery per post per follower nest (not per follower), is immediate, and reuses the fleet's one retrying fan-out queue. The discovery feed query stays what it is: a peer read of public floor data, its posture unchanged by this doc.

**The queue is the existing `outbox`** (`db/schema.rs` `outbox`; worker `outbox.rs::drain_outbox_once`), generalized rather than twinned: the table gains an additive nullable `peer_nest_id BLOB` (NULL = the paired forward's pairing-resolved targets, as today), the entry types `follow_deliver`, `follow_delete` (§ Decision 5) and `follow_state` (§ Decision 2), and the worker drains follow entries on every nest mode — the `is_private` gate stays on the paired types alone. A follow entry's target URL resolves from `peer_nest_id` through the nest-wide `nest_addresses` directory, proven addresses first, never a per-row address column (the rule `succession-propagation.md` § Propagation already states for the succession push). **Retry and idempotency:** the outbox's capped backoff for ever, as for a forward; the delivery is idempotent on the post's content address — the receiver's `store_post` upserts and a replayed delivery answers `Ok` without a second store, so the channel's per-call idempotency key carries nothing the payload does not. **Two bounds keep a dead or hostile peer from growing the queue without limit:** an author's pending follow entries for a peer are discarded the moment that peer holds no subscriber of the author any more (the `outbox_discard_for_author` shape, keyed `(author, peer)`); and at most 1,000 pending entries per `(author, peer)`, oldest dropped first — a follower whose nest was away past that bound re-follows to backfill (next paragraph) rather than the author's nest holding an unbounded ledger for it.

**Backfill at approval.** A follower who sees nothing until the author's next post is a broken follow, so when a remote subscriber's request is approved (§ Decision 2) the author's nest enqueues the author's **newest 50 servable posts** (moderation-servable, not deleted — the same gate every non-author surface shares, `db::public_servability::MODERATION_SERVABLE`) to that subscriber's home — skipped when another subscriber of the author is already homed on that nest, since the index there already holds them. No pull kind exists; the backfill is the same `follow_deliver` entry in the same queue. **The bought post is in the backfill whatever its age (ruled 2026-11-08 with the redeem twin):** a request approved for a tier designated `unlocks_post` enqueues the designated post too — the buyer bought that post, and the newest-50 window will not hold an old one; one more `follow_deliver` entry, deduplicated with the window.

## Decision 2 — The subscribe twin: the subscriber's own signed intent, carried by their nest

**On the follower's nest.** `fauna.subscriptions.subscribe` (`libs/fauna-protocol/src/subscriptions.rs::SubscribeRequest`) gains two additive optional fields: `home_nest_url` and a signed `intent` (below). `subscription_handlers::subscribe_core` keeps its first arm — an author `get_user` finds here is a local follow, unchanged — and gains the remote arm: an author NOT hosted here **and** a `home_nest_url` named → the remote follow; neither → `tier_not_found`, exactly today's refusal (an unhosted author with no home named is still nobody this nest can reach). The apps learn a foreign author's home where they meet the author, and the profile carries it (corrected 2026-10-17 — this sentence used to claim the profile *already* resolved a `handle@domain` through `find_user_across_nests`; no app did, the lookup offered only a knock): the profile's open target is `(actor_id, home)`, and its doors are the Find User result (`CrossNestFound::nest_url`), a post's author (`PostSummary.home_nest_url`, which a relayed or delivered post carries — `../architecture/feed-read-model.md` § The read model → *A post homed on another nest*) and a contact row (the edge's recorded home) — owned by [`../ui/profile.md`](../ui/profile.md) § Layout & flow → *Reaching another actor's profile*, with the one shared rule that resolves the page's `actor_home` slot (its § Where logic lives → *The actor's home*); the follow gesture passes that slot's value through, so no app re-derives it. The remote arm writes the follower-side record — **`remote_follows`** `(subscriber_id, author_id, tier, home_nest_id, home_nest_url, state ∈ {pending, approved, rejected, removed}, request_id, created_at, updated_at)`, primary key `(subscriber_id, author_id, tier)` — then originates `fauna.federation.follow.subscribe` to the home over the channel and returns the home's `SubscribeReply` (`Approved` / `Queued`) to the client verbatim, recording the state it implies. `fauna.subscriptions.unsubscribe` gains the same arm (`fauna.federation.follow.unsubscribe`, the intent's `kind = unsubscribe`); `status.get` and `mine.list` read `remote_follows` beside `subscribers`, the `mine.list` entry carrying an additive `home_nest_url` so the consumer page can say where the author lives.

**The intent.** The subscriber's own act is what the author's nest must see, not their nest's word for it — the posture `write_token.mint` set ([`../architecture/federation-shared-folders.md`](../architecture/federation-shared-folders.md) § Cross-nest shared folders + channel append): the structural binding of the carrying nest **plus** the user's own signed statement. `FollowIntent { subscriber: ActorId, author: ActorId, tier, kind: subscribe | unsubscribe, home_nest_id: [u8; 32], created_at }` is canonical dag-cbor signed under `Capability::Follow` (`fauna_core::data::Capability`, already declared) by the subscriber's identity or an authorized device with that capability — minted by `libs/fauna-client-subscriptions` beside the subscribe call, the way a contact request for `fauna.inbox.send` is minted client-side and relayed verbatim. It names its home nest's id so a nest can relay only an intent that names itself. The follower's nest never signs for its user and never alters the intent; it carries it.

**On the author's nest** — `fauna.federation.follow.subscribe { intent_bytes, intent_envelope, signer_auth?, mlkem_encaps_key? }`, nest-signature, net-new. The handler verifies, in order: the intent's authoring envelope (`verify_authoring_envelope` with the D10 cert path, the author binding `post_forward_handler` already runs for a post); `intent.home_nest_id == origin_nest_id` — the structural half, the same binding `require_foreign_member` enforces for a channel fetch, so a hostile nest cannot put a victim homed elsewhere on an author's roster or redirect that victim's deliveries to itself; `intent.author` hosted here, else `tier_not_found`; and that no newer intent for the same `(author, subscriber, tier)` has been seen (the row keeps the latest `created_at`; an older one is answered from the state as it stands and changes nothing, so a replay can never undo a later unsubscribe). A leave spans every paid tier it ends, so an `unsubscribe` intent is admitted only when it is newer than every intent seen from that subscriber for that author, and it stamps each tier it queues a leave for. **An intent from a subscriber the author's nest has never seen spends that author's budget (ruled and built 2026-10-17):** any fresh keypair passes the hello and any fresh subscriber key signs, so a bound on the origin bounds nothing — at most 30 such intents per hour per hosted author, summed over every origin, a subscribe and a leave alike (each writes a seen-stamp row), and a stranger's subscribe is refused while the author already holds 500 pending remote requests, until the author drains the queue; both refusals are `rate_limited`. A subscriber already holding a pending or approved row for this author is free of the cap, since it holds at most one pending request per tier; having been seen is not that (a leave that ended nothing stamps too, so a leave-primed key still meets the cap), and a seen subscriber is free of the budget only. The mechanism is [`../architecture/federation.md`](../architecture/federation.md) § Security → *Rate limiting is the primary DoS defense*'s per-target budget. It then runs `subscribe_core`'s body for `subscriber_id = intent.subscriber` — the followers-tier provision, the hidden-tier and self-follow refusals, the idempotent `Approved`/`Queued` answers, the `insert_subscribe_request` enqueue — with one addition: the request row carries the subscriber's home. `subscribe_requests` and `subscribers` gain additive nullable `home_nest_id BLOB` and `home_nest_url TEXT` (NULL = a local subscriber), the URL resolved by `resolve_origin_home_url` from the verified origin and never from the payload alone — a dial-proven address of that origin first, and the carrying nest's own declared URL (the twin's `origin_nest_url`) only while the origin has no proven address, recorded as an unproven sighting bound to the verified id, the rule the Welcome and room-invite relays already follow; the approval copies both onto the roster row. The author's client's drain needs nothing new: `requests.list`'s `PendingRequest` gains an additive display `home: Option<String>` (the subscriber's `handle@domain`, or the URL), and `mint_key_blob` wraps to the subscriber's actor id as it always has (`ActorId::to_x25519_public`, the X-Wing half from the carried `mlkem_encaps_key`) — the KeyBlob stays author-minted, nest-blind and roster-covering.

**The state leg back.** The follower's nest learns the request's fate the way it learns everything else here — a push from the home: `fauna.federation.follow.state { author, subscriber, tier, state ∈ {approved, rejected, removed}, at }`, enqueued as an outbox `follow_state` entry by `requests.approve` (→ `approved`, which also triggers § Decision 1's backfill), `requests.reject` (→ `rejected`), `subscribers.remove` and the drain's committed unsubscribe (→ `removed`) — the leave being the reader's own, or the removal the home's entitlement engine queues for a voided or lapsed paid window, which the drain commits the same way (ruled 2026-11-09; owner [`monetization.md`](monetization.md) § Pillar 3 → *Expiry & revocation*). The receiver accepts it only for a `remote_follows` row whose `home_nest_id` is the verified origin, and updates `state` — except that `removed` is terminal for a push — no state moves a `removed` row (a `rejected` then `approved` pair included), and a push onto one still answers success so a home's retry stays idempotent: a follow the reader left or the home ended re-opens only through the reader's own new subscribe, which rewrites the row. **A tier the reader's paid follow includes opens its own row (built 2026-11-09):** the home's rank fan-out ([`monetization.md`](monetization.md) § Per-post pay-to-unlock) enrolls a paid subscriber in every per-post tier at or below the paid tier's rank, keeping the subscriber's home on each enrolled request, and the follower's nest holds no ranks, so an `approved` push for a tier with no row there is recorded as a new `approved` row only while the reader holds a live (`approved` or `pending`) follow of a non-`followers` tier of the same author homed at the pushing nest, whose home URL it takes (`db/remote_follows.rs::set_remote_follow_state_from`); any other push naming no row is refused `forbidden`, so a stranger nest, a free follow alone or a follow the reader left opens nothing. Proof: `follow_federation`'s `an_approved_push_opens_an_included_tier_beside_a_live_paid_follow` and the two-nest `a_remote_buyers_nest_follows_the_per_post_tiers_their_paid_tier_includes`. A follower's view is therefore eventually consistent with the roster and never ahead of it — the roster, not the paid window: a buyer's `approved` row stands, the home's gates refusing them meanwhile, until the home's drain commits the removal the window's end queued — with one exception, the reader's own leave: the leave is the reader's act over the reader's own data, so the remote arm of `unsubscribe` relays the leave to the home stored on the follow (its `home_nest_id` and `home_nest_url`), never to a URL the caller names, and moves the rows the leave names (by name, [`subscriptions.md`](subscriptions.md) § The free followers tier — follow and unfollow, rule 2; `approved` and `pending` alike) to `removed` on the reader's own nest once the home has answered or could not be reached at all — the feed's `ForReader` read and the delivery gate then stop admitting that home's deliveries for this reader — but never when the home refuses the leave, so a refusal leaves no half-state (amended 2026-11-08); a leave of an author with no `remote_follows` row is `not_subscribed`. The client's `status.get` reads what its nest holds.

**Paid tiers.** The twin carries any tier name: a paid tier's remote subscribe lands the same pending request a local one does, for the author's judgment or for the payment leg to complete — the claim redeem through the redeem twin below, the signed-in checkout through the webhook's upsert onto that row (ruled 2026-11-08 as Q13, owned by [`monetization.md`](monetization.md) § Pillar 3 → *Buyer↔actor binding (Q4)* → *A checkout-bound buyer homed on another nest*). **The redeem twin (ruled 2026-11-08 — the ruling, its doors and its wire are [`monetization.md`](monetization.md) § Pillar 3 → *Buyer↔actor binding (Q4)* → *Which nest an in-app redeem binds on*; this paragraph owns how the twin admits it).** The claim redeem is the first payment leg that names a remote buyer, and it rides this decision's machinery whole: `fauna.federation.claims.redeem` carries the redeemer's own signed `ClaimRedeemIntent` (redeemer, author, code, home nest, `created_at`; `Capability::Follow`) in the twin's request shape, and the author's nest admits it in the order above — `admit_relayed_intent`'s checks (the envelope; `home_nest_id` = the verified origin; the author hosted here, refused as `fauna.payments.claim_not_found`, never `tier_not_found`, so the kind tells a guesser nothing a wrong code would not), then `admit_stranger_intent`'s budget and pending cap, with one tightening: **the budget is spent by every relayed redeem, a seen subscriber's included**, because a miss answers whether an 8-character code exists (refutable at build time against the local door's own throttle); then `payment_core::redeem_claim` with the subscriber's home, whose payment-entitled request row carries `home_nest_id`/`home_nest_url` exactly as a relayed subscribe's does, so the approval, the state leg, the key-blob relay (served iff on the roster from that origin) and the leave need nothing new; the intent's `created_at` is stamped for `(author, redeemer, tier)` once the code has named the tier, so a later leave orders after it. The follower's nest records the home's reply as a `remote_follows` row for the tier the reply names, `approved` or `pending`. A membership tier's code is refused by the twin (`fauna.payments.claim_admits_elsewhere`): admission is registration on the author's nest, and a redeem across nests never grants it.

## Decision 3 — Envelope residency: the follower's nest stores the envelope, nothing else, marked

**What arrives and how it is admitted.** `fauna.federation.follow.deliver` on the follower's nest verifies the post's own authoring envelope, applies the Fauna-native future bound (`storage::reject_future_created_at`, the ~2-minute cushion — this handler is a new `Bounded` door enrolled in `storage/mod.rs::CREATED_AT_CENSUS` beside `post_forward_handler`, and `every_content_created_at_writer_is_partitioned` fails until it is), answers `Ok` with no store for a post this nest has seen deleted (`CacheDb::post_was_deleted` — the replay rule `feed.md` § Post deletion owns), and then applies **the follow gate**: the post's author must have at least one `remote_follows` row on this nest in state `approved` whose `home_nest_id` is the verified `origin_nest_id`. A nest that is nobody's home for this author is refused (`forbidden`), and a home can deliver only its own authors — the receiver's index is filled only by follows its users made. No submission policy applies: the paired forward's `PairedOnly` gate is about who may write *this nest's own* authors' posts, and a delivery writes none.

**What a reader-named home is trusted for — custody is per home, listing per reader.** The home a follow names is the reader's own word: their nest cannot prove a named nest is the author's home (the author's signed posts name no home), so a follow at nest A admits A's deliveries of the author's genuinely signed posts *for the readers who named A* and for nothing else. A post is one content-addressed row — the same bytes whichever home delivers them — and what is per home is **custody**: which homes have delivered it, held in the additive table **`follow_delivery_homes`** `(post_id, origin_nest_id, origin_nest_url, delivered_at)`, primary key `(post_id, origin_nest_id)`, one row per verified origin that delivered the post, its URL the one the receiver resolved for that origin (ruled 2026-11-04). Four rules bind every delivered row to the homes it came through: **(1) the store** — a delivery from a verified origin that passes the follow gate inserts (or, re-delivered, refreshes the URL of) that origin's custody row and nothing else of the row: the first delivery stores the segment, the projection and the row's `content.origin_nest_url` / `origin_nest_id`, and a later delivery from another home adds its custody and leaves those columns alone (`DeliveredStore::CustodyAdded`, answered `Ok`), so a delivery never relabels whose a row is and never overwrites another home's record; a row this nest already lists nest-wide (a discovery stub, a forwarded post) takes no custody and keeps its standing *and* its origin columns; **(2) the listing** — a delivered row lists only to a reader whose `approved` follow of the author names a home in its custody set (§ Decision 4), and the home that reader is shown — the `FeedPostItem.home_nest_url` / `home_nest_actor_id` the byte legs fetch from and pin against — is that follow's home, resolved per reader from the custody row, never from the row's columns; **(3) the tombstone** — `fauna.federation.post.delete` carries the author's own signed `Tombstone`, so custody decides only the door: the follow arm admits it from any home in the custody set, by verified origin identity and never by address, and the row goes whole, its custody rows with it (§ Decision 5) — a per-home withdrawal would keep a post the author deleted listed to the readers of a home that has not relayed the tombstone yet, the stale copy the replay rule exists to prevent, and a reader-named home can forge no tombstone; **(4) the purge** — a home's custody is dropped once no local reader follows the author at it, and a row with no custody left is removed (*Purge*, below). The `content.origin_nest_url` / `origin_nest_id` columns on a delivered row say "a remote row" to every nest-wide reader (`interact_routes`' routing refusal, the counters' `nest_counts_row`, `get_post_references`, the abuse-report relay) and always name a member of the custody set — the oldest, the purge re-pointing them when it drops that home with custody remaining; nothing per-reader reads them. What a second "home" some reader named for the same author reaches is therefore exactly the feed of the readers who named it: it can neither re-point nor pin a row, nor keep the real home's delivery out — the real home's custody is added beside it, and the real home's followers list the post from the real home, whichever delivered first. What stays, by design: a reader lists only what the home they named delivered, so a named home that withholds a post, or never fetches it, shows that reader nothing; and the custody set of a post is bounded by the homes this nest's own readers' approved follows name, since the follow gate admits no other origin.

**What is stored.** The envelope is stored through `segments::post::store_post` — the author's `__post` segment plus the `content`/`content_meta` projection, content-addressed, so every single-post read (`fauna.posts.get` → `routes::get_post_core`, reference resolution, the room view) resolves it exactly as a stored post — with two marks on the `content` row: `origin_nest_url` = the home's resolved URL (the column that already means "a remote row this nest does not own", which `interact_routes` refuses on and `get_post_references` reads) and the additive `follow_delivered INTEGER NOT NULL DEFAULT 0` = 1, the column every nest-wide read excludes on (§ Decision 4). The list-card preview is written as for any post (`write_post_index` — a public post's text is in the wire; a gated post's preview is its public preview), **but no `content_fts` row**: the Search corpus is nest-wide and a delivered post is one reader's, so in this version a followed remote author's post is not found by the feed's text filters or the page's search — a declared gap, not an oversight (`../architecture/feed-read-model.md` § The read model → *The list-card preview*'s corollary is where a per-reader text index would have to be ruled). No bridge fan-out (`spawn_post_bridge_fanout` is the author's own nest's act), no onward forward, no delivery entry: a delivered post is never re-delivered.

**What is not stored — and where the client reads it.** The sealed body (`GatedInfo.encrypted_ref`), the media and their thumbnails stay on the home and are fetched by the reader's client by content address from the home's public `GET /api/v1/blob/{hash}`, the byte legs the relay ruling already gives every app: `FeedPostItem` gains an additive `home_nest_url` projected from `content.origin_nest_url`, `libs/fauna-feed` folds it onto `PostSummary.home_nest_url` (the relay row's field), and an app's blob fetch reads its host from there. Beside it rides `home_nest_actor_id`, the delivery's verified `origin_nest_id` stored with the envelope (ruled 2026-10-19 — `../architecture/transport-trust.md` § Transport trust, the federation-granted row's *post arm*, owns how the plane trusts the home; stored as `content.origin_nest_id`), so the app's plane graduates a per-process pin against the home before the first byte. **The follower's nest caches no sealed body, no attachment and no thumbnail in this version** — a cache is a second copy of bytes the owner can delete at the home, and the deep-link relay already proved the direct fetch; revisit only on measured latency. **The `KeyBlob` is the one thing a remote follower reads through their own nest rather than by content address**: a `KeyBlob` names every subscriber in the clear (`fauna_core::subscription::types::KeyBlobEntry.subscriber`), so it stays where it rests — `current_key_blobs` behind `fauna.subscriptions.key_blob.get`'s `is_subscriber` gate — and never on a public route. `key_blob.get` on the follower's nest gains the remote arm: an author not hosted here for whom the caller holds an `approved` `remote_follows` row → originate `fauna.federation.follow.key_blob.fetch { author, tier, subscriber }` to the home, which serves the current blob iff `is_subscriber(author, subscriber, tier)` **and** the roster row's `home_nest_id` is the verified origin — the same structural binding as the subscribe twin — and relay the reply. The client's unlock path is unchanged in shape: it asks its own nest for the author's blob, as today. (The relay ruling's wording in `feed.md` § *A post homed on another nest* and `federation.md`'s post-fetch row names the sealed body and attachments as the by-content-address legs; a room post opens under the room's key and has no `KeyBlob`, and a broadcast-gated post's `KeyBlob` follows this paragraph.)

**Purge.** Two sweeps, hourly: the first drops a home's custody of a `follow_delivered` row (`follow_delivery_homes`) once no local reader holds an `approved` or `pending` `remote_follows` row for its author — or for a retired identity the author's succession maps onto — at that home, re-pointing the row's `content.origin_nest_url` / `origin_nest_id` to its oldest remaining custody when the dropped home was the one they named; the second removes a `follow_delivered` row (segment record, projection and room views) once no custody is left; a custody home's relay of the author's tombstone removes it before then (§ Decision 5). Delivered rows are never backed up as the user's data (they are the author's, resting at the home) and never exported.

## Decision 4 — The per-reader source, and the room arm's follower-homed case

**One locality parameter beside the audience.** `db::feeds::query_feed_impl`, `query_feed_scored` (and its three faces) and `query_feed_for_authors` — the author-scoped seam the author read already runs on, which is why the profile's Posts tab of a followed remote author lists from this nest's index by a change inside `fauna.feed.author.posts`'s handler and nowhere else (`../ui/profile.md` § Layout & flow → *A remote actor*) — take a `FeedLocality` next to `FeedAudience`: `NestWide` ANDs `c.follow_delivered = 0` onto the query; `ForReader(reader)` ANDs `(c.follow_delivered = 0 OR EXISTS (SELECT 1 FROM remote_follows rf JOIN follow_delivery_homes h ON h.post_id = c.id AND h.origin_nest_id = rf.home_nest_id WHERE rf.subscriber_id = ?reader AND rf.state = 'approved' AND rf.author_id = c.author))` — a reader-scoped join, never a nest-wide one, and bound to the homes the row came through (§ Decision 3, *custody*): a reader who follows the author at one home never lists what only another nest delivered. The projection is per reader too: `project_home_nest_urls` takes the locality, and for a `follow_delivered` row under `ForReader` projects `home_nest_url` / `home_nest_actor_id` from the custody row the reader's approved follow names (the oldest custody by `delivered_at` when the reader's tiers name several homes), so two readers list one row each from their own home; every other row projects the `content` columns as before. `feed_routes::query_local_feed_core` and `query_author_feed_core` pass `ForReader(account)` for the account on its own box (`FeedReader::Account`) and `NestWide` for every off-box reader; custom feeds for the account likewise; the built-in Trending read, the federation feed query, the ActivityPub outbox, the ATProto projection and the Nostr materializer are `NestWide` by construction (the latter three already hold to `PUBLIC_POST_SERVABLE`, which gains the same `follow_delivered = 0` arm so a delivered envelope never leaves the nest to a third party). The single-post read is a door of the same rule: `routes::get_post_core` consults `db::feeds::delivered_post_hidden_from`, which applies the same per-reader guard, and answers not-found for a delivered row to the anonymous and peer reads (`caller = None`: the federation `post.get` and the HTTP twin) and to a reader who does not follow its author — so a positive answer never reveals that some reader here follows the author (witness `a_delivered_row_is_not_found_to_anonymous_and_non_following_readers`). The guard is checked **first**, ahead of the legal-takedown tombstone and the admin-block notice (`moderation.md` § Legal takedown, § Admin block of a reported item): a disclosure is owed only to a caller who could otherwise read the item, so a flagged delivered row answers not-found to everyone the guard does not admit and its follower still gets the 451 or 403 (witness `a_flagged_delivered_row_is_not_found_to_anonymous_and_non_following_readers`). The blob and segment doors (`GET /api/v1/blob/{hash}`, `/api/v1/video/segments/{hash}`) are doors of the same rule. A delivered row's attachments rest at the author's home (§ Decision 3, *What is not stored*), so a withhold on them here has no bytes to protect, and its 451 or 403, where an unflagged delivered row's attachment answers 404, would tell an anonymous asker that a local reader follows the author. The blob withhold therefore does not count a delivered row as a namer ([`takedown-and-block.md`](takedown-and-block.md) § Legal takedown → *The blob-serve door*), and a flagged delivered row's attachment answers at those doors exactly as an unflagged one's does (witnesses `follow_delivery::tests::a_flagged_delivered_rows_attachment_is_not_withheld_at_the_blob_door`, `…::a_taken_down_delivered_rows_delete_carries_no_withhold_forward`). The viewer's block list and the spam guard apply to a delivered post as to any (`push_viewer_block`, `ensure_spam_filter`); moderation flags on a delivered row are this nest's own and bind as on any row. A predecessor's posts stay listed after the author's succession (§ Decision 5): the join admits `c.author` as the followed author **or** a retired identity that `actor_successions` maps onto it, the home binding unchanged.

**The room arm — ruling 7's "a follower homed there".** When a delivered post is room-addressed and this nest is the room's home, the deliver handler runs `room_post_view::index_room_post` exactly as `post_forward_handler` does after its store, and the view's one new case is where the sealed body comes from: the local blob store does not hold it, so for a `follow_delivered` row the view fetches `encrypted_ref` **by content address, from the public blob route of a custody home** (§ Decision 3, *custody*) — the delivering home first, then the row's other custody homes oldest first, the first body that matches the digest kept and no home dialled past it — bounded to the room-post body cap, never an attachment (the view indexes typed text only, `restricted-posts.md` § Encryption at rest, ruling 7), the bytes opened under the tip's wrap and dropped, nothing retained beyond the view. Each URL is a proven peer address this nest resolved, never user input, and the fetch is one small HTTP read, not a channel kind — so "bulk bytes never ride federation" holds. A body that cannot be fetched gets no view, the same silent rule a relayed post whose blob has not arrived already gets; the post is stored either way — and the view is attempted again on a later delivery that adds a home's custody while the post has no view, so a reader-named nest that holds the envelope alone (it serves no blobs) delivering first does not leave the room without its view once the real home delivers (ruled 2026-11-04).

## Decision 5 — Succession, unsubscribe and the delete leg

**Delete.** The author's tombstone lands at the home as today (`routes::delete_post_core`), and beside `maybe_enqueue_delete_outbox` the home enqueues a `follow_delete` entry per distinct follower home; the worker sends it as `fauna.federation.post.delete`, which **widens** with a second gate arm rather than a new kind: the receiving handler keys on the stored row — a `follow_delivered` row is deleted iff the verified origin holds custody of it (a `follow_delivery_homes` row — any of its delivering homes, by identity, never by address; § Decision 3, *custody*, rule 3), and the row goes whole, its custody rows with it; any other row keeps the existing paired `post_forward` gate — then `delete_post_core` with author-check-2 (the stored author is the tombstone's) and `RenderSite::Now`; `post_was_deleted` keeps a replayed delivery from any home from restoring it. One tombstone door, two custodies.

**Unsubscribe and removal.** What a leave *is* — the subscriber's leave of the tier the intent names and of every tier held above it, the free `followers` tier's leave being the unfollow, a designated tier never in the set — is ruled by [`subscriptions.md`](subscriptions.md) § The free followers tier — follow and unfollow (2026-11-07); this decision owns the legs. The intent of `kind = unsubscribe` rides `fauna.federation.follow.unsubscribe` naming the tier; the home passes that tier to `unsubscribe_core` as the leave's floor and enqueues a `kind='unsubscribe'` row per tier left (the drain commits each by rotation — `subscriptions.md` § Pillar 1 → Flows → Unsubscribe); the follower's nest marks its own `remote_follows` rows by name on the home's answer or an unreachable home, never on its refusal (every row of the author for `followers`, the named row for a paid tier); a leave of a follow still pending withdraws the home's queued request, and each commit's `follow_state removed` marks the rest; the author's `subscribers.remove` ends the same way, and so does the removal the home's entitlement engine queues when a buyer's paid window is voided or lapses ([`monetization.md`](monetization.md) § Pillar 3 → *Expiry & revocation*). Deliveries to that home stop the moment the roster has no subscriber homed there (§ Decision 1's discard), and the purge removes the envelopes once no local reader follows the author.

**Succession.** The author succeeds (B's identity X → X′): B's subscription plane moves as the one deferred coupled family `succession-aftermath.md` § Re-key scope rules, the home columns riding with the rows, and B's `fauna.federation.succession.push` now also reaches every distinct follower home on the roster — those nests hold residue about X. A follower's nest accepts it under the anchor rule as any peer does (`succession-propagation.md` § Propagation), and `db/successions.rs::record_peer_succession` re-points `remote_follows.author_id` beside `channel_foreign_members` and the contact edges; stored posts keep their author, which is why the per-reader join reads through `actor_successions` (§ Decision 4). Future `KeyBlob`s are signed by X′ and subscribers accept via the statement, as already ruled. The follower succeeds (A's S → S′): A's own `fauna.recovery.succession.submit` re-points `remote_follows.subscriber_id` as it re-points every local plane, A's succession push reaches B (B holds the roster row — residue), and B's `record_peer_succession` re-points `subscribers` and `subscribe_requests` rows **whose `home_nest_id` is not NULL** (a local subscriber's succession never arrives by push; only the client-facing door supersedes a local account) — after which the author's next rotation wraps to S′ (`succession-propagation.md` § Propagation, the *Subscriptions* bullet). A subscriber who moves home nests re-follows from the new home; carrying a follow through a migration is the linked-nests plane's question ([`succession-linked-nests.md`](succession-linked-nests.md)), not this doc's.

## Anti-patterns

- **A nest-wide "followed authors" read.** Delivery is per follower; a row that lists to every local reader is the discovery feed's model, not this one. `FeedLocality::NestWide` is the default of every read that is not the account's own.
- **Serving a `KeyBlob` on a public route, or caching one on the follower's nest.** It is a roster.
- **A second delivery kind for backfill, or a pull arm "just for catch-up".** One queue, one kind, one direction.
- **A nest signing a follow for its user.** The intent is the user's; the nest carries and binds it.
- **Re-forwarding or bridging a delivered envelope.** It is the home's post; only the home fans out.
