## Scheduling & invitations (iTIP / iMIP, server-side auto-schedule)

*Only the child below moved here; the parent's other children stay in [`caldav-server.md`](caldav-server.md) § Scheduling & invitations.*

### Who may mutate an existing event over the inbound rail

**Ruling (2026-09-20).** The sealed scheduling rail is stranger-reachable on purpose (anyone may invite you), so *delivery* proves nothing about *authority*. The rule has two halves. **Creation stays open:** a `REQUEST` naming a UID the recipient has never seen materialises into `Personal` — the designed stranger reach, unchanged. **Mutation is the organizer's alone:** a `CANCEL`, or a `REQUEST` whose UID hits an existing event, is applied **only** when it comes from the principal that event is bound to; a `REPLY` only when it comes from the rostered attendee it speaks for. A message that fails the test is **refused — never applied, never silently discarded**: the stored event is left byte-for-byte untouched and the refusal is surfaced to the user (below). This is the inbound mirror of the outbound rule that only the event's own organizer cancels (§ Implementation status today).

**The authenticated principal is the pair (record author, channel home nest) — not the MLS sender, and never anything inside the `.ics`.** The *record author* is the actor the channel's home nest authenticated as the poster of that record: the WS-RPC caller for a Fauna app, the `on_behalf_of_actor`-scoped organizer for the MDA gateway. The home nest persists it beside the record and serves it on `channel.fetch` (and relays it on `fauna.federation.channel.fetch`) as an additive per-record field. The *channel home nest* is the routing datum the recipient's **own** nest stamps from the handshake-verified federation peer (blank = the recipient's own nest) — never a sender-declared URL. The MLS sender is unusable because the gateway signs with a per-delivery ephemeral identity by design (above); the `ORGANIZER` line is unusable because every co-attendee — the very party this rule exists to stop — holds it verbatim, which is also why comparing the message's `ORGANIZER` against the stored one is **not** an accepted fallback at any tier. Trust base, stated plainly: an author attestation is only as honest as the nest making it, so the binding always carries the nest — a hostile nest can attest any actor id, but only for channels homed on itself, and an event bound to `(organizer, their nest)` is not reachable from there. The organizer's own home nest can forge its organizer; it can equally re-point that organizer's handle, so this adds no trust the address book did not already extend.

**Binding at creation, comparison at mutation.** When a `REQUEST` creates an event, the client records the creating record's `(author, home nest)` in the event's sealed Fauna sidecar (`FaunaEventExt`, additive fields — never served to a MUA). Every later `CANCEL` / updating `REQUEST` for that UID must carry the identical pair, and must not change the `ORGANIZER` line. The comparison is local: no lookup, works offline, and cannot lose a legitimate `CANCEL` to a discovery outage. **At creation only**, the client additionally resolves the `ORGANIZER` CAL-ADDRESS to an actor over the same anon `by_handle` discovery the attendee resolver uses, and refuses the `REQUEST` when the answer is **definitively a different actor** (a spoofed organizer, and the UID-squat where a co-attendee plants the event ahead of the real invite); no answer — an unreachable or non-Fauna domain — binds on first use. **An event with no binding** (created locally, PUT by a MUA, imported, or stored before this rule) falls back to resolution at mutation time: the stored `ORGANIZER` must resolve to exactly the message's `(author, home nest)`, and *no answer means refuse*. Consequently an event whose organizer is not a Fauna actor can never be mutated over this rail — correct, because such an organizer cannot reach it: their scheduling traffic is iMIP email. A `REPLY` is checked the same way against each `ATTENDEE` it speaks for — by resolution, *no answer means refuse* — in addition to the existing roster bound. **The `REPLY` check is lookup-gated and roster-bounded (ruled 2026-09-25):** the stored event is located by UID *before* any address is resolved, and only the `ATTENDEE` lines its stored roster already carries are resolved — the ones the roster merge can act on. Every resolution dials the address's own host (on web, a browser `WebSocket` carrying that origin's cookies) and the rail is stranger-reachable, so resolving an address the merge would ignore adds no authority and hands the sender a free choice of the host the seat dials. **A `REPLY` that can change nothing is the no-op it is, not a refusal:** an unknown UID (`NoMatchingEvent`), or one whose on-roster answers are absent or already recorded — every `ATTENDEE` off the roster included (`NoMatchingAttendee`) — resolves no address and writes no refused-change row (below), because no change was made *or could have been*: the refused-change surface reports attempts to alter the user's calendar, and a line the merge ignores is not one. (The organizer's copy carries no per-attendee principal to compare against locally: the `attendee_nest_urls` sidecar records where a mailbox-less invite was *delivered*, a URL, not *who* received it.) **The recipient's own nest is recognised by identity, never by URL.** A resolved principal whose answering nest is the recipient's own must carry the blank home the rail reports for a same-nest channel; the client decides "this is my nest" by comparing the answering nest's `fauna.nest.info` `nest_id` (hop 2 of the discovery chain, [`federation.md`](../architecture/federation.md) § Peer-auth model) with its own nest's, so a client reaching its nest by a LAN address or a second name still matches. Either identity unknown is not a match — the fail-closed side. **No author on the record** (an older home nest, or a record written before the nest stored authors) is *no answer*: creation still works, mutation is refused and surfaced — the fail-closed side of within-major compatibility, healed by the nest upgrade alone.

**A succeeded organizer — the binding names a verified line, not one key (ruled 2026-09-21).** An identity succession ([`identity-succession.md`](identity-succession.md)) retires the organizer's actor id, and the binding is sealed inside each *invitee's* sidecar under the invitee's own key, so no ceremony and no nest can re-point it: under a bare equality rule a succeeded organizer could never again cancel or update an event on anyone's calendar, and every invitee would keep a stranded event. That failure is the safe direction — a comparand that can only match a retired identity admits no one — but it is a stranded state, so the rule is widened by exactly one step. **When the plain comparison misses on a *bound* event, and the message's home nest still equals the bound nest, the client asks whether the bound identity has verifiably succeeded to the message's author, and applies the message only on a yes.** Four properties are load-bearing. *(1) The lookup runs only after the local comparison has missed*, so the ordinary case keeps every property of the paragraph above — local, no lookup, offline — and a lookup outage costs nothing that was not already refused. *(2) The anchor is fetched, never delivered:* the signed statements come from `fauna.recovery.succession.lookup` dialed at the **bound** `organizer_home_nest_url` (blank = the recipient's own nest) — the old identity's home nest, which is where [`identity-succession.md`](identity-succession.md) § The succession statement sends every consumer — and never from any URL, chain or statement the inbound message carries, because a chain delivered by the party asserting the succession proves seed possession and nothing more. Each hop is verified against that identity's registration chain (`recovery_sig` under the registered RecoveryKey, which a seed thief does not hold; `new_sig` under the successor). **Anchor grade, stated:** the resolver hands the walk the chain head the account already holds for the bound identity (the `fauna.state.peer-anchors` kind's chain-head rows, the store the in-group succession witness fills), so a chain that rewrites or truncates what this account has seen is refused; where none is held the walk is first-contact (TOFU) grade — the floor that section already concedes, and strictly harder for a thief than for the owner. A store that cannot be read leaves "is a head held?" unknown, and the answer to unknown is *no answer*, never a walk quietly downgraded to first-contact grade. **The lookup is bounded, and a dial that settles nothing is paid once per bound identity per bound nest, per session:** the dial and the walk share one round-trip budget (the succession witness's, 15 s), because the apply runs inline in the inbound drain; and for each `(bound identity, bound nest)` a verified successor is remembered for the rest of the session, as is a dial that settled nothing — the bound nest unreachable or timed out, or a walk that failed there (a transport error, or a chain that fails the rule) — so an unreachable or hostile bound nest costs that budget once per distinct identity it organized for, and every later message for that identity meets the refusal that was already standing with no dial. A relaunch asks again. A chain that fails the rule is remembered with the unreachable class, not re-asked: an honest bound nest never serves one, so no third party can make the recipient ask it, and asking a nest that does again buys nothing. **A definitive "never succeeded" is not remembered — it is asked again by the next message (ruled 2026-09-24).** It is true only *so far*: any account on the bound nest can make the recipient ask it, with one message for the event's UID, before a succession lands, and a refusal is terminal (the rail never re-applies a refused message, and its cursor has already moved past it), so remembering it would refuse the successor's genuine `CANCEL` or update for the rest of the session and strand the very event this paragraph exists to save. Re-asking a nest that answered costs nothing the rail does not already pay that nest every tick (the first reason below), so a succession that lands mid-session is seen by the next message that asks. A lookup that made no dial (the config unreadable) is not remembered either: it saved nothing, and the next message may find the config readable. **The inline residual is ruled acceptable — the lookup stays in the drain, and no park is built (ruled 2026-09-23).** What the memo leaves is the first cold lookup for each distinct bound identity per bound nest, per session — and a fresh lookup for each message whose bound nest last answered "never succeeded" — running inline in the one receive loop that drains chat, mail, scheduling and folders in sequence, so a slow or hostile bound nest holds the other rails for up to that budget once per such identity — or once per message, for one that answers "never succeeded" just inside the budget, which the first reason prices exactly as it does the cold lookup. That is accepted rather than moved off the loop, for three reasons. *First, it is strictly smaller than the exposure the same rail already carries to the same nest:* every ordinary tick fetches each foreign-homed scheduling channel through the account's own nest, which relays to the channel's home under the federation originate deadline (30 s, `federation_pool::ORIGINATE_DEADLINE`) — per channel, every tick, with no memo — so a bound nest that can stall the lookup once can stall the fetch on every tick, and parking the lookup buys the loop nothing the fetch does not take back. *Second, it trips no dead-rail detector:* web's pass ceiling is ten minutes (`RECEIVE_PASS_CEILING_MS`; the projection is [`ui/conversations.md`](../ui/conversations.md) § Errors & edge cases) and the native loop bounds no pass, so a cold lookup is a delay, never a reported stall. *Third, a park would trade a bounded delay for an unbounded loss:* the scheduling cursor is written to the durable cross-device watermark *before* the apply, so a mutation parked off the loop and lost at relaunch is never refetched — a lost `CANCEL` leaves a stale event with no refused-change record, silently, the exact outcome the refuse-and-surface rule exists to prevent — and making the park durable is a new sealed plane plus exactly-once re-apply semantics, bought to shave a delay the fetch already dwarfs. **Revisit trigger, stated:** only a loop that runs its rails concurrently changes this (a track of its own, none minted), and under that shape the lookup leaves the other rails for free while staying inline in the scheduling drain; nothing about the lookup itself is the reason to build one. *(3) Only the actor-id half is resolved.* The nest half must still equal the binding, since the ceremony runs only on the identity's home nest and an attestation is only as honest as the nest making it; a successor speaking from any other nest is refused **without** a lookup. *(4) Only where the account ended up is admitted* — the final verified successor, never an intermediate hop, which is itself a retired identity. **The theft case.** A seed thief cannot produce `recovery_sig`, so cannot be verified as the successor; and once the owner's recovery ceremony lands, the organizer's home nest refuses the old key's writes, so the thief can no longer be *attested* as the bound identity either — the recovering identity can cancel and the thief cannot. Before the ceremony lands the thief simply *is* the organizer to every system, here as everywhere. **Re-binding.** An updating `REQUEST` admitted this way is a Fauna-app write, so it replaces the sidecar's binding with `(successor, same nest)` and carries every other sidecar field over unchanged — the line is walked once, later mutations compare locally again, and from that point the retired id no longer matches on this device even if some nest were to attest it. A `CANCEL` needs none (the event is deleted). An *unbound* event is untouched by this paragraph: its mutation-time resolution already asks discovery who the `ORGANIZER` address names **today** — whoever holds that handle now — so it has no line to walk. A platform whose resolver has no lookup answers *no answer* and refuses exactly as before.

**UID secrecy is not a control.** Fauna-minted UIDs are random, but every attendee, every mail system an iMIP crossed, and every forward holds them, and imported events carry whatever UID their source chose (web's `.ics` import mints derivable `import-<ts>-<idx>@fauna-web` UIDs). Nothing in this rule depends on a UID being unguessable.

**Surfacing.** A refused message produces a client-side *refused scheduling change* record — method, UID, the event's title, the refused author named by the pair (a handle this device knows only when the nest that attested the author may vouch for it, otherwise a short actor id and that nest; the naming rule and its approximation of the pair are [`../ui/events.md`](../ui/events.md) § Refused scheduling changes; a mailed `REPLY` refused under *The mail rail* holds no actor and names the door-authenticated address instead, in the row's additive `sender_address`, which stands in for the author in the row's key and per-author ceiling), the reason — listed on the events surface so the user learns that someone tried to change their calendar; the drain logs it at `warn`. The record is informational: there is deliberately no "apply anyway" affordance (a user cannot evaluate an authority question the client could not). Element IDs are `ui.yaml`'s to assign when the list is built (§ Implementation status today).

**Where the record rests, and why it is account state rather than device state (built 2026-09-22; on the account plane since 2026-09-30).** The account plane's `fauna.state.refused-scheduling-changes` kind — one fleet-only, generation-tip-sealed row per account, synced to every device by the plane ([`../architecture/config-dissolution.md`](../architecture/config-dissolution.md) owns the kind's birth and its implementation status; the row shape, the ceilings and the merge rule are `fauna_core::data::RefusedSchedulingChange`'s and `RefusedSchedulingChanges`'). Until 2026-09-30 it was `UserConfig.refused_scheduling_changes`, a `__config` field; the field retired with the `__config` rail on 2026-10-02 (that doc's § The `__config` dissolution schedule → *The closure order*, step (6)). **Per-device storage would have been wrong, and not marginally:** a sealed delivery is a one-off MLS group built from *one* of the recipient's key packages, so exactly one device ever drains a given message and refuses it, while the change that message tried to make — had it been allowed — would have appeared on every device, the calendar being account state. Storing the notice where the refusal happened would show it on whichever device held that key package and nowhere else. The principal this surface protects is the user, not a device, the same reasoning `peer_chain_heads` states for its own home. **Retention:** one row per `(uid_hash, author, method, reason)`, so repeat attempts on one event collapse onto a count rather than stacking rows (which also makes a re-drain of the same record a no-op — the inbound cursor is per-session); at most 3 rows per author and 20 in all, evicting the **oldest**. That last choice is deliberately the opposite of the anchor vectors' refuse-rather-than-evict rule, and the asymmetry is the point: an anchor is a comparand a later check reads, so losing an old one silently weakens that check, whereas this is a report to the user, whose failure mode is a surface gone blind — keep-the-oldest would let one flood fill the list permanently and hide every later attempt, including a different attacker's. **Bytes are bounded as well as rows:** every string a row carries has a byte ceiling — the title 256 bytes, cut on a character boundary (on a refused creating `REQUEST` it is the stranger's own `SUMMARY`), each token field 64, the home-nest URL 512 — and an `author` that is not a 64-hex actor id rests as no author at all, so the whole list adds at most 32 KiB to the plane — the budget the kind's row is sized to (that doc's kinds table; on the retired `__config` blob it was about 3% of the 1 MiB cap). Unbounded, one stranger message could pin the row against its cap and every later growing write would be refused, a client-unrecoverable state ([`../architecture/nest/common.md`](../architecture/nest/common.md) § Client-state recoverability). The bound holds on record, on merge and on load, so a row planted before it existed is trimmed by the next write of anything. **Dismissal** records the attempt count it answered (`dismissed_through`), so a later attempt re-opens the row. **Two devices' copies of one row merge lexicographically on the last attempt (2026-09-30):** the copy that saw the later attempt wins whole, and only copies of the same last attempt join field by field — the dismissal most-restrictive-wins, as the adjudication planes rule, and every count as a join, never a sum. So a copy that saw no attempt after a dismissal can never undo it, and a later attempt re-opens the row on every device (even one whose count had outrun the dismissing device's); what the rule gives up is the count's history across a concurrent attempt, a lower bound as it always was. It is the rule under which the capped merge is associative, so three devices converge whatever order they exchange rows in: with a field-wise join, a row the ceiling cut and a newer attempt brought back would read differently by merge order.

**Where it lives.** One implementation in shared Rust: the drain (`fauna-conversations`) hands the sink the record's attested author and the channel's home nest alongside the iMIP; `fauna-client-caldav`'s inbound apply enforces the rule; both `SchedulingSink` implementors (native, web) only pass the values through and supply their platform's resolver — addresses through the one shared `fauna-client-caldav` resolver over anon `by_handle` discovery, whose one platform-divergent piece is the anonymous dial; the verified walk it runs for a succession is `fauna-client-recovery`'s, the same one the succession witness and the launch-path check run. No app carries any of it.

**The mail rail (ruled 2026-09-26).** A `REPLY` that arrives as **email** answers to the same sentence — applied only when it comes from the rostered attendee it speaks for — but a sealed INBOX record hands the client nothing but the raw RFC 5322 bytes: no attested author, no SPF/DKIM/DMARC verdict (those rest nest-side in the record's floor and never reach the wire), and a `From:` line any sender chooses. So the *delivery door* — the one party that authenticated the sender — leaves that fact inside the sealed copy as the reserved delivery stamp `X-Fauna-Authenticated-Sender: <addr-spec>` (`libs/fauna-mail/src/sender_auth.rs`), trustworthy for exactly the reason `X-Fauna-Spam-Threshold` is: every filing door strips the `X-Fauna-*` namespace from bytes it did not compose before writing its own stamps ([`smtp-server.md`](smtp-server.md) § Architectural rules → *The `X-Fauna-*` namespace*, which owns which door writes which stamp). **Each door stamps only the address it verified:** the nest's `fauna.email.send` the handle-gated in-domain `From:` (nothing on the off-domain bypass); the MTA's submission door the envelope sender it validated as owned by the authenticated actor (never the `From:` header, which that door does not check); the MTA's MX door the `From:` addr-spec **only under a DMARC pass** (the domain owner vouches for the mailbox — the trust every iMIP-interop calendar server extends; an ARC-only forward or a DMARC none/fail stamps nothing); the bridge-presented in-domain partition the validated envelope sender the authenticated bridge presents; IMAP `APPEND` and the migration import stamp nothing. **The client rule:** a mailed `REPLY` is applied only for the on-roster `ATTENDEE` whose address equals the stamped address (case-insensitive, `mailto:` stripped); a stamp naming anyone else is refused `not_the_attendee`, and **no stamp is no answer, and no answer refuses** (`sender_unauthenticated`) — the mail-rail twin of `NoAttestedAuthor`. The order is the sealed rail's: locate by UID first, roster-bound second (a `REPLY` that would change nothing is the no-op it is, never a refusal), authorize third, write last. A copy the app's own scorer files Junk is never merged (invitation rule 3; MTA-filed junk never reaches the INBOX poll). **No organizer check** on purpose: the sealed rail has none, and once a `REPLY` is bound to the responder's own line the only thing it can change on a non-organizer's copy is that responder's own answer. The refusal is surfaced as the same record (*Surfacing*), naming the authenticated address in place of an actor. **Compat:** the stamp is additive inside the sealed body — an older app ignores it, and a newer app reading a copy filed by a door that does not stamp refuses and surfaces the `REPLY`, the safe direction.

## Implementation status today

- **A succeeded organizer (§ Who may mutate an existing event over the inbound rail → *A succeeded organizer*) — landed on all 7 apps (2026-09-21).** `fauna-client-caldav`'s `authorize_organizer_mutation` asks `PrincipalResolver::resolve_successor` only after the local comparison misses on a bound event whose nest half still matches, admits only the final verified successor, and re-binds an admitted update — pinned by `a_succeeded_organizers_cancel_is_honoured_only_through_a_verified_succession`, `a_succession_admits_only_the_successor_on_the_bound_nest`, `the_bound_organizers_own_cancel_never_asks_for_a_succession` and `a_successors_update_rebinds_the_event_to_the_successor`. **Both shipped resolvers perform the verified walk.** Native (`fauna-client-conversations`' resolver, constructed by `NestSchedulingSink` — tui and linux directly, the four UniFFI apps through the `fauna-ffi` session factory) dials the bound nest through `fauna_client_recovery::witness::walk_at_nest_url`, the succession witness's own anonymous dial and 15 s budget; web (`fauna-wasm`'s `WebOrganizerResolver`) does the same through its wasm twin in `succession_witness.rs`. Both hand the walk the account's held `peer_chain_heads` entry and answer *no answer* when the config cannot be read. **Once per bound identity per bound nest, per session (2026-09-23):** both platforms' dials (`NativeOrganizerSuccessionDialer`, `WebOrganizerSuccessionDialer`) sit behind the one `fauna-client-caldav` `MemoizedSuccessionResolver`, keyed `(bound actor, anchor)` over a `SuccessionMemo` the session owns — natively `NestSchedulingSink`'s, on web the manager's beside `scheduling_cursors` — remembering verified and dialed-but-unsettled answers and never a no-dial one; pinned by the crate's `a_strangers_repeated_cancels_pay_one_succession_dial_per_session` and the `organizer_succession` unit tests. **A definitive "never succeeded" is re-asked (2026-09-24):** the shared walk returns `fauna_client_recovery::AnchoredWalk` (`Succeeded` / `NeverSucceeded` / `Unsettled`) from both `walk_at_nest_url` twins, both dialers map `NeverSucceeded` to `SuccessionLookup::NotSucceeded`, and the memo never stores it; pinned by `a_strangers_cancel_before_the_succession_does_not_pin_it_for_the_session` (the crate), `the_anchored_walk_tells_never_succeeded_from_a_walk_that_settled_nothing` (`fauna-client-recovery`'s `ceremonies.rs`) and the production-sink case below, whose stranger's `CANCEL` now also lands before the succession. The lookup still runs inline in the drain: a cold lookup on a slow bound nest holds the account's receive loop for up to the budget, once per identity per session.
