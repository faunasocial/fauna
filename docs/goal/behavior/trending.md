## Goal

A user opens the built-in **Trending** feed and sees public posts with unusually high *recent* engagement — from this nest and from the peer nests it talks to — ordered by a transparent velocity score. A post trends because real local users engaged with it recently and/or because several distinct chosen peer nests independently report it trending. The signal decays in hours, is computed entirely from metadata the nest already legitimately holds (public engagement counters + timestamps — never content), and crosses nest boundaries only as k-anonymized `(content-id, factor, score)` aggregates.

**Trending is a signal, not a feed mechanism** (the frame's one-operation rule): the factor is an ordinary bus factor any composition can weight; the "Trending" surface is just a built-in composition dominated by it.

## The factor — `trending`, a nest-computed metadata-only tier-3 scorer

- **Factor key:** `trending` (bare built-in key, like `engagement`). Rows are `ScoreEntry { factor: "trending", score: <per-mille>, tier: 3, scorer_version }` in `content_scores`, `actor_id = NULL` (owner-scope semantics per the List-labeler precedent — public posts have no at-rest owner scope; feed composition JOINs by `content_id`).
- **Placement: the nest itself is the scorer — legitimately.** Inputs are the *metadata* class only (`content-scoring.md` § Input classes, row 1: prior scores, floor fields, timestamps, counts — portable everywhere): the explicit-engagement event log + public counters + `created_at`. No content is read, so "the nest never runs a *content* scorer" is untouched. This is the `report:spam` shape: the nest rewrites the rows on transitions, and the factor stays **out of `model_versions`** (no capability-holder drains it; a curve bump recomputes nest-side, `scorer_version` bumps on any curve change).
- **Public posts only.** Trending scores posts whose audience class is public (`restricted-posts.md` § Encryption at rest). A restricted post never gets a `trending` row and never appears in any export — its engagement is not the network's business. Mail is out of scope by construction (trending is a posts factor).

### The `engagement` factor — trending's decay-free sibling (and the pre-frame monolith's replacement)

`trending` (decayed velocity) and `engagement` (cumulative popularity) are the **pair** the moderation/ranking frame's decomposition split out of the retired pre-frame monolith. Documented here (delegated by `engagement-cues.md` § Retirement) so the two nest-computed metadata scalars are defined together; placement is owned by `../architecture/content-scoring.md`, increment semantics by `../architecture/core-client-kind-catalog.md` § Labels & Engagement.

- **`engagement` = the all-time cumulative signal**, `trending` = the recent-rate signal. The Local-velocity note below ("a month-old post with 500 likes is not trending") is exactly the boundary: those 500 likes *are* that post's `engagement`, they are simply not its *velocity*.
- **Formula — decay-free saturation of the same weighted acts:**
  ```text
  e = Σ w(kind) · count(kind)      over the content_meta explicit-act counters (like/reply/repost/quote)
  w: like = 1.0, reply = 1.5, repost = 2.0, quote = 2.0        (the SAME shared weight table as velocity; views excluded)
  engagement_score = e / (e + ENGAGEMENT_SATURATION)           ∈ [0, 1); 0.5 at e = ENGAGEMENT_SATURATION
  ```
  No time-decay term (that is `trending`) and no age input at all: it is a pure function of the current counts, so it changes only when a count changes — recomputed at the single counter choke point (`db::engagement::increment/decrement_engagement_count`), where it cannot drift, and it needs no periodic sweep (unlike `trending`, whose value drifts with wall-clock).
- **Placement is a plain `content_meta.score` scalar in `[0, 1)`, NOT a `content_scores` bus row** (frame § Composition — "the single nest-computed recency/engagement score becomes one factor among many"; `factor::ENGAGEMENT`). The composed feed reads it straight from `content_meta` and multiplies by the caller's weight (`db/feeds.rs`), mixing it with per-mille bus factors on one scale. Because it never crosses a wire, it stays an f64 (no dag-cbor per-mille integer needed). Like `trending`, it is deliberately **out of `model_versions`** (no bus rows → no re-score obligation).
- **Constants, hard-coded Rust (bucket 1 — no human chooses them):** `ENGAGEMENT_SATURATION = 50`, in `fauna_core::scoring::engagement` beside `scoring::trends` (they share `event_weight_milli`). Refutable — sized so an ordinarily-popular post lands mid-scale; distinct from `TREND_SATURATION_V` (which saturates a decayed velocity, not a cumulative count).
- **This is the frame's decomposition of the retired `fauna_core::score::compute_score` monolith** (deleted 2026-07-13): its freshness-decay term became `trending`; its label-confidence + sender-trust terms became separate composition bus factors (`report:spam`, `labeler:<hex>`, …, each weighted on its own axis); its personalization term became a sealed tier-1 client-side factor (`topic-factors.md`). Only the engagement-counts term legitimately remained a nest metadata scalar — this. The monolith had **no production caller** since the client-dead `fauna.engagement.record` was deleted (frame D9, `engagement-cues.md` § Retirement); what actually filled `content_meta.score` was a flat `+1.0` per like (mis-scaled — unbounded, and blind to reply/repost/quote), now replaced by the above. This is a behavior change to the shipped `engagement` feed-sort factor: it now means all-time weighted engagement, correctly `[0,1)`-scaled, instead of a raw like-count.

### Local velocity

Trending is *rate*, not cumulative popularity — a month-old post with 500 likes is not trending; a post with 12 likes in the last hour is:

```text
v(now) = Σ over qualifying explicit events e:  w(e.kind) · 2^(−(now − e.at) / TREND_HALF_LIFE)
w: like = 1.0, reply = 1.5, repost = 2.0, quote = 2.0        (views deliberately excluded in v1)
local_pm(v) = round(1000 · v / (v + TREND_SATURATION_V))      (saturating; v = TREND_SATURATION_V → 500‰)
```

- **Constants, hard-coded Rust (bucket 1 — no human chooses them):** `TREND_HALF_LIFE_HOURS = 6`, `TREND_SATURATION_V = 20`, in `libs/fauna-core` beside `scoring::ScoreEntry` (the `reports` curve's sibling module, `fauna_core::scoring::trends`), so apps can render the same numbers a nest computes.
- **Inputs are the explicit-act event log** (`engagement_events` rows owned by `fauna.posts.interact` / `fauna.posts.create` — `core-client-kind-catalog.md` § Labels & Engagement). These are explicit public acts whose aggregate counters are already served to every feed reader; no implicit cue joins the v1 input. (v2 MAY weight the k-anonymized `signal:*` aggregates in — owner: `engagement-cues.md`; that lands as a curve change, `scorer_version` bump, no wire change.)
- **Recompute:** on engagement transitions for the affected post, plus a periodic sweep (interval hard-coded, ~15 min) that re-decays live rows and withdraws rows fallen to 0‰. Pure content-free metadata upserts.

### The peer term — the bounded distinct-peer ramp (frame carve-out D7)

```text
peer_pm(n) = min(TREND_PEER_CAP_PM, floor(100 · log2(1 + n)))   n = distinct peers with a live trend entry for this content
score      = min(1000, local_pm + peer_pm)                       TREND_PEER_CAP_PM = 300
```

- **Presence-only per peer.** A peer's claimed velocity buys nothing (hostile-signer rule: magnitudes from self-minted identities are worthless); each distinct peer contributes exactly one presence bit. The imported `score_pm` is kept only as a **fetch-prioritization hint** (§ Import), never summed.
- **Distinct peers ramp, capped.** n = 1 → 100‰, n = 3 → 200‰, n ≥ 7 → 300‰ (the cap). This is the frame's **scoped exception** (D7, ratified 2026-07-12) to the flat single-bucket rule: trending is a promote-only factor into a feed the user chose (informs-never-compels), so the stakes justify a ramp — while reports, which can pin a victim, keep the flat bucket verbatim. The sybil cost is real: buckets count only peers **this nest already talks to** (§ Exchange — there is no push-from-stranger surface), so an attacker must first get n distinct nest identities into the deployment's curated peer set.
- **Peer-only ceiling stays far below saturation:** with zero local engagement a fully peer-corroborated post tops out at 300‰ — visible in a trending-dominated feed, but any locally-engaged post outranks it.

## Federation exchange — `fauna.federation.trends.{exchange,export}`

Two nest-to-nest kinds mirroring the reports pair (`report-sharing.md` § Federation exchange): nest-signature channel auth, open-federation (not `is_paired`-gated), per-origin throttled.

| Kind | Payload | Semantics |
|---|---|---|
| `fauna.federation.trends.exchange` | `{ epoch, entries: [{ content_id (hex CID), score_pm (u16), engager_count (u32) }] }` → `{ imported }` | a peer pushes its k-gate-passed local trend entries. Cap `MAX_TREND_EXCHANGE_ENTRIES = 256`. Upsert into `peer_content_trends` keyed `(content_id, peer_nest_id)` — latest epoch wins per peer; recomputes affected `trending` rows. Importer re-validates the k-gate on the claimed `engager_count` (an entry the exporter's own gate would withhold is not corroboration) and skips non-public / unfetchable ids. |
| `fauna.federation.trends.export` | `{}` → `{ entries }` | serves **this nest's local trend entries only** — each backed by ≥ `TREND_MIN_ENGAGERS` distinct local engagers (the k-gate) and a live `local_pm > 0`. Highest `local_pm` first, bounded to the entry cap. **No laundering:** the export never includes peer-derived terms — `score_pm` on the wire is the exporter's `local_pm`, so a hostile aggregate cannot wash through an honest intermediary. |

- **The k-anonymity gate:** `TREND_MIN_ENGAGERS: u32 = 3` — hard-coded shared-Rust safety constant (sibling of `REPORT_MIN_REPORTERS`; deliberately not a knob), with one gate function `exposed_trend_entry(distinct_local_engagers) -> Option<()>` that **every export path routes through**. What an export discloses is "≥3 distinct users on this nest engaged with this public post recently" — strictly *less* than what serving the post already discloses (the interaction bar's public per-post counters), minus recency granularity; below k, a small nest's engagement pattern never crosses any wire. **Local rows are deliberately NOT k-gated** (unlike reports): the underlying counters are already public per-post on this nest, so withholding the local row would protect nothing the interaction bar doesn't already show — the k-gate exists for the *cross-boundary* disclosure ("this nest's users engaged"), which is new information.
- **Peer storage is bounded + ephemeral:** `peer_content_trends` carries a per-peer row cap (`MAX_PEER_TREND_ROWS = 1024`, oldest-updated evicted) and an age TTL (`TREND_PEER_TTL_HOURS = 48` — a trend entry older than the decay horizon is dead weight); expiry withdraws the peer's presence bit and recomputes. The table is ephemeral/derived by construction (re-populated by the next exchange) — recreatable, never migrated.
- **Cadence** rides the federation exchange originator plane (the same scheduler that originates report exchange — see `../architecture/federation.md`; the originator worker is part of this build since none exists today for any exchange pair). Trends push on local aggregate transitions (throttled) + the periodic tick; peers = the nests this deployment already talks to (paired nests, discovery contributor nests, prior exchange partners).

### Import-triggered public-post fetch

A trending entry for a post this nest has never seen is useless — feed composition JOINs `content_scores` on ids present in `content_meta` (the List-labeler gate: never a blind row for unseen content). So an import schedules a **bounded fetch** of unseen ids: `fauna.federation.post.get` toward the exporting peer, cap `MAX_TREND_FETCHES_PER_EXCHANGE = 64`, ordered by the wire `score_pm` hint, public posts only (content-bound — `blake3` of the fetched bytes must equal the requested id, the id a native post rests under — and signature-verified, then ingested through the normal post-index path exactly like a discovery-poller candidate — `bins/fauna-nest/src/discovery.rs` ingestion precedent). A fetch failure just drops that id; the presence row waits until the post exists. An id this nest has seen deleted is not unseen: it is neither fetched nor ingested ([`../ui/feed.md`](../ui/feed.md) § State & data shape → *Post deletion*).

## The Trending feed — a built-in virtual feed

- **Read:** `fauna.feed.trending.posts` — a sibling of `fauna.feed.local.posts` (the existing virtual-feed precedent; no feed row, works out of the box with zero configuration). Semantics: `query_feed_scored` over public posts with the implicit composition `[(trending, 1000)]` **plus the caller's global factor set** (so a global mute still sinks a trending post — the frame's arithmetic conflict resolution), compound keyset cursor (`../ui/feed.md` § The read model), `has_more` per the returned cursor.
- **App surface:** a "Trending" pseudo-entry in `feed-selector` above the user's own feeds, driven through `FeedManager` like any selection (`selected_feed` gains a variant for the virtual feed, mirroring the `None` = local-feed convention). Proposed IDs `feed-trending-item` (+ nothing else — the post list, cards, and interaction bar are the ordinary feed surfaces): **ui.yaml ratification with the implementing app slice, per rule A.**
- The factor is also offered in `feed-factor-select` like any bus factor — a user can weight `trending` into their own compositions or globally.
- **Tier-1 composition applies unchanged:** sealed factors (topic models, muted keywords) compose client-side on top of the nest-served key via the existing sealed-compose seam (`topic-factors.md` § Scoring) — trending gets no special rendering path.

## Threat model & accepted limitations

- **Engagement-farming a trend (local).** k real local accounts engaging inflate `local_pm` — same class as the k-anonymity count sybil (accepted, `report-sharing.md` § Threat model): a nest's export is only as trustworthy as that nest, which is why importers grant presence-only ramp buckets, never magnitude.
- **Peer-set infiltration (the ramp's cost).** n colluding nests inside the deployment's peer set buy at most `TREND_PEER_CAP_PM = 300‰` of promote — bounded, promote-only, and composable away (any user can weight `trending` to 0 or negative). The frame's flat-bucket rule stands for every harm-capable factor.
- **Membership-oracle residual (accepted).** An exported entry confirms ≥k users of this nest engaged with a public post recently — the disclosure *is* the product, bounded to public content and aggregate counts, sibling of the report-sharing residual.
- **Recency oracle.** Exports reveal *when* a nest's users are active at ~exchange-cadence granularity. Bounded by cadence throttling; accepted.
- **No global view, by design.** Each nest sees only its chosen peers' trends (no central registry). "Trending" is trending-among-your-corner-of-the-network — the honest federated reading, stated in the UI copy, not worked around.
- **Stale-trending window after a post goes public→gated (≤ one sweep interval, ~15 min; accepted, refutable).** `recompute_trend_score_locked` fires on an engagement transition or the ~15-min sweep. The **post-delete half is now closed** (`delete_post_core` recomputes immediately on delete, so a deleted post's stale row is withdrawn at once — 2026-07-15), but there is still **no immediate-withdraw hook on a post going public→gated** — so a now-gated post keeps its stale public `trending` signal until the next sweep re-probes `content_meta` and withdraws it (a DELETE, no data loss). Low-sensitivity metadata; sharper only if a future slice makes audience mutable post-publish, in which case add an explicit recompute on that path (the same one-line call-site fix the delete path used).
- **O(N²) recompute amplification under a viral/attacked post (accepted, perf, refutable).** The per-engagement transition hook rescans the post's entire `engagement_events` (`db/trends.rs`) → O(N) per act, so a viral or attacked post amplifies nest work quadratically. Bounded by engagement rate-limiting; consider an incremental accumulator / debounced recompute if it ever bites.

## Implementation status today

**The `trending` factor's local scorer, the federation exchange handlers, the exchange originator legs (incl. the import-triggered `post.get` fetch), and the virtual feed-read are all implemented (through 2026-07-14), and the cross-nest cycle has a two-binary tier_3 test (`test_trending_federation.py`) — which does not witness the fetch leg: its observables are also produced by the discovery poller's index stub for the same id, a `content_meta` row the fetch probes as already seen; the fetch leg's witness is the channel conformance test named below.** Ratified 2026-07-12 (frame D7). `fauna_core::scoring::trends` (curves + constants + export k-gate) landed 2026-07-13 (Phase 2 slice 1). The **nest trend scorer** landed 2026-07-14 (slice 2): `db::CacheDb::recompute_trend_score_locked` (`bins/fauna-nest/src/db/trends.rs`) scans the explicit-act event log (`engagement_events`) for a public post, computes the decayed local velocity, and upserts/withdraws its `content_scores` row `{factor: "trending", tier: 3, actor_id: NULL, scorer_version::TREND}`. It recomputes at the engagement-transition choke point (inside `db::engagement::insert`/`delete_engagement_event`, so a new act shows up at once — the event log drives `trending` exactly as the counter columns drive `engagement`) and via a ~15-min wall-clock sweep (`trend_sweeper::spawn_trend_sweeper` → `CacheDb::sweep_trend_scores`, re-decaying live rows and withdrawing at 0‰). Public posts only (`content_meta.gated_tier IS NULL`); withdraw is a row DELETE, never a zero row; and — like `report:spam` — deliberately OUT of `model_versions` (a curve change bumps `scorer_version::TREND` and the sweep re-scores nest-side). The **federation exchange handlers + the distinct-peer ramp** landed 2026-07-14 (slice 3): the additive `peer_content_trends` table (schema v18; key `(content_id, peer_nest_id)`, latest-epoch-wins, per-peer cap `MAX_PEER_TREND_ROWS = 1024` + 48 h TTL `TREND_PEER_TTL_HOURS`, ephemeral/derived), `CacheDb::{upsert_peer_trend, export_trend_entries}`, the ramp wired into `recompute_trend_score_locked` (a `COUNT(DISTINCT peer_nest_id)` over live rows fed through `trends::trend_score_pm`, entirely *inside* the public+seen gate so peer presence never mints a blind row), the TTL purge folded into the sweep, and the `fauna.federation.trends.{exchange,export}` handlers + shared `import_trend_entries` helper (`federation_handlers.rs`; k-gate re-validated on the claimed `engager_count`, cap `MAX_TREND_EXCHANGE_ENTRIES = 256`, no laundering — the export serves the exporter's un-composed `local_pm`). Proven: `bins/fauna-nest/src/db/trends.rs` unit tests + the over-the-channel `conformance_federation_channel.rs::trends_exchange_is_presence_only_and_export_never_launders`. The **exchange originator legs + the import-triggered fetch** landed 2026-07-14 (slice 4): `exchange_originator::exchange_with_peer` now carries a trends **push** leg (`export_trend_entries` → `originate_trends_exchange`, empty-skip) and a **pull** leg (`originate_trends_export` → `import_trend_entries`), both swallowing a missing-kind error so an older peer that predates the additive trends kind still completes its reports exchange. The **virtual Trending feed read** landed 2026-07-14 (slice 5): `fauna.feed.trending.posts` — the scored sibling of `fauna.feed.local.posts` (no feed row, works out of the box) — serves public posts ordered by the implicit composition `[(trending, 1000)]` plus the caller's global factor set.
