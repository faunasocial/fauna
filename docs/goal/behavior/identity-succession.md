> **Audience:** every area. Anyone touching authentication, signed content, MLS membership, federation identity, or owner-only sealing needs to know that an actor_id can be *superseded* and what a consumer of the succession statement must honor.
> **Purpose:** the recovery story for the one root that previously had none. Theft of the identity seed — the 32-byte Ed25519 secret that *is* the account — becomes a recoverable event instead of a permanent takeover; total loss of every device becomes recoverable too (via the seed escrow).

- **✅ Built — a lost submit reply no longer destroys the account (2026-08-02).** The nest commits a succession *before* it encodes its reply (`record_succession`'s `tx.commit()` precedes `encode_reply`), so a dropped connection in that gap returned `Err` to a client that had already discarded the only copy of the key the account now belonged to — and since `old_actor_id` is the succession table's primary key, the retry was refused `AlreadySucceeded` forever. Terminal state: account, handle, admin role and data unrecoverable by anyone, with no operator to appeal to. It was **an attack, not a robustness nit, because a thief with network position could trigger it deliberately** — the ceremony runs exactly when someone holds the seed. **The fix is the shape this crate had already ratified one ceremony over** (`kit::put_escrow` maps every failure into `EscrowOutcome::Failed` rather than `Err`, for the identical reason — § Seed escrow): `succeed_with_held_kit` returns `SuccessionAttempt::{Confirmed, Unconfirmed}`, and the `Unconfirmed` arm carries the minted seed, the two actor ids and the failure, so no error path can take the key with it. **The boundary is the submit call, not the mint** — before the submit nothing can have committed, so the seed provably authorizes nothing and a wrong kit keeps its actionable `PriorKitMismatch` instead of becoming an unconfirmed ceremony to explain; **past the submit no classification is attempted at all**, not even for a refusal that looks definitive, because the cost of guessing wrong once is the account. **The answer comes from asking, not from inferring:** `reconcile_succession` walks `succession.lookup`, verifies every hop against the registration chain exactly as `resolve_successor` does, and matches on the successor **the caller holds the key for** — so `Landed` (finish the ceremony as if the reply had arrived, with the verbatim statement the sweep needs), `NotLanded` (nothing committed; the seed is worthless and the kit may be used again) and `LandedForAnother` (a second holder of the same kit won; final, and never presented as "try again") are all the *chain's* verdict, never the nest's say-so. ## Goal

The user's Ed25519 identity seed is deliberately powerful: possession = account access on any device, and every owner-audience key (`BackupKey`, `NestBackupKey`, the MLS identity) derives from or is unlocked by it. Before this design, that power was unbounded in time — no revocation existed at the root, so seed theft was declared unrecoverable (`devices.md` § Security Properties). This doc bounds the root: a **RecoveryKey** held only offline outranks the seed, and an identity can be **succeeded** — re-pointed to a fresh keypair — in a way a seed thief cannot forge, cannot block, and cannot race.

Two ceremonies share the RecoveryKey root:

- **Succession (theft response):** mint a successor identity; the home nest atomically re-points the account and refuses the old key; groups, peers, and federation learn the old→new link through a verifiable statement.
- **Escrow restore (loss response):** recover the identity seed itself from an opaque blob on the home nest, sealed to the RecoveryKey — after which no succession is needed (the seed was lost, not stolen).

## The RecoveryKey

- **Material:** a fresh random 32-byte root, independent of the identity seed. Ed25519 signing key = the root used directly as a seed (uniform with the identity-secret idiom); X25519 escrow KEM secret derived via `BLAKE3::derive_key(context = "fauna recovery escrow x25519 2026-07-23", key_material = recovery_root)` (domain-separated per key-material rule #3).
- **Custody — offline only, iron-clad.** The secret is displayed once as 64-hex + QR (the recovery kit) and **never stored on any device, in the account's synced state, or anywhere the identity seed unlocks** — a recovery secret the thief of a device can hold is no recovery secret. There is deliberately no "sync the recovery key" affordance.
- **Kit payload — `fauna://recovery?secret=<64-hex>[&actor=<64-hex>][&handle=<user@domain>]`.** The deliberate twin of `fauna://identity` on its own host, parsed by the shared `fauna_core::recovery::parse_recovery_kit_input` grammar (bare 64-hex · query form · colon form). Both account params are **optional and additive**, and they answer different questions: `actor=` says **which** account and stays authoritative for that (the escrow blob is AAD-bound to the actor id), while `handle=` says **where** it lives — and only a handle's `@domain` can locate the home nest a restore connects to, since the restore is pre-identity and has no session to ask. A minting surface therefore carries whatever it truthfully knows: the Settings path carries both, the onboarding kit screen carries only `actor=` (no handle is chosen yet at its ratified position, so its kit restores by asking — [`onboarding.md`](onboarding.md) § 1 Identity owns that screen and the field that asks). An empty param value reads as absence, and a kit minted before a param existed keeps parsing — a written-down phrase outlives every app version ([`../architecture/version-compatibility.md`](../architecture/version-compatibility.md)).
  - **Which encoding each affordance carries (ruling 2026-08-02, uniform across all 7 apps).** A kit screen renders the same artifact three ways, and they deliberately carry different amounts of truth. The **display** is the bare 64-hex — the human-writable half, what a user copies onto paper. The **QR** and the **copy button** both carry the full URI, with whatever account params that surface truthfully knows. The copy button originally carried the bare hex, which made the two machine-readable encodings disagree: a *scanned* kit restored knowing its account, a *copied* one did not — and copying is overwhelmingly what a desktop or terminal user does, so the common path was the impoverished one. Unifying them costs nothing and is not a compatibility event: `parse_recovery_kit_input` accepts bare hex and the URI alike, so every kit ever saved keeps restoring, and a hand-written 64-hex code still takes the ask-for-the-handle path (`recovery-entry-account-field`) exactly as before. The **identity-secret export twin deliberately does NOT follow**: `identity_import` needs no locator — the seed alone *is* the account, and the client already has (or is about to choose) a nest — so there is no equivalent gain to buy with a changed shipped affordance.
- **Creation UX (user ruling 2026-07-23):** a strongly-encouraged, skippable recovery-kit screen at identity creation (the screen after the identity secret), plus a Settings/Security path that creates or re-issues the kit later. Skipping leaves a standing warning in Settings. Existing accounts retrofit from Settings; first registration wins (the pre-registration window is the accepted alpha exposure — surfaced, not hidden). **Ceremony timing at onboarding (user-ratified 2026-08-01):** the onboarding screen *mints and displays only* — at its ratified position no nest exists yet, so registration and the escrow `put` run **in-process at the wizard's signed-in handoff**, the only point custody permits (the secret is never persisted, so a minted-but-unregistered kit cannot survive to a later session). A wizard exit that never signs in (e.g. invite-pending) loses the minted kit; the standing Settings warning then tells the truth and the written phrase is inert — the screen must say the kit activates when the account comes online, never that the account is protected. This is the pre-registration window made concrete; first registration still wins. **Both surfaces are ratified as of 2026-08-01** and own their element IDs elsewhere: the onboarding screen is `recovery_kit` ([`onboarding.md`](onboarding.md) § 1 Identity), and the Settings path is a section on the Account sub-page, *not* a new rail entry ([`../ui/settings.md`](../ui/settings.md) § Recovery kit, which owns its placement and view state — the standing warning is that section's `recovery-kit-status` never-created state).
- **Registration:** a `RecoveryKeyRegistration { actor_id, recovery_pubkey, seq, created_at }` record, signed by **both** the identity seed (binds it to the account) and the RecoveryKey (proves possession), each over a domain-separated tag. The home nest stores it and serves it; the user's signed `Profile` gains an additive `recovery_head` field mirroring the chain head — `(recovery_pubkey, seq)` **coupled in one value**, so a cached binding always carries the seq the verification rule's head-extension check needs, and an editor whose decoder predates the field drops the binding whole rather than stripping the seq half (ruled: a pubkey-only mirror was unconsumable by the anchored walk) — so peers cache the binding with the profile they already hold. `seq` is monotonic per identity.
- **Replacement (user ruling 2026-07-23):**
  - *By the RecoveryKey:* immediate — a new registration record at `seq+1`, co-signed old-R + new-R + seed.
  - *By the seed alone* (honest R loss): enters a **30-day pending window** (`RECOVERY_REPLACE_GRACE = 30 d`, hard-coded constant — bucket 1, mirroring the backup custody grace), with loud notification on every device and app surface for the whole window. The current RecoveryKey can veto/override instantly; a landed succession cancels any pending replacement (succession outranks). Only after an uncontested window does the new registration take effect. **The client-surface half of "loud" is a critical-alerts feeder** (`recovery-replacement-pending:<actor_id>` — [`critical-alerts.md`](critical-alerts.md) § Feeders, which owns the banner contract; ratified 2026-08-01): a seed-alone request the user did not make means someone else holds their seed, which clears that surface's severity bar outright, and its every-authenticated-page, non-dismissable rendering is what a Settings-page element could not be. The **veto action** lives in Settings ([`../ui/settings.md`](../ui/settings.md) § Recovery kit) because an alert row is text. The one-shot notification half (inbox + push + email, at pending/landed/cancelled) is the nest's `SecurityNotifier` and is unchanged.
- **At succession**, the successor identity mints a **fresh RecoveryKey** (a new kit is part of the ceremony); the old one retires with the old identity. Each chain link is authorized by the RecoveryKey registered for the identity it succeeds.
  - **What "part of the ceremony" means, ratified 2026-08-03 — mint *and show*, as the ceremony's closing act, never a silent background mint and never only a standing warning.** The window this closes is the sharpest in the product: the succession transaction deletes the old `recovery_escrow` row and the old kit retires with the old identity, so from the moment the statement lands until this mint the account has **no recovery kit and no escrow at all** — a second seed loss is unrecoverable, for a user who has just proven they are a theft target. **A background mint cannot close it**, and that is what forces the shape rather than a preference: the secret may never be persisted (*Custody*, above), so an unshown mint would register a kit **nobody holds**, leaving the user strictly *worse* off than never-created — their only route back to a held kit would be the seed-alone replacement's 30-day window (*Replacement*), which would additionally fire the pending-replacement critical alert on their own remediation. So the successor's first authenticated session mints, registers, escrows and **displays** the kit unbidden, landing the user on the surface that renders it. The **shape is the onboarding kit screen's**, deliberately — strongly encouraged, skippable, and a skip leaves the standing Settings warning telling the truth (*Creation UX*) — because a successor is in exactly a new identity's position (a fresh seed with no kit and no escrow), only with more reason to act; inventing a second, sterner shape for the same moment would be per-app-divergence's cousin at the concept level. This supersedes the earlier "a prompt, not an automatic re-mint" gap note, which described what row 12 had built rather than what this doc had ratified.

## Seed escrow (loss recovery) — default on

The recovery kit includes an **escrow blob**: the identity seed HPKE-sealed (same suite as the other wrapped blobs: DHKEM(X25519, HKDF-SHA-256), HKDF-SHA-256, ChaCha20-Poly1305) to the RecoveryKey's derived X25519 public key, AAD-bound to `(kind = "seed-escrow", actor_id)`, stored opaque on the home nest. The nest cannot read it (rule #4 unbroken); it is re-written only when the sealed value or the sealing key changes (at kit creation; at kit **replacement** — the same seed re-sealed to the new RecoveryKey, since replacement changes the sealing key; and at succession — the new seed sealed to the new RecoveryKey).

- **The successor's blob carries the predecessor seed(s) alongside the new seed (ratified 2026-08-03; container built same day — `SeedEscrowBlob.pred` + `seal/unseal_seed_escrow_with_predecessors`, `libs/fauna-mls/src/wrapped_blob/`, pinned incl. the old-decoder degradation and the auxiliary-never-costs-the-primary rule; **the producer and the consumer are threaded end to end as of 2026-08-03** — see § Implementation status today).** Post-succession the old seed exists only on the user's own devices while the corpus is still sealed under it ([`succession-aftermath.md`](succession-aftermath.md) § Re-key scope), so the kit ceremony the succession owes (§ The RecoveryKey → *At succession*) seals `[new seed, predecessor seed(s)…]` rather than the new seed alone — a total-device-loss restore then recovers the account *and* the material to open the not-yet-re-sealed corpus. The predecessor material is **additive in the container** (an older client's restore recovers the account and merely misses the predecessor corpus until a newer client restores) and **temporary in intent — but the drop is gated far harder than the intent suggests (narrowed 2026-08-05, in the bound (3) design pass).** The only observable that may ever license a blob write to drop the predecessor seeds is an **account-complete, verification-derived drained statement** — every set the account owns verified re-sealed under the current root — and no such observable exists yet. Explicitly NOT a license: the per-device capability drop of `sync-agent-credentials.md` § Credential model bound (3) (it speaks only for the sets one device binds, and it is allowed to be recomputable precisely because being wrong there is a recoverable read failure), any `CorpusResealPass` record, and any progress surface — a wrong drop *here* is permanent corpus loss on total device loss, while carrying the seeds costs nothing (the no-trust-expanded sentence below holds either way). Until that observable exists, **every** blob write — kit replacement included — carries the predecessor seed(s), and it carries the **whole chain**, never one hop: the previous kit's blob died in the succession transaction (lifecycle bullet below), so an A→B→C chain whose first window never drained has no other carrier of A's seed. No trust is expanded: the blob is already fetchable only by the current kit holder, who already controls the account outright, and the corpus ends up sealed under the new `BackupKey` — which the new seed derives — anyway. ⚠ **The carrying rule is keyed on the RESTING BLOB, not on the writing device's registry (narrowed 2026-08-05).** The registry is a strictly weaker source — it is empty on any device that never held the predecessor's row, which an ordinary second device is (`add_account` carries no succession link) and which one device becomes the moment the user removes the retired account — and `escrow.put` **replaces** the row, so a section the writer cannot see is a section it destroys. A **replacement** therefore reads the blob it is about to overwrite *before it registers* — under the prior kit, the last moment that key is still the head the fetch verifies against — and writes the **union** of that section with whatever it resolved locally (the local material wins a duplicate `actor_id`, being the copy just proven to parse). If that read fails — unfetchable, or a section that does not open — the replacement is **refused** rather than narrowed, before anything lands, so the account is untouched and the retry is free; `no_escrow` is not that case, being a definite *nothing rests*. Two arms are outside this and stay registry-only by construction, both honest: a **first registration** overwrites nothing (no key was ever registered, so no row rests), and the **seed-alone** replacement lands after the nest has already deleted the row, with its whole premise being that the caller no longer holds the key that would open it. There, and only there, does *"a kit with no predecessor section beats no kit at all"* apply — it is the **create** case's justification and never licenses a replace that overwrites an existing section.

- **Restore path:** a fresh client with only the phrase fetches the blob over a **pre-identity, RecoveryKey-challenge-gated** kind (the nest issues a nonce; the client signs it with the RecoveryKey; rate-limited) and unseals the identity seed locally — full account recovery after total device loss: `BackupKey` corpus, `__mls` state, everything, with no succession needed. The nonce is **single-use and account-bound**, so one challenge buys exactly one attempt and a captured exchange cannot be replayed; the signature is verified against the **head** of the registration chain, so a replaced kit stops opening the blob the moment its successor registers. **The predecessor seeds the blob carries reach the account registry before the restored session is built** — after the restored identity is added (the shared `AccountRegistry::persist_restored_predecessors` states why: an add claims `active` when nothing holds it) and before the launch or switch that builds its session, because a session resolves its predecessor keys once in places (Media read custody, the `__mls` replica's re-seal barrier, the drafts rails): a later persist leaves everything sealed under a predecessor dark until a relaunch.
- **Lifecycle on the nest — the row dies with the key it is sealed to.** The nest deletes the escrow row inside the succession transaction (the old kit retires with the old identity — § The RecoveryKey) and on any registration that changes the registered `recovery_pubkey` (both replacement arms: RecoveryKey-authorized and the landed seed-alone window), so a blob no current kit can open never rests or is served — `fetch` answers `no_escrow`, the honest signal that a re-put is owed, instead of silently bricking the new kit. A fetch naming a superseded identity is refused with the `superseded` error naming the successor, uniform with the auth ceremonies. The client's half: re-put the blob at kit replacement (part of the ceremony, right after the registration lands — carrying the predecessor seed(s) the *resting blob* and this device's registry hold between them, bullet above) and at succession (the new seed under the new identity, likewise carrying the predecessor seed(s) — bullet above).
  - **The landed seed-alone re-put is user-prompted by construction (ratified 2026-08-10, the ruling).** For the seed-alone arm, "right after the registration lands" cannot mean a machine-driven call: the landing is a nest-side event 30 days after the gesture, the blob must seal to the **new** key, and by § The RecoveryKey → *Custody* no device holds that key by then — the request showed it once and persisted nothing, so at the landing it exists only on the paper the user kept. No notification, feeder or observable can close the gap by itself; every landed re-put therefore takes the kit from the user's hand. (Sealing early, at request time, is rejected: the old kit remains the chain head for the whole window and `escrow.put` replaces the row, so an early put would break phrase-restore *inside* the window. A nest-side dual-blob row was likewise rejected — a wire + at-rest change for one of the three arrival paths, with the prompted repair still needed for the other two.)
  - **The prompting surface is the Settings Recovery kit section's no-escrow state** ([`../ui/settings.md`](../ui/settings.md) § Recovery kit owns the placement): `recovery-kit-status` resolves `RegisteredNoEscrow`, whose copy must state plainly that the phrase does not currently recover the account **and** name the repair; the repair affordance reads the kit-in-hand field and calls the shared re-seal (`fauna_client_recovery::reseal_escrow_with_held_kit`) — **never `create_kit`**, whose replacement arm registers at `seq+1` and retires the kit the user just spent the window landing (replace stays offered as the deliberate sterner path). The ceremony **verifies the entered kit is the registered chain head before anything is sent**: the nest cannot look inside the blob, so a re-put sealed to a retired kit would rest unfetchably — the very state this deletion rule exists to keep unrepresentable. The nest's landed one-shot notification (`SecurityNotifier`, § The RecoveryKey → *Replacement*) is the without-going-looking pointer at that surface. The same repair serves **every** arrival at `RegisteredNoEscrow` — a landed seed-alone window, or a crash between any minting ceremony's registration and its escrow put — and its predecessor section is registry-resolved (registry-only is exact here: no row rests, so there is nothing to union with or destroy).
- **Who can observe that gap — `fauna.recovery.escrow.status`, the one USER-class kind in this family.** The lifecycle above means a registered kit can legitimately have no blob resting, and the surface that must warn about it is a **signed-in** one (`../ui/settings.md` § Recovery kit — "the only device that can fix it is a signed-in one"). That device cannot ask `fetch`: fetch is gated on a RecoveryKey signature, and the RecoveryKey is offline-only by iron-clad rule (§ The RecoveryKey — *Custody*), so the party able to *repair* the gap was the one party unable to *see* it. This kind closes that: it takes the account from the authenticated connection, carries no actor field at all, and answers presence plus the row's write stamp — never bytes. It is deliberately **not** pre-identity, for the reason `escrow.challenge` issues its nonce unconditionally: an unauthenticated presence answer would be a directory oracle mapping actor ids to "holds an escrow blob". Handler `bins/fauna-nest/src/recovery_handlers.rs::escrow_status_handler`, pinned in `bins/fauna-nest/tests/conformance_recovery_escrow.rs` (presence tracks the lifecycle; one account never learns another's).
- **Escrow adds no takeover power:** the RecoveryKey already authorizes succession (the superior credential); phrase theft was already game-over-grade. What escrow adds is that the phrase alone also recovers from *loss* — the behavior users expect of a recovery phrase.
- Declining the kit (or pre-kit accounts) keeps today's behavior: seed loss is unrecoverable, and no succession capability exists. That degradation is honest and surfaced, never silent.

## The succession statement (wire)

`IdentitySuccession { old_actor_id, new_actor_id, recovery_pubkey, seq, created_at }`, carried as canonical DAG-CBOR with detached signatures, each over its own domain-separated tag:

- `recovery_sig` — by the RecoveryKey registered for `old_actor_id` at the statement's `seq`. **Mandatory; the load-bearing authorization.**
- `new_sig` — by the successor identity key. **Mandatory** (proves successor possession/consent; prevents pointing an account at a key the claimant doesn't hold).
- `old_sig` — by the old identity key. **Optional and never load-bearing** — informational continuity only (present in the loss-after-escrow case and the voluntary-rotation case; absent-or-present changes no consumer's verdict, because a thief could always produce it).

**Verification rule (every consumer):** the statement is valid iff `recovery_sig` verifies under the `recovery_pubkey` the consumer knows for `old_actor_id` — from its cached Profile field, a previously-seen registration record, or a fetch of the registration chain from the old identity's home nest — and `new_sig` verifies under `new_actor_id`, and `seq` advances the chain the consumer last saw. **A chain delivered by the party asserting the succession is none of these sources** — a seed thief mints a chain every signature of which is genuine, so delivered bytes may serve as a *hint to go fetch*, never as the anchor; and a consumer that has ever verified the identity's chain must require a later chain to extend the head it saw, not replace it. Consumers that never saw a registration get TOFU-grade assurance from the fetched chain (strictly better than today, where the thief simply *is* the user). A succession statement is **harder for a thief than for the owner by construction** — that property, not any server's say-so, is what makes the statement safe to honor.

**How a consumer comes to hold the cached-Profile anchor — the peer-profile harvest (ratified 2026-08-10, the gate).** The first admitted source above is a *cache*, and for a member who joined a group by Welcome nothing populated it: a leaf credential carries only the actor id, no inbound path carries a sender handle, and every URL the group delivers (the Welcome's inviter-supplied `nest_url`, the roster's channel-home-served `node_url`) is chosen by a party the design refuses as an anchor — the asserting party, the adder, or the channel host, which MLS E2E exists to distrust. The ruling that closes this: **the anchor artifact is the peer's own signed `Profile`, and its signature — not the transport it arrived over — is what carries the trust.** A stored profile is a signed envelope verified against the profile's *own* `actor_id` (`fauna_core::encoding::decode_profile`, the one shared verify face), so a consumer may fetch it over *any* transport, including the very channel-delivered URLs disqualified as anchors — they are re-admitted here as exactly the *hint to go fetch* the verification rule already permits, never as the anchor itself. Five rules bound the harvest, each load-bearing:

1. **Signed by the identity key ITSELF** (amended 2026-09-19). An unsigned profile is inadmissible — it is any host's fabrication, and `decode_profile` (signed-only) refuses it outright. **A *delegated* authoring signature is equally inadmissible**: `decode_profile` also accepts a profile signed by the account's server-held authoring sub-key under an identity-signed `UpdateProfile` cert (`AuthoringOrigin::Delegated`, owned by [`atproto-pds-full.md`](atproto-pds-full.md) D10), and D10 rules that key **home-nest-held** — so the party serving `fauna.profile.get` to a same-nest harvester can sign one. Honoring it would hand that nest a tier-1 anchor for an identity whose key it does not hold, which is exactly the grade this section rests on. A harvest therefore consumes only `AuthoringOrigin::Direct` bytes, and a delegated profile seeds **neither** half — not the head, and not the domain, whose first-write-wins slot a planted value would block for good (`PeerAnchorRefusal::Delegated`, raised before any write). **The honest-flow consequence, stated plainly:** a user who authorized external-app authoring and then edits their profile from that app has a *delegated* profile at rest, so harvests seed nothing about them until one of their own apps publishes under the identity key again. An ordinary profile edit is not enough on its own to restore the two anchors — the writer-side rule ([`../ui/profile.md`](../ui/profile.md) § After an identity succession → the admission rule) drops `recovery_head`/`nests` carried from a delegated base — so what restores them is an identity-key publish that writes both from the device's own facts. **Ruled (2026-09-23): the owner's next sign-in restores them** (`fauna_client_profile::restore_delegated_anchors`, run over the base the per-sign-in succession-link read already fetched). It acts only when the stored profile is the signer's own AND delegated; it takes the head from the identity's registration chain, fetched and verified on the device (`fauna_core::recovery::verify_registration_chain` — every link carries the identity seed's signature, so the serving nest can withhold links but never mint one), **never** from the served profile's own `recovery_head`, and the home `nests` entry from the dialed URL plus the TOFU-pinned `nest_id`; it then re-publishes through the landed-registration mirror (`publish_recovery_head`), so the external app's display edits travel and the anchors are the device's. Three consequences, each deliberate: **(a)** the head is exactly the grade a peer gets from its own cache-miss fetch of the same chain — never better, never worse; **(b)** an identity with no registered RecoveryKey publishes nothing (no head to vouch for, and a domain-only write is not worth a profile write); **(c)** it cannot ping-pong with an external app that re-writes after every change — it runs once per sign-in, acts only on a delegated base, and a direct base costs no request at all — so the worst case is one extra profile write per sign-in. Until that sign-in the degrade is the ratified cache miss (absent, never poisoned): peers fall back to a chain fetch. Pinned by `fauna-client-profile`'s `a_delegated_edit_regains_its_anchors_at_the_owners_next_sign_in` (delegated edit → the door refuses it → the owner's restore → the harvest seeds the verified head and home), with its no-op, no-chain and unverified-chain arms beside it. **The census behind that rule (taken 2026-09-19):** exactly two consumers trust a profile's `recovery_head` or `nests` for anything beyond display, and both now require the identity key's own signature — this door, and the profile writers' admission rule ([`../ui/profile.md`](../ui/profile.md) § After an identity succession), which carries the two fields only from a base the signer signed directly. The nest's own `foreign_recovery_heads` table is not a third: it learns a head only from a registration chain it verified itself (`bins/fauna-nest/src/succession_pull.rs`), never from a profile. Every other reader of those fields — the ActivityPub actor serve, the ATProto projection — treats the profile as display and never reads them at all.
2. **The profile must name the peer asked about.** Envelope verification is self-consistent, not self-targeted: a lying host can serve a *different* identity's genuine signed profile, so `profile.actor_id` must equal the actor being harvested or the bytes are refused.
3. **A harvest may SEED, never ADVANCE — and, since 2026-09-20, DEMOTE.** A harvested `recovery_head` fills an empty `peer_chain_heads` slot; against a slot already held it never moves the head, and the one thing it may still do is mark that head **outrun** when it claims past it (*What a held head may settle offline*, below, owns that half); only a **completed verified walk** may advance a held head. A profile admitted here is signed by the *identity* key — exactly what a seed thief holds — so a thief who doctors the profile during the compromise window could otherwise stamp an arbitrarily high `seq` into the durable store and make the rewrite/truncation guard refuse the *genuine* chain forever. Rule 1 is what makes that sentence true of every admitted profile: without its delegated arm the *second* signer of a profile is the account's home-nest-held authoring sub-key, and this rule's seed-only bound would be the only thing standing between that nest and a planted anchor. Seed-only writes cost nothing in the honest flow — a stale seeded head that no longer names the kit which signed the statement fails tier 1, falls through to the walk, and the walk advances it (corrected 2026-09-20: that sentence used to claim it of *every* stale head, and a stale head that still names the **signing** kit does not fail tier 1 at all — which is the hole the outrun rule below exists for) — and cap the window-doctored profile at the TOFU grade this section already concedes. The same rule covers the domain half: a harvested home domain (from the profile's self-asserted `nests` entries) fills an empty per-actor slot first-write-wins and never displaces one.
4. **Read paths only, never at verify time.** The harvest runs where a client ordinarily reads a peer profile — at group join for the new roster, on the profile page, on a roster refresh — and **never from the verification path**: re-fetching the profile when a statement arrives would anchor the check on whoever holds the identity key at that moment, i.e. on the attacker in exactly the case that matters.
5. **The store is bounded on both axes — 253 bytes per host, 256 entries per vector** (length ratified 2026-09-08, count 2026-09-09). `peer_anchor_domains` and `peer_chain_heads` were the config plane's only **per-peer** vectors (the account plane's `fauna.state.peer-anchors` since 2026-09-30, one row per actor — *Implementation status today*), they merge by per-actor **union**, they have no prune, no eviction and no app affordance to remove an entry, and since the harvest became a background sweep they are filled with **no human in the loop** — while the sealed `__config` blob they then rested in was hard-capped at 1 MiB. So an unbounded host is not a cosmetic defect: measured 2026-09-03, four group peers each publishing one ~400 KiB host pushed a victim's sealed config past that cap, the first three persisting, after which every `save_cas` fails on every device, permanently — the client-causable unrecoverable state [`../architecture/nest/common.md`](../architecture/nest/common.md) § Client-state recoverability forbids. The bound therefore lives at the **one door** (a `HostTooLong` refusal, checked before anything is written, so the arm's "nothing is seeded" contract holds for the chain head too) and is re-asserted at the `seed_anchor_domain` writer, so no call site can hold a weaker subset. That host bound is a **length** bound, never a shape one: the harvest admits a bracketed IPv6 literal that the stricter admin-typed custom-domain shape rules refuse, and those rules are not imported here. The 1 MiB blob cap retired with the rail on 2026-10-02 ([`../architecture/config-dissolution.md`](../architecture/config-dissolution.md) § The `__config` dissolution schedule → *The closure order*, step (6)); on the plane each anchor is its own row, bounded as [`../architecture/config-dissolution.md`](../architecture/config-dissolution.md) § The `__config` dissolution schedule → *The kinds* states, and a future per-peer field is still bounded at its own writer.

    **The count axis, and why a length bound alone was not the ceiling (ratified 2026-09-09).** Bounding the host moved the wedge from *four* peers to roughly four thousand — real defence, not a closed door, and on a large shared nest four thousand same-nest actors sharing a thread with the victim is a population rather than an attack budget. So each vector is additionally bounded at **256 entries** (`fauna_core::data::MAX_PEER_ANCHOR_ENTRIES`), which holds the pair under ~89 KiB — a tenth of the cap — from 285 B worst case per domain entry (32 B actor + a 253 B host) and 72 B per head entry. It is generous against the honest population: only peers homed on the member's own nest ever reach the store — `fauna.profile.get` answers for no one else — so the population is the same-nest peers the member shares any thread with (since 2026-09-15 the sweep attempts every Fauna row, named or not; *which participant handles anchor tier 2*, below, owns why, and why that leaves the bound where it is). The bound sits at **each writer** (`seed_chain_head`, `seed_anchor_domain` — both `pub`, so a door-only rule would bind only the door's own callers) and the door raises `PeerAnchorRefusal::StoreFull` only when *neither* vector has room, since the two are seeded independently and refusing a chain head that fits because the *domain* vector is full would discard the more useful half for the fuller one's sake. **The ceiling refuses NEW SEEDS and nothing else (amended 2026-09-21).** Rule 3's demotion flips a flag on an entry already held and takes no slot, so the door runs its writers *first* and raises `StoreFull` only when neither vector has room, the profile offered something that needed a slot, and nothing was written; a peer already anchored in both vectors is the ordinary nothing-new. The refusal used to sit ahead of the head match, and a full store — which a big roster reaches organically and which the party the residual below concedes can arrange — then hid every later kit rotation from the device: the sweep settled the peer as refused, the harvest wait released, and the retired kit's statement settled at tier 1. A store that can be filled to silence the outrun rule is not a bounded loss of function, so it is not among the residuals this ceiling takes. Pinned by `data.rs::a_full_store_still_marks_a_held_head_outrun` and `witness.rs::a_full_anchor_store_still_demotes_so_the_retired_kits_statement_is_walked`. **It is re-asserted a third time on the merge path**, and that one is load-bearing rather than belt-and-braces: the writers bound what ONE replica may add, which does not bound the per-actor union — two devices holding 256 disjoint peers each merge to 512, a fleet of D devices to D×256, walking the sealed blob straight back toward the cap. `merge_user_configs` therefore truncates each merged vector to the ceiling, keeping the fleet's **oldest** entries — smallest `(first_seen, actor)`, where `first_seen` is a per-entry stamp the owner's own device writes at the first seed and every merge takes as the per-actor **minimum**; the actor id is only the equal-stamp tiebreak (amended 2026-09-09). That is the writers' own first-write-wins value order carried onto the merge, and it is **convergent**, which the merge cannot trade away: under a join where an entry's key can only fall, "keep the k smallest" is associative — an entry outside the k smallest of one union stays outside the k smallest of any larger one — so no merge order lets a dropped entry survive and a three-device fleet cannot oscillate. **The key must be one no peer can mint.** The first ratification truncated by the actor id alone, and an actor id is a value the *peer* chooses — grinding one that sorts first is seconds of work — so a same-nest attacker whose ground batch reached one replica's harvest displaced the honest anchors held on the other at the next merge, silently: the writers' refused eviction primitive, reached through the merge. The stamp is additive (`version-compatibility.md` § I4): an entry from a pre-stamp device decodes as the epoch and sorts *oldest*, so the upgrade never evicts the honest back-catalogue, and a pre-stamp device re-writing the config flattens the order back to the id tiebreak for its entries — a degrade that outlasts the upgrade window, not a break: nothing ever re-stamps an entry once it exists (only a first write sets `first_seen`; an advance keeps it), so every entry that has ever decoded at the epoch — whether it pre-dates the field or was written by a not-yet-upgraded device during the window — sits in that cohort **permanently**, ordered among its own members by actor id alone — precisely the tiebreak removed; and once a fleet's per-actor union holds 256 such entries, no newly-stamped anchor can ever win a merge slot again. Clock skew between the owner's devices degrades the *value* order the same way and touches convergence not at all.

    **The writer refuses; it does not evict — and that is the ratified trade, not an unfinished half.** Entries are first-write-wins, which makes the OLDEST ones the most likely to be load-bearing, so "keep the newest N" would invert the value order *and* hand an attacker an eviction primitive: publish entries until the honest anchors are pushed out. (The merge's truncation above is a *different* act — a writer refuses to evict a held anchor to make room for a NEW peer, whereas the merge reconciles two sets that were each already legal and must land on exactly one answer — but it is *that primitive* the moment its key is peer-chosen, which the first ratification's actor-id key was: reaching the truncation needs only the owner's fleet to hold more than 256 distinct nameless peers across devices, and on a large shared nest a same-nest attacker supplies those peers. What the stamp key leaves a peer is being *early* — exactly the writer's own residual below — and what the truncation costs the owner is the newest anchors on the divergent side of a merge degrading to the bare add.) Scoping entries to a live roster row was the other candidate and is also refused — a departure is exactly when a peer's anchor is still needed, and reaching a conversation roster from the config plane inverts the layering. **The residual, stated plainly:** a peer who fills a vector first denies *future* anchoring, and statements about un-anchored peers degrade to the bare add — the same graceful degrade rule 3 already concedes for a peer with no `nests` entry or one homed on another nest. A bounded loss of function is not a brick, and the brick is what [`../architecture/nest/common.md`](../architecture/nest/common.md) § Client-state recoverability forbids (its per-object-remedy carve-out does not rescue the unbounded shape: condition 2 wants an ordinary in-app flow reaching equivalent function, and these vectors have none).

    **The sweep that fills the store is bounded too (ratified 2026-09-09).** The harvest's retryable arms re-attempt one peer at most `MAX_HARVEST_ATTEMPTS` (5) times per session, on an exponential ladder in sweep ticks (5s → 10s → 20s → 40s), then settle it. Before this, a retryable failure simply un-marked the peer, so a store refusing the write **permanently** — the over-cap `malformed` reject above being exactly that — had the sweep re-fetching that peer's profile every 5 s for the session's whole life. The bound is deliberately a **budget rather than a classification**: `fauna.config.put`'s rejection reached the client, while the anchors rode the blob, as one opaque `StoreError::Save(String)` (every non-CAS rejection folded together), so the sweep *could not* tell a permanent refusal from a disconnect — and a budget means no misclassification, present or future, can produce an unbounded loop. Threading a rejection class through that seam was not needed for the bound, and the seam retired with the rail on 2026-10-02 ([`../architecture/config-dissolution.md`](../architecture/config-dissolution.md) § The `__config` dissolution schedule → *The closure order*, step (6)).

**Which participant handles anchor tier 2 — handle provenance (ratified 2026-09-15).** The URL rule above extends to handles, because a handle's `@domain` *is* a dial target: [`succession-propagation.md`](succession-propagation.md)'s tier 2 walks `resolve_successor` against the nest serving a participant row's handle domain, so whoever chose that handle chose which nest a first-contact consumer treats as the identity's home — and that nest's answer is the anchor. Three provenances write a `handle@domain` into a thread's participant row, and they are not equal. **(1) The owner's own gesture** — a recipient the owner typed and accepted at compose, a member the owner added — is **anchor-grade**, the one provenance no other party shapes. **(2) A name the room home served** — joined nest-side from its own `users` row for its own local user, or relayed for a foreign member from that member's nest's announce (the host binds the domain, [`../architecture/federation.md`](../architecture/federation.md) § Cross-nest shared folders + channel append, but the host is still the party serving it) — is **display only** ([`conversation-rooms.md`](conversation-rooms.md) § Implementation status today already says *never an identity claim*; this adds *never a succession anchor*): it is the channel host's choice of dial target, refused above whether or not that host is the consumer's own nest — the harvest already reads through that same nest and trusts nothing but the envelope. For a same-nest peer the served name happens to name the right nest, and the harvest's signed-profile domain reaches that nest by an admissible path, so refusing it loses nothing; for a foreign member of a third-nest room it is the one case where the host could steer the dial, and before 2026-09-10 such a member simply had no tier-2 anchor and degraded to the bare add — which this restores. **(3) A name copied off another thread at seat time** inherits the provenance of the row it copied: anchor-grade only when this device holds an owner-typed row for the same actor bearing the same handle. Three consequences, all shared Rust. **(a) The row carries its provenance:** the thread store marks the rows the owner's gesture named (`ThreadStore::mark_anchor_grade` — a positive set, so a seat site that forgets to mark fails safe as display only), and the history slice persists the set as the required `anchor_grade_handles` field — every writer records it, an empty set included, and a slice without it fails to decode (the pre-field reading of *every* named row as anchor-grade was removed by the compat-remnant sweep, [`../architecture/version-compatibility.md`](../architecture/version-compatibility.md) § Dimension 2: no slice written before the field exists). **(b) Tier 2 dials only an anchor-grade handle's domain** (`SuccessionAnchors::known_handle` reads provenance, never the rendered string); a display-only name falls through to the harvested domain, then to the bare add. **(c) The harvest attempts every Fauna row, named or not** — the skip on a name (*"a row with a resolvable handle already anchors tier 2"*) is RETIRED, for two reasons: a rendered name is no longer evidence the owner typed it, and the chain head the harvest seeds is the **offline** tier-1 anchor, which no handle replaces — an owner-typed same-nest peer had no offline path at all. The narrower alternative, skipping only anchor-grade rows, was considered and refused for that second reason. The count axis is restated accordingly: **reach, not the skip, bounds the store** — `fauna.profile.get` answers only for identities homed on the member's own nest, so the honest population is the same-nest peers the member shares any thread with, owner-typed ones now included, while a cross-nest row costs one `NoProfile` round trip per session and settles; the 256 bound at the writers stands, and the attacker's leverage is unchanged, since the retired skip excluded only rows the owner typed, which no attacker can manufacture. Pinned by `witness.rs::{a_roster_named_handle_does_not_anchor_the_walk, the_sweep_attempts_a_roster_named_row, the_sweep_attempts_an_owner_typed_row_as_well}` beside the resolver-side control, and by the thread store's provenance pins (`store::threads::tests`, `store::history::tests`). The member-path journey `test_a_real_member_re_points_the_succeeded_participant_on_their_own_client` is what this ruling turned back green on linux and tui: its harvest barrier stood on *"bob's row for alice is nameless"*, which the id-keyed roster read had falsified the day it landed.

**A community policy's names join the harvest's walk (ratified 2026-09-20).** The sweep walked only the member's own thread rosters, so a community room's policy name the member never shared a thread with — typically an owner or admin the succession ceremony retired before this member joined — held no anchor on this device, and every floor delete record behind the version its successor signed parked for the session ([`community-rooms.md`](community-rooms.md) § Implementation status today → *Delete any message* → residual (a)). **The ruling: the room backend OFFERS the sweep the policy names a rank refusal asked the witness about and found no anchor for, and the sweep harvests them through the same door, at the same grade, under the same once-per-session guard and retry ladder.** A wider walk set, not a new anchor source: `resolve_line`'s dial target is still only what this device's own anchors hold, and the two candidates that would have made the room or its beneficiary the source stay refused — the successor's own profile naming its predecessor's home is self-asserted by the party the chain benefits, and the member's own nest as a resolver is closed below (§ Don't do these, the "doubly dead" record). Four bounds, each load-bearing. **(1) Only names an ANCHORED version carries.** The names a refusal asks about include the served version still waiting on it, and that version is bytes the room's home nest chose and nobody on a verified line has signed; a name only it carries is never offered, so the home nest chooses nothing about *whom* this device fetches. What it can choose is *when* a never-met identity is first fetched — by serving a record at all — and for an identity this device has never anchored there is no earlier moment to prefer: rule 3 caps what that fetch may write at seed-only either way, and rule 4 still holds where it bites — nothing is fetched on the judgment path; the sweep reads the offer on its own next pass, and the parked record is judged again on the next poll, exactly as for a thread peer. **(2) Bounded per room** — `MAX_POLICY_NAME_OFFERS` (8), in chain order (owners before admins, version 1's names before a later version's) — because an admin set is unbounded and its author is whoever founded a room this member chose to join. **The eight are a FIXED PREFIX of the chain, never a rolling window (amended 2026-09-21):** the cut is taken *before* the still-unanchored filter, so a name that settles leaves the offer and nothing takes its place. Cut after the filter, every settle admitted the next name, and a founder naming K same-nest identities seeded K entries; and because the anchor store persists while the room's seat state is per-session, no in-memory budget could have held the bound either — a fixed prefix of a signed chain holds it with nothing persisted. The price is declared: a name past the eighth in chain order is never offered and its records stay parked, residual (a) exactly as for a cross-nest name. What the prefix does **not** bound is a chain that differs between sessions — it is re-anchored from the room's home nest each session, so a founder who signs rival version 2s *and* holds a home nest willing to serve a different one per session moves positions two to eight, seven names a session; that attacker buys only the store's conceded residual, since a full store denies future anchoring and never a demotion (rule 5's count axis). **(3) Same-nest only, permanently.** The fetch is `fauna.profile.get` over the member's own connection, which answers for identities homed on the member's nest and nobody else, and the anonymous read was declined (§ Don't do these): a retired name homed elsewhere stays unanchored and its records stay parked. **Residual (a) is therefore NARROWED to the cross-nest case, never removed**, and what the member sees of an unprovable chain is the room's to own ([`conversation-rooms.md`](conversation-rooms.md), same residual). **(4) Rule 5's count axis, restated.** The honest population is the same-nest peers the member shares any thread with **plus at most eight same-nest retired names per community room the member joined**; the 256 bound at the writers stands; the leverage this hands an attacker is of the class the writer's residual already accepts — being early enough to fill a vector — with a room's founder now among the parties who can be early, at eight entries per room the member chose to join — per *chain*, by bound (2)'s fixed prefix, with the rival-chain remainder stated there. Shared Rust end to end: `FaunaMlsBackend::offer_policy_names_to_harvest` → `ConversationsManager::set_policy_anchor_wants` / `harvest_walk_actors` → `PeerAnchorSweepState::run_pass` (the walk's third feeder, the folder-channel owners a rail pulls in through `RailBackend::harvest_anchor_wants`, is [`../architecture/federation.md`](../architecture/federation.md)'s — *The folder commit walk inherits the harvest wait*). Pinned by `fauna_mls_backend_tests::a_never_met_policy_name_is_offered_to_the_harvest_from_the_anchored_chain_only` (the served-only exclusion and the cap each mutation-verified to red; the fixed prefix asserted in the same test, red against the rolling window) and `witness.rs::{the_sweep_harvests_a_policy_name_no_thread_carries, resolve_line_still_takes_no_caller_supplied_dial_target}` — the second a compile-time pin that the resolver grew no parameter through which an asking context could supply a domain.

**Losing the race to the statement — the parked-statement re-drive (ratified 2026-08-10, the ruling).** A statement can arrive before the harvest has anchored its peer, and the delivery it arrives in is the **only copy the member will ever hold**: a consumed MLS application message cannot be re-decrypted (forward secrecy consumes the message key), so there is no re-delivery to wait for — an earlier draft of this escape ("a heal re-delivery converges") named a producer that does not exist. Convergence is therefore the receive path's own job: a witness-refused statement is **parked** — latest per `(thread, participant)`, admitted **only for an identity currently on that thread's roster row set** (the only row a re-drive could ever re-point, and the bound that caps a member forging arbitrary `old_actor_id`s at roster size), session-lifetime — and a harvest that seeds something new for that peer **re-drives** the parked statement through the ordinary verify, which settles from the harvested head with no dial and no fetch (since 2026-09-20 a harvest that *settles* the peer without seeding anything re-drives too — *the harvest wait*, below, owns that half and why it is not a seed). Three rules bound the re-drive; the first two are corollaries of rule 4. **The harvest's schedule stays blind to parked statements** (a statement's arrival must never schedule, hasten or prioritize a harvest — otherwise a forged statement chooses the fetch's moment, a weaker form of the verify-time fetch). **The re-drive itself fetches nothing** (it re-asks the witness; the anchor's provenance is unchanged). And — the third, ratified 2026-08-14 with the read bound below — **the re-drive announces the landed seed to the witness before it consults the parked set, whether or not anything is parked**: a witness that may not re-read the anchor store per statement has no other way to learn the store changed under it, so announcing *after* the re-verify would settle nothing (and re-park a statement whose anchor is already at rest, with no second harvest event coming for a peer already seeded), while announcing *only when something was parked* would leave the ordinary case — the sweep winning the race, which is most seeds — serving a stale "no anchor held" view for the rest of the session.

**What a statement may cost the member who receives it (ratified 2026-08-14).** The witness is consulted for **every** decodable `Succession` body, and *before* the roster gate above — that gate bounds what may be **parked**, never what is **verified** — so an in-group member can name arbitrary `old_actor_id`s and drive the no-anchor arm as often as it likes, on the receiving member's **inline** receive path, against an arm that deliberately records no verdict to memoize. The anchor read is therefore bounded by what the member holds, never by what a peer sent: the durable anchor store is read **once per seed generation** — once for the session, plus once for each harvest that seeds something new — and never once per statement. **A store that cannot be read is backed off, never cached (2026-09-23):** a failed read says nothing about what is at rest, so no empty view is stamped for the generation — but neither is the store re-read on every consult; it is read again at most once per `UNREADABLE_STORE_BACKOFF_SECS` (a hard-coded 10 s), and at once after a landed seed, since a seed is itself proof the store was reachable. Without the backoff a broken `__config` turned every re-ask behind the anchor read — a statement's, or a community policy name's from a parked floor delete record on each inbound pass — into one round trip. (A per-consult read is one nest round trip plus one config unseal; the arm's own guarding comment once called that "proportionally cheap", which described the honest flow and priced the hostile one at zero.) The generation is what keeps the read both bounded and fresh, and it is memoization of a *fact* rather than a staleness window: only a harvest can put a harvested anchor at rest, and rule 3 above routes every one of them through the announcement. Parking is deliberately **not at rest** — considered and rejected: a durable parked statement is re-drive work an in-group forger mints once and every later session pays, plus an expiry policy for statements that can never verify, to close a seconds-wide compound race. **The residual that leaves, stated honestly:** a session that ends between the delivery and the harvest's completion keeps the bare add durably — no future session re-delivers the statement — until the *next* statement about that identity arrives (a later ceremony, or [`succession-propagation.md`](succession-propagation.md) § Propagation's member re-add remedy carrying it).

**What a held head may settle offline — the outrun rule (ratified 2026-09-20).** The verification rule checks the key the consumer holds, the two signatures and an advancing `seq` — and nothing about whether that key is *still* the registered one. A RecoveryKey the owner rotated away keeps producing valid signatures for ever (the property that makes `old_actor_id` a PRIMARY KEY nest-side, § Enforcement on the home nest), so a consumer still holding the pre-rotation head verifies a statement signed by the retired kit exactly as it would the owner's own. The anchored walk refuses it — the chain is verified first, its head names the new key, and the statement's key no longer matches — so **the offline tier is the only door a retired kit still opens**, and it re-points the owner's participant row, durably, at an identity the retired kit's holder chose (the carrier is any group member, since the transport sender is deliberately unbound, or a consumer outside any group such as the contact re-point). Nor is the stale head an edge case: nothing refreshes a held head but a walk, and a walk runs only when a statement arrives, so after **any** rotation every consumer that anchored the owner beforehand holds the stale head indefinitely. **Offline, the forgery and the honest statement the offline tier exists for are indistinguishable** — same key, same `seq` shape — so no rule evaluated at verify time can tell them apart, which is what refuses each verify-time candidate: a *`seq` distance* (the signer picks `seq`, and held+1 verifies identically for forger and owner); *settling only at or below the held `seq`* (the rule refuses any statement that does not advance, so that switches the tier off rather than narrowing it); a *refresh the statement triggers* (rule 4 forbids the verify-time fetch, re-drive rule 1 forbids a statement scheduling a harvest, and the read bound above exists because a forger can burst statements); and an *age bound* (it may not reuse `first_seen`, which dates the entry and is deliberately never re-stamped by an advance — and even with a stamp of its own, an age window turns the offline tier off for every quiet honest peer while leaving the retired kit the whole window: the most continuity spent for the least protection). **The rule therefore uses what the consumer learned *before* the statement, on the read paths rule 4 already admits.** The sweep re-reads every Fauna row's profile once per session regardless, and a profile admitted under rules 1 and 2 whose `recovery_head` is **past** the held head — a higher `seq`, or a different key at the same `seq` — marks the held head **outrun**. An outrun head is not moved (rule 3 stands: the claim's signer is the key a seed thief holds), stays at rest as the walk's rewrite/truncation guard, and **no longer settles a statement offline**: the statement is walked, which is where a rotated-away key fails and where an honest post-rotation statement succeeds and advances the head. A claim at or behind the held head marks nothing — the profile mirror is best-effort and may lag a walk. Only a verified walk clears the mark, by replacing the head it was about. **Why a profile may demote what it may never advance:** the worst a doctored claim buys is a statement that has to be walked — the honest path anyway — so the cost of a false mark is one dial, or, for a member holding no dial domain for that peer, the bare add; a false *advance* poisons the guard for good, and a forged offline settle poisons the participant row. Absent, never poisoned, is the grade every cache in this section is held to, and the mark moves the offline tier from the second to the first. That cost is open to a seed thief too — publishing a high claim demotes the victim's head on every same-nest peer, so the owner's genuine statement must walk — and it is accepted on the same ground. **What a member does after an owner rotates a kit: nothing.** The owner's client mirrors the new head into their profile at the landed registration (`fauna_client_profile::publish_recovery_head`, automatic), and each member's next session reads it; there is no affordance, because there is no choice in it for anyone to make. **The residuals, stated plainly.** (1) *The window before the member's next harvest of that peer.* A forged re-point landed inside that window is durable on that device and nothing repairs it automatically; the rule closes the retired kit found *later*, not the one used the instant it is retired — whose holder could have run a genuine succession a moment earlier anyway. Since 2026-09-20 that window no longer reaches into the session that closes it: the first moments of a session, where a statement already waiting in the channel used to be processed before the sweep's fetch returned, belong to *the harvest wait*, below — what remains is a member whose sessions since the rotation never reached the peer's profile at all. (2) *Peers the harvest cannot read* — a cross-nest peer (the declined read, below), a peer whose profile rests delegated (rule 1), a peer who never mirrored a head: what is held about them came from a walk, is never demoted, and keeps the pre-rule behavior. (3) *A home nest replaying the pre-rotation profile* hides the rotation from its own members; that takes the nest and the retired kit's holder acting together. (4) *The session memo*: a forged offline settle records a verified verdict for the rest of that session, so it also decides the genuine statement about that identity until relaunch — while the durable head store stays clean, since the offline tier never writes it. (5) *A pre-field device* — closed with the blob (ruled 2026-10-02): while the anchors rode it, a device whose decoder lacked the field re-wrote the config and dropped the mark until the next harvest or merge restored it ([`../architecture/version-compatibility.md`](../architecture/version-compatibility.md) § I4). On the `fauna.state.peer-anchors` rows (since 2026-09-30; the kind and its join: [`../architecture/config-dissolution.md`](../architecture/config-dissolution.md) § The `__config` dissolution schedule → *The kinds*) a mark cannot be stripped: the rows decode strictly (`fauna_core::peer_anchor_rows::decode_exact` refuses a row whose bytes it cannot reproduce), so a decoder lacking a field refuses a marked head outright — loudly, on read and on write — while an unmarked head encodes exactly as it did before the field existed; and no such decoder exists, since every binary that knows the kind knows the field. What remains is the join itself: a replica whose higher head wins `PeerChainHead::join_from` replaces the marked head, which is the walk clearing the mark, as ruled above. **Not a substitute for narrowing who may carry a statement** — the roster-pair gate ([`succession-propagation.md`](succession-propagation.md) § Propagation → *MLS groups*, built 2026-09-20): that gate bounds the in-group path only, and a consumer that verifies outside any group never passes through it; the two close different doors. Shared Rust end to end, so no app owes a line: the door `PeerAnchors::seed_from_peer_profile_bytes` → `mark_chain_head_outrun`, `PeerChainHead.outrun`, `SuccessionAnchors::known_head_is_outrun`, and both of `ChainWitness::verify_statement`'s offline arms. **The mark reaches a session already answering for the head**: a demotion is a `Seeded` harvest outcome, so the ratified re-drive announces it and the anchors re-read — and, because that made the head half generation-aware, heads, marks and harvested domains now fold from **one** store read per seed generation where there were two. Pinned by `witness.rs::{a_retired_kits_statement_does_not_settle_against_a_head_the_profile_outran, a_demotion_landing_mid_session_reaches_a_head_the_cache_already_answers_for, a_walk_that_advances_the_head_restores_the_offline_path}` — the first carrying residual (1) as its in-test control, and each of the two offline arms' gates mutation-verified to red the first two — beside `data.rs::{a_profile_claiming_past_the_held_head_demotes_it_and_never_advances_it, an_unmarked_chain_head_encodes_as_it_did_before_the_mark_existed}` and `format_user_config.rs::the_outrun_mark_ors_on_the_same_head_and_never_follows_a_stale_one`.

**The statement that beats the session's own harvest — the harvest wait (ratified 2026-09-20).** The outrun rule knows only what a harvest has *read*, and the sweep that reads runs beside the receive loop, not before it: it is launched first in the loop's prologue, but fire-and-forget, one peer after another, so a statement already waiting in the channel is decoded before the sweep's `fauna.profile.get` for that peer returns — reliably, for a peer late in the sweep's order. And the forger picks the moment: a retired kit found a month after the rotation still re-points the owner's row on every member whose *first session since the rotation* starts with the statement waiting, which is the "found later" threat the outrun rule claims to close. **The rule: in a session that runs a harvest sweep, a held head settles no statement until the sweep has settled that peer** — by any arm. Until then the witness refuses *for now*: no dial, no store read, and **no verdict recorded** (a memoized refusal would answer the settle's own re-drive for the rest of the session — the wedge the no-anchor arm already avoids), so the statement is parked by the ordinary roster-bounded parking above and the settle re-drives it, into tier 1 when the harvest found nothing past the held head, into the walk when it demoted it. Only a head that *would have settled* the statement waits; one that decides nothing falls through to the walk at once, since the walk is the path the wait exists to force. Five bounds, each ratified elsewhere and each kept. **(a) The wait is passive.** It ends on the sweep's own order; a parked statement never schedules, hastens or prioritizes a harvest (re-drive rule 1, unchanged). **(b) Every settle re-drives, not only a seed** — amending the re-drive rule above, which fired on a seed alone because the only parked statement was then one with no anchor. The settle arms are the sweep pass's three branches: a **seed**; a **standing outcome** (nothing new, no profile at this nest, any refusal); and the **retry budget running out** — once, at the fifth attempt, never per attempt, which would end the wait on a nest that was merely slow to come up. **(c) A settle is not a seed.** It is announced to the witness separately, *after* the seed announcement when both apply (a witness released before it re-read the store would settle on the very head the seed just demoted), and it never advances the seed generation — most settles seed nothing, and the anchor store is read once per seed generation, not once per settled roster peer. **(d) The wait ends at the budget, not at success.** An offline member's sweep never succeeds; the ladder spends ~75 s of waits plus one 5 s request deadline per attempt — about 100 s, for the whole roster at once, because a pass's first `Unreachable` answers for the rest of that pass (every fetch rides the member's one connection; tried peer by peer, a down nest cost ~25 s *per queued peer* and the wait scaled with the roster; amended 2026-09-21, pinned by `witness.rs::an_unreachable_nest_costs_one_fetch_per_pass_not_one_per_peer`) — and the held head then settles the statement — the offline tier exists for exactly that member, and what the wait costs them is that delay, once per peer per session. **(e) Nothing new is parked, persisted or read.** Parking keeps its roster bound and session lifetime (the folder rail's park, which guards a forked seat rather than this race, rests with the engine instead — owned by [`../architecture/federation.md`](../architecture/federation.md) § Cross-nest shared folders + channel append → *The folder commit walk inherits the harvest wait*); the settled set is in memory, written only by the sweep, and therefore bounded by the member's rosters rather than by what a peer sends. **Who waits, and who does not.** A witness waits only once told a sweep runs (`SuccessionWitness::harvest_armed` — the native receive loop's prologue says so right behind the sweep launch and before the poll task exists; web says so where it builds the sweep its receive pump ticks), because the wait **fails open by construction**: where nothing will ever announce a settle, a waiting witness would never settle a statement offline again. So a consumer outside any conversations session would keep the immediate tier 1, and with it residual (1) in full, having no parking to hold a statement in — and **none is declared** (scoped 2026-09-20; an earlier draft of this sentence named the contact re-point, wrongly): the private contact overlay's fold-forward, the one client-held contact re-point there is, rides *the same witness-verified statement that re-points a thread participant row* ([`../ui/contacts.md`](../ui/contacts.md) § The private overlay owns it), so it is served by the session's own armed witness and **inherits the wait at no cost**, while its second leg — the reconcile at projection load — verifies no statement at all and only consumes a verdict already given. A consumer that ever does verify outside a session must either ask the session's witness or bring a settle signal of its own before it may hold a verdict back; asking an un-armed witness is the fail-open path, never a design. Inside a session the distinction is *not yet attempted* versus *never attempted*: a peer on any thread roster is attempted within one sweep tick of its row appearing — the sweep walks the **rosters** (`ConversationsManager::fauna_roster_actors`), no longer the conversations page's snapshot, whose search filter used to narrow which peers were ever harvested — and everything parked names a roster row by the parking bound, so nothing parked waits on a harvest that will not come (the folder rail's park names a folder channel's recorded owner instead, and since 2026-09-28 that owner joins the walk for the same reason — its commit walk waits on the settle — owned by [`../architecture/federation.md`](../architecture/federation.md) § Cross-nest shared folders + channel append → *The folder commit walk inherits the harvest wait*). A statement whose verdict would rest on a held head but which names an identity on **no** roster is refused and not parked — no sweep will ever settle that peer, so for them the wait does not end, and the offline tier is simply off in a session that runs a sweep. For participant rows that loses nothing: there is no row it could have re-pointed. It is **not** nothing for a store keyed by the person rather than by a roster row — the contact overlay is deliberately not gated on a contact edge or a thread — and the ruling there is to accept it rather than widen parking: the ceremony posts its statement into groups the succeeding identity *is a member of*, so in the honest flow the named identity holds a row wherever the statement arrives, is swept, and is parked; a statement naming a non-member is the shape a forger of arbitrary `old_actor_id`s produces, which is precisely what the roster bound on parking exists to cap. The honest remainder — a statement decoded on a thread after that thread's own row for the identity is already gone, while the identity rests on no other roster — folds nothing on that delivery, and the overlay's projection-load reconcile picks it up from the next verified verdict for that identity (a later ceremony, or the member re-add remedy). **The residuals, stated plainly.** *The offline first session*: the wait ends at the budget with the rotation unread, and the retired kit's statement settles — tier 1's price, unchanged; a forger cannot put a member offline, though the member's own nest can. *A harvest that read the rotation and could not store the mark* (the retryable store arms, spent — never the count ceiling, which refuses no demotion: rule 5's count axis): settled with the head un-demoted. *The honest cost*: a genuine statement decoded in a session's first moments re-points when the sweep reaches that peer — a profile fetch later online, up to the budget offline — and a session that ends first keeps the bare add durably, the residual the parked-statement paragraph already states, now reaching anchored peers for that window as well as un-anchored ones. *The outrun rule's other residuals* — its second to fifth — are untouched: a peer the harvest cannot read settles `NoProfile` or a refusal at once, and what is held about them settles as before. Shared Rust end to end — `ChainWitness`'s two offline arms, `PeerAnchorSweepState::run_pass`, the `ParkedStatementRedrive` seam's two shared-lib implementations and `fauna_mls::{redrive,settle}_parked_successions` — so no app owes a line; the witness reports the arm as `awaiting_harvest`. Pinned by `witness.rs::{a_statement_that_beats_the_sessions_harvest_waits_for_it, an_unseeded_settle_releases_the_wait_without_another_store_read, the_pass_redrives_every_settle_arm_and_only_a_settle, the_sweep_attempts_a_peer_the_search_box_is_hiding}`, `fauna_mls_backend_tests.rs::a_settle_that_seeded_nothing_redrives_the_parked_statement_and_announces_no_seed` and `harvest_wait_arming_tests.rs` (armed behind a launched sweep and before the first poll; never armed without one).

**The residual grade, stated plainly:** against a seed thief, every source available to a first-contact consumer is temporal — a profile minted (or a group created) inside the compromise window is thief-authored, and no signature can distinguish it; that is the TOFU grade the paragraph above already ratifies, entered knowingly. What the harvest is *strictly better than* is every URL-pinning alternative: the signed profile is tamper-proof against the channel host, the group creator, and every third party, none of whom hold the identity key. Against the owner's own home nest it holds only because two doors are shut, and the writer-side one alone is not enough — the nest can sign a profile directly with the delegated authoring sub-key D10 leaves in its hands, with no owner edit anywhere in the story, which is what rule 1's delegated arm refuses. The other door is the writers': every profile edit re-signs a base that nest serves, so a writer admits only an envelope-verified base of its own or of a predecessor its registry records (witnessed at the ceremony, or proven from the landed statement's own `new_sig`), and carries `recovery_head` and `nests` only from one its own identity key signed ([`../ui/profile.md`](../ui/profile.md) § After an identity succession, the successor RE-PUBLISHES → the admission rule, amended 2026-09-19). A peer that never registered a kit has no `recovery_head` to harvest and statements about them stay unverifiable (correct — they have no chain to anchor); a peer whose profile carries no `nests` entry yields a head but no dial domain, so a chain that has advanced past the harvested head degrades to the bare add until a domain source exists. **The `nests` producer landed 2026-08-16**: `fauna_client_profile::publish_recovery_head` — the landed-registration profile mirror every app already owes — now also fills the primary home `nests` entry when the list is empty (fill-if-absent only: a `nests` list the user authored is never displaced; the entry carries the dialed URL plus the TOFU-pinned `nest_actor_id` when the caller holds one, resolved app-side because the pin store is a connection-layer concern). So every kit registration from that date publishes the dial domain the harvest's rule-3 arm consumes; profiles published before it, and identities that never re-register, still lack one — the degrade-to-bare-add sentence above keeps describing those. **The third residual — cross-nest reach — is PERMANENT as of 2026-08-17: the anonymous profile read was put to the user and DECLINED.** `fauna.profile.get` is `User|Admin` and answers only for identities homed on the serving nest, so a consumer can harvest a group peer only where a transport to that peer's home nest already exists — in practice the same-nest read, which works today. Serving profile bytes (display name, bio, avatar/banner hashes, links, `nests`, `recovery_head`) to anonymous pre-identity callers, rate-limited through the anonymous-surface gate the way `fauna.recovery.registration.chain` already is, was the one candidate that would have closed it; it is declined, and a trimmed twin omitting `recovery_head`/`nests` was offered in the same ask and is covered by the decline. So a cross-nest peer degrades to the bare add until a transport exists, exactly as the no-`nests`-entry case above does — this is now a ratified end state, not a gap awaiting a build. **Do not re-ask**; what the ask laid out is recorded in § Don't do these, and a future session that thinks the reach has become load-bearing re-opens against that record rather than re-deriving the trade. The considered-and-rejected alternatives are recorded in § Don't do these.

Succession takes effect **immediately** on submission — no pending window. (The RecoveryKey needs no delay-and-veto: the thief cannot hold it. Delay exists only in the seed-initiated replacement flow above, where an asymmetric veto credential exists.)

## Enforcement on the home nest

Submission rides a **pre-identity** kind (the owner may have no working session — the thief can revoke sessions and invoke the seed-signed emergency lockout; a RecoveryKey-signed submission is exempt from both). The nest validates the statement against its stored registration chain, then in **one transaction**:

1. Records the succession (an `actor_successions` table: `old_actor_id` → `new_actor_id`, statement bytes, `seq`, `succeeded_at`) — the row every enforcement point consults.
2. Creates/re-points the account: new `users` row inherits tier and (via the two-step handle move) the handle; admin role rows move; **authority-conferring rows re-point** (see the scope table); attribution/history rows stay under the old id.
3. Revokes every session/bearer of the old identity, cancels any pending seed-initiated RecoveryKey replacement, and deletes the old identity's seed-escrow blob (§ Seed escrow — the old kit retires with the old identity). The socket half of that revocation spares **one frame on the submitting connection** so this ceremony can answer the client that asked — the shape, its bound and why no other kind gets it are [`../architecture/transport-connection.md`](../architecture/transport-connection.md) § Connection lifecycle → *Revocation teardown*'s.
4. From then on **refuses the old key**: `fauna.auth.handshake` / `fauna.auth.verify` / the emergency-lockout kind return a `superseded` refusal naming the new actor_id and where to fetch the statement; inbox/forward/content writes authored by the superseded key are refused (self-describing verification alone would keep accepting them — the refusal is this table consult). A superseded identity as the *recipient* of an inbox arrival is not refused but re-pointed to its terminal successor before routing — the delivery plane's rule, owned by [`succession-repoint-axis.md`](succession-repoint-axis.md) § Re-key scope, the delivery-plane blockquote. **The registration doors consult the table too (ruled 2026-09-24): every kind that can create a `users` row — `fauna.account.register`, `fauna.account.invite_request.submit`, `fauna.auth.claim_admin` — answers the same `superseded` refusal**, placed after the signature and before every account gate, exactly as the bearer-minting ceremonies place it. Until then those doors leaned on the retired identity's handle-less `users` row answering `actor_exists`; that row goes with the chain when the successor is deleted (`../architecture/account-data-plane.md` § Nest-side requirements item 1), so a refusal resting on it would have reopened registration to a thief-held retired key the moment the owner deleted the account. `actor_successions` is `Policy::Retain` and outlives every deletion, which is what makes it the right row. **One deliberate exception, ruled 2026-08-24: `GET /api/v1/export` does NOT re-check supersession.** The refusal list above exists at the *credential* plane, and for succession that plane already closes every path a thief could take to the export door — step 3 revokes the sessions, tears down the sockets, and burns the eviction token — so a use-time gate would be redundant for the threat (unlike the export route's suspension/lockout re-checks, which exist precisely because those standings revoke nothing). It would also be destructive: the old id's rows are the old id's **content** — the `Succession::Stay` audit row is keyed on `old_actor_id` and exported there, every attribution/history row stays under the old id (step 2) — and the sanctioned post-succession door to that archive, an **admin-re-issued eviction token** (`succession-aftermath.md` § the eviction-token fallback), authenticates through exactly this route with the one credential the refusal plane never consults. Gating the old id would leave that archive exportable by nobody without building a successor-side view, which may NOT be a second `ACTOR_TABLES` entry keyed on `new_actor_id` (the double-emission rule, `account-data-plane.md` § cluster 14) and is not wanted while the old-id route stands. Pinned by `export_api.rs::the_identity_plane_rides_including_the_published_key_halves` — a superseded actor's export carries its own `Stay` row.
5. Serves the chain: a pre-identity `succession` lookup kind answers for any old actor_id (peers hold old ids; discovery must work *from* them), and `by_handle` resolves to the successor.

The nest is **enforcer and distributor, never authorizer**: it cannot forge a succession (no RecoveryKey), and a hostile nest can at worst withhold the statement — which the client-side propagation in [`succession-propagation.md`](succession-propagation.md) § Propagation routes around.

## Don't do these

- **Don't wire the dormant `KeyRotation` struct** (old+new dual-sig, no recovery factor) — a thief holds the old key; that shape is takeover, not recovery. Retire it.
- **Don't store the RecoveryKey secret anywhere the identity seed unlocks** — no device keystore, no account-state plane row, no cloud sync. Offline only, no exceptions, including "encrypted" copies.
- **Don't make the nest (or any admin) a succession authorizer.** The nest enforces and distributes; authorization is the RecoveryKey signature, verified end-to-end by peers and members. An admin-mediated re-point is an account-takeover primitive.
- **Don't add a seed-only succession path** ("no RecoveryKey registered, but let the seed rotate itself with a delay") — between two seed holders there is no winner, only DoS or coin-flip. No RecoveryKey → no succession; that degradation is honest.
- **Don't let any consumer treat `old_sig` as load-bearing** — its presence must never upgrade trust (a thief can always produce it) and its absence must never block a valid succession (the loss case has no old key until escrow restore).
- **Don't rewrite historical attribution** — old-key content is genuinely old-key content; continuity is a display concern driven by the statement, not a data migration.
- **Don't anchor a member's verification on channel-delivered URLs** (the Welcome's inviter-supplied `nest_url`, the roster's channel-home-served `node_url`, the consumer's own nest relaying either) — each is chosen by the asserting party, the adder, or the channel host, and pinning one hands that party a false-`Proven` primitive; they are admissible only as fetch *transport* for the signed-profile harvest (§ The succession statement), where the envelope signature carries the trust. The "ask your own nest to resolve the actor" variant was checked and is doubly dead: the nest holds no actor→handle/URL knowledge at all for a foreign group peer, and the residue rows it could expose (`channel_foreign_members.home_nest_id`, unproven `nest_addresses`) are mintable by any User-class caller — the same hole behind a trusted-looking surface. **The same rule covers a handle the room home served for a participant row** (the id-keyed roster read, 2026-09-10): its `@domain` is a dial target chosen by the channel host, so it is display only and never tier 2's anchor — § The succession statement → *which participant handles anchor tier 2* (ratified 2026-09-15).
- **Don't allowlist `fauna.profile.get` (or a trimmed twin of it) to anonymous pre-identity callers** — put to the user 2026-08-17 and declined, which closes the harvest's cross-nest reach as a permanent residual (§ The succession statement → *the peer-profile harvest*) rather than a gap awaiting a build. What the ask laid out: the two surfaces that look like precedent are both narrower than the ask was. The ActivityPub actor route is public *per account and opt-in* — a profile is readable there because that user enabled federation — and `fauna.recovery.registration.chain` is directory data rather than personal content. Allowlisting the profile read would instead have made every account on every nest anonymously readable whether or not its owner opted into anything public, and would have handed unauthenticated callers `recovery_head` and the `nests` list — identity-infrastructure metadata that lets a caller enumerate where a given identity is homed. The narrowed twin omitting those two fields was offered alongside and is covered by the same decline. If cross-nest harvest reach ever proves load-bearing, re-open against this record rather than re-deriving it.
- **Don't put a self-asserted handle/domain in the MLS leaf credential to close the member-anchor gap** — considered 2026-08-10 and rejected: it is the same temporal-TOFU grade as the signed-profile harvest (the identity key signs the credential, and a thief holds it), for the price of an MLS wire-format surface, a version-compat ceremony, and no coverage of existing leaves. If the harvest's reach ever proves insufficient, re-open against that record rather than re-deriving.
