**The reach pillar binds on every inbox-write path (2026-07-10).** Thesecurity review (tracked internally) found two bypasses — the slice-3a/3c gates were correctly written but installed on one delivery path while siblings wrote the same inbox. Both closed: the decision is one shared primitive, `fauna_core::data::supervised_reach_verdict` (+ `unauthenticated_reach_verdict` for the sender-less cross-nest Welcome), composed nest-side by `routes::reach_floor` from the two facts the recipient's own nest owns (stored contact edge, stored guardian policy). Three callers, no bypass: the social inbox core (`deliver_inbox_payload_core`), the same-nest MLS Welcome plane (`welcome_deliver_core`, inbound AND outbound), and the federation Welcome twin. The `schema == "group/v1"` exemption is deleted outright (owner: `groups.md` § Security Properties); initiation is told by *this nest's* `actor_channels` roster, never the sender's self-declaration. Honest v1 bound: a ward with `contact_approval = on` cannot be *reached* by a cross-nest Welcome at all (the wire names no signed sender to approve); the ward initiates outbound to approved cross-nest contacts. Knocked arrivals are held in the knock (`knocks.payload`, capped) and released on the guardian's accept. Also closed: `fauna.knocks.{unblock,dismiss}` refused for a ward under `contact_approval` (reads stay open). The suspended-guardian finding is fixed and superseded by the general dispatch-time suspension fix (a suspended actor resolves to no caller class; owner: [`admin.md`](admin.md) § 2 Users → *Cutting a user off*); `deny_if_suspended` remains as defense in depth.

**All three enforced reach knobs now bind (2026-07-10).** With 3b landed, every `guardian_policies` column the reach pillar owns is enforced nest-side: `contact_approval` + `federation_contact` at the routing floor, `feed_sources` at the bridge-connect handlers, and `unknown_sender_mail` at the mail gate's two ingress points. The mail gate is the pillar's one knob whose subject is not an actor, so it composes a separate primitive (`supervised_mail_verdict` over `guardian_mail_allowlist` + `guardian_mail_sent_msgids`) rather than routing through `reach_floor`, which keys on a `ContactStatus` between actors. They share the fail-closed shape, never the code.

**The null-reverse-path bypass is closed (2026-07-11).** The 3b verify-review's new finding — `MAIL FROM:<>` bypassing both `reject` and `hold` — is fixed per § The mail gate's null-path rule: `MailIngress::NullReversePath`, `is_known_mail_sender` no longer fail-opens on the empty address, and the goal-doc/docstring "must hold against a non-conforming bridge" overclaim is corrected to the real TCB boundary.

**…and its replacement correlation is now unforgeable (2026-07-12).** The fix above recognized a legitimate bounce by the *address* the report claimed to bounce, which the 2026-07-12 verify-review broke: an address the ward has mailed is often public — a guardian's, a school's — so an address is not something a report can be authorized by. The gate now authorizes on the **original Message-ID** the report names, matched against a per-ward `guardian_mail_sent_msgids` set seeded at the same two outbound chokepoints as the allowlist (§ The mail gate owns the mechanism, the two honest residual bounds, and the rejected bouncer-domain alternative). The address is no longer authorizing; the `dsn_recipient` hint that carried it for an older nest left the wire with the compat-remnant sweep (2026-09-24, `../architecture/version-compatibility.md` § Dimension 2). **This required a second fix in shared Rust:** the Conversations compose path minted a **predictable** id (a timestamp plus a per-process counter), which is *guessable* (so the new correlation would have been forgeable) and *collision-prone* across a user's devices (a dedup-key collision hides a message from a later import — `mailbox-migration.md` § Dedup keys on the Message-ID). It is now 128 random bits (`fauna_conversations::rfc5322::new_message_id`), matching what the SMTP submission path already stamped. Proof: nine null-path tests in `conformance_family_mail_gate.rs` (tier_3) — including the forged-but-address-correlated regression pin — plus six `db::family` unit tests (per-ward scoping, retention, the unsupervised no-op), the `supervised_mail_verdict` unit arms, the wire round-trip + pre-field-bridge decode pin, and the Go `DsnCorrelation` tests.

**…and that correlation is now consumable and mint-bounded, not durable (2026-07-12, second verify-review pass).** A learned Message-ID is not a durable credential: ids leak beyond recipients via RFC 5322 threading (`References:` reaches every later thread participant), the population the gate targets, so the rule is per § The mail gate's three bounds: a per-id consumption budget (remote recipients + 2, spent atomically per delivery), seeding only sends with ≥ 1 remote recipient, seeding only Fauna-minted-shape ids (a third-party MUA's id has unknowable entropy — undecidable as "weak?", decidable as "ours?"; that ward's bounces are held for guardian release). The 30-day window moved onto the correlation probe itself (the seed-side prune only fires when the ward next sends, so a ward who stopped sending was correlatable indefinitely). The same pass struck the "a costume without a delivery-status part cannot dress mail as a bounce" inference: that check is well-formedness, and the gate's security is the unguessable id plus the budget, nothing else. Proof: three new tier_3 pins (budget exhaustion, MUA-shape never seeds, in-domain-only never seeds — all confirmed RED first) plus the reworked `db::family` unit arms (read-path window, budget never restacks, the native mint stays seedable).

**…and one correlated delivery no longer buys anything beyond itself (2026-07-13, third verify-review pass).** The budget bounded the correlation's *volume*; the *consequence* is bounded too — a ward's reply to a correlation-delivered report never allowlists that report's author. Per § The mail gate bound 4: the MTA extracts the report's address-header set (additive `dsn_report_addresses`), a delivered correlated report records it per ward (`guardian_mail_correlated_origins`, schema v17), and the outbound auto-seed declines those addresses — the reply sends, the allowlist is untouched, the guardian's approve overrides; an implausible address set (empty / > 16) holds without burning budget. The same pass closed the sibling finding — that bound 3's mint gate was a shape test, not a provenance test: its predicate was a shape test that MD5/`uuid4().hex` lookalikes of unknowable entropy also pass, so the mint is now **self-describing** (`fauna_mail::msgid`, one implementation for both stamp paths: 96 random bits ‖ verifiable 32-bit tag, still 32-hex for older nests) and the seed *verifies* provenance. Proof: three new tier_3 pins (the reply-to-correlated-report escalation, the no-address hold with budget intact, the stuffed-address hold — the escalation pin and the db-level lookalike pin confirmed RED first) plus `db::family` origin arms (decline, guardian override, window expiry, unsupervised no-op, both cascades) and the `fauna_mail::msgid` golden-vector/flip-a-bit unit arms; Go `DsnCorrelation` address-extraction tests.

## Goal

A household nest can admit a **supervised account**: a full Fauna account for a minor that carries a **guardianship link** to another account on the same nest. The guardian sets a bounded, transparent oversight policy — who can reach the child, what flagged content their apps collapse or block, when their apps are usable — and receives category-level notifications, **never content**. For young children, a guardian can hold full visibility by enrolling one of their own devices into the child's account — the existing multi-device machinery, transparent in the child's device list. When the child grows up, **graduation** converts the account to an ordinary full account in place: same identity, same data, no migration.

Supervised accounts are an *account-level relationship*, not a nest mode: every nest is sealed-at-rest by construction (there is no storage-mode axis, `storage-modes.md`), and supervised accounts exist identically on any deployment shape (`storage-modes.md` § Don't do these — no special-cased "family nest").

## The trust shape — guardian ≠ admin (ratified 2026-07-09)

The access asymmetry that oversight requires attaches to the **guardianship link between two accounts**, never to the admin role. Four standing invariants survive verbatim:

1. **The admin still administers capacity, not content.** No admin page gains a content-facing power; a guardian who is also the admin (the usual household case) exercises guardianship through the Family surface, as the linked guardian *account*, not through the admin shell. The tier stays the only quota control (`admin.md` § Don't do these).
2. **The nest still decrypts nothing.** No family-safety mechanism gives the nest a decrypt path (`storage-modes.md` § Don't do these). Content-level enforcement runs only where plaintext legitimately exists: the child's own clients, post-decrypt (`content-scoring.md`).
3. **Social-content enforcement stays user-side** (`moderation.md` § Categories & enforcement). For a supervised account, "the user side" is defined to include the guardian's policy — the guardian policy is *additional obligations for that account*, exactly the "parent-linked obligations" extension the moderation framework reserved. Nothing is removed nest-wide for anyone else.
4. **Supervision is transparent by construction.** The supervised user always sees that they are supervised, by whom, and what the policy is (the supervised indicator, § App surface). There is no silent-surveillance mode; a guardian-enrolled device is an ordinary, visible row in the child's device list.

The one *deliberate, bounded* exception to "user always controls their data": a supervised account cannot unilaterally remove its guardianship link or the guardian's enrolled device — that is the feature (what that covers for the device, at the nest and on the account's client-held fleet: § Full visibility for young children → *The device marker*). The bound is that the exception is declared at account creation, permanently visible, scoped to the named guardian account, and ends at graduation.

## The guardianship link (firmed 2026-07-09)

- Set **at admission**: the invite code, request-approval, **or direct admission** that admits the account carries the supervised designation + the guardian's actor, alongside the tier — riding the `admin.md` § 2 lifecycle unchanged, the same way the `backup` tier did (no new admission primitive). **All three account-creation paths carry the designation** (ratified 2026-08-30 — when this bullet was firmed the two invite paths were the only admission paths; `fauna.admin.users.create` landed 2026-07-31, and excluding it would make direct admission the one path that irreversibly mints never-supervisable accounts, exactly the household trap the no-conversion rule below exists to avoid — a parent admitting their own child through the Admit section is the designation's most natural use). The one bounded exception: a **handle-less** direct admission (the blank-handle legacy-compat shape, `public-mode.md` § Registration & Identity) cannot carry the designation — both invite paths always admit under a handle, and a supervised admission is a full admission. v1 does not convert an existing full account into a supervised one (avoids the "retroactively supervise a private adult account" trap); that conversion, if ever wanted, is its own future decision.
- **Same nest only (v1).** Guardian and supervised account live on one nest. Cross-nest guardianship (divorced households, kid-at-boarding-school) is future work with real federation implications — explicitly out of v1.
- Stored in **nest state** (§ Wire & data shape), surfaced to both parties' apps; every knob it carries is app-UI-configured (no-operator invariant).
- **The supervised designation IS the link row's existence** — no separate `users.supervised` flag, so "supervised with no guardian" is unrepresentable (the make-bad-states-unrepresentable technique, `../architecture/nest/common.md` § Client-state recoverability).
- **Exactly one guardian per supervised account in v1**; the storage shape (composite key on both actors) deliberately keeps co-guardians representable later without a schema rebuild, but v1 admission creates one link and v1 code may assume at most one. **Chains are impossible by construction**: the guardian named at admission must be an existing, non-supervised, non-suspended user on this nest (and not the admitted actor itself) — and since supervision is only ever set at admission, an account that already guards someone can never itself become supervised.
- **Removal = graduation** (below). A guardian being evicted or deleting their account must not strand the child in an unsupervisable half-state — the eviction/delete gate in § Lifecycle gates enforces link resolution (transfer or graduate) *before* the guardian account can go away (a client-state-recoverability concern, `../architecture/nest/common.md` § Client-state recoverability).

## Wire & data shape (v1, firmed 2026-07-09)

**Nest tables — two, separating the relationship from the policy document:**

- `guardianships(supervised_actor_id, guardian_actor_id, created_at)`, primary key on both actors, both referencing `users(actor_id)`, indexed on `guardian_actor_id`. Existence of an edge is plaintext-floor routing metadata (same class as the contact-relationship subset — `../architecture/encryption-at-rest.md` § Plaintext floor § Relationships).
- `guardian_policies(supervised_actor_id PRIMARY KEY, …policy columns…, updated_at)` — the reach-policy document (§ Guardian policy pillar 1 owns the column semantics). Per supervised account, not per link (co-guardian-proof); grows by additive columns (auto-reconciled — `../architecture/nest/common.md` § Upgrade safety). **Every default is the unsupervised-equivalent value**: a fresh link with an untouched policy changes nothing until the guardian tightens it — the guardian opts *into* each restriction (works-out-of-the-box). (One deliberate refinement since 2026-08-24: an admission carrying an **age band** starts the fresh row from `ReachPolicy::age_band_defaults(band)` instead — the guardian choosing the band IS the opt-in, and the dial stays defaults-only per § The account age band; a band-less admission keeps this rule verbatim.)
- `guardian_mail_allowlist(supervised_actor_id, address, added_by, created_at)`, primary key on `(supervised_actor_id, address)` — the ward's known-mail-sender set (§ The mail gate); rows are floor metadata (routing addresses). **Seeded from the ward's own outbound mail on both submission paths**, which do not share a chokepoint: `fauna.email.send` (the native Conversations app — the ward's actor is the authenticated caller) and `fauna.bridges.enqueue_outbound_mail` (an external MUA over SMTP submission — authenticated as the *bridge*, so the ward is resolved from the envelope sender). Seeding only one leaves replies to the child's own mail gated; seed the full recipient set on each, *before* the in-domain/remote partition, since same-nest recipients never enter the outbound queue.
- `guardian_mail_sent_msgids(supervised_actor_id, message_id, created_at, correlation_budget)`, primary key on `(supervised_actor_id, message_id)` — the ward's **sent**-Message-ID set, seeded at those same two chokepoints from the message's own `Message-ID:` when the send has ≥ 1 remote recipient and the id **verifies** as Fauna-minted (§ The mail gate owns all four bounds, incl. the self-describing mint). It is what a null-reverse-path delivery-status report must name — and *consume*: `correlation_budget` starts at remote-recipient count + 2 and each correlated delivery atomically spends a unit — to be delivered rather than held: the allowlist's *address* says the ward once mailed someone, which anybody may claim; a verifiably-minted Message-ID is unguessable, though threading discloses it (hence the budget). Retained 30 days, enforced on the correlation probe itself and pruned on seed.
- `guardian_mail_correlated_origins(supervised_actor_id, address, created_at)`, primary key on `(supervised_actor_id, address)` — the address-header set of each **delivered** correlated null-path report (§ The mail gate bound 4). The outbound auto-seed declines these addresses, so the ward's reply to a correlated report cannot allowlist its author; the guardian's approve overrides. Budget-bounded at the source (each recording cost the report a correlation-budget unit), read through the same 30-day window, refreshed on repeat delivery, pruned opportunistically. All three mail-gate side tables are a **no-op for an unsupervised account** (an `INSERT … WHERE EXISTS (guardianship)` guard), so no adult's mail metadata is retained and no caller need pre-check the link; all cascade with the ward and are dropped at graduation.
- `guardian_mail_holds(message_id PRIMARY KEY, supervised_actor_id, sender_address, created_at)` — the envelope sidecar for a held message (§ Reach approvals). Not a hold *store*: the message's presence in the ward's held mailbox is the hold; this row records the one fact the guardian's queue must render and the approve path must allowlist. Cascades with the ward, and is released (not dropped) at graduation.
- `guardian_transfers(supervised_actor_id PRIMARY KEY, proposed_guardian_actor_id, initiated_by, created_at)` — the pending transfer proposal (§ Graduation & transfer). The primary key **is** the one-pending-per-ward invariant; a new proposal replaces the row. Indexed on `proposed_guardian_actor_id` (the incoming-prompt read + the target-deletion cascade). Rows older than the 7-day window are expired lazily (invisible to reads, pruned opportunistically). Floor metadata — the same relationship class as `guardianships` itself.
- **v1.x additions (designed 2026-07-15):** `guardian_policies` grows additive defaulted columns for the content pillar (`content_nsfw` / `content_spam` / `content_phishing` / `content_commercial`, closed enum `inherit|collapse|block`, default `inherit`; `content_notify` boolean, default off) and screen time (`screen_window_start` / `screen_window_end` / `screen_daily_minutes`, nullable integers, default NULL) — auto-reconciled onto existing DBs; **built** (schema v19, § Implementation status today Slice B). ## Guardian policy — the enforcement split

The policy document the guardian edits has three pillars, each enforced where enforcement is *honest* under E2EE:

1. **Reach policy — nest-enforced (routing floor; holds even against a non-conforming client). Firmed 2026-07-09.** Who can reach the child and whom the child can reach. All of this is plaintext-floor metadata — recipient routing and contact-relationship existence are on the floor (`../architecture/encryption-at-rest.md` § Plaintext floor) — so the nest enforces it **without any content access**, and it cannot be bypassed by using a different client. The policy is a per-recipient extension of the nest's existing canonical reach gate, and it lives in shared Rust beside the disposition mapping it extends: `fauna_core::data::supervised_reach_verdict` (with `unauthenticated_reach_verdict` for the sender-less cross-nest Welcome), a pure function of *(contact status, reach knobs, ingress origin)*, composed nest-side by `routes::reach_floor`. **Every path that writes a recipient's inbox on behalf of a named sender passes through it** — the social inbox core, the same-nest Welcome plane, the federation Welcome twin — and it consults nothing the sender declares about itself. It follows the established "mirror the routing-hot subset into a plaintext-floor table" pattern (`inbox_modes` precedent). The v1 policy document (`guardian_policies` columns; defaults = unsupervised-equivalent):

   | Knob | Values (default first) | Enforcement point |
   |---|---|---|
   | `contact_approval` | off / **on** requires guardian approval for new contact edges, both directions | Inbound: a new-party arrival still stores its knock + pending edge exactly as today, but **acceptance authority moves to the guardian** — the ward's `fauna.knocks.accept` is refused with an approval-pending error and the knock surfaces in the guardian's queue (§ Reach approvals). Outbound: the ward's send to a non-contact is refused with a typed "ask your guardian" error; the guardian pre-approves via `fauna.family.contact.add`, and the in-app ask is § Child-initiated contact requests (v1.x; nest half built 2026-07-16). Share-knocks inherit the gate through the same contact-status machinery. |
   | `unknown_sender_mail` | allow / hold / reject | **Two enforcement points, one per ingress class — see § The mail gate below.** `reject` refuses the recipient at the SMTP `RCPT TO` stage (per-recipient, so a message addressed to both the ward and an adult still reaches the adult); the in-domain twin refuses the sending Fauna user with a typed error. `hold` is a *placement* decision at the sealed-ingest path (mail is already sealed to the ward — the gate never touches content), routing the message to the ward's **held mailbox** and surfacing **envelope metadata only** (sender address, timestamp — never subject-derived text, never content) in the guardian's queue; approve releases to INBOX + allowlists the sender, deny discards. **Known sender** = the `guardian_mail_allowlist` ∪ addresses the ward has itself sent mail to (outbound auto-seeds the allowlist, so replies to the child's own mail always flow; cold inbound from strangers is what's gated). |
   | `federation_contact` | on / off | At federation ingress (`fauna.federation.inbox.deliver` already funnels into the same shared delivery core; the handler passes a federation-origin flag). `off` = actors on other nests cannot *initiate* contact with the ward — arrivals from non-contacts are suppressed; existing accepted/confirmed cross-nest contacts continue to deliver. |
   | `feed_sources` | allow / block | At the per-source external-account connect + follow-add handlers (Bluesky / Nostr / ActivityPub per-user bridge accounts). `block` = the ward cannot connect new external sources or add follows on them (typed refusal); already-connected sources keep flowing until the guardian removes them from the Family surface. **Scope, stated on any surface that exposes this knob:** this gate stops *new* connections and follows and says nothing about messages arriving over an account already connected — that is the `unknown_peer_dm` knob's job (§ The bridge-DM gate). The old wording stated an *absence* ("not separately gated in v1"), which stopped being true on **2026-08-14**, when tui shipped `family-policy-unknown-peer-dm-select` and a guardian could first set the knob; the caption (`family.policy_feed_sources_caveat`) now names the governing control instead. The six apps still lifting the select render the same caption — it names the control, never its position. |
   | `unknown_peer_dm` *(v1.x, firmed 2026-07-16 — nest half built 2026-07-17; **settable from tui 2026-08-14**, linux + web 2026-08-22, macOS + iOS 2026-08-27, android 2026-08-28; windows still lifting the select)* | allow / hold | At every inbound bridge-DM write path (today: the Nostr sealed-DM ingest, gated at the one shared seam both its delivery paths funnel through), per § The bridge-DM gate: `hold` marks a conversation from a cold external peer held for guardian review; the ward's own outbound DMs seed the known-peer set, so replies always flow. No `reject` arm — pulled ingest has nothing to bounce from. |
2. **Content policy — client-enforced (post-decrypt; conforming clients).** Category floors over the canonical label set (`moderation.md` § Categories & enforcement — e.g. `nsfw` → block rather than collapse) evaluated by the same shared classifiers every app already runs post-decrypt, plus guardian **Notify**: the guardian is notified with *category + count, never content*. Mechanism: § Content policy (v1.x design) below. **Honest limitation, stated here on purpose:** client-side enforcement binds only conforming clients — a determined teenager with a modified client bypasses the content pillar (not the reach pillar). This is the same trust model as on-device Communication Safety in commercial ecosystems, and Fauna does not pretend otherwise; the mitigation for younger children is pillar 3 + the guardian-enrolled device.
3. **Screen time — client-enforced.** Usage windows / daily limits rendered and enforced by the child's apps (§ Screen time (v1.x design) below). Same conforming-client caveat as pillar 2.

A fourth pillar — per-feature availability/quota gates for controversial-class features (payments, bulk file sharing) — is owned by [`../architecture/dynamic-features.md`](../architecture/dynamic-features.md) (its guardian tier, **built 2026-08-11**); it rides `fauna.family.policy.update` as an additive sub-document per § Policy-update compatibility, and is nest-enforced, so it carries no conforming-client caveat. **The guardian edits a ward's feature limits from the ward's policy screen — built on tui (2026-10-04), the other six apps following in one batched trickle-down:** § App surface → *Feature limits* owns the slot, the IDs and the binding sentence, § Wire & data shape the editor's seed and the write rule, and the screen opens the one shared editor rather than a family-specific form ([`../architecture/dynamic-features.md`](../architecture/dynamic-features.md) § Authoring surfaces owns what that editor expresses). The FFI policy conversion still drops the `features` sub-document in both directions (`libs/fauna-ffi/src/family.rs`, the `FfiReachPolicy` conversion), now by rule rather than by omission: the plane reaches the native apps through the shared seam's own faces, never through the reach form.

## Where logic lives

Default is shared Rust (priority #2); a per-app shape needs a stated platform reason.

- **Reach enforcement (the routing floor).** Shared Rust — `fauna_core::data::supervised_reach_verdict` (+ `unauthenticated_reach_verdict` for the sender-less cross-nest Welcome), a pure function of *(contact status, reach knobs, ingress origin)*, composed nest-side by `routes::reach_floor`. Pillar 1 above owns the rule; the nest is the only enforcement point.
- **The two closed knob enums + their fail-closed wire parse.** Shared Rust — `fauna_core::data::{UnknownSenderMail, FeedSources}`. `from_wire` is the single implementation of the fail-closed rule (§ Implementation status — *"an unrecognized value renders as the strictest option (`hold` / `block`), never the permissive one"*); each carries its own `FAIL_CLOSED` option, and they are **deliberately separate types** because the two knobs have different value sets *and different strict options* — a merged value→label map renders one knob's value under the other knob's label.
- **The policy editor's option catalogs + value labels + summary lines.** Shared Rust — `fauna_core::format::{unknown_sender_options, feed_sources_options, unknown_peer_dm_options, unknown_sender_label, feed_sources_label, unknown_peer_dm_label, reach_policy_summary}`, returning `ReachPolicyOption { value, label }` / `PolicySummaryLine { label, value }` over `LocalizedText` keys (the `ConflictPolicyOption` pattern: shared Rust owns the option *set*, the app owns the widget). Callers holding a `ReachPolicy` use `ReachPolicy::summary_lines()` (`fauna-protocol`), which forwards to the same function. Summary lines are **structured, never pre-joined** — each app keeps its own line layout.
- **App glue (per-app by platform necessity).** Only the widget: building the select from the catalog, resolving a `LocalizedText` key through the platform's i18n pipeline, and — for an index-addressed picker (linux's `ComboRow`) — the index ↔ option bridge, whose out-of-range fallback degrades to the knob's `FAIL_CLOSED` option, never index 0. Apple's `Picker` binds `selection` directly to the wire value (`.tag(option.value)`, no index bridge needed); its automation layer's label→wire reverse lookup (`FamilyReachPolicyFormat.wire(forPickedLabel:)`) matches by rendered label text against the catalog, same shape as web's `<select>`. The knob *values*, their order, their labels, and the fail-closed rule are never re-declared per app.
- **Write validation.** Shared Rust — `ScreenTimePolicy::validate` (§ Screen time), so clients pre-validate identically to the nest's `policy.update` refusal.
- **Recognising the guardian-approval refusal.** Shared Rust — `fauna_protocol::RpcError::is_guardian_approval_required()` over `CODE_SUFFIX_GUARDIAN_APPROVAL_REQUIRED`. The nest emits the refusal *namespaced by the handler that refused* (`fauna.{inbox,knocks,contacts,conversations,bridges,account}.guardian_approval_required`), and an app offering "ask your guardian" cares only that a guardian must approve — so the namespace set can grow nest-side without seven apps each learning the new one. Matched as a whole suffix over a single non-empty namespace, never a `contains()`, so a future `…_required_notice` code cannot trip it. **Over UniFFI the verdict crosses as its own variant**, `FfiError::GuardianApprovalRequired { msg }`, routed once in `fauna-ffi`'s shared `stringify` (the seam every authenticated call already goes through, as for `NestIdentityChanged`) — the localized `msg` carries no wire code, so a native app cannot classify the refusal from its text and must never match a string. Only that variant offers the ask; every other failure stays `General`.
- **The `family-approval-item` row's display-text rule (§ Reach approvals).** Shared Rust — `fauna_core::format::approval_display_text(kind, peer_address, peer_handle, summary, bridge_id, operation, target)`, wrapped by `FamilyApprovalEntry::display_text()` (`fauna-protocol`) for native callers; exposed over UniFFI (`fauna-ffi/src/family.rs::approval_display_text`, which takes the whole entry) and wasm (`approvalDisplayText`). Each kind reads the one field that actually names its item: **`peer_address`** for the two envelope-class kinds (`mail_hold`, `dm_hold` — both of whose `summary` is always empty, because a subject line or message preview is content sealed to the ward), **`peer_handle`** for a `contact_request` (which deliberately carries no message text at all — the ask is *who*, never *why*), **`summary`** for `contact`, and for `feed_source` **the grant's own key**, composed by `feed_source_display_text` as `bridge · operation · target` (the target omitted for a `link`) with the ward's label quoted after it — `bluesky · follow · did:plc:… — “Grandma's photos”`. The card names what Approve grants because the grant matches the triple, never the label (§ Feed-source approvals): a label-only card let a ward have a harmless name approved and redeem the grant for an object the guardian never saw. Every part is ward-supplied, so each passes `fauna_core::control_chars::sanitize_plain_line`; the composition is language-neutral (wire identifiers plus a quoted label, as the envelope kinds show a raw address), so the one string reaches all 7 apps with no per-app formatting, and a `feed_source` with an empty key yields `None` even when it carries a label. **An empty field yields `None` for every kind**, not just the held null-path `mail_hold` that first motivated the rule: a blank row beside live Approve/Deny buttons is the defect, and its causes are per-kind (a null-path bounce, an off-nest `contact_request` peer with no local handle to join, a knock sent without a summary). The caller renders its own localized `family.approval_no_sender` placeholder for the `None` case — the one piece that stays client-side, since it needs the platform's i18n pipeline.

## The mail gate (re-firmed 2026-07-09 against current code)

`unknown_sender_mail` is the one reach knob whose subject is an **email address**, not an actor — which forces two enforcement points rather than one. *(This section supersedes the first firming pass, which specified a single nest-side ingest gate. That pass rested on three premises the code refutes: mail has no quarantine store — `SpamDisposition::Quarantine` merely routes to `Junk`; a per-recipient refusal at ingest is impossible because ingest runs inside SMTP `DATA`, where one reply covers every recipient; and the full envelope sender never reaches the nest, only `sender_domain`. The intent below is unchanged; only the mechanism is.)*

**Known-sender resolution** is the shared primitive both points call: an address is *known* to a ward iff it appears in `guardian_mail_allowlist` for that ward. The ward's own outbound mail auto-seeds it — see § Wire & data shape for the two submission paths that must both seed, or replies to the child's own mail get gated. The auto-seed has one carve-out: it **declines an address recorded as a correlated-delivery origin** (bound 4 below — a reply to a null-path report whose author chose the addresses is not the ward choosing a correspondent); the guardian's own approve/pre-approve is exempt.

1. **`reject` → the `RCPT TO` stage.** SMTP resolves recipients one at a time at `RCPT TO` but emits a single reply for `DATA`, so a refusal decided at ingest would bounce the message for *every* recipient — a stranger mailing both the ward and a parent would deny the parent their copy. The refusal therefore rides the nest's existing per-recipient RCPT resolver (`smtp-server.md` § Inbound policy stack — the resolver already returns a typed per-recipient reject with an SMTP code), which the guardian policy extends with a reject for an unknown sender. That reject reuses the resolver's **generic `550 "User unknown"`** reply, indistinguishable from a nonexistent address, so the refusal never advertises that this mailbox is a supervised ward (`../architecture/nest/network-exposure.md` § Rulings F5). The nest stays the authority; the bridge only relays the verdict. The **in-domain twin** (a Fauna user mailing a ward on the same nest — no SMTP in the path) refuses the *sender* with a typed `fauna.email.guardian_approval_required`, mirroring the outbound refusal `contact_approval` already uses.
2. **`hold` → the sealed-ingest placement.** A hold is not a refusal; the message is accepted, sealed to the ward, and *placed*. It lands in the ward's **held mailbox** — `Guardian Review`, a mailbox of its own, never `Junk`, because the ward must be able to tell "your guardian is reviewing this" from "this looked like spam" (transparency, § The trust shape invariant 4), and because the per-user spam scorer independently re-files INBOX→Junk (`mail-spam.md`) and would race a release. It is deliberately **not** a seventh standard mailbox: the six are seeded for every actor and carry RFC 6154 SPECIAL-USE attributes, of which there is none for "held" (`../behavior/imap-server.md` § Standard mailboxes); this one is auto-created on a ward's first hold, so no other account ever sees it. The nest **recomputes the hold verdict at ingest** from the ward's stored policy rather than trusting a *placement flag* carried by the bridge, and the hold **outranks the ward's own filter rules and the spam disposition**, or the message would be one `FileInto INBOX` rule away from escaping. The converse holds too: no filter rule files a message *into* the held mailbox — [`email-filters.md`](email-filters.md) § Email filter rules owns which mailboxes a `FileInto` may target. The honest trust boundary (ratified by the 3b security review): the gate holds against **external senders and non-conforming clients** — it cannot, and does not claim to, hold against a compromised MTA bridge, which is inside the mail TCB (the bridge already supplies the recipient actor, the sealed body, and the envelope facts the verdict keys on; the gate can never be stronger than the bridge feeding it, exactly as the reach floor can never be stronger than the client that authenticates).

**While a hold is live, the held message is the ward's to read, never to relocate.** The ward's own MUA reaches the held mailbox over IMAP, and an ungated IMAP surface would let it move or expunge the message out from under the guardian's queue — no fresh reach (the message is already readable by design, § Reach approvals), but the same erosion of guardian authority that forced gating `fauna.knocks.{unblock,dismiss}`. So the nest's IMAP handlers refuse, **keyed on the live `guardian_mail_holds` sidecar row, never on mailbox membership**: MOVE out of the held mailbox with any held message refuses the whole command (MOVE is atomic per RFC 6851) with a typed error the mail bridge maps to a tagged `NO`; EXPUNGE silently *skips* held messages rather than failing (routine expunges and CLOSE keep working for everything else in the mailbox); and the mailbox name itself is protected alongside the six standard names against CREATE/DELETE/RENAME (protected ≠ standard: still not seeded, still no SPECIAL-USE attribute). A non-held message the ward parked in the mailbox moves and expunges freely, COPY stays open (the original and its hold are untouched), flags — including `\Deleted` — stay writable, and reading is never gated (§ The trust shape invariant 4). The guardian's own release/discard and graduation run server-side below the IMAP handlers, so the gate never touches them.

**System-generated mail is never gated.** Notices the nest itself writes (its own DSNs, forwarder NDRs, security notifications) reach the same ingest core as external mail; a held delivery-failure notice would strand the ward. The gate keys on an explicit ingress origin (`MailIngress::System` vs. `MailIngress::Sender`), mirroring the `ArrivalOrigin` discriminator the contact-approval gate already threads through the shared inbox delivery core.

**The null reverse-path is gated; a report *correlated to a message the ward sent* is not (closed 2026-07-11; correlation hardened 2026-07-12).** Anyone on the internet can claim `MAIL FROM:<>`, so the empty envelope address must never read as a known sender — treating it as one was a full bypass of both `reject` and `hold`. The one legitimate null-path arrival is a **remote MTA bouncing mail the ward sent**, and it is recognized by *correlation*, not by the empty address.

**The correlation is the original Message-ID, and it must be, because an address is not a secret.** The MTA perimeter (which alone sees the plaintext) parses the message iff it is a genuine RFC 3464 `multipart/report; report-type=delivery-status`, and extracts two things: the `Final-Recipient`/`Original-Recipient` **address** the report claims to bounce, and — from the report's `message/rfc822` / `text/rfc822-headers` part — the **Message-ID of the message being reported on**. The nest delivers only when that **Message-ID is one the ward itself sent**; every other null-path message is held.

The address is a *hint only*, and keying on it was a real hole (found by the 2026-07-12 verify-review, closed the same day). Allowlist membership establishes just *"the ward once mailed that address"* — **not** that the report is genuine: an address is not a secret, and the addresses a supervised child mails first (a guardian's, a school's) are among the most guessable there are. Envelope authentication is no substitute either — a report can be perfectly SPF/DKIM/DMARC-aligned *for its own domain* while claiming to be *about* someone else's mail, so those checks say nothing about the claim being made. **The rule that now holds: a null-path delivery is authorized by an unguessable, verifiably-minted Message-ID the ward itself sent — never by an address.** A Message-ID a Fauna path minted is 96 random bits plus a verifiable provenance tag (`fauna_mail::msgid`, via `fauna_conversations::rfc5322::new_message_id` and the Go submission stamp), so it cannot be *guessed* — but it can be *learned*, which is why the correlation is bounded on every axis below.

**A Message-ID's disclosure set is the transitive closure of its thread, not its recipient set** — the second verify-review pass (2026-07-12) broke the earlier "recipients are already allowlisted, so disclosure grants nothing" claim. RFC 5322 § 3.6.4 threading copies the id into `In-Reply-To:`/`References:` on **every reply in the thread**, and standard MUAs accumulate the whole `References:` chain — our own compose path emits both headers. So the ward mails Alice, Alice replies-all adding Carol, and Carol — never mailed by the ward, correctly *not* allowlisted — now holds a correlatable token; a CC, a forward, a quoted `message/rfc822` attachment, or a public list archive leak it the same way. That population — adjacent to the child's correspondence but not chosen by them — is exactly who the gate exists to exclude, so the correlation is bounded four ways, each failing toward *holding*:

1. **Consumed, never durable.** Each seeded id carries a **correlation budget** of `remote recipients + 2` (room for one bounce per remote recipient plus a delayed/failed DSN pair); every correlated delivery atomically spends a unit, and a report past the budget is **held**. A leaked id is then at most a handful of deliveries — not an open arbitrary-content channel — and over-holding a legitimate extra bounce merely queues it for guardian release, never loses it.
2. **Seeded only when a bounce is possible.** An in-domain-only message never enters the outbound queue, so no remote MTA can legitimately bounce it; seeding its id would mint a correlatable token with zero legitimate use. The seed requires ≥ 1 remote recipient.
3. **Seeded only for ids a Fauna path *verifiably* minted.** Whether a third-party MUA's id is *guessable* is undecidable; whether *we* minted one is not — but that must be **verified, not inferred from shape** (2026-07-13: 32-lowercase-hex is also the exact shape of an MD5 hex digest and of `uuid4().hex`, common third-party local parts, some derived predictably). So the mint is **self-describing**: both stamp paths share one implementation (`fauna_mail::msgid`, called by `fauna_conversations::rfc5322::new_message_id` and by the Go submission server over UniFFI) emitting `local = hex(96 random bits ‖ 32-bit tag)`, `tag = sha256("fauna-msgid-v1" ‖ random)[..4]` — still 32 hex, so an older nest's shape predicate keeps seeding it — and the seed predicate (`fauna_mail::msgid::is_fauna_minted_msgid`) **recomputes the tag**. An innocent lookalike passes with probability 2⁻³². The tag is deliberately **keyless**: the property needed is distinguishability from innocent third-party formats, not unforgeability — the only party who could deliberately mint a tag-valid weak id into a ward's seed set is the ward's own modified client, and a colluding ward gains nothing (any ward can already admit any correspondent by mailing them). A supervised ward on a third-party MUA therefore never correlates: their real bounces are held and the guardian releases them — the exact trade the gate exists to make. *(Compat: a pre-tag client's plain-random mint stops seeding on a new nest — that ward's bounces hold for guardian release until the client updates; fails toward holding, loses nothing.)*
4. **A correlated delivery buys delivery, and nothing else** (2026-07-13: the budget bounded the *volume*; the ward's reply to a delivered report still auto-seeded the report's attacker-chosen `From:` into the allowlist — permanent, unbudgeted, guardian-invisible). The auto-seed's premise — *the ward chose to mail them* — is false when the ward replies to a report whose **author** chose the addresses. So: the MTA extracts the report's own reply-target headers (`From:`/`Sender:`/`Reply-To:`/`To:`/`Cc:`, plus the `Mail-Reply-To:`/`Mail-Followup-To:` a mutt-class MUA silently addresses a one-click reply to ahead of `From:`/`Reply-To:` — everything a one-click reply or reply-all can be addressed to; `dsn_report_addresses` on the ingest wire), a **delivered** correlated report has that set recorded per ward (`guardian_mail_correlated_origins`), and the outbound auto-seed **declines those addresses** for 30 days — the reply still sends, it just doesn't bootstrap the allowlist; nothing legitimate is lost because nobody replies to a genuine `MAILER-DAEMON` bounce. The **guardian's own approve/pre-approve overrides** the decline (that consent is exactly what the suppression preserves the need for). A correlated report whose address set is **empty or implausibly large** (> 16 — a genuine DSN carries `From: MAILER-DAEMON@…` and little else) is **held without probing**, so a costume built to dodge the suppression burns no budget; the Go MTA truncates its extraction one past the cap so an over-stuffed list still arrives as over-stuffed. Matching on the recorded *addresses* rather than the reply's `In-Reply-To:` is deliberate: the report's author controls its own `Message-ID:` header and could simply omit it (no `In-Reply-To:` on the reply), or park an accomplice in `Cc:` for a reply-all — the address set catches both, and what escapes it (body text saying "write to me at X") requires the ward to *initiate* a fresh compose, which is the allowlist premise working as designed, the same residual as any content that reaches a child.

**The delivery-status-part requirement is well-formedness, not a security boundary.** The MTA extracts facts only from a structurally genuine RFC 3464 report, but an attacker composing the whole message trivially includes a `message/delivery-status` part — three lines of MIME. Nothing may treat that check as a second factor: **the gate's entire security is Message-ID unguessability (of verifiably-minted ids), the consumption budget, and the bound-4 rule that a delivery buys nothing beyond itself.**

**What a correlated delivery still buys — stated, not papered over.** Within the budget, a thread participant holding a leaked id can land a handful of arbitrary-content messages in the ward's INBOX with **no guardian-side record** — a correlated `Deliver` writes no hold row and no notice, deliberately: the queue is a *decision* surface and there is nothing to decide about an already-delivered message, while for a *genuine* bounce a notification would be pure noise, and the nest cannot tell the two apart (that indistinguishability is the whole premise). What bounds it: the budget caps the volume, bound 4 caps the consequence at zero — no allowlist entry, no future access — and every message costs a unit of a budget only the ward's own sending replenishes. A guardian-side "N null-path reports were auto-delivered this month" count is derivable from the stored budgets if v1.x wants the visibility surface. Two more deliberate residuals: an attacker can **poison** a future correspondent by listing their address in a correlated report's headers — that correspondent's replies then hold for guardian release until the window lapses or the guardian approves (fail-toward-holding, guardian-recoverable, and each poisoning delivery costs budget); and the recorded set cannot catch a ward hand-composing to an address named only in the report's *body* — that is the child initiating contact, which no reach gate claims to police.

**Rejected alternative — binding the report to the bouncer's authenticated identity** (requiring the DKIM/SPF-passing domain to equal the `Final-Recipient`'s domain). It needs a new wire field regardless (`AuthVerdicts` carries pass/fail *kinds*, never the domain that passed), it over-holds every legitimate smarthost/forwarder bounce, and it is strictly weaker than an unguessable token. Not built; don't re-litigate. (Pair-binding the report's `Final-Recipient` to a recipient of the named message was likewise considered and set aside: a thread participant sees `To:`/`Cc:` anyway, so it adds nothing the budget doesn't.)

**Everything else with an empty sender is held** — no report, a report costume without a delivery-status part, a report that returns no message, a report on a Message-ID the ward never sent (or one whose budget is spent), a report whose address-header set is empty or stuffed past the plausibility cap (bound 4), or a pre-field MTA that carries no id at all — under `hold` *and* under `reject`: a per-recipient `550` cannot fire for `<>` (RCPT precedes `DATA`, where DSN-ness is undecidable) and a post-`DATA` refusal could never be bounced to a null path, so `reject` downgrades to the hold — the strictest verdict that loses no mail, the same rationale as the unrecognized-knob rule below. A held null-path message carries a truthfully **empty sender address** on the wire; every app's queue row renders the localized no-sender label (`family.approval_no_sender`) instead of a blank title.

**The ward's sent-Message-ID set** (`guardian_mail_sent_msgids`, § Wire & data shape) is seeded at the **same two outbound chokepoints as the allowlist** — after each path's in-domain/remote partition, which supplies the ≥ 1-remote gate and the budget size — and retained **30 days**, comfortably past the point any real MTA is still retrying (RFC 5321 § 4.5.4.1 puts give-up at 4–5 days). **The window is enforced on the read path** (the correlation probe carries the age predicate), not merely by the seed-side prune, which only fires when the ward next sends — a ward who stopped sending must not keep old ids correlatable indefinitely. A bounce older than the window is *held*, not lost. The set is deliberately **not** the outbound queue's `original_msgid`, which keys on the sender *address*, carries forwarded and nest-generated rows the ward never composed, and has its own lifetime: a security correlation must not rest on another table's incidental retention.

**An unrecognized knob value fails closed to `hold`.** Evolution is additive-everywhere, so a newer nest may write a value an older binary cannot parse (`../architecture/version-compatibility.md`). `hold` is the strictest verdict that still loses no mail: `allow` would silently void the guardian's policy, `reject` would bounce mail the sender cannot re-send.

**What the nest may see.** Both points read the envelope sender address, which is plaintext floor (`../architecture/encryption-at-rest.md` § Plaintext floor § Routing tables — "Envelope FROM"). Nothing else about the message is read: the body and the index hint are sealed to the ward before the nest sees them, so "envelope metadata only" is not a discipline the nest must observe — it is the only thing the nest *has*.

## Reach approvals (firmed 2026-07-09)

The guardian's approval queue **reuses existing pending-state machinery — no new hold store**:

- **Contact approvals** are the ward's pending knocks, read guardian-side. `fauna.family.approvals.list` returns, per ward, the knock rows whose acceptance contact-approval mode has moved to the guardian; `fauna.family.approvals.decide { approve }` performs the accept (creates the accepted edge) or block on the ward's behalf — the same underlying operations the ward's own knock review would run. The ward still *sees* the knock with an approval-pending state (transparency; § App surface).
- **Mail holds** are messages placed in the ward's held mailbox (§ The mail gate). The message itself *is* the pending state — there is no second lifecycle to reconcile — and a small envelope sidecar keyed on the placed message id records the sender address the guardian sees and the approve path allowlists; the sidecar row and the placement commit in **one transaction**, so neither can exist without the other. The queue entry carries envelope metadata only (never content — the message is sealed to the ward). Approve = move to INBOX + allowlist the sender; deny = discard. Because the ward can see the held message in their own mailbox, a hold is never silent breakage: the ward learns *that* mail is being held and by whom, without the guardian ever seeing its content.
- The queue is typed — v1 shipped `kind: contact | mail_hold`; the v1.x follow-ons (design firmed 2026-07-16) extend it additively with `contact_request`, `feed_source`, and `dm_hold` (§§ Child-initiated contact requests / Feed-source approvals / The bridge-DM gate below own each kind's semantics). A mail hold's peer is an **address**, not an actor, so entries carry an additive `peer_address` alongside `peer_actor_id` rather than overloading `summary` — keeping `peer_actor_id` honest; a `dm_hold`'s external peer id rides the same `peer_address` field (with an additive `bridge_id`). Each kind names its item with a different key: a `contact` or `contact_request` by `peer_actor_id`, a `mail_hold` by `message_id`, a `feed_source` by `(bridge_id, operation, target)`, a `dm_hold` by `(bridge_id, peer_address)`. **Deciding a `mail_hold` is keyed on the message, never the address**, so approving one held message never sweeps every other message from that sender (the sender does become known, so *future* mail flows).
- **The kinds gate differently on the policy.** `contact` and `contact_request` entries surface only while `contact_approval` is on — the knob is what moves acceptance authority to the guardian, and with it off the ward reviews their own knocks and contacts freely (a pending ask is moot). `feed_source` entries surface only while `feed_sources` is `block`, for the same reason. `mail_hold` entries surface **whenever a hold row exists, whatever `unknown_sender_mail` currently says**: the knob governs whether *new* mail is held, never whether already-held mail can be drained — otherwise relaxing the knob would strand held messages in a mailbox no queue entry could release. `dm_hold` entries surface while `unknown_peer_dm` is `hold` and need **no** drainage rule: a DM hold is a *computed* placement, not stored state (§ The bridge-DM gate), so relaxing the knob releases every held conversation by construction — nothing strandable exists.
- **Graduation releases, never drops**: any items still pending when a ward graduates are released to the now-full account's ordinary knock/quarantine review (no user-data loss). Mail holds are moved to INBOX **before** the link is dropped — the crash-safe order, since a crash between the two leaves the mail delivered and the link intact, and the replay is a no-op.

## Child-initiated contact requests (v1.x design, firmed 2026-07-16)

The in-app half of pillar 1's outbound arm: today the ward's send to a non-contact is refused with the typed "ask your guardian" error and the guardian pre-approves out-of-band (`fauna.family.contact.add` — "can I talk to X?" across the dinner table). This flow makes the ask in-app: the ward asks, the ask lands in the guardian's existing approvals queue, approve mints the same accepted edge `contact.add` would.

- **Wire:** `fauna.family.contact.request { peer_actor_id }`, called by the **supervised account**. Typed refusals: caller not supervised; `contact_approval` off (with it off the ward contacts freely — the refused-send error that carries the affordance can never have fired); `peer == ward`; peer already guardian-`blocked` (the guardian decided; the block is visible on the ward's own contact list). A peer already `accepted`/`confirmed` returns plain `ok` with no row — nothing to approve. **A request carries no message text, deliberately**: the ask is identified by *who*, never *why* — ward-authored free text is a disclosure surface the queue does not need (§ Don't do these).
- **Store:** `guardian_contact_requests(supervised_actor_id, peer_actor_id, created_at)`, primary key on both actors (§ Wire & data shape). It cannot ride `contacts.status` — `store_knock` upserts `"pending"` onto the same `(ward, peer)` key, so an incoming knock would clobber the ask — and it cannot ride `knocks` (a knock is the *peer's* held arrival, not the ward's intent). `guardian_transfers` is the precedent: a pending-intent row, lazily expired — here after **30 days** (hard-coded; between transfer's 7 and the knock TTL's 90: an ask should survive a guardian's vacation week and not outlive a season). Pending asks are capped per ward (hard-coded, generous) so a conforming-client bug can never flood the queue.
- **Doorbell:** creating a row (and only creating — the primary key makes a re-ask while pending a quiet no-op) rings the guardian once via the ordinary notification machinery, exactly as Guardian Notify does.
- **Queue:** kind `contact_request`, keyed on `peer_actor_id`; the entry carries an additive `peer_handle` (nest-joined for a local peer) so the row renders without a second lookup; `summary` stays empty. **Approve** = the same `accept_contact_core` as `contact.add` (edge accepted; a matching pending knock is dismissed and its held arrival delivered) + the row dropped. **Deny** = the row dropped, and **deliberately not** a block: a knock-deny blocks an unsolicited stranger, but a request-deny refuses the *child's* ask — auto-blocking the named peer would punish a third party for the child's question. The child may re-ask; each re-ask after a deny re-rings, bounded by the pending cap and the guardian's patience being the point.
- **Ward transparency:** the supervised side of `fauna.family.status` carries the pending asks additively, so the refused-send surface can render "asked — waiting for your guardian" instead of a dead refusal.
- **App affordance (IDs user-approved 2026-08-13 (item 14, confirmed live with the user); ui.yaml owns them):** an "ask your guardian" action on the refused-send surfaces (`contact-request-guardian-button`) and the pending-ask state (`contact-request-pending`), on the contacts and profile pages; the guardian side reuses the existing queue rows unchanged (one `family-approval-item` row shape absorbs all five kinds — ruling user-approved 2026-08-13, item 14). **Built on tui's contacts page 2026-08-14** (the lead app; the profile page and the other six apps follow). Three rules the build settled, binding on every later leg: **(a) the ask is offered only on the *typed* refusal** — recognised through the shared `fauna_protocol::RpcError::is_guardian_approval_required()`, never a per-app string match, because the namespace names *which* handler refused (`inbox`/`knocks`/`contacts`/…) and only the suffix is stable. It crosses both app boundaries typed (2026-09-26): the UniFFI apps receive `FfiError::GuardianApprovalRequired` (`libs/fauna-ffi/src/lib.rs`, the shared `stringify`), and the SPA receives the rejection prefixed `guardian_approval_required: ` (`libs/fauna-wasm/src/rpc.rs::err_to_js`, read by `apps/fauna-web/src/lib/guardian-refusal.ts`), so no app ever reads the nest's message text to decide; painting the affordance on an ordinary transport failure would teach an *unsupervised* user that their account is supervised. **(b) The refusal is still an error** — the send genuinely did not happen, so it stays on `error-message`; what changes is that it is no longer a dead end. **(c) The pending state is durable, read from the ward's own `status.contact_requests`**, not a session flag: a ward who asked yesterday must see "asked — waiting" on reopening rather than a send button that would only be refused again. **The ask's own reply is a bare ack, so the app re-reads `fauna.family.status` in the same operation** — without that the durable state would not arrive until the next family-page visit or a restart, and the affordance would silently revert to a send button the moment the ward looked the same peer up again (a tier_3 reload caught exactly this). A failed re-read is not a failed ask — the guardian has been rung — so it must neither drop the asks already held nor un-render the pending state; a just-sent local flag is the fallback for that one case. Both are keyed on the *resolved peer*, so a new lookup drops the local flag and the durable list is consulted per peer — the app never offers to ask about somebody the ward did not name.

## Feed-source approvals — the grant flow (v1.x design, firmed 2026-07-16)

Turns a `feed_sources = block` refusal into a request the guardian can approve, without the nest ever replaying a bridge operation. **Approve mints a grant, never performs the operation**: `fauna.bridges.link` is interactive (OAuth redirects, provider dialogue) — parameters staged at ask time go stale, and a nest-side replay would run a bridge operation *as the ward* hours later. The grant is **single-use and time-bounded**; the ward's client redeems it by simply re-performing the operation, which now passes the gate.

- **Wire:** `fauna.family.feed_source.request { bridge_id, operation, target, label }`, called by the **supervised account**. `operation` is the closed set `link | follow | feed` (refuse anything else); `target` identifies the object — the follow id for `follow`, the feed URI for `feed`, and **empty for `link`** (approving a link approves *connecting that bridge*; the OAuth mode is mechanism, not scope). `label` is an optional, length-capped display string (the petname / feed name the ward sees) — **display-only, never authorizing**: the grant matches on `(bridge_id, operation, target)` exactly. Typed refusals mirror the contact-request set (not supervised; `feed_sources` not `block`; pending cap).
- **Store:** `guardian_feed_requests` (§ Wire & data shape): `approved_at NULL` = a pending ask, set = a grant. Pending asks expire lazily after 30 days; a grant expires **7 days** after approval (a grant is a fresh guardian decision; the redeeming retry is prompt).
- **Redeem:** on `feed_sources = block`, the gate (`require_feed_sources_allowed`) looks for a matching unexpired grant and **consumes it before the operation runs** — an atomic delete, then the provider call. Consume-first is the crash-safe order: a crash after the consume burns the grant and the ward re-asks (fail-closed); perform-first would leave a double-spend window. A grant outliving a knob flip to `allow` is inert (the gate no longer fires).
- **A grant is never spent on a retry that was doomed anyway.** The gate runs after every *deterministic, read-only* check the operation makes (shape validation, the bridge's existence in the registry, its availability) and immediately before the operation itself. Spending the grant on a bridge that is unconfigured or temporarily down would trap the ward in a loop it cannot exit — re-asking only mints another grant the next retry burns the same way — so a grant survives a transient outage and still redeems once the bridge is back. This does **not** soften consume-first: once the gate has run, a failure *inside* the operation still burns the grant, because the nest cannot know whether a side effect landed on the far side of a provider call. The two failure classes are distinct — a doomed pre-condition costs nothing, a reached-and-failed operation costs the grant.
- **Queue:** kind `feed_source`, keyed `(bridge_id, operation, target)` (additive entry + decide fields); `label` rides `summary` — it is the ward's own ask naming the *thing being approved*, not third-party content — but the card leads with the key Approve actually grants and only quotes the label after it as the ward's own words (§ Where logic lives, the `family-approval-item` display rule): a display-only label never stands in for the target. **Approve** = set `approved_at` + ring the **ward** once via the ordinary notification machinery ("approved — try again"): unlike a contact approve, a grant produces no organically observable state, so without the doorbell the ward could only poll. **Deny** = drop the row.
- **Ward transparency:** the supervised side of `fauna.family.status` carries the asks additively with their state (`pending | approved`), so the blocked bridges surface renders the ask/approved state in place.
- **App affordance (IDs user-approved 2026-08-13 (item 14, confirmed live with the user); ui.yaml owns them):** an "ask your guardian" action beside the `feed_sources` refusal on the bridges surface (`bridge-source-request-button`) with the ask/approved state in place (`bridge-source-request-state` — approved renders the "try again" prompt; the app never auto-retries); the guardian side reuses the queue rows. ✅ **Built on tui 2026-08-28, the lead app** (the other six follow). Three properties of that build are the transferable half. **(1) The supervision gate is DERIVED, not re-tested.** No app-side "is this account supervised" branch exists or should: `own_feed_requests` is gated on `supervised_by` where the status read folds it in, and the session-local refusal set fills only on the nest's TYPED `guardian_approval_required` suffix, so an unsupervised account cannot reach either branch. ## The bridge-DM gate (v1.x design, firmed 2026-07-16)

Closes pillar 1's stated honest v1 bound: inbound DMs riding an allowed, already-connected bridge account (the per-user Nostr / Bluesky / ActivityPub accounts) reach the ward with no reach gate — `contact_approval` cannot see them (an external peer is not an actor on this nest) and `feed_sources` only gates *new* connections. The gate is the mail gate's shape generalized: the subject is an external peer identity, not an actor, so it composes its own primitive beside `supervised_mail_verdict`, never through `reach_floor`.

- **Knob:** `unknown_peer_dm`, closed enum `allow | hold`, default `allow` (unsupervised-equivalent; § Wire & data shape). There is **deliberately no `reject` arm**: bridge DMs arrive by *pull* (relay subscription, poller) — there is no per-sender refusal stage to bounce from, so `hold` is the strictest verdict that loses no message, the same rationale as the mail gate's null-path downgrade. An unrecognized stored value fails closed to `hold` (nest parse and client render — the established knob rule; a fifth `fauna_core::data` closed enum, separate type per the two-knobs lesson).
- **Known peer** = a `guardian_dm_peers` row (§ Wire & data shape), per `(ward, bridge, peer)`, verdict `allow | block`. `allow` rows are **seeded by the ward's own outbound DM sends** (the mail-allowlist premise: the child initiating chose the correspondent, so replies always flow) — a guardianship-guarded no-op for unsupervised accounts that **never overwrites a `block`**. Guardian decisions write both verdicts.
- **Placement is computed, never stored.** Every inbound DM is stored sealed exactly as today — nothing is destroyed or withheld pre-decision. A conversation is *held* iff the knob says `hold` and the peer has no verdict row; the DM read surfaces (`fauna.bridges.conversation.rooms.list` / `inbox.fetch`, every bridge's rooms alike) carry an additive guardian-state marker the conforming ward client renders as held. Reading is never gated (§ The trust shape invariant 4 — the ward can read a held *mailbox* too). Because hold-ness is computed, there is no drainage rule and no strandable state: relaxing the knob releases everything by construction.
- **Queue:** kind `dm_hold`, one entry per held **conversation** — keyed `(bridge_id, peer_address)` (the external peer id string rides the existing `peer_address` field; envelope-class metadata only, the message content is sealed to the ward). **Approve** = verdict `allow`: the conversation releases and future DMs deliver. **Deny** = verdict `block`: already-stored rows remain the ward's to read (marked), and the ingest thereafter refuses **new** DMs from that peer before storage — refusing new arrivals is the RCPT-reject analogue, not destruction of stored data.
- **Outbound:** a send to a guardian-`block`ed peer is refused with the typed guardian-approval error; every other send flows and seeds the `allow` row — outbound stays ungated exactly as mail's does (cold *inbound* from strangers is what's gated).
- **Enforcement points, today and the standing rule:** three first-party inbound bridge-DM write paths are live (since 2026-10-03), and all three store through one function — the first-party leg seam's `bridge_legs::deposit_gated`, which composes this verdict, seals to the recipient key and deposits, in that order, so no leg can store a deliverable DM without it. Each leg hands that seam the peer identity its own transport proves (next bullet): **(1) Nostr** — the sync worker's and the `/nostr` relay inbox's gift-wrap ingest, keyed on the seal's pubkey, which the NIP-44 open that decrypted the rumor proves; **(2) Bluesky** — the DM worker's `chat.bsky` poll of a consume-side linked account, keyed on the DID the chat service states under that account's own OAuth session (a message's `sender.did`, or — for a copy of something the account sent elsewhere, decided from that same service-stated sender — the one-to-one conversation's other member); **(3) ActivityPub** — the inbox's direct `Create{Note}`, keyed on the activity's top-level `actor`, whose fetched key verified the delivery's HTTP signature, never the Note's self-claimed `attributedTo` (which must equal it, or the Note is dropped). **The rule binds every future inbound bridge-DM write path the moment it goes live** — it must compose this verdict before storing a deliverable DM, the same every-path discipline as the reach floor; wiring a new DM ingress without it is the same class of bypass the 2026-07-09 review found. **The deposit family is that next path (ruled 2026-10-02; built 2026-10-03 — `bridged_conversation_handlers.rs`, composed through the shared `bridge_dm_gate.rs` both bridge-DM families read):** `fauna.bridges.conversation.deposit` composes the verdict before storing, keyed `(bridge_id, far sender)` where the sender is what the **authenticated** bridge principal asserts for its own network — the bridge is inside the TCB of the account it rides (bound (b) below), exactly as the nest's NIP-44 open vouches for Nostr's — and the migrated Nostr leg hands that seam the seal's pubkey it proves today ([`../ui/conversations.md`](../ui/conversations.md) § Where logic lives → *The `Bridged` adapter*).
- **The peer identity the verdict keys on must be one the peer could not have chosen (ratified 2026-08-30, after a HIGH finding).** Composing the verdict is not enough; *what it is composed against* is the other half of the guarantee, and it is the half that is easy to get wrong because the wrong field is usually right there and more convenient. A bridge protocol hands the ingest several candidate identities, and typically only some are authenticated: derive the peer from one the transport itself proves — an identity a decryption, a signature check or a session binding already established — never from a field the counterparty merely *asserts* inside a payload. **This binds the discriminator as much as the value**: a branch that decides *which* candidate is the peer must not be steerable by the counterparty either, or they simply pick the arm whose value they control. Nostr's ingest is the worked example: the seal's pubkey is proven by the NIP-44 open that just decrypted the rumor, while the rumor's own `p` tag is plaintext signed by nobody — and the ingest chose between them by comparing that `p` tag, so a `block`ed peer who spelled it as anything else (uppercase, a third party, or omitted) landed their DM keyed on their own string, defeating "a `block`ed peer's new DMs never land" with no fault, race, or cooperation from the ward. When a bridge offers no authenticated peer identity at all, that bridge cannot carry this gate and must not be wired to it silently.
- **Honest bounds, stated on any surface that exposes this knob:** (a) the *placement* binds conforming clients — a modified ward client renders held DMs it could already read; what holds nest-side regardless: a cold peer never auto-becomes known, the guardian always sees the queue entry, and a `block`ed peer's new DMs never land. (b) The gate can never be stronger than the bridge account it rides (the nest custodies the bridge keys and is inside that TCB already — the mail gate's bridge-boundary rationale, unchanged).
- **App affordance (IDs user-approved 2026-08-13 (item 14, confirmed live with the user); ui.yaml owns them):** the knob's select in the policy editor (`family-policy-unknown-peer-dm-select`); the held/blocked conversation state (`conversation-guardian-state`, scoped to the `conversations` page); the guardian side reuses the queue rows. **The marker has no host until bridge DMs render in the unified Conversations list** (user ruling 2026-09-25): tui and web (both since 2026-10-03) and linux and android (2026-10-04) list a bridge-DM conversation on their `conversations` page — macOS, iOS and Windows receive bridged rooms on it since 2026-10-04 but do not paint the marker yet, and web's older bridge-DM list on the `nostr` page, never where ui.yaml scoped this id, retired 2026-10-03 with its `nostr-dm-item` element. tui and web host it since 2026-10-03 and linux and android since 2026-10-04, on their unified list's bridged rooms (§ Implementation status today); on macOS, iOS and Windows it waits on that paint, owned by [`../ui/conversations.md`](../ui/conversations.md) § Where logic lives → *The `Bridged` adapter* (ratified 2026-09-05; built on tui and web 2026-10-03), and renders on each app's unified list once bridge DMs reach it — never on the `nostr` page as a stand-in. The enforcement it would report on is nest-side and holds without it: a held thread stays fully readable, a `block`ed peer's new DMs never land, and a send to one is refused.
- **The un-deny surface (IDs user-approved 2026-08-13, item 14):** a deny writes verdict `block`, and the flip back is already wire-supported (`approvals_decide { kind: "dm_hold", approve: true }` — idempotent, not queue-scoped), but **no read exposes the denied-peer set to the guardian**, so the family page owes a denied-peers list (`family-blocked-peer-item` / `family-blocked-peer-allow-button`, user-approved 2026-08-13) **and the nest owes a small follow-on**: the guardian side of `fauna.family.status` (or a sibling read) carries the ward's `block`-verdict `guardian_dm_peers` rows additively. Until that read lands the list cannot render — build the read with the first app leg. ✅ **BOTH LANDED 2026-08-28, together as that sentence directs** (tui, the lead app; macOS, iOS, linux, web and android followed 2026-09-26, windows still to come; the UniFFI apps read it as `FfiFamilyWardInfo::blocked_dm_peers` since 2026-09-26, the SPA through the serde pass-through). The nest half is `FamilyWardInfo::blocked_dm_peers` — the ward's `block`-verdict rows, carried additively on the guardian side of `fauna.family.status`, inside the same guardianship-guarded loop as `devices` so the same gate covers both. **`allow` rows are deliberately NOT carried**: an allowed peer is simply un-held, and listing them would turn a list of *decisions the guardian can undo* into a roster they must curate. The verdict is matched by parsing through the shared `DmPeerVerdict` rather than comparing to a literal, so an unnameable stored verdict is simply not `Block` and drops out — the same fail-quiet the gate itself takes. The app half renders one `family-blocked-peer-item` per row, its text the peer id (the only name this nest has for an external bridge peer — which is why the gate exists at all), each with its own `family-blocked-peer-allow-button` carrying **that row's** `(bridge_id, peer_id)`: a button addressing the first row's peer would un-deny the wrong person while the surface still looked correct, which is why that is the property tui's unit tests mutation-prove and the cross-app un-deny journey proves on every app that renders the list (§ Implementation status today). ## Full visibility for young children — the guardian-enrolled device (sanctioned pattern, ratified 2026-07-09)

For a young child, bounded oversight is not enough; the guardian legitimately needs to *see* the account. Fauna's answer is **not key escrow**: the guardian enrolls one of their own devices into the child's account via ordinary multi-device enrollment (`devices.md`). That device is simply one of the child's devices — full read access with **zero new cryptography, zero new decrypt paths, and no key-hierarchy change** — and it is transparent by construction: it appears in the child's device list like any other device. At graduation it is removed by ordinary device revocation. This pattern is documented precisely so that no future session designs a guardian key-escrow mechanism to solve a problem the device model already solves (see § Don't do these).

**The device marker (design firmed 2026-07-09; build mechanics firmed 2026-07-15; the nest half + client seam built 2026-07-16 — § Implementation status today; NOT in v1 build scope).** Enrollment authenticates as the child's account, so the nest cannot by itself tell the guardian's enrolled device from the child's own — which would leave two promises unenforceable: "the child cannot unilaterally remove the guardian's device" (§ The trust shape) and graduation's auto-revoke. The firmed mechanism, still zero new cryptography: a `guardian_marked` flag on the ward's device row (additive `sync_devices` column), set/cleared only by the guardian (`fauna.family.device.mark`, link-authorized). The child's device list renders the mark (transparency — an additive field on the `fauna.sync.devices.list` reply); device removal by the supervised account refuses a marked row (guardian or graduation removes it); MLS removal stays client-driven exactly as `devices.md` § Removing a Device specifies. Two crash-safety rules bind the build: **(a) the removal refusal's predicate is `marked AND currently supervised`** — after graduation a stale mark is inert, so a crash that leaves one can never strand a now-full account with an undeletable device row (client-state recoverability); **(b) graduation revokes marked devices in its handler** (the DB transaction has no reach into live connections), **before** the link-drop transaction — the same release-first ordering as mail holds: a crash between the two leaves the device revoked and the link intact, and the replay is a no-op. The guardian removes the device by unmarking it, after which ordinary device removal (typically from the guardian's own enrolled device, which authenticates as the ward) proceeds. The enrolled-device pattern *worked* even before the marker shipped — the marker only adds the removal protection and the graduation auto-revoke; nothing about that gap ever justified escrow. **The nest half + client seam landed 2026-07-16; the ward-facing app legs (the marker badge + the delete refusal) landed on linux + web 2026-07-19 (windows + android joined the same day; apple joined 2026-08-11, the last of the 7 apps).** The nest gap that had blocked the guardian's mark control — `fauna.family.status` not yet carrying a ward-device-list — closed 2026-07-19 (`FamilyWardInfo.devices`, § Implementation status today); the guardian's own `family-device-mark-toggle`/`-item` control landed on linux + web 2026-08-01, android and tui the same day, apple 2026-08-11, and windows 2026-08-25 (the last app, § Implementation status today). **The refusal covers the credential, not only the row (ruled 2026-10-01; built 2026-10-01 — § Implementation status today).** `fauna.sync.device_grant.revoke` clears the very credential the deletion refusal protects, so its app/user arm — the account's own session naming another key — refuses a key that rides a marked row under the same predicate, `marked AND currently supervised`, with the same typed `fauna.sync.guardian_marked`. The self arm is untouched: a proof of possession by the named key is that device retiring itself, which the ward cannot forge. What sends that arm, and why a runtime skips a marked row before asking: [`../architecture/account-data-taxonomy.md`](../architecture/account-data-taxonomy.md) § The generation machinery → *Fleet-scope reclamation*, clause (4) → *The nest half follows merged state*. **A replacement is not refused: the device takes its row back (measured + ruled 2026-10-01; nothing to build).** `fauna.sync.device_grant.register` reaches the same credential. A grant is signed by the account's seed, which the ward's device holds, so a modified app can sign one for a key of its own and register it onto the marked row, and the nest takes it ([`../architecture/apps/sync-agent-credentials.md`](../architecture/apps/sync-agent-credentials.md) § Credential model owns what a grant register does to a row that carries another key). No stock app sends it: every app registers on its own machine's row. **Measured, on the nest's real kinds and with two real runtimes on one nest:** the row keeps its mark and carries the ward's key; the nest stops minting for the guardian's key; the bearer that key already minted is not revoked and lives out its hour; nothing is tombstoned. The guardian's device does not learn it from a pass, because its registration latch still names the row. It learns it from its next bearer mint, which the nest refuses; the refusal voids the latch, and its first pass after that registers the same grant on the same row, the principal unchanged. One write costs one registration. By reading, not measured: the device's account-plane sync rides that principal's connection, so a modified app that repeats the write stalls that sync on the guardian's device for as long as it repeats; what the app reads over its own sign-in is untouched, and the sync resumes by itself once the writes stop. **The ruling: the register stays open, and that is the bound.** The nest must keep taking two writes from the guardian's own device — the key the row already carries, registered again, and a different key, after a sign-out and sign-in or a principal succession — and it cannot tell the ward's write from the second: both are a session of the one account presenting a root-signed grant. **Refused: a register that changes the key on a marked row needing the current key's say** (a proof of possession, as the revoke's self arm takes). It protects whichever key is on the row, not the guardian's. The row is empty between a sign-out and the next sign-in, and at the moment the guardian marks it carries whatever was registered last; a ward's key written in either window would be held there by the marker against the guardian's own device until the guardian unmarks, which opens the row again. And it costs an honest family a stuck device whenever the old key is gone — a lost credential slot, a sign-out the nest never heard — for a protection a modified app walks around. **Why the revoke is refused and the replacement is not:** a revoke tombstones, so the key never registers again and the device must return under a successor principal, and refusing it costs no honest path, because a device retiring itself proves possession of its key; a replacement tombstones nothing, and a refusal of it cannot be told apart from refusing the device's own next key. **The revoke refusal reads the row, so a displaced key is outside it.** Once another key is on the marked row the guardian's rides no marked row, and the app/user arm revokes it. Measured: its bearers end, it is tombstoned, the running device reads itself removed from the account, and its next seed-holding assembly mints a successor principal and registers it on the same row, the ward's key displaced and the mark kept. Replace-then-revoke reaches in two calls what the refusal stops in one; it costs the device one enrollment, which is what a fleet-plane removal costs it (next paragraph), paced the same way — one rotation for each round the ward writes ([`../architecture/account-replica-posture.md`](../architecture/account-replica-posture.md) § The store device principal → *Principal succession after a device delete* owns the rotation and its cap).

**What the marker promises on the fleet plane — the device's read, not its membership (measured + ruled 2026-10-01; part (3) built the same day).** The marker is the nest's: it protects the device's row and its credential, and the nest can protect nothing else. Which devices are the account's fleet — the wrap targets of each generation, the devices admitted on the peer leg — is `fauna.state.device-set`, rows sealed where the nest cannot read them, in which any enrolled device may write an absorbing `Removed` row for any other and every reader excludes on it unconditionally ([`../architecture/account-data-taxonomy.md`](../architecture/account-data-taxonomy.md) § The generation machinery, the device-set kind, owns why). The guardian's enrolled device is one of the ward's devices, so the ward's device can write that row for it, and no nest check stands in the way. **Measured, two real runtimes on one nest, the guardian's row marked:** the stock Devices page gives the ward no way to write it there — the row gesture is refused by the nest and writes nothing, and the member-addressed door does not list a member its own roster row accounts for (it would list one bound to another of the account's nests) — but the door's by-key writer, which is what a modified app calls, accepts it. Afterwards the nest still lists the row, marked, and still mints bearers for its key. Every device excludes the guardian's from the fleet, and the ward's next write under the generation tip mints a generation wrapped to the ward's device alone. The guardian's device keys that generation from its escrow wrap in its next pass, unasked, and reads the row; a row it then writes is read by the ward's device. What it has lost is membership: it is no wrap target, no sibling tops it up, and by the kind's rules it is not admitted on the peer leg. As measured, before part (3) was built, it never found out — every pass answered its enrollment `Current`, and after six rounds and a restart it still held the removed principal — while signing that device out and in again enrolled a fresh principal on the same row, the mark kept, and it was a member again. **The ruling, in three parts. (1) The promise is the read.** "Cannot unilaterally remove the guardian's enrolled device" (§ The trust shape) means the ward cannot end that device's session, its place in their own device list, or what it reads. The first two are the nest's refusals above; where a refusal cannot reach — a credential replaced on the marked row, a displaced key revoked — the device takes its session back by itself (the replacement bound above). The third rests on the account's seed, which the device holds and no row can take back: a removed seed holder keeps full account access ([`../architecture/account-data-taxonomy.md`](../architecture/account-data-taxonomy.md) § The generation machinery → *Escrow recovery*, (4)). Fleet membership is not promised. A ward running a modified app can cost the device its membership, which is vandalism of their own account and reaches nothing the device reads. **(2) A full-visibility device holds the ward's seed.** Every app enrollment does today, and here that is load-bearing: a device enrolled without the seed ([`../architecture/account-replica-posture.md`](../architecture/account-replica-posture.md) § The store device principal, *Seed residency*) and then removed on the fleet plane has no escrow recovery and no successor, so every generation minted after its removal is closed to it for good, while its marked row stands. A build that lets an app enroll without the seed owes the guardian's device a seed-holding enrollment, or re-opens this ruling. **(3) The device returns by itself.** A seed-holding machine whose own device-set row reads `Removed` mints a successor principal and enrolls it on its own nest row, which here is the marked one, and a re-registration never clears the mark ([`../architecture/account-replica-posture.md`](../architecture/account-replica-posture.md) § The store device principal → *Principal succession after a device delete*, decision 1, the third trigger, owns the rule). The marker is why that trigger is needed: it keeps the grant, so the nest's revoked answer, which is what a removed machine otherwise mints its successor on, never comes. Nothing is shown to anyone and nobody has anything to do: the ward's device list shows the same marked row throughout. A ward who writes the row again gets the same answer again, at one enrollment each time. **Refused: a stock-app refusal** — the member-addressed door and its writer refusing a member whose roster row is marked. The door offers only a member no roster row accounts for, so wherever it offers the guardian's device there is no marked row tied to that member to read, and wherever the marked row is tied to it the door does not offer it. The check would never fire in a stock app, and a modified app does not run it. **Refused: a plane-side rule** — readers honouring a `Removed` row for a guardian-enrolled member only when the guardian wrote it. It needs an enrollment that proves who enrolled it, which is the new cryptography this pattern exists to avoid, and it makes exclusion depend on who wrote the row, which the device-set kind rules out because two replicas would then disagree for good. The loss it would prevent is measured above at one re-enrollment.

**How the guardian's rows NAME a ward device (ruled 2026-08-02 — the display-identity ruling).** The ward's user-chosen device labels are sealed content under the ward's own root (the paths-are-content ruling — `path-sealing.md` § Sealed names & paths, whose ruled gap (c) records the mechanics), and the guardian is a *different actor*: they hold no key for that root, may never be handed one (no guardian key escrow — § Don't do these), and no disclosure carve-out re-plaintexts a deliberately sealed field on precisely the surface this doc is most protective of (§ Don't do these — no content-derived text to the guardian). So the guardian's `family-device-mark-item` rows identify a device by its **display identity**, not the ward's label: the nest populates `FamilyWardDeviceInfo.label` with the device's **code** — the hex `device_id`, elided to the narrowest width that separates every device in *that ward's list* — with the machine-authored plaintext label rendered beside it when one rests (`fauna_core::format::device_display_identities` — `value-formatting.md` § Device display identity), and the apps render it verbatim. The rows are guaranteed non-empty and **per-device distinct**, and that distinctness is *earned, not asserted* (corrected 2026-08-05): **`device_id` is chosen by the client, not derived by the nest** — `fauna.sync.register` stores the caller's hex verbatim, and the ward is handed the guardian device's id by this very pattern, since the enrolled device appears in the ward's own `fauna.sync.devices.list`. A ward can therefore match any *fixed-width* prefix of it, so the chooser takes the **whole device list at once** and widens the code only as far as an actual collision demands (from 12 characters up to the full id); a per-device function cannot keep this guarantee and no longer exists. The same set-relative rule covers the collision that needs no adversary at all — `SELF_REGISTER_LABEL` (`"fauna"`) is the label every self-registering client passes, so two of a ward's devices routinely carry the same machine label. The control loses nothing that matters: the toggle keys on `device_id`, and once marked, the mark is the durable name that survives every seal — but the mark cannot be its *own* discriminator at the moment the guardian first picks a row, which is precisely why the code must already distinguish them. **Full visibility is unaffected, which is what makes this coherent rather than merely austere:** the guardian's enrolled device authenticates *as the ward* (this section's whole pattern), holds the ward's root, and renders every label fine — visibility lives exactly where this doc places it, and only the guardian's *own-account* Family page is id-only. The e2e pin is `test_family.py::test_family_guardian_device_mark_toggle` (guardian rows key on short ids; the ward-side leg still keys on real labels under the ward's own custody).

## Graduation & transfer (firmed 2026-07-09; transfer consent handshake ratified 2026-07-12)

**Graduation** — supervised → full account is an in-place conversion: `fauna.family.graduate` deletes the `guardianships` + `guardian_policies` rows in one transaction, revokes a *marked* guardian device (once the marker exists; until then the now-full account holder removes it via ordinary device management), and releases pending approvals (§ Reach approvals) — keep everything else: same actor, same keys, same handle, same data, same tier (the same one-field-change spirit as promoting a `backup`-tier guest, `admin.md` § 2; here zero fields on `users` change). Callable by the **guardian** (the normal path) or the **admin** — admin-graduate only *removes* oversight (the safe direction) and mirrors that the admin designated supervision at admission; it is also what makes eviction resolution (§ Lifecycle gates) always completable. Whether an age-based automatic path exists is an open future question, not v1 — *how a nest would know an age* now has a ratified answer, the account age band (§ The account age band), but no band boundary graduates anyone automatically.

**Transfer** — re-points the ward's link to a new guardian, keeping the policy document intact, **with the proposed guardian's consent**. Guardianship is an ongoing duty (an approvals queue to attend, a policy to own), and thesecurity review correctly observed that re-pointing it onto an actor who never agreed conscripts them. So transfer is a **handshake, mirroring knock→accept** (the same pending-until-accepted concept every contact edge already uses — no new lifecycle idea):

- **`fauna.family.transfer`** — initiated by the **current guardian** (handing off to the other parent) **or the admin** (consistent with admission-time designation being an admin act). It validates the proposed guardian exactly as admission does (existing, non-supervised, non-suspended, ≠ the ward, and — new — ≠ the current guardian, which would be a no-op) and records a **pending transfer**; the link is untouched until the proposed guardian accepts. At most one pending transfer exists per ward (the store is keyed on the ward); **initiating while one is pending replaces it** — the initiator authority (guardian-or-admin) is the same either way, and a superseded proposal's target can no longer accept it. **Consent is the proposed guardian's, whoever initiates** — the admin arm is *not* exempt: conscription by an admin imposes the same unconsented duty, and the trust shape attaches no oversight power to the admin role. The one case that completes immediately is the **initiator proposing themself** (an admin taking over guardianship): initiating a transfer to yourself *is* consenting, so no pending state is created.
- **`fauna.family.transfer.accept`** — the proposed guardian completes the re-point. Admissibility is **re-validated at accept time** (the world may have changed since initiation: the target may have been suspended, or the ward graduated), and the link update + pending-row removal commit in **one transaction** — the single atomic decision point (`../architecture/nest/common.md` § Client-state recoverability); between initiate and accept the link simply still points at the old guardian, a valid state.
- **`fauna.family.transfer.decline`** — the proposed guardian refuses; the pending row is dropped. Declining is always available and costs nothing (self-healing was the conscription finding's saving grace; the handshake keeps it).
- **`fauna.family.transfer.cancel`** — the current guardian or the admin withdraws a pending transfer.
- **Expiry** — a pending transfer not answered within **7 days** lapses (lazily: an expired row is invisible to reads and un-acceptable, and is pruned opportunistically — no background job, the same shape as `guardian_mail_sent_msgids` retention). The initiator can always re-initiate; nothing dangles forever. The window is a hard-coded constant (nobody would configure it — the no-operator invariant's bucket 1).
- **Lifecycle** — a pending transfer cascades away with the ward (graduation or deletion) and with the *proposed guardian's* deletion; a proposed guardian who is merely suspended is caught by accept-time re-validation.
- **Visibility** — `fauna.family.status` carries the pending state to both sides: the ward's entry on the guardian side shows the outstanding proposal (with cancel), and a proposed guardian sees an **incoming transfer prompt** (ward + current guardian, accept/decline). The `family-tab` gate widens accordingly: any relationship **or a pending/incoming transfer** reveals the page, or a target who is not yet in any family relationship could never see the prompt. The ward's supervised indicator reflects a completed transfer on next `fauna.family.status` read.

*Version note (additive-everywhere):* the `fauna.family.transfer` request/reply shapes are unchanged — only the semantics moved from "re-point now" to "propose"; a client predating the handshake initiates a proposal and observes the link unchanged until the target accepts, which is the fix working as designed, not a compat break. No shipped client UI called transfer before the handshake existed.

## Lifecycle gates (firmed 2026-07-09)

Every gate below keeps the system in a valid state after any single-write crash (each resolution step is its own atomic transaction; between steps the link either points at the old guardian, the new one, or is gone — all valid; `../architecture/nest/common.md` § Client-state recoverability):

- **Supervised self-delete is refused.** `fauna.account.delete` by a supervised account returns a typed approval-required error — deleting the account would unilaterally sever the link (the one bounded exception to user-controls-their-data, § The trust shape). The guardian graduates first; the full account may then delete. The guardian **cannot** delete the ward's account either — guardianship carries no data-destruction power.
- **Guardian eviction/delete is blocked while links exist.** `fauna.admin.users.evict` / `.delete` / `fauna.account.delete` targeting an account that guards someone returns a typed error listing the supervised accounts; the admin (or the guardian themself, pre-self-delete) resolves each link via `fauna.family.transfer` or `fauna.family.graduate`, then the eviction/delete proceeds. A stranded ward is unrepresentable: no path removes a guardian account while a link references it. Since transfer is a handshake (§ Graduation & transfer), transfer-resolution completes only when the proposed guardian accepts — **graduate keeps resolution always completable without waiting on anyone**, and an admin who must take over *now* self-proposes (which completes immediately).
- **Identity succession carries the link, in BOTH directions (ratified + built 2026-08-13).** A succession is the one lifecycle event that retires an account without evicting or deleting it, so neither gate above fires — and until this ruling the family plane had no succession leg at all, which broke both gates' guarantees by a path they never covered. **A ward's own ceremony carries their supervision with them:** the link row and the whole policy document re-point to the successor, because the designation IS the link row's existence (§ The guardianship link) and every enforcement gate keys on it — so leaving it behind silently emancipated the ward, and *irreversibly*, since supervision is only ever set at admission. That made the recovery ceremony an unguarded version of the very gesture the first gate above refuses, reachable by any ward holding their own recovery kit (the succession kind is pre-identity — authorized by the recovery chain alone, with no session, tier or supervision check). **A guardian's own ceremony carries their wards with them:** the link's guardian side re-points too, or the ward is left supervised by an identity refused everywhere while the successor's app lists no wards — the stranded half-state the second gate declares unrepresentable. This is not the conscription the transfer handshake guards against: a succession is identity rotation, not a change of person, so no new guardian is being enrolled and consent is already given. The ward's side of the plane (link, policy and the ten side tables) is declared `Move` in `bins/fauna-nest/src/db/actor_tables.rs`; the guardian side and the ward's pending contact asks re-point in the ceremony. The declared-axis machinery and the reasoning per column are owned by [`succession-repoint-axis.md`](succession-repoint-axis.md) § The declared re-point axis.
- **Evicting/deleting the supervised account** works exactly as for any user (admin capacity power, unchanged) and cascades the link, policy, allowlist, and pending approvals away with it — the same cleanup pattern as folders / backup custody on user deletion.
- **A suspended guardian** leaves the ward's policy enforced but its approvals queue unattended; the admin resolves by **restoring** the guardian (`admin-users-cancel-eviction-button` — the single exit from the suspended state, [`admin.md`](admin.md) § 2 Users → *Cutting a user off*) or by transferring guardianship. Suspension is temporary by design, so this is documented behavior, not a gate.
- `fauna.account.upgrade` (self-serve tier change) stays available to a supervised account — the tier is quota, not oversight.

## The account age band (ratified 2026-08-22 — decision gate D1–D6; nest/wire half BUILT 2026-08-24; android's silent store-age arm BUILT the same day; app-surface element IDs user-approved and the kids app's shape ratified 2026-09-25; the tui lead BUILT the same day, the other six trickle down)

The six decisions recorded here were made by the user 2026-08-22 at the decision gate on the store/age research memo (2026-08-22, tracked internally — the full rationale, store-by-store facts, the device-vs-nest Addendum, and the rejected alternatives live there; the memo's "no open self-registration" premise was refuted at ratification — `open`-mode self-registration exists, hence D6). **The nest/wire half is BUILT (2026-08-24 — § Implementation status today's age-band row: schema, admission carry, defaults dial, require-knob, minor refusals, App Attest verifier + nonce plumbing, status surface); still open there: the android arm awaits the org's Play Console response keys (until then an android attestation is one no nest can check — the *cannot check* bullet below, ruled 2026-10-01 and not yet built, so today's nests still refuse the admission on it; the Play Integrity plumbing itself was ruled and built 2026-08-24, below; the iOS arm was armed with the org's App ID 2026-09-30). Android's silent store-age arm (Play Age Signals + a best-effort Play Integrity verdict riding the invite submit/redeem, no new ui.yaml IDs) is likewise BUILT 2026-08-24, and iOS's (Declared Age Range + App Attest, FaunaKit) 2026-09-30; every app-VISIBLE surface remains**. **The seven element IDs those surfaces need were user-approved 2026-09-25 and are in ui.yaml (§ App surface → *Age-band surfaces*), so the app legs build against them — tui BUILT 2026-09-25 (the lead), macos + ios + windows 2026-09-26, web 2026-09-29, linux + android 2026-10-06 — all 7 apps; the kids app's shape was ratified the same day (the kids-app bullet below) and its shared spine is the next slice.**

- **The band (D2 — adopted as recommended).** A supervised account carries an **age band** — `U13 | 13-15 | 16-17 | 18+`, the same bands the store age APIs use — as per-account nest state on the guardianship plane, set **at admission by the admitting guardian** exactly where the supervised designation itself is set (§ The guardianship link; the admission carry grows the band beside `guardian_actor`). The band is a **defaults dial, never a fourth enforcement pillar**: it selects the guardian-policy *defaults* offered at admission — content-pillar floors, reach knobs, and the dynamic-features guardian tier ([`../architecture/dynamic-features.md`](../architecture/dynamic-features.md) § The rule-setter model, tier 4) — which the guardian then edits per-knob exactly as today; enforcement stays where the three pillars put it. An account with no guardianship link is `18+` by construction (the unsupervised-equivalent-defaults rule, § Wire & data shape). The band auto-graduates nobody (§ Graduation & transfer — an age-based automatic path stays future).
- **Provenance (D5).** The band records *how it was established*: `attested-ios | attested-android | guardian-asserted | none`. A guardian-set band is `guardian-asserted`; an open-mode self-registration is `none` (below); the attested values require the platform attestation below. Admission policy and audits key on provenance; enforcement never does — the band's value, not its pedigree, drives the defaults.
- **Attested age-at-admission (D5 — adopted).** The mobile app may carry the platform's age signal into the admission application, hardened by platform attestation: **Apple App Attest** / **Google Play Integrity** over `{nest-issued nonce, age band, application id}` — the verifying signature is Apple's/Google's, **never a key embedded in the Fauna app** (a key in a publicly shipped artifact is extractable and hardens nothing). The claim **corroborates the admitting adult's judgment and never overrides it**: an `18+` attestation must not silently override a guardian's judgment that the applicant is a child (hand-me-down and parent's-own-account devices make false-adult the common failure), and coverage is honestly partial (iOS 26+ / Play-serviced devices only; the platform APIs are declinable outside mandated regions; on android only the **Play-distributed build types** carry the two Google client libraries — the `foss` F-Droid / direct-download artifact links no Play code and makes no claim, [`../architecture/installers/android.md`](../architecture/installers/android.md) § Goal). **Play Integrity plumbing — RULED 2026-08-24 (user, closing the question the memo's Addendum item 7 left open): nest-local, no relay.** App Attest chains verify nest-locally against Apple's published root; Play Integrity *standard* tokens are Google-server-decode-only, so the android app makes a **classic** request (the mode Google reserves for infrequent high-value actions — account admission is exactly that) whose nonce is the **SHA-256 of the claim message** — Google's logs see a digest, never the band or the actor id, exactly as Apple sees only the App Attest client-data hash — and the nest opens the verdict **locally** with the app's Play Console **response-encryption keys pinned as nest constants** (`age_attest::FAUNA_ANDROID_PLAY_INTEGRITY_KEYS`, `None` until the org downloads them — an unarmed arm is one the nest *cannot check*, the next bullet) — structurally the iOS arm: sovereign, works out of the box, no Fauna-org service in any admission. **Why those keys may live in a public tree:** Google signs the verdict with a key only Google holds; the pinned pair only *opens the envelope*, so publishing it costs verdict *secrecy* (a holder of a Fauna token can read it), never authenticity. Accepted costs, deliberately: the classic-request quota is fleet-wide per app (~10k/day across every nest; admissions are rare, and Google raises it on request); a key rotation is a nest release that swaps both constants. Rejected: a Fauna-org verification relay (the memo's lean — the org would have to operate it, every self-hosted nest would phone the org per android admission, and an outage would block android attested admissions fleet-wide), and distributing org Google-Cloud credentials to nests (a real secret, unlike the response keys). The verdict checks the nest makes are listed on `age_attest::verify_play_integrity`; the nonce contract is `fauna_protocol::age::age_claim_signed_message`'s.
- **An attestation the nest cannot check (ruled 2026-10-01 under D5 and [`../architecture/version-compatibility.md`](../architecture/version-compatibility.md) I2).** *Cannot check* is not *failed the check*. The nest sorts an attested claim into exactly three outcomes, by what **this nest build** holds for the attestation's `platform`. **(1) Verified** — the nest holds that platform's verifier (iOS: the pinned App ID; android: the pinned response keys) and the platform's signature checks out over the claim message and a live nest-minted nonce: the claim earns `attested-*`. **(2) Failed the check** — the nest holds the verifier and the attestation does not pass (an unknown, spent or expired nonce; a chain, binding or verdict that does not verify): the **whole admission is refused**, `fauna.account.age_attestation_invalid`, never downgraded — a claim presented as attested that fails a check the nest can run is tampering, a replay, or a transient the app heals by re-minting, and a silent downgrade would hide all three. **(3) Cannot check** — the nest holds **no verifier for that platform**: the platform is unarmed in this build, or its token is one this build does not know (a platform a newer app speaks): the attestation is **ignored unread** (no field of it parsed, its nonce not consumed) and the claim is handled **exactly as the declared-only claim it would be without it** — provenance `none` on the request row, no band row from a registration, the store-says-minor refusal still keyed on the band — and the admission proceeds. *Why this weakens nothing:* whoever sends the claim decides whether an attestation is attached, so ignoring an unverifiable one grants precisely what omitting it grants. What fails closed is the **provenance** — `attested-*` is never recorded without a verified platform signature — and the **require-knob**, which only outcome 1 satisfies: with the knob on, a cannot-check claim is refused as every declared-only claim is, `fauna.account.age_verification_required` ([`../architecture/nest/public-mode.md`](../architecture/nest/public-mode.md) § Registration Modes → *Age at registration*), so an admin who turns it on admits exactly the platforms their nest can verify. Refusing the admission instead bought no assurance and broke two cases in which nothing is forged: every Play-distributed android admission while the response keys are unset, and any app whose platform is armed talking to a nest built before the arming (I2 — the newer peer degrades, it does not fail). **The nest says what it can check, and the app attaches only that.** `fauna.account.age_nonce`'s reply carries the additive `attestation_platforms` — the `AgeAttestation.platform` tokens this nest holds a verifier for, computed from the same arming the verifier reads (a pinned value that fails to decode counts as unarmed, on both sides of that one function) — riding the ceremony's own reply rather than the connect-time capability set, because the fact is needed exactly when the app holds the nonce; an absent or empty list reads as **verifies nothing**. The shared onboarding machine is the one enforcement point: it puts an attestation on the wire only when its nonce is the one last minted from the nest the admission goes to and that reply listed the attestation's platform, and otherwise sends the claim declared-only. The mobile glue reads the same list off the nonce it was handed to skip its platform round (android's classic-request quota is fleet-wide) — an economy, never the guarantee. Together the two halves cover every pairing of releases: a nest from this ruling on degrades whatever a blind or newer app attaches, and an app from this ruling on attaches nothing to a nest that predates the list and would have refused it. Reading absence as *verifies nothing* costs one thing — a nest built between the iOS arming (2026-09-30) and this ruling verifies iOS without saying so and is sent declared-only claims; no shipped iOS build could attest in that window (the entitlements were pending, § Implementation status today), so no working pair regresses. **No app compiles its platform's arming in as a gate:** arming is a property of the nest being addressed, never of the app's own build, so android gains no client-side key-presence bit, and `fauna_ffi::ios_age_attestation_app_id` stays only what the signed message needs — its `application_id`. **The onboarding notice follows the claim that will be sent** (§ App surface → *Age-band surfaces*).
- **Store age signals at onboarding (D3 — adopted).** With ios/android store distribution (listing registration began 2026-08-22), the mobile apps request **Declared Age Range** (iOS 26+) / **Play Age Signals** during onboarding as **corroboration + compliance, never the enforcement point** (at most 2 of 7 apps can ever receive a store signal): store-says-minor + an unsupervised admission path → require supervised admission or refuse. This also adopts the TX/UT/LA developer duties (age-category consumption, Significant Change re-consent) into the ios/android backlogs. **Of those duties only age-category consumption has a ratified shape (it is the store-age arm above, built on both mobile apps).** Significant Change re-consent, the store's age-rating read and the consent-revocation server notification have a worked design that is **not ratified**: it awaits the user's decision gate D-S1…D-S8, and until that is answered this bullet's one-clause adoption is all this doc states about them — no surface, no flow, no element ID, and no claim about what the state laws require.
- **The admin require-knob, and open mode (D5 + D6 — adopted; open mode covered).** Admins gain the nest-state knob **"accept only signups carrying app age verification"** (default **off** — works out of the box: web/desktop signups have no attestation path; strict family/community nests opt in). The registration-mode interplay — the open-mode self-registrant's `18+`/`none` band, the store-says-minor refusal of self-registration, and the knob's exact gating scope — is owned by [`../architecture/nest/public-mode.md`](../architecture/nest/public-mode.md) § Registration Modes → *Age at registration*; the admin UI surface by [`admin.md`](admin.md) § 2 Users → Registration.
- **The kids app (D1 — in scope) and listing posture (D4 — one main app per store).** Under-13 users on iOS/Android are in product scope, and the store-compatible door for them is a **separate contacts-only kids app** (guardian-approved contacts, no open feed — the shape the reach pillar already enforces per-account; Apple's social-feed force-rating ≥13+ makes the main app uninstallable on a supervised under-13 device). The kids artifact may carry a **compiled-in content-policy floor** composing strictest-wins with the account's guardian policy — a tier-0 application of the existing flavor mechanism ([`../architecture/dynamic-features.md`](../architecture/dynamic-features.md) § Compile-time excision), defense in depth on the kid's own device, never a substitute for the account's policy. The **main app stays one listing per store**, rated honestly by the questionnaire (expect 13+/16+ on Apple) — no 18+/13+ tier split (no enforcement gain, an adult-confirmation download tax in BR/AU/SG, multiplied listing maintenance); per-store channel mechanics stay with the packaging docs ([`../architecture/installers/android.md`](../architecture/installers/android.md); the ios packaging doc when minted). **The kids app's shape — ratified 2026-09-25 at the decision gate D-K1…D-K7, every recommendation taken.** (1) **A build flavor on the release train, not an eighth app (D-K6).** The kids app is the android and ios apps built under a `kids` flavor — the tier-0 excision mechanism ([`../architecture/dynamic-features.md`](../architecture/dynamic-features.md) § Compile-time excision) — with its own store identity and listing, shipped from the same commit at the fleet version ([`../architecture/product-version.md`](../architecture/product-version.md) § Bumping: one train, one version); the feature catalog gains a column per kids channel, never a second catalog. What is genuinely separate is the store identity, because the stores demand it: a Kids-Category listing cannot share an identifier with the 13+ main listing, so it is one more registry unit under the leaf rule. (3) **Account model — keys on *supervised*, never on the band.** Sign-in is the ordinary sign-in (same identity, same onboarding pages, same nest); a kid never self-registers — the guardian mints a supervised code or approves the request (the admission surfaces above), and the kid redeems it in the kids app. Eligibility is one shared-Rust verdict, `kids_app_eligible(status) = status.supervised_by.is_some()` in `fauna-client-family`; an account that is not supervised is refused past sign-in by one explanatory surface with a sign-out affordance (net-new IDs, proposed with the first kids leg under rule A), and a graduated account flips the verdict on its next status read — it never becomes an open-feed client, because the artifact still carries the floor and the excision. (7) **Legal review (D-K7) gates the first kids store submission** on either store — off-machine, the user's act. *Build state:* the shared spine is BUILT (2026-10-03 — `kids_app_eligible`, `fauna-ffi`'s `kids-floor` and `kids-safe` complement, the tier_1 pins; the mechanism's status is [`../architecture/dynamic-features.md`](../architecture/dynamic-features.md) § Implementation status today's); android is in flight — its build type, identity and FFI flavor landed 2026-10-03, the shell split and the `android-kids-check` witness 2026-10-05 (the kids artifact assembles); open are the not-supervised surface and the Families listing draft and the kind-string half of the excision — then iOS.

## App surface (all 7 apps — IDs user-approved 2026-07-09; ui.yaml owns them)

One `family` page on all 7 apps, rendering role-dependent sections (both roles for a user who is somehow both is fine — the sections are independent):

- **Guardian side:** the supervised accounts they guard (`family-ward-item`, indexed, with `family-ward-handle`), the reach-policy editor (`family-policy-contact-approval-toggle`, `family-policy-unknown-sender-select`, `family-policy-federation-toggle`, `family-policy-feed-sources-select`, `family-policy-save-button`), the approvals queue (`family-approval-item` indexed + `family-approval-approve-button` / `family-approval-deny-button`), pre-approve a contact (`family-contact-add-input` + `family-contact-add-button`), transfer initiation per selected ward (`family-transfer-input` + `family-transfer-button`, with the pending proposal rendered as `family-transfer-pending` + `family-transfer-cancel-button` — § Graduation & transfer; IDs user-approved 2026-07-12), and graduation (`family-graduate-button` + `family-graduate-confirm-button`). v1.x adds the content/screen-time pillars to the same editor, plus the selected ward's device-mark rows (`family-device-mark-item` indexed + `family-device-mark-toggle` — § Full visibility for young children): because that editor is per-selected-ward, every indexed ID in it addresses one ward's devices unambiguously, which is why the rows belong here and not on the `family-ward-item` rows.

## v1 scope (ratified 2026-07-09) vs. future

**v1:** the guardianship link at admission (same nest) + the supervised indicator + the reach-policy pillar (nest-enforced) + graduation. **v1.x (designed 2026-07-15):** content-policy pillar (guardian floors + Notify — § Content policy / § Guardian Notify), screen time (§ Screen time), and the guardian-device marker build — shared-Rust + nest halves now built for all three (§ Implementation status today); app legs are landing per-slice, not all unbuilt (§ Implementation status today tracks each slice). ## Don't do these

- **Don't build guardian key escrow** — no wrapping of the child's keys to the guardian, no guardian decrypt capability on the nest. Full visibility is the guardian-enrolled device, nothing else.
- **Don't attach any oversight power to the admin role.** Guardianship is an account-to-account link; the admin shell gains no content-facing surface.
- **Don't make supervision silent.** No mode hides the supervised indicator or the guardian's enrolled device from the child.
- **Don't give the nest content access for enforcement.** Reach policy is floor-metadata only; content policy runs post-decrypt on the child's clients (`content-scoring.md` placement rule is not re-litigated here).
- **Don't make "family nest" a deployment shape or storage mode.** Supervised accounts are per-account, orthogonal to both axes (`storage-modes.md` § Don't do these).
- **Don't oversell client-side enforcement.** Any surface describing the content/screen-time pillars states the conforming-client bound honestly.
- **Don't surface content (or content-derived text) to the guardian.** A mail hold shows envelope metadata (sender, time) only; notifications are category + count. If a future surface seems to need more, the answer is the guardian-enrolled device, not a bigger disclosure.
- **Don't invent a parallel pending-state machine for approvals.** Contact approvals are the ward's pending knocks read guardian-side; a mail hold is the placed message itself, in the ward's held mailbox, plus an envelope sidecar the guardian's queue reads (§ Reach approvals). Neither introduces a second lifecycle that could drift from the thing it describes. *(The first firming pass said "mail holds are quarantine placements" — mail has no quarantine store; `SpamDisposition::Quarantine` routes to `Junk`. Don't reintroduce that premise.)*
- **Don't decide a per-recipient mail refusal at ingest.** Ingest runs inside SMTP `DATA`, whose single reply covers every recipient — refusing there denies the message to the ward's co-recipients too. Per-recipient refusals belong at `RCPT TO` (§ The mail gate).
- **Don't let a Notify or usage report carry a content id.** Category + count (Notify) and minutes (usage) are the whole disclosure; a content id would let the guardian's surface point at *which* item, which is content-derived targeting the pillar promises not to do. If a future surface seems to need it, the answer is the guardian-enrolled device.
- **Don't put screen-time or content enforcement on the nest.** Both are conforming-client render rules; a nest-side gate would either be theatre (it can't see device foreground state) or a reach restriction wearing a content-policy costume. The nest's whole role is storing the policy and the coarse aggregates.
- **Don't build the 2026-03-15 `NotifyConfig`/`NotifyPayload` callback shape.** Superseded — § Guardian Notify rides the ordinary notification machinery; no operator-configured endpoints exist. The draft types and `ScanObligation.notify_config` left `fauna-core` 2026-10-01 with the baseline removal of dead shapes ([`../architecture/compat-remnant-sweep.md`](../architecture/compat-remnant-sweep.md)).
- **Don't let the bridge decide a guardian verdict.** The MTA relays the envelope sender; the nest reads the policy and decides. A reach knob that a modified bridge could set is not a routing floor.
- **Don't auto-mint a request from a refused attempt.** A `contact_request` / `feed_source` ask is an explicit ward action — a refusal is not consent to ask, and auto-minting turns every blocked probe into guardian-queue noise.
- **Don't replay a bridge operation nest-side on approval.** A feed-source approve mints a grant the ward's client redeems by retrying (§ Feed-source approvals); `link` is interactive OAuth, and the nest never runs a bridge operation *as the ward* from stale staged parameters.
- **Don't store bridge-DM hold state.** The DM hold is computed from `(knob, verdict rows)` at read time (§ The bridge-DM gate) — a stored hold flag would recreate the drainage/stranding problem the mail gate needs a rule for, for no gain.
