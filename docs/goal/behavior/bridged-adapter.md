## Where logic lives

**The `Bridged` adapter and this chain's half of the room model (TP8 + TP10, ratified 2026-09-05 — the third-party integration chain; the shared-Rust slice built 2026-10-02, § Implementation status today).** Three rulings, in the order they depend on each other.

1. **Transport adapters are separate from membership; principals with keys may be members; a room's confidentiality class derives from its member set.** All members user devices ⇒ end-to-end (MLS); the user's home nest a member ⇒ nest-readable community room; a bridge principal or a mail transfer agent a member ⇒ perimeter class, transport-only, labeled honestly. `ThreadEncryption` therefore becomes a **derived** field computed in shared Rust from the roster — derived on every rail but the Nostr leg since 2026-10-02 (`room::transport_room` seats the bridge or mail transfer agent), and a per-rail constant in `derive_capabilities` (`libs/fauna-conversations/src/capabilities.rs`) only there, until the leg migrates. Under this rule "never presume a bridge is an MLS member" (TP12) is a theorem: a bridge can only be a member of a room whose class already says the bridge reads it, and an end-to-end room is by definition one with no such member. **The room model itself — nest-readable community rooms, a single home nest per room transferable by succession, per-room history-for-joiners policy, roles enforced cryptographically in end-to-end rooms and at the floor in community rooms, and the duplicate `fauna.conversations.group.*` plane's retirement as the last step (executed 2026-09-26) — is ratified in [`../behavior/conversation-rooms.md`](../behavior/conversation-rooms.md) (2026-09-08), not here**; this chain contributed exactly one constraint to it: the bridged family never depends on the group plane in either form ([`../behavior/conversation-rooms.md`](../behavior/conversation-rooms.md) § Bridged rooms).
2. **One variant, one backend.** `Rail::Bridged` is the only enum change (`libs/fauna-conversations/src/address.rs` — the closed, exhaustively-matched `Rail`), and one shared-Rust `BridgedBackend` serves every bridge. Per-bridge identity — id, glyph, address grammar, capability vector — comes from the principal's metadata document at registration ([`../architecture/third-party.md`](../architecture/third-party.md) § The manifest's `bridge` block, whose `capabilities` is the `ThreadCapabilities` record verbatim); no per-bridge Rust, no per-bridge app change. **How the identity rides (ruled 2026-10-02; refutable until the first bridged room renders):** `Rail::Bridged` is a **unit** variant — `Rail` stays `Copy`, backend registration stays keyed per rail, and a unit variant cannot read a declared value, so the 2026-09-05 sentence "the `Rail::glyph` match and `derive_capabilities` gain one arm each that read the declared values" is corrected to what the code can do. (a) **The thread carries the identity:** `ThreadSummary` and `ThreadDetail` gain an additive `bridge: Option<BridgeIdentitySnapshot { id, label, glyph }>`, filled by the manager from the backend's registry for a `Bridged` thread and `None` on every other rail; the snapshot's existing `glyph` field — what every app already paints — is the declared glyph on a bridged thread, and `Rail::glyph` answers the generic `SourceGlyph::Bridge` only as the fallback for an identity-less read. (b) **The capability vector is overlaid where the trait already takes the thread:** `derive_capabilities(Bridged, _)` answers the most restrictive vector (every affordance withheld, `delivery_mode: Async`), and `BridgedBackend::capabilities(thread)` replaces it with the vector declared for `thread.bridge.id` — the same read-time overlay `RoomSnapshot::gate` performs for roles; `encryption` is never declared, it is the room's derived class (ruling 1), and the backend's `room_state` seats the bridge principal, so the class is transport-only by construction. (c) **The address is `TypedAddress::Bridged { bridge_id, address }`** — the far network's own spelling, `rail()` → `Bridged`; the test agents' inject commands (`conversations_inject_*`, convention 11) name the bridge id beside the rail through a `TypedAddress::unresolved_bridged(bridge_id, raw)` twin of `unresolved_for_rail`, which cannot answer `Bridged` from a bare string. (d) **The address grammar is enforced nest-side only** — compiled at manifest resolution and matched at `conversation.rooms.open` (bounds: [`../architecture/third-party.md`](../architecture/third-party.md) § The manifest), so no app ever compiles a third party's pattern: the recipient picker lists each registered bridge by its declared label, and a typed address resolves through the backend's `resolve_address`, which asks the nest (the Fauna rail's own shape) and surfaces its typed refusal. (e) **The first-party legs are bridges of the same shape:** the in-process Nostr leg registers identity `{ id: "nostr", label: "Nostr", glyph: "bolt" }` with its declared vector (today's `(Nostr, _)` constants minus `encryption`), so a Nostr DM thread renders ⚡ as before. The wire is `fauna.bridges.conversation.{deposit, outbox.fetch, outbox.ack, room.upsert, room.members, receipt}` for the bridge and `fauna.bridges.conversation.{rooms.list, rooms.open, inbox.fetch, send}` for the user's app, plus the push `fauna.bridges.push.conversation_changed` — mail's shape generalized; the kinds' existence and caller classes are owned by [`../architecture/apps/bridges.md`](../architecture/apps/bridges.md) § Bridge-kind catalogue → Phase G. **The user-side contract (ruled 2026-10-02, refutable as above):** `rooms.list` is the caller's bridged rooms across every bridge serving the account, each row carrying the bridge identity, the far room id and participants, the declared vector, `bridge_x25519` (the key an outbound item is sealed to — carried on the row so a first-party leg with no roster row needs no join against `fauna.principals.list`), `last_at`, the family gate's `guardian_state`, and — for a room no live principal serves — `disconnected`, which the glue honours by registering identities from connected rows only, so the thread falls to the rail's most-restrictive vector under (b), and which the backend keeps per room as `RoomSnapshot.bridge_disconnected` (the fate itself: [`../architecture/apps/bridges.md`](../architecture/apps/bridges.md) § Bridge-kind catalogue → Phase G → *When the bridge stops serving*); `rooms.open` finds or mints the room for `(bridge_id, far_address)` — idempotent, grammar-checked, the floor roster seated with the user and the bridge principal ([`../behavior/conversation-rooms.md`](../behavior/conversation-rooms.md) § Bridged rooms); `inbox.fetch` is one room's rows in **both** directions, sealed to the user — inbound deposits and the caller's own Sent copies — ordered and paginated on the nest's `received_at`, never the far side's claimed timestamp (the Nostr lesson, [`nostr.md`](../ui/nostr.md) § Implementation status today), with the reply-level `guardian_state` and a Sent copy's `undelivered` stamp; **the stamp reaches a row the app has already read (2026-10-17):** the receive driver pages the inbox by row cursor and never re-reads a row, while the nest stamps a Sent copy when its principal ends, usually long after the app read it, so the glue re-reads a room one-room-from-the-start the first time it sees it `disconnected` (and again after the room is served and disconnected anew), hands the stamped Sent rows' ids across on the room's record, and the backend keeps them — a set that only grows, since a stamp is final — for the manager to project onto `MessageSnapshot.undelivered` on every emit, the `can_delete` shape; a row first read after its stamp carries it itself; `send` takes `{ room_id, sealed_for_bridge, sealed_for_self }` — the item sealed to `bridge_x25519` goes to the outbox the bridge drains, the copy sealed to the user's own recipient key is the Sent row — and refuses a guardian-`block`ed peer (the gate's outbound rule) while seeding the `allow` row; `conversation_changed { room_id }` is the nudge, and carries no content. Read state is the native thread's carrier, keyed by room id ([`../behavior/conversation-read-state.md`](../behavior/conversation-read-state.md)). **The nest is blind in both directions:** inbound, the bridge fetches the user's registered recipient key (`fetch_recipient_mls_pubkey`, widened to `ThirdParty`), HPKE-seals each message to it, deposits ciphertext; outbound, the user's app seals the message to the **bridge principal's** X25519 key and the nest queues ciphertext only the bridge opens. The one honest exception is a first-party in-process leg whose key the nest itself holds (the custodial Nostr signer, [`nostr.md`](../ui/nostr.md) § Architecture): there the nest opens the outbound to wrap and relay it, which is exactly what the room's transport-only label already says. A bridged room is a room whose transport is the bridge and whose member set includes the bridge principal — hence transport-only by derivation; membership ops reach the bridge only where its vector says so. No first-party bridge does better today; TP11 migrates them onto the same shape.
3. **The three first-party legs migrate onto the family as internal callers, and the deferred per-rail backends are superseded.** The Nostr DM ingest ([`nostr.md`](../ui/nostr.md) § Implementation status today → DMs), the Bluesky DM poll (a consume-side linked account's `chat.bsky` conversations, read under its OAuth session — never a nest-hosted-backed account's, whose chat traffic rides the proxy path: [`../behavior/atproto-pds-full.md`](../behavior/atproto-pds-full.md) § D7) and the ActivityPub direct-message ingest (the direct Notes the audience gate keeps out of the public projection, [`../behavior/activitypub.md`](../behavior/activitypub.md)) all become internal callers of the deposit family, and `BlueskyBackend` / `NostrBackend` / `ActivityPubBackend` — the three deferred stubs the paragraph above names — are **deleted in favour of the one backend** rather than registered per app. First-party use proves the seam before any third party arrives. Feed legs go generic the same way ([`feed.md`](../ui/feed.md) § Implementation status today, `SourceKind::Bridged`); identity legs are third-party-implementable in the target state through the oracle and nest-terminated ingress, their build order step 4's. **Deletion timing (ruled 2026-10-02):** `BlueskyBackend`, `ActivityPubBackend`, `Rail::{Bluesky, ActivityPub}` and `TypedAddress::{Bluesky, ActivityPub}` go with the adapter's first slice — no app registers the backends and nothing produces their addresses (the one e2e injection of a Bluesky thread becomes a bridged one); `NostrBackend`, its `NostrDmSink` / `NostrDmSource` seams, `Rail::Nostr` and `TypedAddress::Nostr` go with the Nostr leg's migration ([`nostr.md`](../ui/nostr.md) § Implementation status today → DMs). Each is a code-only alpha-carve-out deletion — no user data, no approval gate, said so in the commit. A variant a pre-deletion build wrote into a drafts blob would read back as the carried `Unknown` arm; under the 2026-09-24 baseline reset no such blob exists. Every exhaustive `Rail` / `TypedAddress` / `SourceGlyph` switch in the six non-Rust apps gains and loses its arms in the same change (cross-area cleanup rule 1). **The legs' addresses and rooms (ruled 2026-10-10):** a leg's room is one-to-one and keyed on the far network's stable, transport-proven id — the Nostr key's hex, the Bluesky DID, the ActivityPub canonical actor URI — which the bridge-DM gate's verdict keys on too ([`../behavior/family-safety.md`](../behavior/family-safety.md) § The bridge-DM gate → *The peer identity the verdict keys on*); each leg's grammar also admits the spelling people know, which `conversation.rooms.open` resolves to that id before the room is found or minted: an `npub1…` decoded, a Bluesky handle (with or without its leading `@`, in any case) asked of the service under the account's own session, a Fediverse account by WebFinger at its host through the SSRF-guarded dialer, the actor document it names fetched and cached. The Fediverse spelling is the two-`@` `@user@host`: a bare `user@host` is an email address to every app's address probe, which never asks the bridged rail about a string the mail rail claims. A spelling that names no one is the family's typed `address_refused`. **A Bluesky conversation of more than two members is not carried:** the gate's verdict keys on a room's one peer and the outbound addresses that peer, so a group would need a verdict composed per sender and a send addressed to the conversation — a new shape for the family gate, not a widening of this leg — and the poll skips it whole.

## Removing bridged rows

**The user's local remove of a bridged message or room (ruled 2026-11-06, design pass; refutable until the first build renders it).** A bridged row rests on the nest sealed to the user's own recipient key, written by a party outside the nest, under the inbound caps [`activitypub.md`](activitypub.md) § Security posture → *Inbound* rules for the direct-Note leg and the store applies to every leg (10,000 rows a room, 50,000 an account and leg, 250,000 a leg, 500,000 the store); before this ruling no path deleted one, so a room or an account's plane that reached a cap stayed full for good, and [`../principles.md`](../principles.md) § The user always controls their data — read, share, export and delete affordances live in the user's app — had no delete on this rail. Six rules:

1. **Two verbs, one class — a *remove*, never a *delete*.** On this page *delete* is the cooperative tombstone every member honours (`supports_message_delete`, `dm-message-delete-button`; [`message-reactions.md`](message-reactions.md) § Reactions & message delete), a far-side act. The remove is the nest's copy only: one bridged message (`remove_message`) or one whole bridged room (`remove_thread`); the far side sees nothing, no outbox item is withdrawn (a send already queued stands — the user removed their copy of it, not the message), and a receipt or a `room.members` report naming a removed row is dropped. It is its own capability, **`ThreadCapabilities.supports_local_remove`** — derived, never declared, like `encryption` and `can_search_room`: `true` for `Rail::Bridged`, `false` on every other rail today (an end-to-end room's record is the group's and lives in the history slice, mail's is the mailbox's — each a ruling of its own when wanted, through this flag and no new UI) — and the overlay keeps it from the rail's base beside `encryption` and `can_send`, so a manifest cannot declare it. `MessageSnapshot.can_remove` is assigned on every message of such a thread, the user's own Sent copies included. **A bridged room's declared `supports_message_delete` is masked to `false` by the overlay until the family carries a far-side delete:** Phase G has no outbound delete item (the outbox carries sealed messages only), so a declared `true` today lights a *Delete for everyone* that reaches no one — the manager's `delete_message` posts through the FaunaMls backend, which has no channel for a bridged thread, and the local mark lives in session memory. The far-side delete is a Phase G extension of its own, not ruled here; until it lands a bridged bubble offers the remove alone, and when it lands the two sit side by side in the ⋯ menu under distinct ids and distinct words — *Delete for everyone* and *Remove from my nest* — so no user mistakes one for the other.

2. **A removed row leaves a tombstone keyed as the row was.** The three first-party legs dedup on the store, not on a cursor: the Bluesky poll keeps no persisted cursor and re-reads each head page, the Nostr sweep's relay `since` cannot bound a gift wrap (NIP-59 randomizes its outer time by ±48 h, `nostr/sync_worker.rs`), and an ActivityPub sender retries a `429`d Note — so a hard delete would see the same message deposited again at the next sweep and the removal undone within minutes. The remove keeps the row, empties `sealed_content` (a zero-length blob) and stamps `removed_at`; `(room, far_message_id)` stays unique, so a re-served far message answers `duplicate` and is never stored again. **The caps count live rows only** (`removed_at IS NULL`, every arm of `inbound_full`), so a remove frees exactly what it removes — the room's slot, the account's, the leg's, the store's. The content is gone for the user the moment the row is emptied: the bytes were sealed to a key only the user's devices hold, so nothing the nest might keep in a freed page is readable by anyone, as nothing was before.

3. **Tombstones are bounded by count, not age.** A room keeps at most `MAX_BRIDGED_INBOUND_PER_ROOM` tombstones; a remove past that prunes the room's oldest tombstones first in the same transaction, so a room's rows never exceed twice its cap and a flood removed ten times costs the store one room's worth, not ten. The oldest far ids are the ones least likely to be re-served — an ActivityPub retry lands within hours, the Bluesky head page is a page, a relay's sweep is its newest `limit` — and age itself is the wrong axis on this plane ([`../ui/nostr.md`](../ui/nostr.md) § Implementation status today records the lesson: a gift wrap's time is a random draw).

4. **A removed room is hidden, not forgotten, and a new row reopens it.** `remove_thread` tombstones every row of the room and stamps the room `removed_at`: `rooms.list` omits it, its tombstones keep the dedup, and the room's own material (`bridge_x25519`, the far room id, the roster seat) stays. The next live row — a far message with an unseen id, the user's own `rooms.open` or a send — clears `removed_at` and the room lists again holding only what arrived since: a removed conversation's peer writing again is a new conversation, as a deleted SMS thread is. A remove is therefore not a block, and never refuses a peer; nor is it a verdict: a ward's remove of a room or of its rows never takes a held peer off the guardian's queue, which reads the ward's rooms removed or not, tombstones included (the bound is [`family-safety.md`](family-safety.md) § The bridge-DM gate's honest bound (a)); refusing one is the family gate's for a ward ([`family-safety.md`](family-safety.md) § The bridge-DM gate) and, for an adult, a verb this page does not have yet — a room a stranger refills is capped at 10,000 rows and removed again in one gesture, which is the recovery this ruling exists for.

5. **Every device of the account learns, by the undelivered stamp's road.** The removing device drops the row, or the thread, from the manager when the nest answers — after the reply, never optimistically: the nest is the store, and a refusal surfaces on `error-message`. The inbox is read by cursor and never re-reads a row (§ Where logic lives → *The `Bridged` adapter*, ruling 2 — the stamp's paragraph), so another device holding a removed row would keep it until relaunch; as for the undelivered stamp, the news rides the room record: `BridgedRoomInfo` carries `last_removed_at` (the room's newest removal, unix ms; 0 when none), the glue re-reads a room one-room-from-the-start whenever that value moves past the one it last saw (its first sight is the baseline — a fresh login reads from the start and skips tombstones) and hands the tombstoned ids across on the room record (`removed_rows`, the `undelivered_rows` shape), and the backend has the manager forget them; a room gone from `rooms.list` is dropped by `set_rooms`, thread and all. `inbox.fetch` serves a tombstone as a row with `removed: true` and no content, so a from-the-start read can name it and a fresh login skips it. The push `fauna.bridges.push.conversation_changed` fires on every remove, so the other devices re-list at once rather than at the next ticker.

6. **The wire and the surface.** Two user-class kinds of the Phase G family, caller-scoped like the four ([`../architecture/apps/bridges.md`](../architecture/apps/bridges.md) § Bridge-kind catalogue → Phase G owns their existence): `fauna.bridges.conversation.messages.remove` `{ room_id, ids }` (at most `BRIDGED_PAGE_MAX` ids; a row of another account's or of no room is not found; an id already removed counts nothing) and `fauna.bridges.conversation.rooms.remove` `{ room_id }`, each answering `{ removed }`, both `OnlineOnly` and idempotent. Shared Rust: `RailBackend::remove_message` / `remove_thread` (default `NotSupported`; the `Bridged` rail overrides them over a `BridgedSink` seam the glue performs through `BridgedConversationClient::messages_remove` / `rooms_remove`), the manager's `remove_message(thread, msg)` (refused unless the snapshot says `can_remove`) and `remove_thread(thread)` (refused unless `supports_local_remove`), and their UniFFI and wasm faces. The surface, on all 7 apps off the flags and never off the rail ([`../ui/conversations.md`](../ui/conversations.md) § Architectural rules, 5): in the ⋯ menu `dm-message-remove-button` → `dm-message-remove-confirm-button` on a bubble whose snapshot says `can_remove`; in the room policy editor `room-remove-button` → `room-remove-confirm` beside the walk-out, greyed unless `capabilities.supports_local_remove` — the editor already opens on every room and carries the one plain member's verb, and a remove is an immediate act, never staged through `room-settings-save-button`. Copy: *Remove from my nest* and *Remove conversation from my nest*, the confirm naming that the far side keeps its copy. The four ids are proposed, pending the owner's Rule A approval; their rows are in [`../ui/conversations.md`](../ui/conversations.md) § User actions. tui leads; the other six follow in one trickle-down.
