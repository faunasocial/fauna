## Goal

The Moderation page reviews items the spam / phishing classifier flagged on the user's **own** content and provides per-item training corrections (mark not-spam / mark spam) so the user refines their personal classifier over time. It is the user's view onto *why their content was labeled / actioned* and their lever to correct it — **not** an admin content-removal console (see § Categories & enforcement). **Ratified widening (designed 2026-08-27, ratified 2026-09-25 — § User-initiated reporting; built on tui 2026-09-27, owed on the other six apps):** the page also lists the reports the user *made* about others' content — their own moderation ledger, still never an admin console. **Unified shape (ratified, shipped on all 7 as of 2026-07-29):** a standalone Moderation page on the standalone apps (windows/linux/macos/ios/android/tui), embedded in Settings on web, **the same IDs either way** — mirroring the Status/Settings split (§ Architectural rules).

## Layout & flow

- **Moderation queue** (component `moderation-queue`) — list of the caller's flagged/actioned items on their **own** content; each row carries a per-item train-correction button (`train-correction-button`). Each row renders: a content-label badge (category) + confidence + an *optional* enforcement-action label + the train button. The queue is the **union of two sources** (ratified 2026-07-02 — § State & data shape): (1) the nest's server-issued `ObligationAction`s from `fauna.moderation.actions` (content ref, category, confidence, action taken, timestamp — the *enforcement* half: legal-takedown rows and their appeals; a mail disposition never enters this queue — § Categories & enforcement item 2); and (2) the client's own *post-decrypt local detections* (category, confidence, **no** action — the only social-content classifier signal on every conversation the nest holds no key for — a nest never classifies social content it cannot open; the one nest-position producer is a **community room's home nest** applying the transparent labelers the room policy names (ratified and built 2026-09-10 — `community-rooms.md` § The three classes → *What the home nest does with its read*; placement `../architecture/content-scoring.md` § The placement matrix, the *capability-holder* row), whose rows arrive as server rows on the same superset — `content-scoring.md` § Architectural rules). A local-only row shows a blank action column; server and local rows deduplicate by `content_id`.
- **Cross-reference:** spam **preferences** (thresholds, training toggles) live in `spam-moderation-controls` on the Settings page (`spam-preferences`, `spam-threshold`, `phishing-threshold`, `save-spam-prefs`; the former `auto-train` and `share-model` toggles are retired — `mail-spam.md` § Implicit signals are forbidden) — owned by [`settings.md`](../ui/settings.md), not here. The Moderation page consumes those preferences but does not host them.

## Categories & enforcement

**Canonical category set (ratified 2026-06-28, user):** `spam`, `trusted`, `nsfw`, `phishing`, `commercial`. This is the richest existing pattern (priority #4) — already rendered by web + android `ContentLabelBadge`, and `content_labels.category` is a free `String` column so the schema does not constrain it. The text heuristic (`classify_text`) fills **`spam`** and **`phishing`** today; `trusted` / `nsfw` / `commercial` are **reserved** for future classifiers (or user/admin labelling) and render when a producer emits them. Adding a *new* category is a product decision (extend this list + the shared enum); a classifier must never emit a category outside this set.

**Enforcement model (ratified 2026-06-28, user) — two tiers, do not conflate (plus one planned third surface, item 3, and a fourth in design, item 4):**

1. **Social content (feed / posts / conversations) → user-side, never nest-wide *policy* removal.** A label produces a **badge**; the user's *own* `spam_threshold` / `phishing_threshold` (per-user `spam_preferences`) optionally collapse/hide flagged content **in their own view**, and their per-user Bayesian model (`spam_models`) personalizes scoring. **No social post is removed nest-wide for everyone as a matter of policy or opinion.** This is the direct consequence of the *user-controls-their-data* product invariant: moderation of social content is a personal filter, not admin censorship. There is no admin "hide this post for all users" surface in the ratified shape. **The one exception is legal compulsion, not policy** — the narrow, transparent, appealable takedown mechanism this doc owns: § Legal takedown below (the *decision* to carve it out is the frame's — `../architecture/content-moderation-and-ranking.md` § Resolved design decisions Q5, ratified 2026-07-05).
2. **Mail ingest → admin policy at the perimeter.** Inbound mail is scored at the MTA perimeter and the admin's `mail_spam_policy` thresholds (junk / reject — owned by [`mail-policy-config.md`](mail-policy-config.md)) decide filing. This is standard MTA behaviour, scoped to mail, and is the only *discretionary* admin-side enforcement (the legal-compulsion carve-out in item 1 is non-discretionary and legally bounded, not admin policy). **Mail disposition is the Junk folder plus spam training — RATIFIED 2026-09-26 (user ruling, closing the fork the 2026-07-19 sweep opened): a mail quarantine or reject writes NO moderation-queue row and has NO appeal.** The disposition (accept / accept-to-Junk on the score / filed to Junk by a rule) is recorded as the `spam_disposition` column on the ingested-mail row (`persist_decoded_inbound_mail`, flowing into `db/bridge_routing.rs::insert_inbound_mail`) plus a `message_scan_results` row for the malware-scan verdict (`insert_scan_result`) — never an `obligation_action_records` row, which is the legal-takedown ledger (item 1) and nothing else. The user's recourse is the one every mail system they already know offers — the Junk folder, and the per-message train-correction (`fauna.moderation.train`) that teaches their own model; the admin's is the `mail_spam_policy` thresholds. `fauna.moderation.appeal` is gated on an enforcement record (§ Errors & edge cases), so for mail it correctly has nothing to appeal. Until this ruling the item's text promised a queue row the user could appeal, and the sweep recorded the difference as a gap; the user ruled the code's shape the target ("that matches how every mail system works today"), so the promise is withdrawn — not the code owed.

3. **Region blocking (planned — direction user-ratified 2026-07-29; owner: [`region-blocking.md`](region-blocking.md), Status: partially-specified).** A region's legal authority publishes a completely transparent blocking algorithm; each app applies its region's algorithm on the device, marking a blocked item visibly with the algorithm's reason (never a silent removal), and the nest applies it only where it is itself the publisher (web content it publishes to the open internet). This is the third compulsory surface beside item 2's mail-perimeter policy and item 1's legal-takedown exception — compulsory like them, but region-scoped, app-side, and algorithmic: it removes nothing nest-wide, hides nothing outside the region, and adds no admin lever, so item 1's invariant stands untouched. Mechanism, invariants, the open TBDs and the build state are `region-blocking.md`'s (§ Implementation status today).

4. **User-initiated reporting (ratified 2026-09-25 — owner: § User-initiated reporting below; unbuilt).** The fourth surface: a user reports someone else's content or account, and the report is **evidence to a human with authority — never a removal.** It changes what the *reporter* sees (their own personal filter — item 1's plane) and lands on an admin's queue, where the only levers are the ones that already exist (§ Legal takedown; [`admin.md`](admin.md)'s suspension). It removes nothing nest-wide, labels nothing, and adds no admin lever — item 1's invariant stands untouched.

(`trusted` is a positive signal — e.g. a sender allowlist / reputation hint — not an action; it never hides content. `nsfw` is a badge/collapse hint subject to the same user-side filtering, never nest-wide removal.)

Guardian (parent-linked) obligations for **supervised accounts** extend this framework — for such an account, "user-side" includes the guardian's policy. Owner: [`family-safety.md`](family-safety.md). **Implementation honesty (updated 2026-08-11):** item 1's threshold-driven collapse/hide is **built on all 7 apps** (linux + web the reference legs, android third, apple — macOS + iOS — fourth on 2026-07-22, windows fifth on 2026-07-23, tui's reader leg the last on 2026-08-02 — `family-client-enforcement.md` § Content policy owns the per-app state) — the one shared render-verdict engine (`fauna_core::obligation::render_verdict_composed`, which folds the per-post/message `ContentLabelEntry`-list verdict fold together with the two rule assemblers as of 2026-07-22) serves every user's own `spam_preferences` thresholds (→ `Collapse`) with the guardian floor composing in strictest-wins, owned by `family-client-enforcement.md` § Content policy (read its `## Implementation status today` for the per-app state). It is one engine, not two — the same verdict (`content_policy::verdict_for` on linux, the shared `ContentPolicyStore.verdictFor` → `content_render_verdict` on android and apple, `contentVerdict` on web, `ContentPolicyCache.VerdictFor` on windows) drives both the feed and conversations on every app that has this surface.

## Legal takedown (the legal-compulsion carve-out)

The one nest-wide social-content removal, non-discretionary and legally bounded (Q5 decision owner: the frame; this doc owns the **mechanism**). Built for posts + conversations (2026-07-05, hardened 2026-07-07).

- **Wire:** `fauna.moderation.legal_takedown { content_id, content_type, legal_reference, restore }` — Admin-only (`bridge_method_allowlist.rs`). A takedown **requires** a non-empty `legal_reference`; structurally it can never be a silent or discretionary lever: every takedown produces the **transparency triple** — a visible tombstone, `fauna.moderation.appeal` by `content_id`, and a permanent `moderation:legal-takedown` audit row. (All three legs are built: the appeal affordance lands on the author's moderation queue — `appeal-button` on each nest-issued row, the reason form, the recorded-for-review outcome line — on **tui** since 2026-09-21, with the six-app trickle-down to follow; the nest-side gate that makes the handle honest is § Errors & edge cases' appeal bullet.) **Tombstone-not-delete:** `restore=true` clears the flag and re-serves (the takedown row stays as additive history) + audits the overturn.
- **Posts** (`content_type="post"`): one atomic SQLite transaction writes a nullable `content_meta.legal_takedown_ref` flag (schema v3, additive; the flag `UPDATE` asserted to match exactly one row) + an `ObligationAction::TakenDown` (=7) row (surfaced to the author via `fauna.moderation.actions`) + the audit row. Serve: `get_post_core` withholds the body from **every** viewer (incl. author/admin) and `PostGetReply.legal_takedown` carries the tombstone reference; feeds exclude the post (`db/feeds.rs`); the self-scoped author enumeration `fauna.posts.list` (`content-index-ingest.md` § Ingest triggers, v1) withholds the body text too, returning the row with an empty `body` — it is an **authenticated body-serving path**, so it is bound by the every-viewer rule exactly as `get` is, and the row is still enumerated because dropping it would make that corpus listing silently partial. ⚠ The authenticated paths are a different enumeration from the anonymous off-box one below: `PUBLIC_POST_SERVABLE` is scoped to an off-box audience and is deliberately *not* the predicate here, because quarantine and `gated_tier` are author-visible. **Any new path that serves post body content to its author belongs in this bullet on the day it is written.** **The HLS manifest doors serve post body content and are enrolled here (2026-09-09).** `GET /api/v1/video/{post_id}/master.m3u8` and `…/{variant}.m3u8` render a video post's segment list — every variant, every segment duration and an absolute URL per segment — and had read it through the deliberately flag-blind body primitive, so they answered 200 for a post this bullet withholds. They now route through `get_post_core` like the post read itself, inheriting both gates in its order: a taken-down post answers **451, no body** (the answer the HTTP twin and the blob door already give — a playlist carries no tombstone render of its own, so the reference reaches the client through the post's own read), and a quarantined one is author/admin-only, which is why these routes take an optional bearer and are no longer unconditionally public. The gate belongs on the serve path and **not** in the body primitive: `moderation_withhold` reads *flagged* posts through that primitive to compute the blob withhold set, so teaching it to withhold would silently empty that set. **The rest of that primitive's serve paths are enrolled here too (2026-09-10), and the enrolment is now checked rather than asserted.** A partition of every production caller of the flag-blind post-body reads (`segments::post::load_post_body` and the reads beneath it) found five more serve paths, four of them ungated: (1) **the author's web site** — the render turns each published post into `post/{slug}.html`, the index and `feed.xml` for the open internet, so its enumeration now drops any post a moderation flag withholds, and a takedown or its overturn re-renders the site before the reply (nothing re-renders on a schedule, so a page rendered before the takedown would otherwise keep serving it); a render that fails there clears the author's rendered site instead — derived output, and the clear leaves its restore owed; the takedown's own transaction also records that render as owed, so a nest that stops before rendering drains it at boot ([`web-content-hosting.md`](web-content-hosting.md) § Routing, render, serving → *A revoke is durable* and *A blanked site is owed its restore* own the mechanism) — and the overturn re-renders because the author's publish link is the consent and a takedown never withdrew it, the ActivityPub outbox's pull-surface behaviour; (2) **the account export's `posts` domain** — the author's own archive, so only the takedown binds (quarantine is author-visible): the entry stays, following `fauna.posts.list`, with an empty `data_hex` and the citation in `legal_takedown_ref`; (3) **`fauna.moderation.train`** — the model it trains is derived from the body and read back by the caller, so it reads through `get_post_core`, the takedown gate ahead of quarantine; (4) **Search** — every hit is a snippet of the indexed body, so the public phase applies all three moderation flags and the author's own phase the takedown alone; and (5) the **federation feed query**, which withheld a takedown but handed a peer naming an author that author's quarantined and suppressed posts, now applies all three. Every SQL gate among them is one named fragment, `db::public_servability::MODERATION_SERVABLE` — the moderation half of `PUBLIC_POST_SERVABLE`, which composes it — so the feed reads, the federation query, Search and the render cannot disagree about which flags exist. **The enforcement:** `segments::post`'s `every_flag_blind_post_body_read_is_partitioned` walks every production call of those reads and fails on a caller nobody has classified as a serve path naming its gate or a reader saying why nothing leaves the process, and on a serve path whose production body no longer calls the gate it names — so "on the day it is written" is a gate, not a promise. **The export's `post` segment pairs are enrolled too (2026-09-10)** — the `include_blobs` leg every app requests, which copies each `__post` segment file into the archive verbatim and so carried a taken-down body by a door none of those reads touch: a pair cannot be verbatim and withhold one record, so a `post` pair holding a currently taken-down post is withheld whole and declared in the manifest (`segment_store.withheld`, reason `legal_takedown`) for exactly as long as the flag stands — the ruling and its rejected alternatives are [`../architecture/account-data-plane.md`](../architecture/account-data-plane.md) § Nest-side requirements item 1, *Payload stores* decision (6). **And the withhold survives the author's own DELETE of a taken-down post (2026-09-11).** Every post-side withhold above keys on `content_meta.legal_takedown_ref`, and the delete verb removes exactly that row while tombstoning only the mirror — so compaction kept the compelled bytes and the next `include_blobs` export shipped the pair again, with the blob door re-serving the record's attachments until the GC sweep. **The delete is not the door to close**: it destroys where these verbs gate, it is the *user always controls their data* affordance ([`../ui/feed.md`](../ui/feed.md) § Post deletion — “three verbs, three owners, no overlap”), and the same core runs under account deletion and admin eviction, which a refusal would wedge forever on one taken-down post. So the FACT moves instead: the delete records the post, its citation and the blob digests it named in `legal_takedown_deleted_posts` (schema v64) before removing anything, and the export's pair set and the blob door's rebuild read that beside the live flag. It has to be captured there and nowhere later, for two independent reasons: the permanent `TakenDown` obligation row cannot answer the question afterwards — **an overturn writes no counter-row**, so *taken down → deleted* and *taken down → restored → deleted* are indistinguishable in that ledger, and keying a withhold to it would withhold a whole segment pair forever for a post legitimately restored first — and the blob digests are unreadable once the record is tombstoned, which is exactly why the blob rebuild's record walk cannot recover them. The row is permanent and inert: once compaction has reclaimed the record and the GC dropped the blobs, it withholds bytes that are already gone. Unlike the derived blob set below this one **is** a floor, and necessarily — after the flag row is deleted the box holds nothing left to recompute from. Federation **discloses** the withholding (HTTP twin → 451; WS peer fetch returns the additive `FedPostGetReply.legal_takedown` marker with an empty body) — deliberate, unlike policy-quarantine's existence-hiding `not_found` (ratified 2026-07-06). **The tombstone row is registered on all three per-actor axes (2026-09-11)** — retained at account deletion (the same delete core that writes it runs there, before the purge walk), moved by an identity succession (the export's withhold set is keyed by the exporting actor, and the corpus it withholds from moves), and exported verbatim (every column is the author's own compelled fact); the deletion and export reasons are the registry entry's (`db/actor_tables.rs`), the succession half is [`succession-repoint-axis.md`](succession-repoint-axis.md) § The declared re-point axis. **And the opposite ordering — deleted, then the order arrives (2026-09-13) — is accepted, not refused.** Before this, `legal_takedown_core` resolved a post's author (and its very existence) through the `content` row a prior delete had already removed, so a compelled order against an already-deleted post was answered `not_found`: no transparency triple, nothing recorded, and the compelled bytes rode the export pair and the blob door exactly as an ordinary deleted post's would until compaction and the next GC sweep caught up. The post arm now resolves the author from the segment's tombstone-inclusive scope lookup (`segments::post::lookup_scope_by_post_id_including_tombstoned`) and reads the digests through the matching tombstone-inclusive body read (`read_body_by_post_id_including_tombstoned` — a reader only, closed and test-enforced alongside the flag-blind primitives above) whenever the ordinary `content`-row lookup comes back empty; a post compaction has already reclaimed still answers `not_found`, same as one never stored. The write lands the obligation row (the author's queue still shows "taken_down") + the audit row + `legal_takedown_deleted_posts` in one transaction — never the `content_meta` flag (no row exists to carry it) and never the legs the delete already ran on its way past (the ATProto retraction witness, the Nostr kind-5, the web re-render). `restore=true` against such a row is audit-only: the floor stays exactly as permanent and inert as the rest of this bullet already rules it, since the bytes left with the author's own delete regardless of which order the compelled order arrived in.
- **The off-box publish surfaces withhold too (closed 2026-07-29; the Nostr relay joined them 2026-08-04).** The ActivityPub outbox + note dereference, the ATProto public-projection stream, and the **Nostr materializer** are serve paths that *publish to third-party public networks*, so a takedown must stop them or it is silently defeated where it matters most. **The Nostr materializer was the last to adopt the predicate (2026-08-04)** — `unmaterialized_exposed_post_ids` now gates on the same constant, pinned per-flag by `nostr::store`'s `a_flagged_post_is_never_materialized_onto_the_public_relay`. All four now apply **one** named predicate — `db::public_servability::PUBLIC_POST_SERVABLE`, the single owner of "may this post be served to an anonymous, off-box audience" — which gates on `legal_takedown_ref`, `quarantined` and `suppressed` exactly as the feed read does, and additionally requires the `content_meta` row to **exist**, so a post whose best-effort index write failed — reachable, see § State & data shape's `put_post` note — is withheld rather than read as ungated. Before this, each path carried its own copy of an incomplete filter; the shared constant is what stops them drifting apart again. **The two create-time push legs apply the predicate's twin (added 2026-09-07).** The ActivityPub `Create`-push and the Bluesky write-through publish a post the instant it lands, on the decoded post, before any `content`/`content_meta` row exists for a SQL predicate to filter — so each had carried its own one-arm copy (`gated` only) for four months, and ruling 1 of `archive-import.md` (an import is served on Fauna, never re-broadcast), landed in the predicate alone, would not have reached them. They now apply `db::public_servability::publishable_off_box_at_create`, kept beside the SQL: the two arms decidable from the post itself, `gated` and the archive-platform `source`. The moderation flags have no create-time meaning (a post cannot carry a takedown at the instant it is created), so the twin deliberately covers only those two; an arm added to the SQL that *has* a create-time meaning is added to the twin in the same change. **Retraction of an already-projected post (closed 2026-07-29).** The predicate alone stops only *future* publication, so a takedown now also writes the **same `tombstone/post` witness a user delete writes** — in the same transaction as the flag, obligation and audit rows, dated at the takedown instant and carrying the post's digest. The ATProto bridge's built retraction path resolves that digest against its record map and issues a real `deleteRecord`, so a post published to Bluesky *before* its takedown is withdrawn rather than left standing. The witness id is derived from the post digest, so it is the same row a later delete would write (at most one retraction per post), and a retraction the bridge has already applied re-reads as a clean no-op. **On the fediverse side, delivered Notes cannot be recalled at all** — an inherent limit, the same one § Legal takedown already records for the conversation plane. **On the Nostr side a takedown retracts — ruled + built 2026-08-04 (refutable while no deployment is live): it emits a NIP-09 kind-5 deletion request through the same leg a self-service delete uses** (`nostr::propagate_post_delete`: signed with the author's deposited key at the established signing position, NIP-09-applied to the nest's own relay store so the derived event stops being served locally, broadcast live, and pushed to the author's configured relays; the key-less degradation arm as for deletes). The takedown handler triggers it immediately, and the sync worker's tick reconciles any takedown whose propagation a crash swallowed — which also healed takedowns predating the ruling. Three grounds, in order: (1) **it is the already-ratified derived-event lifecycle honored on a second trigger, not a new admin power over the author's key** — a derived Nostr event must not outlive the post it was derived from ([`../ui/feed.md`](../ui/feed.md) § State & data shape → *Post deletion*), a takedown ends the post's servable life, and the kind-5's `content` stays empty: it asserts only that the source is gone, never a motive, and never leaks the legal reference. (2) **Parity across the off-box surfaces**: the same transaction's witness already drives a real ATProto `deleteRecord`; retract-where-expressible is the one consistent position, leaving the fediverse's structural inability as the sole exception. (3) **Under-publishing is the safe failure direction** (the overturn ruling's own ground) — a deletion request can only reduce distribution. **Honest reach, recorded so it is never overclaimed:** NIP-09 leaves honoring a kind-5 to each relay's discretion, and copies already fetched by clients or mirrored by non-honoring relays persist; what a takedown may claim is exactly that the nest's own relay no longer serves the derived event, and that a signed deletion request was broadcast to live subscribers and pushed to the author's configured relays — nothing stronger. **The overturn takes the same position as the ATProto restore below, enforced against inverted mechanics:** Nostr materialization is absence-driven (a servable post with no outbound map row materializes on the next sweep), so where the projection stream's watermark makes non-republication the ATProto default, the sweep makes *re*-publication the Nostr default — the materializer input query therefore excludes any post bearing a permanent `TakenDown` obligation row (written in the takedown transaction, kept on restore as additive history), so no-auto-republish holds by construction on every materialization path (the expose toggle, the periodic sweep, a re-expose after unlink) — stronger than the ATProto corollary, where a full re-derive does honor the cleared flag. The author's recourse is unchanged: posting again is a fresh post with a fresh digest, materialized normally. **Quarantine and suppression still only gate future materialization and never retract** — reversible policy levers must not take a permanent-intent act their own reversal cannot undo off-box. Proof: tier_3 `tests/nostr_legal_takedown_kind5.rs` (immediate leg incl. empty-content pin + restore-does-not-rematerialize + reconcile heal).

**An overturn does not re-publish off-box — a ruling, not an inherent limit (2026-07-29; refutable while no deployment is live).** `restore=true` clears the flag and the post re-serves on Fauna, but **no re-publication witness is written**, so the projection stream — whose watermark is long past the post's own row — never re-projects it. Three reasons, in order: (1) **a re-publication is a publication**, and publishing to a searchable, cache-persistent external network is exactly what [`atproto-pds-bridge.md`](atproto-pds-bridge.md) § Projection & backfill makes a deliberate user opt-in — an admin's overturn is not the user's consent to publish again. (2) For a legally compelled mechanism the safe failure direction is **under**-publishing: a bug in an auto-republish path re-publishes legally compelled content, which cannot be recalled once off-box. (3) The user keeps the recourse that matters — they can post again. **State this as a choice, not a limit:** the record key is derived deterministically from the post, so a restore witness *would* return the record to its original address; building one is an option this ruling declines, not a capability the protocol lacks. Two honest corollaries: the overturn *is* honoured by any future full re-derive of the bridge's (explicitly re-derivable) store, which re-walks from the user's consent floor where the cleared flag now passes the predicate; and consuming AppViews index at ingest time, so even a re-publication would surface as fresh activity rather than restore the post in place.
- **Conversations** (`content_type="conversation"`): **best-effort MLS relay-withholding** — the flag is `segment_records.legal_takedown_ref`, keyed on the message's record Cid, and the withhold-on-`Some` check lives in the conv read primitives — `segments::conv::read_after_seq` (single-channel: `channel.fetch`, `federation.channel.fetch`) and its multi-channel twin `read_for_scopes_after_seq` (`mls.pull`), which duplicates the identical never-load-sealed-bytes gate rather than calling into the former — so **every** per-record serve path withholds it: the sealed envelope is withheld from **future** fetches and the tombstone reference is carried in-thread. Boundaries (documented, not bugs — the content is E2E-sealed): it cannot recall content already delivered before the takedown, and cannot reach client-sealed snapshot/replica blobs. Conv records persist no sender, so there is no obligation-queue row — the in-thread tombstone + appeal + audit row are the triple.
- **The blob-serve door withholds the record's attachments too (ruled + built 2026-09-09).** A withheld record's body is withheld on every per-record serve path — for a conversation at the read primitive every such path flows through (§ Conversations below), for a post on each serve path in front of the deliberately flag-blind body primitive, a set that is closed and test-enforced (§ Posts above; the export's `post` segment-pair leg is the one open exception, recorded there) — but a record's *attachments* are not in that body. They are separate content-addressed blobs served by the unauthenticated `GET /api/v1/blob/{id}` (a post's photo, its thumbnail and a gated post's sealed body; a conversation's sealed attachment), named by a hash every recipient already holds: it is inside the message they received, or the post they saw before the flag. So until this ruling a takedown blanked the text and kept relaying the picture, and — since a withheld record is a LIVE mirror row (tombstone-not-delete) — the GC kept the bytes pinned for it to relay. **The rule: a blob is withheld from that door iff every LIVE record naming it is under a takedown — plus every blob named by a post its author deleted while taken down (2026-09-11, § Posts above).** That second clause is not an exception to the first but the same rule where the walk goes blind: such a record is neither live nor readable (its `content` row is gone and its tombstoned body answers `None`), so the rebuild below can neither enumerate it nor learn which blobs it named, and its digests come from what the delete captured. They join the **flagged** side only — a deleted record is not a live record, so it exempts nothing, and a blob some live unflagged record also names keeps serving exactly as the per-record exemption says. The exemption is load-bearing, not decoration: a takedown is compelled against *one record* and its transparency triple is per-record, so withholding a blob that a live *unflagged* record also stands behind would break that record's media with no tombstone, no appeal handle and no audit row — precisely the silent, discretionary removal this section exists to make structurally impossible. The nest can answer the question without reading anything sealed because the plaintext floor already records exactly which blobs a record names (`GatedInfo.attachment_refs` for posts, `conv_attachment_refs` for conversation records — [`../architecture/encryption-at-rest.md`](../architecture/encryption-at-rest.md) § Plaintext floor). **Answer shape: 451 Unavailable For Legal Reasons, no body** — the same disclose-rather-than-hide posture the federation post fetch takes (§ Posts above), and deliberately without the tombstone reference: a blob has no tombstone render of its own, and the reference reaches the client through the record's own serve path, which is what paints it. Both branches of the door are gated **on the resolved digest before either runs** — a hex id and its `b…` multibase CID address the same bytes, so a hex-only gate is bypassed by asking again in base32 — and the gate precedes the `?thumb=1` lookup, so a withheld original never serves a thumbnail of itself. **Tombstone-not-delete is untouched:** the GC pin is deliberately *not* gated on the flag, so the bytes stay on the box and `restore=true` re-serves the very same blob. *Where the predicate lives:* `nest::moderation_withhold`, over a derived `blob_legal_withhold` set — never a floor (every row is recomputable from the flag and the reference sets the box already holds) and never edited incrementally, because both sides of that set-difference move as records come and go and a missed hook would drift in the direction that keeps serving compelled bytes. It is rebuilt **wholesale** in two places: by the takedown/restore handler the instant a flag moves, synchronously before the reply, so an admin's confirm returning “taken_down” means the door is already shut; and by every blob-GC sweep, folded into the record walk it already performs ([`backup-restore.md`](backup-restore.md) § 9 steps 2f/2g), which is what keeps it true afterwards. **Completeness decides only whether a walk may RELEASE:** a walk that read every live record replaces the set, an incomplete one may only add to it — a run that could not read some record cannot know which blobs it named, so it fails toward over-withholding (which the next complete walk corrects) rather than back toward serving compelled bytes (which nothing corrects). What the reconcile buys: a new record naming a withheld blob releases it, and the removal of the last unflagged record naming a shared one withholds it. A per-request join was weighed and rejected — conversations have a real reverse index but posts deliberately have none ([`backup-restore.md`](backup-restore.md) § 9 step 2f walks live records rather than a table written at ingest), so answering per GET would hand an anonymous caller a full-store walk. **The withhold is a property of the BYTES, so it binds every route onto the blob store — not one door (2026-09-10).** `GET /api/v1/blob/{id}` is the door the rule was written for, but the video plane serves the same digests out of the same store at `GET /api/v1/video/segments/{hash}`, and a video post's manifest, thumbnail and every segment hash are in the withheld set (`Post::blob_refs`). Until this was closed a takedown was bypassed by a **path-prefix substitution on the very hex digest the requester already holds** — 451 at one path, 200 at the other. **The same substitution worked at two further paths until 2026-09-11**: `GET /api/v1/chunks/{hash}` and `GET /api/v1/manifests/{hash}` — the file-sync data plane's own content-addressed reads, mounted unauthenticated on the identical store on the rationale that a ciphertext hash is the capability — served the withheld digest 200 while the two doors above answered 451. Every such door calls the one named gate (`blob_routes::legal_takedown_gate`, over the predicate `blob_routes::is_legally_withheld`) rather than restating the posture, so the answer shape cannot drift between them. **The gate precedes the store read AND any arm that would fetch the bytes back from elsewhere**, and that ordering is load-bearing rather than tidy: the segment door's cache-on-fetch pulls a missing segment from a delivery-source peer and stores it back, and the chunk door's folder-hinted arm relays a missing chunk from one of the folder owner's seats and — for a full-residency folder — `store.put`s it back, so a gate placed after either would let that door *re-import* compelled bytes onto a box that no longer had them: the takedown running backwards. **The manifest→chunks walk is gated at the walk, not once per door.** `web_content::file_bytes::read_file_by_manifest` reassembles a file out of the digests a manifest names, and any registered user may upload a manifest naming a withheld digest (`POST /api/v1/manifests` is `BulkWriteAuth`, and for a single-chunk manifest the declared `file_hash` IS that digest, so crafting one needs nothing beyond the hex the requester already holds) — so the withhold is consulted on the manifest hash and on every chunk the walk reads, which binds its three caller families at once: the public share link (`share_routes::handle_share`), the author's own web site (`web_content::serve`) and the site render (`web_content::service`). The refusal is a 451 wherever the request names the withheld file, and the one arm that answers otherwise does so deliberately: when the withheld file is the site's own custom `404.html`, a request for some *other*, missing path gets the built-in 404 page, because that request never named the withheld bytes and a 451 there would misreport a path that simply does not exist — while a request for `/404.html` itself resolves to the same `web_files` row and answers 451. **One consequence, stated here rather than discovered later:** `/api/v1/chunks` is the file-sync data plane and the store is content-addressed, so a legitimate sync of a chunk whose bytes dedup to a withheld digest is answered 451 like any other request for those bytes. That is the rule working as written — the withhold is keyed on the BYTES, and a second namer is exactly the case the per-record exemption above already decides at set-rebuild time — not a defect of the gate. **How the next door is enrolled: mechanically, not by this paragraph.** The standing duty went unchecked long enough for four doors to ship ungated, each found only when somebody happened to look — and writing the mechanism found a fifth route that nobody ever had: the **mail byte plane's upward body reference** (`mail_body_plane::resolve_body_ref`), whose chunk digests come off the REQUEST and whose join is authenticated only against a declared total, so an authenticated bridge session could name a withheld digest as a body chunk, pad to the total, and read the compelled bytes back out as its own message. It is gated on the same predicate; its staged twin (`resolve_staged_body`) deliberately is **not**, because that join carries an AEAD tag under the key the caller's own reference supplies, so a blob they merely named can never satisfy it — which its witness asserts adversarially, by naming a withheld digest in a staged reference. So the duty is now a test rather than a promise: `backup::service`'s partition walks every production call site onto the store — every caller of `BackupService::local_blob_store` and, because the handle outlives the call that acquires it, every use of the handle a long-lived holder keeps (the chunk resolver, the payload store and the web-content service) and every caller of a holder method that hands stored bytes back — and requires each to be classified: a **reader-named** serve path, which must name the gate that withholds and whose production body the test checks actually calls it (a private helper names instead the doors it is reached from, and must be reached from nothing else); an **owner-scoped** serve path, which no caller can aim at a digest because the digest comes from a row this nest wrote or from bytes the caller itself supplied; a **primitive**, a holder method whose every caller is enrolled in its own right; or a **reader**, from which nothing derived from the store leaves the process. It fails on a caller nobody has classified and on a classified door whose gate call is gone, so a new door is enrolled on the day it is written and an enrolled one cannot quietly lose its gate. That partition, never a count written in prose, is the authority on how many doors there are; the gate is `pub(crate)` to make reuse the path of least resistance. **What the withhold binds on OWNER- and ADMIN-scoped routes (ruled 2026-09-12).** The partition's owner-scoped class holds routes no caller can aim at a digest, and "binds every route onto the store" cannot be read there as "gate them all" — gating an admin's own export or an owner's own backup mirror could destroy data the user is entitled to — so each such route needs a decision, and the decision follows from what a takedown removes. **A takedown removes a record's AVAILABILITY THROUGH THIS NEST, never the box's CUSTODY of the bytes**; tombstone-not-delete is the same statement from the storage side (the bytes stay on disk precisely so `restore=true` can re-serve them). Three questions, asked in order, decide any owner- or admin-scoped route — today's four and the next one: **(a) Is it custody moving with the box?** The box's own store copied whole, to the operator the order was served on (who already holds every byte under their own root) or into the deployment's own new backend — NOT bound, and MUST carry the bytes: the flag (`content_meta`), the order (`audit_log`) and the post-delete floor (`legal_takedown_deleted_posts`) ride beside them in the logical dump, and a copy that dropped the withheld bytes would turn the tombstone into a deletion at the next restore, so an overturn could re-serve nothing. **(b) Does it serve the record to a viewer the record's own serve path withholds it from?** Bound, in § *Posts*' shape: withheld AND declared, never silently dropped — and the author is such a viewer, since § *Posts* withholds a taken-down body from every viewer including the author, so the author's own archive is bound on every leg. **(c) Does it put the bytes behind a door this nest does not control?** A push that lands them, under the same digest, in another box's content-addressed store — whose own unauthenticated door holds no flag — is the path-prefix substitution above with the *host* substituted instead: bound, withhold-and-declare, and retract-where-expressible (the ATProto and Nostr legs' posture), because that door serves a copy pushed before the flag exactly as one pushed after. The four, decided: **(1) the author export's blob leg** (`export_routes::gather_export_data`; question (b)) — bound, and DORMANT: its extractor `scan_blob_refs` is a no-op, so the leg ships no store blob today and the archive's real blob carriage is the `post` segment-pair leg, already withheld whole and declared (decision (6)); the day an extractor lands, each gathered digest passes `blob_routes::is_legally_withheld` before the store read and a withheld one is declared in the manifest. A witness cannot be written against a leg that carries nothing, so the binding is recorded at the loop and in the partition entry rather than fabricated. **(2) the admin whole-store export** (`admin_export_routes::handle_admin_export_all`; question (a)) — not bound: carries, and must. **(3) the folder mirror** (`segment_backup::run_folder_once`; question (c)) — bound. A covered folder is mirrored latest-per-path into the destination's own store under the source's store keys ([`backup-destinations.md`](backup-destinations.md) § Ordinary-folder coverage), where `/api/v1/chunks/{hash}` would serve a public-audience folder's plaintext chunk to anyone holding the hex this nest answers 451 for. A folder file is never itself the subject of an order (`content_type` is `post` or `conversation`), so a withheld digest reaches a folder only by content-address dedup with a taken-down record's attachment — the same second-namer collateral the chunk door's 451 already carries, and bounded here to *delay*: a live path naming a withheld digest is skipped, counted in the pass report (`FolderRunReport::withheld_paths`), retracted at the destination if an earlier pass mirrored it (the ordinary path delete, retained under the destination's grace window), and re-tried every pass, so it mirrors the moment the flag lifts; the owner's local file is never touched. The set is read once per pass and tested in memory, so a box with no takedown pays nothing. **A per-(destination, folder) memo (`folder_withhold_checkpoint`) skips re-opening an already-mirrored manifest once its content is confirmed against the current withheld set, and is stamped only after BOTH the live loop and the dropped-paths teardown finish clean — never before the teardown — so a teardown failure never leaves a stamp claiming to have rechecked a path it did not.** Reachable, not hypothetical: a path can be deleted and later recreated with byte-identical content, which is the same manifest hash (a pure function of the bytes, uncorrelated with when they were written), so a stale-but-trusted stamp over such a path is a real failure mode, pinned directly. (The reserved-rail segment pass is not this route: `BACKED_UP_KINDS` is `mail`, sealed under a key the destination never holds.) **(4) the derived room-post view** (`room_post_view::index_room_post`; question (b), asked of the view rather than the read) — the reception pass's store read runs in the act that stores the post, before any flag can exist on it, and is not gated; what it derives IS a serve path for the body: a `room.search` hit is an existence oracle over the withheld text (search for a rare word, get the post id back) and a `posts.room_labels` verdict was scored from it, both served to the room's floor — a non-author audience. Both maps (`db::rooms::room_post_ids_for_docs` and `rooms_indexing_posts`, behind `ROOM_POST_VIEW_MODERATION`) apply `MODERATION_SERVABLE` over `content_meta` — the fragment every non-author surface shares — so a taken-down room post yields no hit and no verdict while the flag stands; the view rows stay (tombstone-not-delete — a re-derivation after the members rotated this nest out would be impossible), the overturn re-serves them with nothing recomputed, and the post's own delete still purges them through its unconditional walk. Not reopened here: the chunk door's dedup ruling above. ## User-initiated reporting (designed 2026-08-27; ratified 2026-09-25 — routing + ID package user-approved; nest local loop, federation triad and block-hides built, built on tui 2026-09-27, web 2026-10-07, macos + ios 2026-10-07, windows 2026-10-08 and android 2026-10-08 (compile + Robolectric on Linux; the emulator run is host-owed), owed on linux; sequencing ruled 2026-10-07 — iOS paints it before the first external TestFlight build)

**Why this exists.** Apple's App Review Guideline 1.2 requires every app with user-generated content to ship four things — a way to filter objectionable material, **a mechanism to report offensive content with timely responses to concerns**, the ability to block abusive users, and published contact information. Fauna meets three (filtering: § Categories & enforcement item 1's engine plus muted keywords; block: `fauna.knocks.block`, [`../ui/contacts.md`](../ui/contacts.md) § Where logic lives → *Unblock*; contact information: a store-listing task) and **has no report mechanism at all**. Google Play's user-generated-content policy asks the same of an in-app report path. The gap gates external TestFlight and the first public submission, not internal testing.

**The shape must hold five things this doc and its neighbours already ratify.** (1) **No nest-wide policy removal** (§ Categories & enforcement item 1) — a report may not become a lever. (2) **The user controls their data** ([`../principles.md`](../principles.md) § The user always controls their data) — the nest reads no sealed content to adjudicate, and nobody's content is disclosed by a mechanism, only by a person who could already read it. (3) **One configuration surface, no operator** — the humans are users and admins in the apps; a report goes to an *admin's app*, never to a mailbox, a file, or a dashboard outside the product. (4) **Federation** — the reported author frequently lives on a nest this admin has no authority over. (5) **Reporter identity never crosses a nest boundary** — the report-sharing invariant ([`../architecture/content-moderation-and-ranking.md`](../architecture/content-moderation-and-ranking.md) § Distributed report sharing), applied verbatim to attributed notices.

### What exists, and why none of it is this

Six lookalikes, each something else — listed so no session mistakes one for the mechanism:

- `fauna.moderation.appeal` — the **author's** appeal against a takedown of their *own* content (§ Legal takedown).
- The moderation queue — the caller's flagged items on their **own** content (§ Goal — "*not* an admin content-removal console").
- `fauna.moderation.report_share.*` / `report-share-published-list` — the k-anonymized **spam-aggregate** exchange ([`report-sharing.md`](report-sharing.md)): machine signal over content hashes; no human reads a report.
- `fauna.moderation.scan_report` — gone (a retired compliance beacon; off the wire 2026-09-24 — § State & data shape).
- `dm-message-mark-as-spam-button` — the one existing gesture on *someone else's* content: it trains the **reporter's own sealed spam model** ([`mail-spam.md`](mail-spam.md)) and reaches no human. It is, however, the richest existing UI pattern for a per-item verb on received content — the `⋯` overflow → an indexed verb gated `!is_own` — and the report verb sits **beside** it, never replacing it.
- the sender-**reputation** plane — gone: the peer reputation exchange and its `sender_reputation` store were removed 2026-10-02 ([`../architecture/federation.md`](../architecture/federation.md) § Federation residue surface → *The reputation leg is ruled removed*). **A human report never feeds a per-sender reputation signal, nor anything that succeeds that plane**: a per-reporter attributed reputation signal would turn reporting into a reputation weapon — a rule that outlives the plane.

### Two candidate shapes, against the invariants

**Decided 2026-09-25 (user): B ⊇ A, the superset, as recommended.** Both shapes stay stated here so the decision reads against the invariants it was made against.

- **Shape A — reporter-local.** A report is a personal-filter act: the content is hidden *for the reporter* (optionally with a block), and nothing leaves the reporter's device. Every invariant holds trivially. **It does not meet the requirement**: no human with authority ever learns of the report, so there is no "timely response", and a reviewer pressing *Report* sees only a hide. It is also already most of the way built — it is mark-as-spam plus block.
- **Shape B — evidence to a human with authority.** A report lands on an admin's queue; the admin acts, if at all, only with the levers they already hold — § Legal takedown (posts + conversations; transparent, appealable, audited) and [`admin.md`](admin.md)'s suspension (an account power, not a content one). Every invariant holds: a report changes nothing for anyone but the reporter, sealed content stays sealed unless the reporter chooses to attach what they could already read, and the reporter's identity stays on their own nest.
- **Ratified: B ⊇ A, the superset.** Every report has A's immediate reporter-side effect *and* B's routing. The reporter is protected the moment they report, regardless of whether or when an admin acts; the requirement's "response" half is met by B.

### Routing — whose queue, and how a report crosses a nest boundary

**A report always lands on the reporter's own nest admin's queue, and is forwarded — reporter-anonymously — to the reported author's home nest when that author is foreign.** Both admins hold a real lever and nothing more: the *home* admin holds suspension over the account and the source-side takedown (whose withholding federation discloses); the *reporter's* admin holds the takedown over the local projection of a foreign post (`content_meta` rows exist for federated posts this nest projects, so § Legal takedown's flag applies to the local copy) and is the one who can see false-report abuse by their own user. Neither is a "report to a global authority" — there is none. The fediverse converged on this same shape independently (report to your own admins, forward to the remote instance), which is also what makes it legible to a store reviewer.

- **Home-nest resolution** is the provenance every existing path already uses: a post's author actor → the local `users` row, or the origin nest the post arrived from; a conversation message's sender → the channel roster's home `nest_id` ([`../architecture/federation.md`](../architecture/federation.md) § Federation residue surface, the channel roster-read row); a profile → the actor's home nest.
- **Forwarding is automatic, not a per-report checkbox (ratified 2026-09-25)** — reporter identity never crosses, so forwarding has no privacy cost, and a report against an author is by nature addressed to the one admin who holds a lever over that account. The fediverse's opt-in variant (a per-report forward checkbox) was offered and declined.
- **The receiving nest accepts only reports about content or accounts it hosts** — a nest is never a relay for reports about third nests — and applies the per-origin throttle every federation kind carries.
- **Withdrawal propagates** (the forwarded copy carries the reporter's note and excerpt — their data, revocable from their app), and **the outcome propagates back** (§ What the reporter is told).
- **Delivery is durable and pinned (built 2026-09-26).** Each leg rides a per-nest send queue, retried with backoff for up to seven days, so a home nest that is down when the report is filed still receives it; the submit tries its delivery once inline, briefly, so the acknowledgement names the home nest when it was reached, and names only the reporter's own nest otherwise — a destination is claimed only once it accepted. A refusal (a subject the receiver does not host, a peer that serves no such kind) is final. The withdrawal and the outcome are pinned to the nest identity the delivery was verified as, so no other nest can withdraw a report or speak its outcome; a withdrawal racing an in-flight delivery is queued the moment that delivery lands. The receiver records the origin's declared address against its verified identity (`federation.md`'s relayed-origin rule) — that is how the outcome finds a nest it may never have dialled.

### What a report carries

`{ subject, reason, note?, excerpt?, block_author }`. The **subject** is one of `post { cid }`, `message { channel, record_cid }` (the same key § Legal takedown's conversation half uses, so an admin can withhold a sealed message without ever reading it), or `actor { actor_id }` (a profile/account report). The **reason** is a shared-Rust enum — `spam · harassment · hate · violence · sexual · illegal · impersonation · other` — a *report-reason* vocabulary deliberately distinct from the canonical-5 label categories (those are classifier emissions; a report emits no label), rendered as `LocalizedText`, never per-app strings. The **note** is bounded free text. The **excerpt** is the reporter-attached plaintext of a **sealed** subject (a conversation message, a gated post): bounded, and **opt-in per report through a checkbox whose label names the consequence** ("the admins will be able to read this message") — the nest holds no readable bytes for sealed kinds, so without it an admin can act on the *account* and withhold by id, but cannot judge the text. Attaching it is the reporter's own act on content they already hold — the same act as forwarding a message — so the mechanism discloses nothing on its own (invariant 2). Public and feed posts need no excerpt: the admin opens them by id. **`block_author`** chains the existing `fauna.knocks.block` — one gesture, two existing mechanisms; the block's semantics stay `contacts.md`'s, untouched.

### Where it lands, who acts, and with what

- **Persistence (nest-side, additive):** `abuse_reports` — `id, created_at, reporter_actor (local reporters; NULL on a forwarded copy), origin_nest_id (forwarded copies; NULL when local), subject_kind, subject_id, subject_actor, reason, note, excerpt, forwarded_to, forwarded_nest_id, status (open | resolved | withdrawn), outcome (acted | dismissed), resolved_at, resolved_by` — on a forwarded copy `origin_nest_id` is the channel-verified origin, and `forwarded_to`/`forwarded_nest_id` are set only once the home nest accepted the delivery — beside `abuse_report_outbox`, the triad's send queue (derived send state, never user data). Resolved rows stay as the admin's audit record, like every obligation row; a reporter may withdraw an *open* report (their data), and withdrawal deletes the note and excerpt everywhere they went. **Account deletion is the reporter's withdrawal of every open report they filed, and nothing more (ruled 2026-09-26):** the deletion leg withdraws each open row exactly as the living door does — status `withdrawn`, note and excerpt deleted, a queued delivery dropped, a delivery that reached the home nest followed by a `withdraw` queued in the same step as the withdrawal, so a crash loses neither and the forwarder sends it after the account is gone (the queue follows the report by id, not the actor; a delivery in flight is covered by its landing) — and leaves every resolved row whole, note, excerpt and the retired reporter id included: resolving turned the reporter's submission into the record of what the admin decided and on what evidence, which survives its author exactly as an appeal's reason does (§ Errors & edge cases), and a living reporter cannot strip a resolved row either, so deletion opens no door withdrawal does not. Withdrawn rows keep the skeleton the living withdrawal leaves (subject, reason, dates, status, the reporter id), naming the retired id as every other act of a deleted account does (`audit_log`). A purge was declined because it would delete the audit record; stripping resolved rows' words was declined because it would leave a decision with no evidence to audit and a since-deleted false reporter with no trail for their admin (§ Anti-abuse posture bound 5). An outcome arriving for a withdrawn row changes nothing (it is taken only while open), so no notification is ever written for a departed reporter. The leg is `abuse_reports`' `Policy::Partial` entry in the nest's actor-table registry (`db/actor_tables.rs`), run by the purge walk every account deletion takes; forwarded copies carry no reporter and are untouched by any local deletion.
- **The admin's levers are exactly the existing ones, and a queue row is only a door to them:** *open takedown* pre-fills § Legal takedown's console with the subject's id + kind — the console's own guards (a required legal reference, the named confirm) still apply, so a report can never become a citation-less takedown; *suspend* is the `admin-users` page's own control, reached by navigation; *resolve as acted* and *dismiss* are **records, not actions** — resolving does nothing to the content or the author. There is no fourth verb. This is what keeps item 1's invariant intact: the report plane adds evidence and an audit trail, never a lever.
- **The doorbell rides the existing notification machinery** ([`notifications.md`](notifications.md)): on a new open report the nest writes an ordinary `notifications` row for each admin (`notif_type: "abuse_report.received"`, `source: "fauna"`), deduped per `(subject_id, day)` — the UTC day, since the nest holds no admin's timezone — — the Guardian Notify flood bound ([`family-client-enforcement.md`](family-client-enforcement.md) § Guardian Notify) — so a pile-on is one doorbell, and the queue's open count is the truth.

### What the reporter is told

- **At submit:** an acknowledgement naming where the report went — the reporter's own nest, and the author's home nest when forwarded — composed in shared Rust, never per-app.
- **At resolution:** a notification (`notif_type: "abuse_report.resolved"`) carrying the **outcome only** — *acted* or *dismissed* — never the admin's reasoning, and nothing about the author beyond what the reporter already knew. For a forwarded report the home nest returns the outcome over the federation channel, and the reporter's nest — the only party holding the reporter's identity — writes the row.
- **Always:** a *Your reports* list on the Moderation page (the one-sentence widening of § Goal): subject, reason, destination(s), status, and withdraw while open.
- **The author is told nothing by a report.** They learn of a consequence only through the lever's own transparency — the takedown tombstone + appeal, or suspension — never that they were reported, or by whom: a report-notified author is a retaliation vector.

### Anti-abuse posture

Reporting is itself an abuse surface; six bounds, all structural:

1. **Informs, never compels.** Nothing happens to anyone but the reporter automatically — no hide, no label, no rank penalty, no reputation. A flood of false reports costs an admin attention and nothing else.
2. **A hard-coded per-reporter rate** (`ABUSE_REPORTS_PER_HOUR`, a shared-Rust constant beside `REPORT_MIN_REPORTERS` — bucket (1) of the one-configuration-surface invariant: no knob on any surface) and **one open report per (reporter, subject)**.
3. **Federation:** per-origin-nest throttle, accepted only for hosted subjects, reporter-anonymous — a hostile nest can send noise about the receiver's accounts only at the throttle, and about anyone else's to nobody.
4. **No feed into any other plane** — not a per-sender reputation signal, not `content_reports` (the k-anonymized `report:*` aggregates, which are opt-in flags over content hashes, not attributed notices; conflating them would let one report plus forwarding manufacture a signal), not `content_labels`.
5. **False-report abuse is the reporter's own admin's case** — the reporter's identity is visible only there, and that admin's lever (suspension) is the same one every other abuse gets.
6. **A supervised account reports like anyone else** — no guardian gate on the gesture ([`family-safety.md`](family-safety.md) owns anything further).

### Corollary — block also hides (ruled with the design pass; carried by the 2026-09-25 ratification)

Block today gates **reach** — `reach_floor` at the inbox-write choke point (`bins/fauna-nest/src/routes.rs`) — and a blocked actor's posts still appear in the blocker's feed (`bins/fauna-nest/src/db/feeds.rs` has no blocked predicate). A reviewer who blocks an account and keeps seeing its posts reads that as the third 1.2 requirement unmet. **Ruling:** blocking also excludes the blocked actor from the viewer's own feed reads — a personal filter, item 1's plane exactly, hiding nothing for anyone else. **Mechanism:** applied **where the viewer becomes known** — the per-viewer stage of the viewer's own feed read (the position `augment_viewer_state` already occupies), as an `AuthorNotInSet` over the viewer's `blocked` contact edges — never inside the viewer-independent query fns a peer nest also calls, and with no second copy in the client's `FeedManager`. *Built 2026-09-26:* the rule's 32 bytes name the **viewer**, whose blocked edges form the set, and it rides the feed read's **mandatory** group (the one the spam guard rides), so a feed's `Any` combination cannot OR it away; the query translator honours that reading in the mandatory group only, and a feed's own rules — which a user authors, and which could name someone else to read their block list — cannot carry it. It binds the local feed, the Trending feed and every feed of the viewer's own, chronological and scored; the federated feed read adds no such rule. Unblock (which clears the edge — `contacts.md`) restores the posts on the next read. **Reported content is likewise hidden for the reporter:** the subject's id joins the sealed per-user `hidden_content` list of the account-state plane kind `fauna.state.moderation` (the E1 cluster — [`../architecture/config-dissolution.md`](../architecture/config-dissolution.md) § The `__config` dissolution schedule → *The kinds*; the `muted_keywords` sibling — same record, same delta-pair seam) that the render-verdict engine ([`family-client-enforcement.md`](family-client-enforcement.md) § Content policy) reads as a `Block` verdict with a "you reported this" placeholder, so a reporter who does not block still stops seeing what they reported. *Built 2026-09-26 (shared Rust):* an entry is the report subject's id — a post or message id, or an account's actor id, which hides everything that account authored — lowercased, deduped, capped at the 2,000 newest; `obligation::render_verdict_for_item` is the one entry point that reads it, answering `Block` attributed `Reported` unless a region's own `Block` holds the attribution (an authority's reason is the user's to read verbatim), and the list is written only through the shared add/remove deltas on both preference rails.

## State & data shape

Reuse the **existing** `fauna.moderation.*` wire (`libs/fauna-protocol/src/moderation.rs`) — do **not** invent a `ModerationSnapshot`/`FlaggedItem`/`TrainingEvent` (the prior draft's speculative shape is dropped in favour of the built types, priority #4):

- **Queue** = `fauna.moderation.actions` → `ModerationActionsReply { actions: Vec<ObligationAction> }`, scoped to the connection actor's own content. `ObligationAction { id, content_type, content_id, category, confidence_per_mille: u16, action: u8, timestamp }`. Each row is a queue item; `action` is the obligation-action discriminant (labeled / quarantined / rejected / taken-down). This server queue never carries social-content classifier actions — every nest classifies nothing at ingest (`process_on_ingest` retired with the storage-mode axis, Phase 4 2026-07-12; `content-scoring.md` § Architectural rules: "the nest never runs a content scorer over content it holds no key for" — the scorers a nest does run, a community room's labelers under its members' grant and a region's scorers over the public pages it publishes, produce per-message labels and page placeholders, not queue actions); it carries **legal-takedown rows only** — a mail quarantine/reject writes no `obligation_action_records` row by ratified design (§ Categories & enforcement item 2, user ruling 2026-09-26), so the queue's server half is the legal-takedown ledger.
  - **The moderation-queue the client renders is the UNION of these server `ObligationAction`s and the client's own post-decrypt local detections** (ratified 2026-07-02 — the superset / richest pattern, priority #4). Every app classifies plaintext it can read (its user's own decrypted content — § Where logic lives) and surfaces those local flags — spam-only `{ category, confidence }`, **no** `action` — alongside the server rows, so the queue is non-empty and train-correctable on every nest, since the server never carries social-content actions. Local detections carry no `action` (blank action column); dedupe server-vs-local by `content_id`. The local detections shown in the queue are held **client-side** (the session-owned shared `LocalDetectionStore`), not round-tripped through the nest — nothing about a local detection reaches the nest (the former coarse `scan_report` compliance beacon is gone from the wire; see its bullet below).
- **Aggregate counts** = `fauna.moderation.stats` → `Vec<LabelStat { category, count, avg_confidence_per_mille }>`.
- **Training correction** = `fauna.moderation.train { content_id, verdict: "spam" | "ham" }` against a stored post — the **nest half** of a correction: the caller-may-read gate (a taken-down, quarantined or absent post answers `not_found`) and the report capture (`report-sharing.md` § Report capture). **It trains nothing** (since 2026-10-02): the per-user model is sealed under the user's key, so the model half is the client's sealed write (`fauna.bridges.put_spam_model`, `mail-spam.md` § Encrypted-mode interaction). One shared flow runs both halves — sealed write when the user's key is present (mail enabled), then this kind always — so every app reports on every correction. A user without mail enabled has no per-user model, and the correction reports only.
- **Appeal** = `fauna.moderation.appeal { content_id, reason }` (an appeal of a legal-takedown decision → audit trail; there is no mail-quarantine appeal — § Categories & enforcement item 2).
- **Client-side scan report** = `fauna.moderation.scan_report { labels: Vec<ScanLabel> }` — **GONE (producer retired 2026-07-19; the kind, its wire types and the nest handler left the wire 2026-09-24 with the compat-remnant sweep — `version-compatibility.md` § Dim 2, the fourth ratified exception).** The kind was a coarse compliance beacon ("a client is scanning"): the handler only audit-logs `labels_count=N` — no label storage, no read path anywhere (the reason `family-client-enforcement.md` § Guardian Notify got its own table). No client produces it **by design**: after the no-modes ratification, local detections are held client-side and never round-tripped, and an *unconsented* client→nest report of per-category counts derived from the user's own decrypted content contradicts the user-controls-their-data invariant — the consented coarse-counts shape lives in Guardian Notify (guardianship-gated, `content_notify`-gated, with a real read path). Pre-migration clients produced the HTTP twin, which kept the WS-RPC handler registered-and-answering (the `fauna.event.rsvp` dead-registration precedent) until the 2026-09-23 user-population ruling retired every such remnant; the client-side producer scaffolding (web `LabelBuffer`/`classifyAndReport`, android `ModerationManager` batch-flush, the shared `ModerationClient::scan_report` / FFI / wasm seams) was **deleted** rather than left dark. Reviving client→nest scan telemetry requires a fresh design pass with an explicit user-facing consent surface (all-7-app UI + nest state, per the configuration invariant) — do not rewire the old shape.
- **Model sync** = `fauna.moderation.model_sync` — **GONE (2026-10-02).** It uploaded a plaintext model for the nest to adopt-if-larger; no app called it, and a nest that stores only sealed models has nothing to compare (`mail-spam.md` § Implementation status item 4b).

Confidence rides as **per-mille `u16`** (0–1000) everywhere — the dag-cbor wire forbids floats — mirroring the `fauna_protocol::spam` threshold encoding.

## Per-row badge data path (feed & conversations) — ratified 2026-07-16

The § Element IDs badge claim ("wherever flagged content renders") has a data-path corollary this section owns: **how a category label reaches an individual feed post or conversation message row.** Today label data reaches only the moderation queue (`ObligationAction` rows ∪ local detections), so the badge renders only there; feed and conversation snapshots carry no label field (gap declared in § Implementation status today).

**One vocabulary, two projections — no third plane.** The per-row unit is the exact pair the queue already speaks: `{ category, confidence_per_mille: u16 }` — a shared `ContentLabelEntry` type living beside `content_label_style` in `fauna_core::content_category`. It **projects the existing label plane (`content_labels`)**; it is not a new store, not a new wire vocabulary, and not a scoring-bus row — `content_scores` remains the composition/ordering substrate, while the label plane is the category-verdict entry on the metadata bus (`../architecture/content-scoring.md` § The scoring-metadata bus).

- **Feed (transparent kinds — nest-projected).** The wire `FeedPostItem` gains an additive `labels: Vec<ContentLabelEntry>` (`#[serde(default)]`; empty from an older nest), projected by `query_feed` / `query_feed_scored` from `content_labels` — a table **already part of the retained feed-index projection** (`../ui/feed.md` § The read model), consumed filter-only today (`FilterRule::{HasLabel,LabelBelow,LabelAbove}`); this adds the per-row read-out. One entry per category (the highest-confidence row), joined by the same hex-id bridge the filter predicates use. `PostSummary` mirrors the field. The nest **serves** stored label metadata; it never computes a label (`../architecture/content-scoring.md` § Architectural rules — placement unchanged).
- **Conversations (sealed kind — client-populated).** `MessageSnapshot` gains the same `labels: Vec<ContentLabelEntry>`, populated by the manager from the same post-decrypt classify pass that feeds the `LocalDetectionStore` (`ConversationsManager::ingest_inbound_to_thread`). For an **end-to-end** room it never round-trips the nest — the nest holds neither plaintext nor label rows for MLS-sealed content. A **community** room's home nest is the one exception, being a reader by its members' grant: it serves the verdicts of the labelers the room names beside each message, to live floor members (`ChannelFetchEntry.labels`), and the manager merges them into `labels` by the queue's superset rule — a server verdict wins its category, a local detection stays on the device (`fauna_core::content_category::merge_server_labels`; the mechanism is [`community-rooms.md`](community-rooms.md)'s, § The three classes → *What the home nest does with its read*). Distinct from `badges.content_warning` (a sender-declared flag, kept) and from the muted-keyword collapse (a keyword penalty scorer, not a label — § Muted keywords).
- **Badge semantics — inform, never compel.** The badge renders whenever any label entry is present: the highest-confidence entry styles via `content_label_style` (canonical-5 → styled; off-list → the grey `Other`). Rendering is transparency, not enforcement: threshold-driven collapse/hide remains the render-verdict engine designed in `family-client-enforcement.md` § Content policy — this data path must not grow its own collapse logic (build that engine once, there, reading these same entries).
- **The attach door enforces that producer set — an ownership question, not a class question (ratified 2026-09-11).** For a feed post, `fauna.labels.attach` admits exactly two callers: the post's **own author** (an unsigned self-label — the honest scope of a label carrying no attestation), and a holder of a **`content.label-write` grant from that author** (the third-party case: per-scope, time-bounded, revocable, audited from the author's own app — `../architecture/encryption-at-rest.md` § Capability tiering). Ownership is resolved from the store, never from anything the caller declares. Any other member's label on someone else's post is refused, and a `content_id` naming no post is refused with it. **Why it is a hard gate and not a preference:** the two nest-side verdicts in the bullet below bind *every* reader, so a class-only gate made any member a nest-wide remover of any member's post — precisely what § Categories & enforcement item 1 forbids the nest to do on any opinion, a member's included.
- **A verdict that binds every reader must name its writer (ratified 2026-09-11).** A `content_labels` row is a category verdict *with provenance* (`../architecture/content-scoring.md` § The scoring-metadata bus), so each writer is recorded on the row it writes — the writing position in `scanner_id`, the producer in `classifier_id`, which being part of the upsert key also stops two producers sharing one last-writer-wins row. The two nest-side reads whose verdict a reader never chose — **the mandatory feed spam group** (the `LabelBelow{spam,500}` every feed read ANDs unless that feed sets its own spam rule) and **the per-post projection** the badge and the nest-as-publisher fold share ([`region-blocking.md`](region-blocking.md) § The nest-as-publisher leg) — therefore admit **only an attributed row**: an unattributed one informs nobody and removes nothing. **The writer columns follow their writer across an identity succession** — a label is revised by attaching again and the upsert keys on the producer, so a row left on a retired identity would outvote its own author's revision for good; the row itself is its content's and leaves with it ([`opaque-carrier-walks.md`](opaque-carrier-walks.md) § The declared re-point axis owns the ruling). A feed's **own** `LabelBelow`/`LabelAbove`/`HasLabel` rules sit deliberately outside this: they are the user's own filter over their own view (item 1's user-side plane), theirs to aim at whatever the plane holds.
- **Producers unchanged.** Labels keep coming only from positions sanctioned by `../architecture/content-scoring.md`: the perimeter (mail), the user's client post-decrypt, or a capability-granted holder — attached over `fauna.labels.attach`. This path adds *serving*, not classification; in particular it does **not** add client-side classification of the feed-list preview (prohibited — § Implementation status today, feed local-detection note).

## Where logic lives

- **Classifier — shared Rust.** The built-in **text heuristic** is `fauna_core::text_heuristic::classify_text`, consumed identically by web (WASM `fauna_wasm::classifyText`) and native apps (the `fauna-ffi` `classifyText` façade over `fauna_client_core::scan`) at post-decrypt. Apps keep **no** hand-rolled copy. **Placement is governed by [`content-scoring.md`](../architecture/content-scoring.md), not here:** a content classifier runs only where plaintext is available — the mail bridge perimeter pre-seal, or the user's client post-decrypt; **never** the nest, on any box. This doc does not re-litigate the trust boundary.
- **Category-label presentation — shared Rust (the drift #157 lift).** The badge label/colour/icon is a shared enum keyed on the wire `category` string (one of the canonical 5) returning a `LocalizedText`, mirroring the `fauna_protocol::spam::spam_threshold_band` precedent (`settings.md` § Spam threshold slider labels): web via a wasm export, Apple/Windows/Android via UniFFI, linux native. **No app hard-codes the category strings or their styling** — this removes web's hard-coded `Spam`/`Trusted`/`NSFW`/`Phishing`/`Commercial` map in `ContentLabelBadge.svelte` (and android's `ContentLabelBadge.kt`). **Any off-list `category` degrades to a neutral grey `Other` badge (`fauna_core::content_category::content_label_style` → `ContentCategory::Other`, rendering the capitalized raw string) — never a panic and never a dropped row.** The canonical-5 rule constrains **classifiers** (§ Don't do these — "don't emit a `category` outside the canonical 5"), **not** admin legal-compulsion actions: a legal-takedown `obligation_action_records` row deliberately carries `category="illegal"` (off-list), and its real signal is the `TakenDown` action label (`fauna_core::obligation::obligation_action_label(7)` → "Removed under legal obligation"), so the moderation-queue row renders the `Other` badge + that action label rather than mis-rendering the off-list category.
- **Training correction submission — shared Rust → nest.** the shared correction flow — the sealed model write (algorithm owned by `mail-spam.md`), then `fauna.moderation.train` (read gate + report capture).
- **Queue retrieval — shared Rust → nest.** `fauna.moderation.actions`.
- **Confidence-percent display — shared Rust.** The queue row's `confidence` column shows a whole percent derived from the wire `confidence_per_mille: u16` (0–1000) by the half-up rounding `fauna_core::format::confidence_percent` (UniFFI `confidence_percent`; `value-formatting.md` § Confidence percent). No app re-derives `(m + 5) / 10` — the rounding is a single contract so no app silently drifts to truncation on a shown number.
- **Cross-app model consistency.** The shared text heuristic is the canonical classifier. The two model-based scorers consume their models differently:
  - The former **ONNX content-moderation model** (a nest-published downloadable binary, `GET /api/v1/moderation/{model,vocab}`) is **retired (2026-10-02)** — it labelled nothing on any app; a deployment-wide transparent text model is a tier-3 `text-model` labeler artifact the user subscribes to, evaluated at the subscriber's client ([`../architecture/content-scoring.md`](../architecture/content-scoring.md) § The placement matrix → *Deployment-wide content models at the client position*; the frame § Tier-3 → *Artifact kinds*).

The admin-opt-in **deployment baseline** ([`mail-spam.md`](mail-spam.md) § Cold start, Path 2) is **not** a separate download either: `fetch_spam_model` folds it into the returned per-user model as a *faded read-time prior* (one fetch returns own⊕baseline for cold-start actors, the baseline fading out as the actor's own model matures). So a client makes a single fetch and scores own + shared baseline with no second artifact.

## Errors & edge cases

- `error-message` page-level (inherited via global).
- Empty queue → `actions: []`; render an empty state, not an error.
- Training submission failure → surfaced via `error-message`.
- **Appeal is gated on there being a decision to appeal, and on whose it is (built 2026-09-21; author scope, reason bound and pending bound 2026-09-22).** `fauna.moderation.appeal` requires a non-empty `reason` no longer than `fauna_protocol::moderation::MAX_APPEAL_REASON_BYTES` (4 KiB of UTF-8 — a human paragraph or three; either failure is `invalid_params`, refused before any write) and a `content_id` this nest holds an enforcement record for — otherwise `not_found` ("no enforcement record for this content"); a 32-byte hex id is keyed lowercase, whatever spelling the caller sent. The predicate is `CacheDb::appeal_subject` (`db/moderation.rs`), the single owner of *is this appealable, and by whom*, and it is a **union of four sources** because the two takedown kinds and the two post orderings record the compelled fact in different places: an `obligation_action_records` row of any action (every post takedown, live or already-deleted — a mail quarantine/reject writes none, § Categories & enforcement item 2), `content_meta.legal_takedown_ref`, a `legal_takedown_deleted_posts` row (§ Legal takedown → *Posts*), or `segment_records.legal_takedown_ref` (§ Legal takedown → *Conversations* — the arm a gate reading obligation rows alone would get wrong, since a conv takedown writes no obligation row at all). **A post appeal is the author's alone** (§ Wire): each post-side source names the author — the obligation row's `author_hex`, the content row's `author` behind the flag, the floor row's `author_id` — and any other caller is refused `permission_denied`. **A conversation appeal is not actor-scoped:** the nest cannot attribute a sealed record to a sender, so any authenticated caller who holds the record's id appeals it — and only a recipient of the sealed record ever learns that id, unlike a public post's. A post-side record no source can attribute (a legacy obligation row with an empty `author_hex`) admits the same way, since the safe failure direction of an attribution gap is to admit: a false refusal denies the author the handle the triple guarantees. **One pending appeal per (appellant, `content_id`):** an appeal is pending until a decision on that content — a `moderation:legal-takedown` or `moderation:legal-takedown-restore` audit row — is recorded after it; a repeat while one is pending **collapses**, replying `status: "appeal_already_recorded"` and writing nothing (idempotent for a retried dispatch), so the permanent chain grows by one bounded row per appellant per decision, never per call (`CacheDb::record_appeal`, check and append under one lock hold). An **overturn does not revoke the handle** — the obligation row survives as additive history (§ Persistence), so a decision that was made stays appealable, and the overturn is itself a decision that re-opens it — whatever spelling the admin sent the id in, since a takedown or overturn records its `target` in the same lowercase hex the appeal is keyed by. The appeal is **recorded for review, never granted**: it lands on the permanent audit trail (`moderation:appeal`) for an admin to act on, keyed like every other audit row to the party who performed it — the appellant in `audit_log.actor_id`, the content id in `target`, and the reason, **verbatim**, in `detail`. **The chain keeps the text, not a digest (ruled 2026-09-22):** an appeal is the user's own submission to the nest's authority, the record an admin reviews, and a digest would leave that review with nothing to read; the text is bounded (above), and the appellant's own account export carries it back to them, because the `audit_log` export slice is exactly the rows whose `actor_id` is the exporting actor (`db/actor_tables.rs`, the `audit_log` entry). Like every other act of theirs on the chain it survives account deletion — deleting a row would break every later row's hash. Client-side the guards are rendered before dispatch by the shared `fauna_client_moderation::appeal_form_view` (empty and over-long reason alike), and the outcome lands on `appeal-status`, never on the page-level `error-message` (a refusal must not blank the queue the user is reading).

## Architectural rules

1. **Unified location across all 7 apps.** Web's embedded-in-Settings pattern is acceptable as a layout choice **iff** the same IDs render on the standalone `/moderation` page (standalone apps) and inside Settings (web) — mirrors the Status / Settings split.
2. **Classifier is shared Rust;** placement obeys `content-scoring.md`.
3. **Category vocabulary is the canonical 5, defined once in shared Rust;** no app hard-codes the set or its styling.
4. **Enforcement is user-side for social content + admin policy for mail ingest only** (§ Categories & enforcement) — no nest-wide social-content *policy* removal (the sole exception is § Legal takedown). The planned region-blocking surface (§ Categories & enforcement item 3; owner [`region-blocking.md`](region-blocking.md)) is app-side, region-scoped and visible — it does not touch this rule's nest-wide-removal invariant.

## Don't do these

- Don't run separate / divergent classifiers per-app (the shared heuristic + published model artifact is canonical).
- Don't compose flagged-item summary text or category labels per-app (shared `LocalizedText`).
- Don't add an admin "remove this social post for everyone" **policy** surface — social moderation is a personal filter (product invariant). The *sole* exception is the narrow, non-discretionary legal-compulsion carve-out (§ Legal takedown) — genuinely illegal content, transparent + appealable + audited, never a policy/opinion lever.
- Don't emit a `category` outside the canonical 5.
- Don't let a user's report *do* anything to anyone but the reporter — no automatic hide, label, removal or rank penalty for others, no reputation or aggregate feed, no notification to the author (§ User-initiated reporting → *What a report is not*).
