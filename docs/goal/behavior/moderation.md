## Goal

The Moderation page reviews items the spam / phishing classifier flagged on the user's **own** content and provides per-item training corrections (mark not-spam / mark spam) so the user refines their personal classifier over time. It is the user's view onto *why their content was labeled / actioned* and their lever to correct it — **not** an admin content-removal console (see § Categories & enforcement). **Ratified widening (designed 2026-08-27, ratified 2026-09-25 — § User-initiated reporting; built on all 7 apps, tui 2026-09-27 to windows, android and linux 2026-10-08):** the page also lists the reports the user *made* about others' content — their own moderation ledger, still never an admin console. **Unified shape (ratified, shipped on all 7 as of 2026-07-29):** a standalone Moderation page on the standalone apps (windows/linux/macos/ios/android/tui), embedded in Settings on web, **the same IDs either way** — mirroring the Status/Settings split (§ Architectural rules).

## Layout & flow

- **Moderation queue** (component `moderation-queue`) — list of the caller's flagged/actioned items on their **own** content; each row carries a per-item train-correction button (`train-correction-button`). Each row renders: a content-label badge (category) + confidence + an *optional* enforcement-action label + the train button. The queue is the **union of two sources** (ratified 2026-07-02 — § State & data shape): (1) the nest's server-issued `ObligationAction`s from `fauna.moderation.actions` (content ref, category, confidence, action taken, timestamp — the *enforcement* half: legal-takedown rows and their appeals; a mail disposition never enters this queue — § Categories & enforcement item 2); and (2) the client's own *post-decrypt local detections* (category, confidence, **no** action — the only social-content classifier signal on every conversation the nest holds no key for — a nest never classifies social content it cannot open; the one nest-position producer is a **community room's home nest** applying the transparent labelers the room policy names (ratified and built 2026-09-10 — `community-rooms.md` § The three classes → *What the home nest does with its read*; placement `../architecture/content-scoring.md` § The placement matrix, the *capability-holder* row), whose rows arrive as server rows on the same superset — `content-scoring.md` § Architectural rules). A local-only row shows a blank action column; server and local rows deduplicate by `content_id`.
- **Cross-reference:** spam **preferences** (thresholds, training toggles) live in `spam-moderation-controls` on the Settings page (`spam-preferences`, `spam-threshold`, `phishing-threshold`, `save-spam-prefs`; the former `auto-train` and `share-model` toggles are retired — `mail-spam.md` § Implicit signals are forbidden) — owned by [`settings.md`](../ui/settings.md), not here. The Moderation page consumes those preferences but does not host them.

## Categories & enforcement

**Canonical category set (ratified 2026-06-28, user):** `spam`, `trusted`, `nsfw`, `phishing`, `commercial`. This is the richest existing pattern (priority #4) — already rendered by web + android `ContentLabelBadge`, and `content_labels.category` is a free `String` column so the schema does not constrain it. The text heuristic (`classify_text`) fills **`spam`** and **`phishing`** today; `trusted` / `nsfw` / `commercial` are **reserved** for future classifiers (or user/admin labelling) and render when a producer emits them. Adding a *new* category is a product decision (extend this list + the shared enum); a classifier must never emit a category outside this set.

**Enforcement model (ratified 2026-06-28, user) — two tiers, do not conflate (plus one planned third surface, item 3, and a fourth, item 4, built on all 7 apps; item 1 names the two by-hand withholds since 2026-10-16):**

1. **Social content (feed / posts / conversations) → user-side, never nest-wide *policy* removal.** A label produces a **badge**; the user's *own* `spam_threshold` / `phishing_threshold` (per-user `spam_preferences`) optionally collapse/hide flagged content **in their own view**, and their per-user Bayesian model (`spam_models`) personalizes scoring. **No social post is removed nest-wide by hand without a record** (amended 2026-10-16 on the owner's ratification of the admin block; until then the sentence read "as a matter of policy or opinion", with legal compulsion the one exception). This is the direct consequence of the *user-controls-their-data* product invariant: moderation of social content is a personal filter, not admin censorship — nothing is removed silently, nothing is removed automatically, and no classifier, threshold, report count or published rule ever removes an item nest-wide. **A published rule applied on the reader's own device is not a removal** (ratified 2026-10-16 with the nest's rules — [`nest-rules.md`](nest-rules.md)): a nest's rules, a region's and a guardian's are the region-blocking shape — the item stays on the nest and with its author, and is withheld only in the view of a reader whose device applies that rule, visibly, with the rule and its reason named. Exactly **two** nest-wide withholds exist, both a human's act on one named item, both transparent (a visible notice naming the ground, the author's appeal, a permanent audit row), both reversible and never a deletion: **legal compulsion** — § Legal takedown below (the *decision* to carve it out is the frame's — `../architecture/content-moderation-and-ranking.md` § Resolved design decisions Q5, ratified 2026-07-05) — and **the admin's block of one reported item** — § Admin block of a reported item below, reachable only from a user's report. There is still no admin "hide this post for all users" surface outside those two, and the author keeps their own view of a blocked item.
2. **Mail ingest → admin policy at the perimeter.** Inbound mail is scored at the MTA perimeter and the admin's `mail_spam_policy` thresholds (junk / reject — owned by [`mail-policy-config.md`](mail-policy-config.md)) decide filing. This is standard MTA behaviour, scoped to mail, and is the only *discretionary* admin-side enforcement (the legal-compulsion carve-out in item 1 is non-discretionary and legally bounded, not admin policy). **Mail disposition is the Junk folder plus spam training — RATIFIED 2026-09-26 (user ruling, closing the fork the 2026-07-19 sweep opened): a mail quarantine or reject writes NO moderation-queue row and has NO appeal.** The disposition (accept / accept-to-Junk on the score / filed to Junk by a rule) is recorded as the `spam_disposition` column on the ingested-mail row (`persist_decoded_inbound_mail`, flowing into `db/bridge_routing.rs::insert_inbound_mail`) plus a `message_scan_results` row for the malware-scan verdict (`insert_scan_result`) — never an `obligation_action_records` row, which is the legal-takedown ledger (item 1) and nothing else. The user's recourse is the one every mail system they already know offers — the Junk folder, and the per-message train-correction (`fauna.moderation.train`) that teaches their own model; the admin's is the `mail_spam_policy` thresholds. `fauna.moderation.appeal` is gated on an enforcement record (§ Errors & edge cases), so for mail it correctly has nothing to appeal. Until this ruling the item's text promised a queue row the user could appeal, and the sweep recorded the difference as a gap; the user ruled the code's shape the target ("that matches how every mail system works today"), so the promise is withdrawn — not the code owed.

3. **Region blocking (planned — direction user-ratified 2026-07-29; owner: [`region-blocking.md`](region-blocking.md), Status: partially-specified).** A region's legal authority publishes a completely transparent blocking algorithm; each app applies its region's algorithm on the device, marking a blocked item visibly with the algorithm's reason (never a silent removal), and the nest applies it only where it is itself the publisher (web content it publishes to the open internet). This is the third compulsory surface beside item 2's mail-perimeter policy and item 1's legal-takedown exception — compulsory like them, but region-scoped, app-side, and algorithmic: it removes nothing nest-wide, hides nothing outside the region, and adds no admin lever, so item 1's invariant stands untouched. Mechanism, invariants, the open TBDs and the build state are `region-blocking.md`'s (§ Implementation status today).

4. **User-initiated reporting (ratified 2026-09-25 — owner: § User-initiated reporting below; built on all 7 apps 2026-10-08).** The fourth surface: a user reports someone else's content or account, and the report is **evidence to a human with authority — never a removal by itself.** It changes what the *reporter* sees (their own personal filter — item 1's plane) and lands on an admin's queue, where the levers are § Legal takedown, [`admin.md`](admin.md)'s suspension, and — since 2026-10-16 — § Admin block of a reported item, the one lever that exists only on a report. The report removes nothing, labels nothing and compels nothing; the block is the admin's recorded act on it, and item 1's amended invariant is what its structure keeps.

(`trusted` is a positive signal — e.g. a sender allowlist / reputation hint — not an action; it never hides content. `nsfw` is a badge/collapse hint subject to the same user-side filtering, never nest-wide removal.)

Guardian (parent-linked) obligations for **supervised accounts** extend this framework — for such an account, "user-side" includes the guardian's policy. Owner: [`family-safety.md`](family-safety.md). **Implementation honesty (updated 2026-08-11):** item 1's threshold-driven collapse/hide is **built on all 7 apps** (linux + web the reference legs, android third, apple — macOS + iOS — fourth on 2026-07-22, windows fifth on 2026-07-23, tui's reader leg the last on 2026-08-02 — `family-client-enforcement.md` § Content policy owns the per-app state) — the one shared render-verdict engine (`fauna_core::obligation::render_verdict_composed`, which folds the per-post/message `ContentLabelEntry`-list verdict fold together with the two rule assemblers as of 2026-07-22) serves every user's own `spam_preferences` thresholds (→ `Collapse`) with the guardian floor composing in strictest-wins, owned by `family-client-enforcement.md` § Content policy (read its `## Implementation status today` for the per-app state). It is one engine, not two — the same verdict (`content_policy::verdict_for` on linux, the shared `ContentPolicyStore.verdictFor` → `content_render_verdict` on android and apple, `contentVerdict` on web, `ContentPolicyCache.VerdictFor` on windows) drives both the feed and conversations on every app that has this surface.

## User-initiated reporting (designed 2026-08-27; ratified 2026-09-25 — routing + ID package user-approved; nest local loop, federation triad and block-hides built, built on tui 2026-09-27, web 2026-10-07, macos + ios 2026-10-07, windows 2026-10-08, android 2026-10-08 (compile + Robolectric; the emulator run is host-owed) and linux 2026-10-08; sequencing ruled 2026-10-07 — iOS paints it before the first external TestFlight build)

**Why this exists.** Apple's App Review Guideline 1.2 requires every app with user-generated content to ship four things — a way to filter objectionable material, **a mechanism to report offensive content with timely responses to concerns**, the ability to block abusive users, and published contact information. Fauna meets three (filtering: § Categories & enforcement item 1's engine plus muted keywords; block: `fauna.knocks.block`, [`../ui/contacts.md`](../ui/contacts.md) § Where logic lives → *Unblock*; contact information: a store-listing task) and **has no report mechanism at all**. Google Play's user-generated-content policy asks the same of an in-app report path. The gap gates external TestFlight and the first public submission, not internal testing. The stores' other demand on user-generated content — a published terms-or-content-guidelines document, accepted before posting — is owned by [`terms-and-content-guidelines.md`](terms-and-content-guidelines.md) (proposed 2026-10-14): its guidelines are this section's report-reason vocabulary spelled out. That doc also owns the three stores' texts as read and dated and their per-store mapping onto the admin's levers (§ The stores' requirements against the admin's levers, 2026-10-16): the report path and the account levers meet every store as built, and the stores' removal-or-disablement clause — Apple's 1.2 states it too, as the developer's responsibility to remove violating content — is met by § Admin block of a reported item — ratified, built on the nest and painted on tui, the lead app, 2026-10-17, the other six apps following in the trickle-down — so the first store submissions no longer wait on a build row of this doc's; what each console's entry still waits on is [`terms-and-content-guidelines.md`](terms-and-content-guidelines.md) § Certification notes wording's to say.

**The shape must hold five things this doc and its neighbours already ratify.** (1) **No nest-wide policy removal** (§ Categories & enforcement item 1) — a report may not become a lever. (2) **The user controls their data** ([`../principles.md`](../principles.md) § The user always controls their data) — the nest reads no sealed content to adjudicate, and nobody's content is disclosed by a mechanism, only by a person who could already read it. (3) **One configuration surface, no operator** — the humans are users and admins in the apps; a report goes to an *admin's app*, never to a mailbox, a file, or a dashboard outside the product. (4) **Federation** — the reported author frequently lives on a nest this admin has no authority over. (5) **Reporter identity never crosses a nest boundary** — the report-sharing invariant ([`../architecture/content-moderation-and-ranking.md`](../architecture/content-moderation-and-ranking.md) § Distributed report sharing), applied verbatim to attributed notices.

### What exists, and why none of it is this

Six lookalikes, each something else — listed so no session mistakes one for the mechanism:

- `fauna.moderation.appeal` — the **author's** appeal against a takedown of their *own* content (§ Legal takedown).
- The moderation queue — the caller's flagged items on their **own** content (§ Goal — "*not* an admin content-removal console").
- `fauna.moderation.report_share.*` / `report-share-published-list` — the k-anonymized **spam-aggregate** exchange ([`report-sharing.md`](report-sharing.md)): machine signal over content hashes; no human reads a report.
- `fauna.moderation.scan_report` — gone (a retired compliance beacon; off the wire 2026-09-24 — § State & data shape).
- `dm-message-mark-as-spam-button` — the one existing gesture on *someone else's* content: it trains the **reporter's own sealed spam model** ([`mail-spam.md`](mail-spam.md)) and reaches no human. It is, however, the richest existing UI pattern for a per-item verb on received content — the `⋯` overflow → an indexed verb gated `!is_own` — and the report verb sits **beside** it, never replacing it.
- the sender-**reputation** plane — gone: the peer reputation exchange and its `sender_reputation` store were removed 2026-10-02 ([`../architecture/federation.md`](../architecture/federation.md) § Federation residue surface → *The reputation leg is ruled removed*). **A human report never feeds a per-sender reputation signal, nor anything that succeeds that plane**: a per-reporter attributed reputation signal would turn reporting into a reputation weapon — a rule that outlives the plane.

### Two candidate shapes, against the invariants

**Decided 2026-09-25 (user): B ⊇ A, the superset, as recommended.** Both shapes stay stated here so the decision reads against the invariants it was made against.

- **Shape A — reporter-local.** A report is a personal-filter act: the content is hidden *for the reporter* (optionally with a block), and nothing leaves the reporter's device. Every invariant holds trivially. **It does not meet the requirement**: no human with authority ever learns of the report, so there is no "timely response", and a reviewer pressing *Report* sees only a hide. It is also already most of the way built — it is mark-as-spam plus block.
- **Shape B — evidence to a human with authority.** A report lands on an admin's queue; the admin acts, if at all, only with the levers they already hold — § Legal takedown (posts + conversations; transparent, appealable, audited) and [`admin.md`](admin.md)'s suspension (an account power, not a content one). Every invariant holds: a report changes nothing for anyone but the reporter, sealed content stays sealed unless the reporter chooses to attach what they could already read, and the reporter's identity stays on their own nest.
- **Ratified: B ⊇ A, the superset.** Every report has A's immediate reporter-side effect *and* B's routing. The reporter is protected the moment they report, regardless of whether or when an admin acts; the requirement's "response" half is met by B.

### Routing — whose queue, and how a report crosses a nest boundary

**A report always lands on the reporter's own nest admin's queue, and is forwarded — reporter-anonymously — to the reported author's home nest when that author is foreign.** Both admins hold a real lever and nothing more: the *home* admin holds suspension over the account and the source-side takedown (whose withholding federation discloses); the *reporter's* admin holds the takedown over the local projection of a foreign post (`content_meta` rows exist for federated posts this nest projects, so § Legal takedown's flag applies to the local copy — and the author's queue row that takedown or block writes reaches them on their home nest through § Legal takedown → *The author homed on another nest*) and is the one who can see false-report abuse by their own user. Neither is a "report to a global authority" — there is none. The fediverse converged on this same shape independently (report to your own admins, forward to the remote instance), which is also what makes it legible to a store reviewer.

- **Home-nest resolution** is the provenance every existing path already uses: a post's author actor → the local `users` row, or the origin nest the post arrived from; a conversation message's sender → the channel roster's home `nest_id` ([`../architecture/federation.md`](../architecture/federation.md) § Federation residue surface, the channel roster-read row); a profile → the actor's home nest.
- **Forwarding is automatic, not a per-report checkbox (ratified 2026-09-25)** — reporter identity never crosses, so forwarding has no privacy cost, and a report against an author is by nature addressed to the one admin who holds a lever over that account. The fediverse's opt-in variant (a per-report forward checkbox) was offered and declined.
- **The receiving nest accepts only reports about content or accounts it hosts** — a nest is never a relay for reports about third nests — and applies the per-origin throttle every federation kind carries. **Because both halves of a delivery's dedupe key `(origin, report_ref)` are the caller's to choose, a new forwarded report also spends two per-target budgets (ruled and built 2026-10-17):** at most 10 per hour about any one hosted account and 100 per hour over the whole nest — the admin's queue and doorbell — summed over every origin; past either it is refused `rate_limited`, which the sender's queue retries, and a re-delivery of a report already stored stays free ([`../architecture/federation.md`](../architecture/federation.md) § Security → *Rate limiting is the primary DoS defense* owns the mechanism).
- **Withdrawal propagates** (the forwarded copy carries the reporter's note and excerpt — their data, revocable from their app), and **the outcome propagates back** (§ What the reporter is told).
- **Delivery is durable and pinned (built 2026-09-26).** Each leg rides a per-nest send queue, retried with backoff for up to seven days, so a home nest that is down when the report is filed still receives it; the submit tries its delivery once inline, briefly, so the acknowledgement names the home nest when it was reached, and names only the reporter's own nest otherwise — a destination is claimed only once it accepted. A refusal (a subject the receiver does not host, a peer that serves no such kind) is final. The withdrawal and the outcome are pinned to the nest identity the delivery was verified as, so no other nest can withdraw a report or speak its outcome; a withdrawal racing an in-flight delivery is queued the moment that delivery lands. The receiver records the origin's declared address against its verified identity (`federation.md`'s relayed-origin rule) — that is how the outcome finds a nest it may never have dialled.

### What a report carries

`{ subject, reason, note?, excerpt?, block_author }`. The **subject** is one of `post { cid }`, `message { channel, record_cid }` (the same key § Legal takedown's conversation half uses, so an admin can withhold a sealed message without ever reading it), or `actor { actor_id }` (a profile/account report). The **reason** is a shared-Rust enum — `spam · harassment · hate · violence · sexual · illegal · impersonation · other` — a *report-reason* vocabulary deliberately distinct from the canonical-5 label categories (those are classifier emissions; a report emits no label), rendered as `LocalizedText`, never per-app strings. The **note** is bounded free text. The **excerpt** is the reporter-attached plaintext of a **sealed** subject (a conversation message, a gated post): bounded, and **opt-in per report through a checkbox whose label names the consequence and the subject** ("Include the text of this message — the admins will be able to read it", *post* in place of *message* on a gated post) — the nest holds no readable bytes for sealed kinds, so without it an admin can act on the *account* and withhold by id, but cannot judge the text. Attaching it is the reporter's own act on content they already hold — the same act as forwarding a message — so the mechanism discloses nothing on its own (invariant 2). Public and feed posts need no excerpt: the admin opens them by id. **`block_author`** chains the existing `fauna.knocks.block` — one gesture, two existing mechanisms; the block's semantics stay `contacts.md`'s, untouched.

### Where it lands, who acts, and with what

- **Persistence (nest-side, additive):** `abuse_reports` — `id, created_at, reporter_actor (local reporters; NULL on a forwarded copy), origin_nest_id (forwarded copies; NULL when local), subject_kind, subject_id, subject_actor, reason, note, excerpt, forwarded_to, forwarded_nest_id, status (open | resolved | withdrawn), outcome (acted | dismissed), resolved_at, resolved_by` — on a forwarded copy `origin_nest_id` is the channel-verified origin, and `forwarded_to`/`forwarded_nest_id` are set only once the home nest accepted the delivery — beside `abuse_report_outbox`, the triad's send queue (derived send state, never user data). Resolved rows stay as the admin's audit record, like every obligation row; a reporter may withdraw an *open* report (their data), and withdrawal deletes the note and excerpt everywhere they went. **Account deletion is the reporter's withdrawal of every open report they filed, and nothing more (ruled 2026-09-26):** the deletion leg withdraws each open row exactly as the living door does — status `withdrawn`, note and excerpt deleted, a queued delivery dropped, a delivery that reached the home nest followed by a `withdraw` queued in the same step as the withdrawal, so a crash loses neither and the forwarder sends it after the account is gone (the queue follows the report by id, not the actor; a delivery in flight is covered by its landing) — and leaves every resolved row whole, note, excerpt and the retired reporter id included: resolving turned the reporter's submission into the record of what the admin decided and on what evidence, which survives its author exactly as an appeal's reason does (§ Errors & edge cases), and a living reporter cannot strip a resolved row either, so deletion opens no door withdrawal does not. Withdrawn rows keep the skeleton the living withdrawal leaves (subject, reason, dates, status, the reporter id), naming the retired id as every other act of a deleted account does (`audit_log`). A purge was declined because it would delete the audit record; stripping resolved rows' words was declined because it would leave a decision with no evidence to audit and a since-deleted false reporter with no trail for their admin (§ Anti-abuse posture bound 5). An outcome arriving for a withdrawn row changes nothing (it is taken only while open), so no notification is ever written for a departed reporter. The leg is `abuse_reports`' `Policy::Partial` entry in the nest's actor-table registry (`db/actor_tables.rs`), run by the purge walk every account deletion takes; forwarded copies carry no reporter and are untouched by any local deletion.
- **The admin's levers on a queue row (amended 2026-10-16):** *open takedown* pre-fills § Legal takedown's console with the subject's id + kind — the console's own guards (a required legal reference, the named confirm) still apply, so a report can never become a citation-less takedown; *suspend* is the `admin-users` page's own control, reached by navigation; *resolve as acted* and *dismiss* are **records, not actions** — resolving does nothing to the content or the author; and **Block** — the fourth verb, the one lever that exists only here (§ Admin block of a reported item, owner-ratified 2026-10-16 after being declined 2026-10-15): it withholds the reported item from everyone this nest serves but the author through the takedown's own withhold with a second ground, with the same transparency triple, and resolves the report as *acted* in the same transaction. The report plane still adds no lever of its own: a report is evidence, the block is a human's recorded act on it, and item 1's amended invariant — no removal by hand without a record — is what the verb's structure keeps. **Owner direction 2026-10-16 (in the terms page's review):** after keeping the three levers on 2026-10-15, the owner directed that a manual content block for admins be designed as the lead of a design row carrying the decisions a block needs, and ratified that design the same day.
- **The doorbell rides the existing notification machinery** ([`notifications.md`](notifications.md)): on a new open report the nest writes an ordinary `notifications` row for each admin (`notif_type: "abuse_report.received"`, `source: "fauna"`), deduped per `(subject_id, day)` — the UTC day, since the nest holds no admin's timezone — — the Guardian Notify flood bound ([`family-client-enforcement.md`](family-client-enforcement.md) § Guardian Notify) — so a pile-on is one doorbell, and the queue's open count is the truth.

### What the reporter is told

- **At submit:** an acknowledgement naming where the report went — the reporter's own nest, and the author's home nest when forwarded — composed in shared Rust, never per-app.
- **At resolution:** a notification (`notif_type: "abuse_report.resolved"`) carrying the **outcome only** — *acted* or *dismissed* — never the admin's reasoning, and nothing about the author beyond what the reporter already knew. For a forwarded report the home nest returns the outcome over the federation channel, and the reporter's nest — the only party holding the reporter's identity — writes the row.
- **Always:** a *Your reports* list on the Moderation page (the one-sentence widening of § Goal): subject, reason, destination(s), status, and withdraw while open.
- **The author is told nothing by a report.** They learn of a consequence only through the lever's own transparency — the takedown tombstone + appeal, the block notice + appeal (§ Admin block of a reported item), or suspension — never that they were reported, or by whom: a report-notified author is a retaliation vector.

### Anti-abuse posture

Reporting is itself an abuse surface; six bounds, all structural:

1. **Informs, never compels.** Nothing happens to anyone but the reporter automatically — no hide, no label, no rank penalty, no reputation. A flood of false reports costs an admin attention and nothing else.
2. **A hard-coded per-reporter rate** (`ABUSE_REPORTS_PER_HOUR`, a shared-Rust constant beside `REPORT_MIN_REPORTERS` — bucket (1) of the one-configuration-surface invariant: no knob on any surface) and **one open report per (reporter, subject)**.
3. **Federation:** per-origin-nest throttle and the per-target budgets (§ Routing), accepted only for hosted subjects, reporter-anonymous — a hostile nest, or a swarm of fresh ones, can send noise about the receiver's accounts only within those budgets, and about anyone else's to nobody.
4. **No feed into any other plane** — not a per-sender reputation signal, not `content_reports` (the k-anonymized `report:*` aggregates, which are opt-in flags over content hashes, not attributed notices; conflating them would let one report plus forwarding manufacture a signal), not `content_labels`.
5. **False-report abuse is the reporter's own admin's case** — the reporter's identity is visible only there, and that admin's lever (suspension) is the same one every other abuse gets.
6. **A supervised account reports like anyone else** — no guardian gate on the gesture ([`family-safety.md`](family-safety.md) owns anything further).

### Corollary — block also hides (ruled with the design pass; carried by the 2026-09-25 ratification)

Block today gates **reach** — `reach_floor` at the inbox-write choke point (`bins/fauna-nest/src/routes.rs`) — and a blocked actor's posts still appear in the blocker's feed (`bins/fauna-nest/src/db/feeds.rs` has no blocked predicate). A reviewer who blocks an account and keeps seeing its posts reads that as the third 1.2 requirement unmet. **Ruling:** blocking also excludes the blocked actor from the viewer's own feed reads — a personal filter, item 1's plane exactly, hiding nothing for anyone else. **Mechanism:** applied **where the viewer becomes known** — the per-viewer stage of the viewer's own feed read (the position `augment_viewer_state` already occupies), as an `AuthorNotInSet` over the viewer's `blocked` contact edges — never inside the viewer-independent query fns a peer nest also calls, and with no second copy in the client's `FeedManager`. *Built 2026-09-26:* the rule's 32 bytes name the **viewer**, whose blocked edges form the set, and it rides the feed read's **mandatory** group (the one the spam guard rides), so a feed's `Any` combination cannot OR it away; the query translator honours that reading in the mandatory group only, and a feed's own rules — which a user authors, and which could name someone else to read their block list — cannot carry it. It binds the local feed, the Trending feed and every feed of the viewer's own, chronological and scored; the federated feed read adds no such rule. Unblock (which clears the edge — `contacts.md`) restores the posts on the next read. **Reported content is likewise hidden for the reporter:** the subject's id joins the sealed per-user `hidden_content` list of the account-state plane kind `fauna.state.moderation` (the E1 cluster — [`../architecture/config-dissolution.md`](../architecture/config-dissolution.md) § The `__config` dissolution schedule → *The kinds*; the `muted_keywords` sibling — same record, same delta-pair seam) that the render-verdict engine ([`family-client-enforcement.md`](family-client-enforcement.md) § Content policy) reads as a `Block` verdict with a "you reported this" placeholder, so a reporter who does not block still stops seeing what they reported. *Built 2026-09-26 (shared Rust):* an entry is the report subject's id — a post or message id, or an account's actor id, which hides everything that account authored — lowercased, deduped, capped at the 2,000 newest; `obligation::render_verdict_for_item` is the one entry point that reads it, answering `Block` attributed `Reported` unless a region's own `Block` holds the attribution (an authority's reason is the user's to read verbatim), and the list is written only through the shared add/remove deltas on both preference rails.

## State & data shape

Reuse the **existing** `fauna.moderation.*` wire (`libs/fauna-protocol/src/moderation.rs`) — do **not** invent a `ModerationSnapshot`/`FlaggedItem`/`TrainingEvent` (the prior draft's speculative shape is dropped in favour of the built types, priority #4):

- **Queue** = `fauna.moderation.actions` → `ModerationActionsReply { actions: Vec<ObligationAction> }`, scoped to the connection actor's own content. `ObligationAction { id, content_type, content_id, category, confidence_per_mille: u16, action: u8, timestamp }` — plus the additive `issuer_nest: Option<String>` on a row another nest delivered (§ Legal takedown → *The author homed on another nest*, ruled 2026-10-18, built 2026-10-19). Each row is a queue item; `action` is the obligation-action discriminant (labeled / quarantined / rejected / taken-down; the nest writes only taken-down (=7) and blocked (=10) rows — for a post today, and for a conversation record whose author it attested once § Legal takedown → *Conversations*' ruling of 2026-10-18 is built — below). This server queue never carries social-content classifier actions — every nest classifies nothing at ingest (`process_on_ingest` retired with the storage-mode axis, Phase 4 2026-07-12; `content-scoring.md` § Architectural rules: "the nest never runs a content scorer over content it holds no key for" — the scorers a nest does run, a community room's labelers under its members' grant and a region's scorers over the public pages it publishes, produce per-message labels and page placeholders, not queue actions); it carries **legal-takedown rows only** — a mail quarantine/reject writes no `obligation_action_records` row by ratified design (§ Categories & enforcement item 2, user ruling 2026-09-26), so the queue's server half is the legal-takedown ledger.
  - **The moderation-queue the client renders is the UNION of these server `ObligationAction`s and the client's own post-decrypt local detections** (ratified 2026-07-02 — the superset / richest pattern, priority #4). Every app classifies plaintext it can read (its user's own decrypted content — § Where logic lives) and surfaces those local flags — spam-only `{ category, confidence }`, **no** `action` — alongside the server rows, so the queue is non-empty and train-correctable on every nest, since the server never carries social-content actions. Local detections carry no `action` (blank action column); dedupe server-vs-local by `content_id`. The local detections shown in the queue are held **client-side** (the session-owned shared `LocalDetectionStore`), not round-tripped through the nest — nothing about a local detection reaches the nest (the former coarse `scan_report` compliance beacon is gone from the wire; see its bullet below).
- **Aggregate counts** = `fauna.moderation.stats` → `Vec<LabelStat { category, count, avg_confidence_per_mille }>`.
- **Training correction** = `fauna.moderation.train { content_id, verdict: "spam" | "ham" }` against a stored post — the **nest half** of a correction: the caller-may-read gate (a taken-down, quarantined or absent post answers `not_found`) and the report capture (`report-sharing.md` § Report capture). **It trains nothing** (since 2026-10-02): the per-user model is sealed under the user's key, so the model half is the client's sealed write (`fauna.bridges.put_spam_model`, `mail-spam.md` § Encrypted-mode interaction). One shared flow runs both halves — sealed write when the user's key is present (mail enabled), then this kind always — so every app reports on every correction. A user without mail enabled has no per-user model, and the correction reports only.
- **Appeal** = `fauna.moderation.appeal { content_id, reason }` (an appeal of a legal-takedown decision → audit trail; there is no mail-quarantine appeal — § Categories & enforcement item 2).
- **Client-side scan report** = `fauna.moderation.scan_report { labels: Vec<ScanLabel> }` — **GONE (producer retired 2026-07-19; the kind, its wire types and the nest handler left the wire 2026-09-24 with the compat-remnant sweep — `version-compatibility.md` § Dim 2, the fourth ratified exception).** The kind was a coarse compliance beacon ("a client is scanning"): the handler only audit-logs `labels_count=N` — no label storage, no read path anywhere (the reason `family-client-enforcement.md` § Guardian Notify got its own table). No client produces it **by design**: after the no-modes ratification, local detections are held client-side and never round-tripped, and an *unconsented* client→nest report of per-category counts derived from the user's own decrypted content contradicts the user-controls-their-data invariant — the consented coarse-counts shape lives in Guardian Notify (guardianship-gated, `content_notify`-gated, with a real read path). Pre-migration clients produced the HTTP twin, which kept the WS-RPC handler registered-and-answering (the `fauna.event.rsvp` dead-registration precedent) until the 2026-09-23 user-population ruling retired every such remnant; the client-side producer scaffolding (web `LabelBuffer`/`classifyAndReport`, android `ModerationManager` batch-flush, the shared `ModerationClient::scan_report` / FFI / wasm seams) was **deleted** rather than left dark. Reviving client→nest scan telemetry requires a fresh design pass with an explicit user-facing consent surface (all-7-app UI + nest state, per the configuration invariant) — do not rewire the old shape.
- **Model sync** = `fauna.moderation.model_sync` — **GONE (2026-10-02).** It uploaded a plaintext model for the nest to adopt-if-larger; no app called it, and a nest that stores only sealed models has nothing to compare (`mail-spam.md` § Implementation status item 4b).

Confidence rides as **per-mille `u16`** (0–1000) everywhere — the dag-cbor wire forbids floats — mirroring the `fauna_protocol::spam` threshold encoding.

## Per-row badge data path (feed & conversations) — ratified 2026-07-16

The § Element IDs badge claim ("wherever flagged content renders") has a data-path corollary this section owns: **how a category label reaches an individual feed post or conversation message row.** Today label data reaches only the moderation queue (`ObligationAction` rows ∪ local detections), so the badge rendered only there; the bullets below built the feed and conversation label fields, done on all 7 apps (§ Implementation status today, the per-row badge entry).

**One vocabulary, two projections — no third plane.** The per-row unit is the exact pair the queue already speaks: `{ category, confidence_per_mille: u16 }` — a shared `ContentLabelEntry` type living beside `content_label_style` in `fauna_core::content_category`. It **projects the existing label plane (`content_labels`)**; it is not a new store, not a new wire vocabulary, and not a scoring-bus row — `content_scores` remains the composition/ordering substrate, while the label plane is the category-verdict entry on the metadata bus (`../architecture/content-scoring.md` § The scoring-metadata bus).

- **Feed (transparent kinds — nest-projected).** The wire `FeedPostItem` gains an additive `labels: Vec<ContentLabelEntry>` (`#[serde(default)]`; empty from an older nest), projected by `query_feed` / `query_feed_scored` from `content_labels` — a table **already part of the retained feed-index projection** (`../architecture/feed-read-model.md` § The read model), consumed filter-only today (`FilterRule::{HasLabel,LabelBelow,LabelAbove}`); this adds the per-row read-out. One entry per category (the highest-confidence row), joined by the same hex-id bridge the filter predicates use. `PostSummary` mirrors the field. The nest **serves** stored label metadata; it never computes a label (`../architecture/content-scoring.md` § Architectural rules — placement unchanged).
- **Conversations (sealed kind — client-populated).** `MessageSnapshot` gains the same `labels: Vec<ContentLabelEntry>`, populated by the manager from the same post-decrypt classify pass that feeds the `LocalDetectionStore` (`ConversationsManager::ingest_inbound_to_thread`). For an **end-to-end** room it never round-trips the nest — the nest holds neither plaintext nor label rows for MLS-sealed content. A **community** room's home nest is the one exception, being a reader by its members' grant: it serves the verdicts of the labelers the room names beside each message, to live floor members (`ChannelFetchEntry.labels`), and the manager merges them into `labels` by the queue's superset rule — a server verdict wins its category, a local detection stays on the device (`fauna_core::content_category::merge_server_labels`; the mechanism is [`community-rooms.md`](community-rooms.md)'s, § The three classes → *What the home nest does with its read*). Distinct from `badges.content_warning` (a sender-declared flag, kept) and from the muted-keyword collapse (a keyword penalty scorer, not a label — § Muted keywords).
- **Badge semantics — inform, never compel.** The badge renders whenever any label entry is present: the highest-confidence entry styles via `content_label_style` (canonical-5 → styled; off-list → the grey `Other`). Rendering is transparency, not enforcement: threshold-driven collapse/hide remains the render-verdict engine designed in `family-client-enforcement.md` § Content policy — this data path must not grow its own collapse logic (build that engine once, there, reading these same entries).
- **The attach door enforces that producer set — an ownership question, not a class question (ratified 2026-09-11).** For a feed post, `fauna.labels.attach` admits exactly two callers: the post's **own author** (an unsigned self-label — the honest scope of a label carrying no attestation), and a holder of a **`content.label-write` grant from that author** (the third-party case: per-scope, time-bounded, revocable, audited from the author's own app — `../architecture/encryption-at-rest.md` § Capability tiering). Ownership is resolved from the store, never from anything the caller declares. Any other member's label on someone else's post is refused, and a `content_id` naming no post is refused with it. **Why it is a hard gate and not a preference:** the two nest-side verdicts in the bullet below bind *every* reader, so a class-only gate made any member a nest-wide remover of any member's post — precisely what § Categories & enforcement item 1 forbids the nest to do on any opinion, a member's included.
- **A verdict that binds every reader must name its writer (ratified 2026-09-11).** A `content_labels` row is a category verdict *with provenance* (`../architecture/content-scoring.md` § The scoring-metadata bus), so each writer is recorded on the row it writes — the writing position in `scanner_id`, the producer in `classifier_id`, which being part of the upsert key also stops two producers sharing one last-writer-wins row. The two nest-side reads whose verdict a reader never chose — **the mandatory feed spam group** (the `LabelBelow{spam,500}` every feed read ANDs unless that feed sets its own spam rule) and **the per-post projection** the badge and the nest-as-publisher fold share ([`region-blocking.md`](region-blocking.md) § The nest-as-publisher leg) — therefore admit **only an attributed row**: an unattributed one informs nobody and removes nothing. **The writer columns follow their writer across an identity succession** — a label is revised by attaching again and the upsert keys on the producer, so a row left on a retired identity would outvote its own author's revision for good; the row itself is its content's and leaves with it ([`opaque-carrier-walks.md`](opaque-carrier-walks.md) § The declared re-point axis owns the ruling). A feed's **own** `LabelBelow`/`LabelAbove`/`HasLabel` rules sit deliberately outside this: they are the user's own filter over their own view (item 1's user-side plane), theirs to aim at whatever the plane holds.
- **Producers unchanged.** Labels keep coming only from positions sanctioned by `../architecture/content-scoring.md`: the perimeter (mail), the user's client post-decrypt, or a capability-granted holder — attached over `fauna.labels.attach`. This path adds *serving*, not classification; in particular it does **not** add client-side classification of the feed-list preview (prohibited — § Implementation status today, feed local-detection note).

## Where logic lives

- **Classifier — shared Rust.** The built-in **text heuristic** is `fauna_core::text_heuristic::classify_text`, consumed identically by web (WASM `fauna_wasm::classifyText`) and native apps (inside the shared `ConversationsManager` post-decrypt pass, over `fauna_client_core::scan` — no per-app façade call) at post-decrypt. Apps keep **no** hand-rolled copy. **Placement is governed by [`content-scoring.md`](../architecture/content-scoring.md), not here:** a content classifier runs only where plaintext is available — the mail bridge perimeter pre-seal, or the user's client post-decrypt; **never** the nest, on any box. This doc does not re-litigate the trust boundary.
- **Category-label presentation — shared Rust (the drift #157 lift).** The badge label/colour/icon is a shared enum keyed on the wire `category` string (one of the canonical 5) returning a `LocalizedText`, mirroring the `fauna_protocol::spam::spam_threshold_band` precedent (`settings.md` § Spam threshold slider labels): web via a wasm export, Apple/Windows/Android via UniFFI, linux native. **No app hard-codes the category strings or their styling** — this removes web's hard-coded `Spam`/`Trusted`/`NSFW`/`Phishing`/`Commercial` map in `ContentLabelBadge.svelte` (and android's `ContentLabelBadge.kt`). **Any off-list `category` degrades to a neutral grey `Other` badge (`fauna_core::content_category::content_label_style` → `ContentCategory::Other`, rendering the capitalized raw string) — never a panic and never a dropped row.** The canonical-5 rule constrains **classifiers** (§ Don't do these — "don't emit a `category` outside the canonical 5"), **not** admin legal-compulsion actions: a legal-takedown `obligation_action_records` row deliberately carries `category="illegal"` (off-list), and its real signal is the `TakenDown` action label (`fauna_core::obligation::obligation_action_label(7)` → "Removed under legal obligation"), so the moderation-queue row renders the `Other` badge + that action label rather than mis-rendering the off-list category.
- **Training correction submission — shared Rust → nest.** the shared correction flow — the sealed model write (algorithm owned by `mail-spam.md`), then `fauna.moderation.train` (read gate + report capture).
- **Queue retrieval — shared Rust → nest.** `fauna.moderation.actions`.
- **Confidence-percent display — shared Rust.** The queue row's `confidence` column shows a whole percent derived from the wire `confidence_per_mille: u16` (0–1000) by the half-up rounding `fauna_core::format::confidence_percent` (UniFFI `confidence_percent`; `value-formatting.md` § Confidence percent). No app re-derives `(m + 5) / 10` — the rounding is a single contract so no app silently drifts to truncation on a shown number.
- **Cross-app model consistency.** The shared text heuristic is the canonical classifier. The two model-based scorers consume their models differently:
  - The former **ONNX content-moderation model** (a nest-published downloadable binary, `GET /api/v1/moderation/{model,vocab}`) is **retired (2026-10-02)** — it labelled nothing on any app; a deployment-wide transparent text model is a tier-3 `text-model` labeler artifact the user subscribes to, evaluated at the subscriber's client ([`../architecture/content-scoring.md`](../architecture/content-scoring.md) § The placement matrix → *Deployment-wide content models at the client position*; the frame § Tier-3 → *Artifact kinds*).
  - The **per-user Bayesian spam model** is fetched per-actor over WS-RPC (`fauna.bridges.fetch_spam_model`, sealed seal-on-read), **not** a downloadable artifact. So a client makes a single fetch and scores own + shared baseline with no second artifact.

## Errors & edge cases

- `error-message` page-level (inherited via global).
- Empty queue → `actions: []`; render an empty state, not an error.
- Training submission failure → surfaced via `error-message`.
- **Appeal is gated on there being a decision to appeal, and on whose it is (built 2026-09-21; author scope, reason bound and pending bound 2026-09-22).** `fauna.moderation.appeal` requires a non-empty `reason` no longer than `fauna_protocol::moderation::MAX_APPEAL_REASON_BYTES` (4 KiB of UTF-8 — a human paragraph or three; either failure is `invalid_params`, refused before any write) and a `content_id` this nest holds an enforcement record for — otherwise `not_found` ("no enforcement record for this content"); a 32-byte hex id is keyed lowercase, whatever spelling the caller sent. The predicate is `CacheDb::appeal_subject` (`db/moderation.rs`), the single owner of *is this appealable, and by whom*, and it is a **union of four sources** because the two takedown kinds and the two post orderings record the compelled fact in different places: an `obligation_action_records` row of any action (every post takedown, live or already-deleted — a mail quarantine/reject writes none, § Categories & enforcement item 2), `content_meta.legal_takedown_ref`, a `legal_takedown_deleted_posts` row (§ Legal takedown → *Posts*), or `segment_records.legal_takedown_ref` (§ Legal takedown → *Conversations* — the arm a gate reading obligation rows alone would get wrong, since a conv takedown of a record no attestation names writes no obligation row). **A post appeal is the author's alone** (§ Wire): each post-side source names the author — the obligation row's `author_hex`, the content row's `author` behind the flag, the floor row's `author_id` — and any other caller is refused `permission_denied`. **A conversation appeal is the attested author's where the nest holds an attestation (ruled 2026-10-18; built 2026-09-21 as any-holder, the author's row 2026-10-18):** a takedown or block of a record `conv_record_authors` names writes the author's obligation row (§ Legal takedown → *Conversations*), so the first source names the author and any other caller is refused, as for a post. A record no attestation names writes no row, and the fourth source admits any authenticated caller who holds the record's id — only a recipient of the sealed record ever learns that id, unlike a public post's — which is the attribution-gap-admits rule next, not a separate posture. A post-side record no source can attribute (a legacy obligation row with an empty `author_hex`) admits the same way, since the safe failure direction of an attribution gap is to admit: a false refusal denies the author the handle the triple guarantees. **A row another nest issued is appealed through the author's home nest, which relays it to the issuer — whose gate, pending bound and chain decide (§ Legal takedown → *The author homed on another nest*, ruled 2026-10-18).** **One pending appeal per (appellant, `content_id`):** an appeal is pending until a decision on that content — a `moderation:legal-takedown` or `moderation:legal-takedown-restore` audit row — is recorded after it; a repeat while one is pending **collapses**, replying `status: "appeal_already_recorded"` and writing nothing (idempotent for a retried dispatch), so the permanent chain grows by one bounded row per appellant per decision, never per call (`CacheDb::record_appeal`, check and append under one lock hold). An **overturn does not revoke the handle** — the obligation row survives as additive history (§ Persistence), so a decision that was made stays appealable, and the overturn is itself a decision that re-opens it — whatever spelling the admin sent the id in, since a takedown or overturn records its `target` in the same lowercase hex the appeal is keyed by. The appeal is **recorded for review, never granted**: it lands on the permanent audit trail (`moderation:appeal`) for an admin to act on, keyed like every other audit row to the party who performed it — the appellant in `audit_log.actor_id`, the content id in `target`, and the reason, **verbatim**, in `detail`. **The chain keeps the text, not a digest (ruled 2026-09-22):** an appeal is the user's own submission to the nest's authority, the record an admin reviews, and a digest would leave that review with nothing to read; the text is bounded (above), and the appellant's own account export carries it back to them, because the `audit_log` export slice is exactly the rows whose `actor_id` is the exporting actor (`db/actor_tables.rs`, the `audit_log` entry). Like every other act of theirs on the chain it survives account deletion — deleting a row would break every later row's hash. Client-side the guards are rendered before dispatch by the shared `fauna_client_moderation::appeal_form_view` (empty and over-long reason alike), and the outcome lands on `appeal-status`, never on the page-level `error-message` (a refusal must not blank the queue the user is reading).

## Architectural rules

1. **Unified location across all 7 apps.** Web's embedded-in-Settings pattern is acceptable as a layout choice **iff** the same IDs render on the standalone `/moderation` page (standalone apps) and inside Settings (web) — mirrors the Status / Settings split.
2. **Classifier is shared Rust;** placement obeys `content-scoring.md`.
3. **Category vocabulary is the canonical 5, defined once in shared Rust;** no app hard-codes the set or its styling.
4. **Enforcement is user-side for social content + admin policy for mail ingest only** (§ Categories & enforcement) — no nest-wide social-content *policy* removal (the sole exception is § Legal takedown). The planned region-blocking surface (§ Categories & enforcement item 3; owner [`region-blocking.md`](region-blocking.md)) is app-side, region-scoped and visible — it does not touch this rule's nest-wide-removal invariant.

## Don't do these

- Don't run separate / divergent classifiers per-app (the shared heuristic + published model artifact is canonical).
- Don't compose flagged-item summary text or category labels per-app (shared `LocalizedText`).
- Don't add an admin "remove this social post for everyone" **policy** surface — social moderation is a personal filter (product invariant). The *sole* exception is the narrow, non-discretionary legal-compulsion carve-out (§ Legal takedown) — genuinely illegal content, transparent + appealable + audited, never a policy/opinion lever.
- Don't emit a `category` outside the canonical 5.
- Don't let a user's report *do* anything to anyone but the reporter — no automatic hide, label, removal or rank penalty for others, no reputation or aggregate feed, no notification to the author (§ User-initiated reporting → *What a report is not*).
