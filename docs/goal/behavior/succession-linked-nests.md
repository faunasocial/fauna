# Succession at every linked nest — target state

> **Audience:** nest and every app. Anyone touching linking, the secondary leg, the recovery ceremonies or the succession transaction is a consumer: an identity linked to two nests must be retired at both, or the thief keeps the account at the one the ceremony missed.
> **Purpose:** carry a succession, and the registration chain it verifies against, to every nest that holds an account for the identity, without letting any nest accept evidence a seed holder could mint.

## Every nest the identity is linked to (ruled 2026-10-01)

An identity can hold an account on more than one nest. A link is "the user's same identity, registered on both" ([`linked-nests.md`](linked-nests.md) § The surface), a device binds to one of them, and the other may hold what no other nest holds: the home box of [`../architecture/nest/deployment-home-with-public-relay.md`](../architecture/nest/deployment-home-with-public-relay.md) rests the canonical mail store. The registration records and the succession statement name no nest and are signed end to end, so they are facts about the identity, and **"the home nest" in this doc is every nest that holds an account for the identity: each holds the same chain, and each runs the five steps above when the statement reaches it.** A ceremony that retired the identity at one nest and left it signing in at another would leave the thief the account there, and the successor nothing.

**Acceptance: a nest's own chain, and nothing else.** A nest applies a succession to a local account only through `fauna.recovery.succession.submit`, on a statement that verifies against the registration chain that nest itself holds for the identity. Who delivers it does not matter (the kind is pre-identity), and no other evidence is admissible: a chain fetched from another nest, or delivered by one, is a chain a seed holder can mint (§ The succession statement, the verification rule), and the only pointer a nest holds to the identity's other nests is the pairing row, which the seed writes. So a statement that arrives by federation for a local identity stays refused (`SuccessionRefusal::OldIsLocal`), and a nest that holds an account and no chain can verify nothing: the statement is refused there and the retired key keeps signing in, the degradation of an account that never made a kit. The transaction is the whole one, never a lesser one: the account moves, the old key is refused, and that nest's own pairing rows burn.

**The chain follows the link.** A linked nest can verify only if it holds the chain, so the client carries it there over the account's own session, as the verbatim records `fauna.recovery.registration.submit` already verifies link by link. (a) *The link action reconciles the two chains before it writes a pairing row.* Whichever nest holds the longer chain extends the other; two chains of which neither extends the other refuse the link, with an error that says the two nests hold different recovery keys for this identity. A fork is never linked silently. (b) *Every full pass of a seed-holding runtime reconciles each linked nest it reaches against its bound nest* the same way, whatever capabilities the pairing carries. A kit ceremony that moved the chain runs that reconcile at once, and a nest it could not reach is owed until the next pass. (c) *A seed-alone replacement is requested at every linked nest, and each runs its own 30-day window.* A landed seed-alone link carries no prior-key signature, so it cannot be replayed (§ The RecoveryKey → *Replacement*). The reconcile also reads each linked nest's pending replacement and feeds the standing alert with it ([`critical-alerts.md`](critical-alerts.md) § Feeders), because a seed holder can open a window at a nest no device is bound to; the veto contests the window at every nest that holds one.

**The road: the nest that applied the statement remembers which nests are owed, and the successor's devices deliver.** The pairings burn in the transaction ([`succession-aftermath.md`](succession-aftermath.md) § Re-key scope, the table's *Nest pairings* row), and they are the only record of the identity's other nests: a device's registry keeps one nest per identity, and a ceremony run with no session, or through a lockout, cannot list them. So the transaction keeps the burned rows' destinations as that succession's **owed nests** (the nest id, and the address when the row carried one; oldest rows first, capped, since the seed can add rows), serves them to the successor alone on `fauna.recovery.succession.status`, and clears one when the successor says it is settled. Every seed-holding runtime of the successor delivers in its full pass, to each owed nest it can reach: it submits the statement path the bound nest's lookup serves, over an anonymous connection. A landed statement clears the entry, and so do "already succeeded" and a nest that holds no account for the retired identity. Where the nest holds the account and cannot verify (no chain, or a chain behind) and the device holds the retired identity's seed, it signs in there as that identity, replays the chain, and submits again. What still cannot land stays owed and is reported, never dropped. A nest that applied the statement burned pairings of its own and keeps its own owed nests, which the successor's runtime reads when it next completes that nest, so the set closes over any number of nests. **The link action delivers first:** linking a nest as an identity that has predecessors submits their statements there before it connects, so re-linking, the one visible gesture the burn already asks for, lands the succession at that nest whether or not a list named it.

**The stated bounds.** (1) A linked nest is retired when a successor device reaches it: at once for a nest on the internet, and for a home box reachable only on its own network when a device is next at home, which is also the only place the thief reaches it. (2) An account the identity registered on a nest it never linked is tied to the others by no record anywhere. Nothing delivers there, and it holds no chain, until the user links it. (3) At a nest that held no chain when the seed was stolen, the thief can register a RecoveryKey of their own first. The replay is then refused and the entry stays owed: between two seed holders at a nest with no chain there is no winner (§ Don't do these), which is why (a) carries the chain at link time, before any theft. (4) A successor registered by hand at an owed nest before the statement landed there is refused as `successor_exists`, and the entry stays owed until that account is deleted there.

**Rejected.** *Burning the second nest's account* (a replica by construction, which the successor re-links): a nest cannot know that. The home box holds the only copy of the mail it has acked off the relay, and any nest may hold posts and files made there, so a burn destroys data the user cannot recreate, and the whole transaction moves it. *Applying a federation-delivered statement on a chain the nest fetches* from the nest its pairing row names: the row is seed-written and the chain seed-mintable, so a thief pairs a nest of their own and succeeds the account to themselves. *Nest-to-nest delivery* (the applying nest pushes, or the linked nest pulls on its pairing channel): sound for acceptance, since the statement verifies against the receiving nest's own chain whatever carried it. But the pairing channel is dialed one way, so it needs a push leg, a pull leg and a durable push list, to carry what a device carries through a door that already exists. It stays open as an optimization if device reach proves too slow, never as a second acceptance rule. *A list kept by the ceremony's client* (read `fauna.pair.list` before the submit): unreadable from a ceremony with no session, and lost with the process.
