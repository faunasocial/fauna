## Re-key scope (what re-points, what re-mints, what stays, what is lost)

The classification rule: **rows that confer future authority or access re-point; rows that record history/attribution stay** (embedded signatures are immutable and genuinely name the old key); **key material re-mints through each plane's existing rotation machinery**; **sealed corpus re-seals client-driven, urgently** (the re-seal races device loss — the ratified blockquote below owns the wording).

> **Who owns the corpus after the ceremony — ratified 2026-08-03 (settling the same-day finding that the corpus did not move). Ownership moves in the succession transaction; participation moves by propagation.** `record_succession` re-points every row that makes the old identity an *owner* of resting data — not only `users` / the handle / `admin_actor_ids` — so the successor reaches its own corpus by ordinary authenticated reads, and the refusal plane stays exactly one rule ("the old key is refused everywhere"; the successor is an ordinary owner, no second authorization rule anywhere). **Which rows those are is NOT enumerated here — it is declared per table in `bins/fauna-nest/src/db/actor_tables.rs`'s `ACTOR_TABLES`, and this paragraph deliberately points at it rather than restating it** (the declared-axis blockquote below). This sentence replaced a table-by-table list on 2026-08-12, and the list's state when it was removed is the argument for removing it: it named `segment_records.actor_id` when the column is `scope_id`, and it had never grown the `nostr_*` family the ceremony had been re-pointing since 2026-08-11 — a prose copy of a list that is authoritative elsewhere drifts silently, because nothing reads it. The alternative considered and **rejected** was a lint checking this prose against the registry: that is machinery built to keep a restatement in sync, where the one-owner rule says to delete the restatement. What stays here is what the registry cannot say — the *class* rule (ownership moves, participation does not) and the two column-level moves no table-level declaration covers: both quota counters (`users.{inbox,storage}_bytes_used` move to the successor row, so the handle-less old row cannot strand quota nobody can reclaim) and the handle itself. `blob_metadata` needs no edit: blobs are content-addressed and ownerless (ref-counted), so corpus ownership was always the referencing rows, which is why the re-point is a handful of indexed `UPDATE`s, not a data migration. The old `users` row still survives handle-less — no longer "quota home of content that still exists", and (corrected 2026-09-24) not an FK target either: no table declares a foreign key onto `users`. What it still does is mark the retired id as **local** — the predecessor walks join on it and a peer-relayed statement is refused on it — and it goes with the chain when the successor is deleted (`account-data-plane.md` § Nest-side requirements item 1, *Deletion reaches the account's predecessors*, which owns that rule). The refusal of the retired key at the registration doors is `actor_successions`' ([`identity-succession.md`](identity-succession.md) § Enforcement on the home nest, step 4), never this row's. What deliberately stays out: **participation** — membership in *other* owners' sets and groups moves by the ratified propagation paths (the MLS sweep, the federation peer re-point), never by this transaction.
>
> **An address is ownership, and it was the one member of this list that was also a live exposure (found + closed 2026-08-11).** `account_aliases` was absent from the ratified enumeration above until that date, and the gap did not merely strand a mailbox — it kept the predecessor's mail credentials **working**. On any nest that has claimed a mail domain, `validate_recipient` (the bridge's AUTH-time address→actor resolution, both roles) resolves through the `account_aliases` exact row and *only* that row; the handle→actor fallback beside it is gated on the domain **not** being registered, so it never fires in a real mail deployment. A stale alias therefore kept pointing `<handle>@<domain>` at the retired actor after the ceremony — and the successor cannot clean up behind it, because `revoke_wrapped_mls_blob` / `revoke_wrapped_submission_token` refuse any target that is not the caller, so the retired actor's resting blobs are unreachable to the very client whose burn is supposed to delete them. The two compose into the exact failure [`mail-credentials.md`](mail-credentials.md) § Rotation and recovery → *Succession* declares impossible: a thief holding the pre-succession `__config` goes on authenticating with the same password, and inbound mail to the user's own address goes on being sealed to the MSEK-derived key that thief holds. Re-pointing the alias is what makes that section's third bullet true rather than aspirational — and it is also what arms the `succession_pending` tempfail, which can only be reached once the address resolves to an actor with no registered pubkey. The successor's own mail-enable could never have healed this: `ensure_canonical_handle_alias` declines to take a localpart another actor already owns (the uniqueness invariant in [`mail-aliases.md`](mail-aliases.md) § Kind 1), so the row was unrecoverable from the client side by design. **Not** re-pointed, deliberately and for the same reason the MSEK burns rather than moves: `actor_mls_pubkeys` / `actor_epoch_seal_keys` — the retired actor's recipient seal keys derive from material the thief read, so carrying them to the successor would seal fresh mail to a compromised key. The successor re-registers its own from MSEK′.
>
> Two properties make the re-point safe and cheap. **(1) Collision-free by construction:** `record_succession` refuses `NewAlreadyRegistered`, so at the moment of succession the successor owns nothing — the bulk `UPDATE … SET actor_id = new WHERE actor_id = old` cannot collide with anything the successor holds, and no merge question exists. **(2) The rejected alternative is recorded with its reasons** — a grandfathered read path for the succeeded pair would add a second rule to the one plane whose whole point is a single rule (the `superseded` consult at five call sites), compound on twice-succeeded chains, strand storage the successor could never delete or reclaim (read-only by its own definition), and either *worsen* the device-loss race (a bounded window that lapses locks the owner out with their devices intact) or become permanent complexity (an unbounded one).
>
> **Already-succeeded accounts (the backfill) — retired 2026-09-30.** Every succession moves its rows inside its one transaction, and under the 2026-09-24 baseline reset no succession recorded before the in-transaction re-point exists anywhere, so no boot pass re-points rows ([`../architecture/compat-remnant-sweep.md`](../architecture/compat-remnant-sweep.md) § Still queued, and what stays, the early audit pass's reading (4)). What a crash can still split is the filesystem half — the actor-scoped directories renamed after the commit — and the boot heal that finishes it is [`succession-propagation.md`](succession-propagation.md)'s, the ownership re-point bullet.
>
> **Until the re-seal completes, the old seed is client-only-resident key material guarding user-irrecoverable data — the re-seal is a race against device loss, never a background nicety.** The old escrow row is deleted in the succession transaction and the new blob seals the *new* seed, so post-succession the old seed exists only in the account registry on the user's own device(s); lose every device before the re-seal and the account restores from escrow while the corpus stays sealed to a key that exists nowhere. Two consequences, both ratified 2026-08-03: **(i)** the successor's client treats the corpus re-seal as **urgent aftermath** — started at first successor sign-in, surfaced with progress, resumed until complete — and **(ii)** the successor's escrow blob **carries the predecessor seed(s) alongside the new seed** (§ Seed escrow owns the container rule), which closes the race from the moment the successor's kit ceremony completes: a total-device-loss restore then recovers the new seed *and* the material to open the not-yet-re-sealed corpus. (Keeping the *old* escrow row instead was rejected: it would keep the retired kit a live authorization root — exactly what "the old kit retires with the old identity" exists to prevent.)

**The account-state plane's generation-0 delegable rows are carried by the successor's walk (ruled 2026-10-01; built — § Implementation status today).** These rows are the classification rule's *sealed corpus*. The delegable rung's entries — the preference cluster (`moderation`, `sync-prefs`, `personalization`, `delegation`), the seen-set and the read markers — are sealed under per-kind keys derived from the owner's `BackupKey` ([`../architecture/owner-key-material.md`](../architecture/owner-key-material.md) § Path A-sibling-2), and a successor's schedule derives from its own seed, so its walk leaves every row its predecessor wrote unopened. They re-seal client-driven like the rest of the corpus, and the plane's own walk is the driver. **The rule.** A successor's runtime is handed, for each attested predecessor — the identities whose seeds the device's account registry holds, the same set that is the `prior` half of the writer-door trust — that identity's **delegable** generation-0 schedule and nothing wider. A walked row that does not open under the successor's own schedule is tried under each of them. One that opens is **carried**: merged into the successor's state by its kind's ordinary merge policy and, where the merge takes it, re-authored as the walking device's own row, value and merge metadata verbatim, so it publishes sealed under the successor's schedule. This is the walk's existing carry shape ([`../architecture/account-replica-posture.md`](../architecture/account-replica-posture.md) § The store device principal, refinement 11): nothing is written at the predecessor writer's coordinate, and a row the successor's state already covers writes nothing.

**What the carry buys, and what it must never do.** (1) *No leg ordering.* The carry reads plane rows alone, so it depends on no other leg's order (leg 1, the `__config` re-seal it ran beside when ruled, retired with the rail on 2026-10-02 — [`../architecture/config-dissolution.md`](../architecture/config-dissolution.md) § The `__config` dissolution schedule → *The closure order*, step (6)), and it runs in every walk of the bound nest's delegable plane: the prologue's, a nudged one, a reconcile (*Which walks carry*, the next paragraph). (2) *The floor returns to the seed alone.* Once one device has carried a row, a device holding only the successor seed reads it, and a grant minted from the successor's schedule opens it. (3) *Latest-wins is kept.* The carried row keeps its predecessor-era stamp, so a value the successor wrote since outranks it. A walk that meets a row its state already holds writes nothing. A second device whose walk is served the predecessor's row ahead of the first device's re-authored copy re-authors it once more: an equal row, so at most one per device per item, the plane's ordinary shape. (4) *The retired schedule only ever opens.* The runtime holds it as a trial-open key, so no row is sealed under it; and this carry is the delegable branch alone, so a predecessor's fleet-only generation-0 rows (the generation machinery's device-set, wrap, escrow and reach kinds) stay unopened exactly as before this ruling. How the machinery and the random generations cross is the generation rider's (`owner-key-material.md` § Path A-sibling-2 → *Rotation*, the succession rider, re-ruled the same day): its walk opens one fleet-only kind, the mint record of a generation whose key the device carries, under that kind's retired keys and nothing wider. What the reclamation pass opens under the other machinery kinds' retired keys, only to retire it, is that rider's to say as well.

**Which walks carry: the bound nest's, and no other leg's (ruled 2026-10-01).** The predecessors' schedules are handed to one plane: the delegable scope's plane on the nest the runtime is bound to. The peer leg's planes ([`../architecture/account-sync-plane.md`](../architecture/account-sync-plane.md) § The peer leg) and a linked nest's (the same doc, § The bind leg, ruling 4) are handed none, so a predecessor's row met there is kept in the relay plane and counted unopened, as it is on any device that holds no predecessor material. Three reasons, and the first decides it alone. (i) *The other legs converge without a retired key.* A carried row is the walking device's own row, sealed under the successor's schedule, and the peer serve and the publish diff move it as they move any own row. Once one device that holds the material has walked the bound nest, every sibling and every linked nest receives the values in a form the successor seed opens. What the other legs would add is a local read in one window: a device that holds the material and cannot reach its nest, pulling from a sibling that walked the nest after the ceremony and before any carry was published. That device's next walk of its nest carries, which is residual (a)'s shape: owed, never damage. (ii) *Residual (b)'s bound is the home nest's write door, and no other leg has one.* A retired schedule opens whatever its key holders can get served into a walk, and its key holders outlive the ceremony: the seed thief, and every holder of a delegable-kind grant the predecessor minted, whose grant the aftermath revokes and whose key it cannot take back. The home nest refuses the retired identity from the commit and admits only the successor's own puts, so on its feed that set is what rested at the ceremony. Peer admission proves who a peer is (a device or a custodian the successor authorized) and nothing about which rows it relays or which predecessor-era keys it kept, and a relay plane keeps rows its holder cannot open, by design. Handing the peer leg the schedules would make every admitted peer's relay plane a source of rows opened under a retired key, each one re-authored under the successor's schedule at whatever stamp its author chose. (iii) *What a linked nest serves under the successor's account is not bounded by the ceremony.* Pairings burn in the succession transaction (the table below), so a successor's runtime completes only nests the successor linked itself, and the publish diff never pushes a row the device cannot open under its own schedule (§ The bind leg, ruling 1(a)), so no device delivers a predecessor-sealed row there. One such a nest serves anyway arrived by a road nothing here bounds: a linked nest moves the account to the successor only when the statement reaches it ([`identity-succession.md`](identity-succession.md) § Enforcement on the home nest → *Every nest the identity is linked to*), and until then its write door admits the retired identity, so what it moved is not what rested at the ceremony. So the carry keeps one more invariant beside clause (4): **the retired schedule opens rows in the bound nest's walk and nowhere else.** No relay-row open tries it, the publish diff's vouching included, so a predecessor-sealed row is never pushed onward by a device, to a linked nest or back to a rebuilt bound one.

**The predecessor's own row is retired behind the carry (measured and ruled 2026-10-01; built 2026-10-02 — § Implementation status today).** The ceremony moves the delegable scope's feed to the successor with every live row the predecessor's devices wrote. The carry writes beside those rows and nothing is written at a predecessor writer's coordinate, so nothing supersedes them, and before this rule no pass sent a retire on this scope. **Measured.** A predecessor whose devices hold P live `(item, writer)` pairs leaves all P live for good. With 1, 3 and 6 predecessor devices that each wrote the same 12 items (the four preference records, three seen-set scopes, five read markers) the feed held 12, 36 and 72 predecessor rows before the ceremony and the same 12, 36 and 72 at the first successor pass and the fifteenth. Beside them each successor device that holds the predecessor's seed had written one own row per item (12 at each of two such devices) and a seed-only device none. The live-entry cap counts every one of them: it is charged per `(item, writer)` pair, in this scope as in the fleet scope ([`../architecture/account-data-taxonomy.md`](../architecture/account-data-taxonomy.md) § The generation machinery → *Fleet-scope reclamation* states the cap). So a succession needs room for a second copy of every carried item while the first copy stays. A predecessor one pair past half the cap (2,049 read markers from one device) showed what happens without it: the successor's walk carried 2,047 rows, the scope stood at 4,096, and the last two items stayed readable only under the retired schedule. A seed-only device read 2,047 of the 2,049 and its own first write was refused `scope_full`. The carrying device fared no better. Its walk aborted at each refused carry, and every later write of its own waited behind the two rows it could not publish, an update of a pair the nest already held included, because a device publishes in journal order. No client act frees the room, which [`../principles.md`](../principles.md) § Client-recoverable nest state calls a bug. **The rule.** A predecessor's row is retired at the bound nest once a row sealed under the successor's schedule carries its item there. (1) *The walk names the candidates.* A predecessor row's writer frontier never advances (the rule above), so every full pass's reconcile is served each live one again, opens it under the retired schedule and merges it (measured: the reconcile's inherited count equals P at every pass). Each row that opened and merged is a candidate, whatever the merge did with it: its coordinates as served, and the logical item it opened to. The list belongs to that walk, lasts one pass and is never stored. (2) *The licence.* The pass retires a candidate when a row at that item's key under the successor's schedule, written by this device or by a verified member of the successor's fleet, opens to this replica's merged entry for the item and is published as the pass saw it ([`../architecture/account-sync-plane.md`](../architecture/account-sync-plane.md) § The bind leg, ruling 1(d)). The merge has just shown that the predecessor's row adds nothing to that entry. The bound nest's own listing shows a row that carries the entry whole, sealed for the successor, by a writer whose later rows for the item can only carry more. So the retire cannot leave the nest serving the item to no one. (3) *The retire.* The existing retire kind, naming the row by its own coordinates. The item key is the one the retired schedule derived, which no row of the successor's sits at. The nest's retention gate applies as to any retire, and the device forgets its relay copy when the nest answers retired or gone. **What the licence covers.** *A row the carry re-authored:* the device's own row is the cover, once it is published as the pass saw it. *A latest-wins item the successor has written since:* the predecessor's row lost the merge and was never re-authored, and the successor's newer row is the cover. *A device whose state already held the value and that wrote nothing* (*What the carry buys*, clause (3)): the sibling's row it took the value from is the cover, when the listing holds it. A sibling's row is enough because the evidence is the bound nest's listing and not this replica's relay plane; the shredder's veto on the fleet scope already lets a shred go on a verified member's listed row (`account-data-taxonomy.md`, the same section, clause (3)(e)). **No cover, no retire.** Where no listed member row carries the merged entry, the predecessor's row stays, and it stays the item's carrier at the nest. That is the case while this device's own row is still pending (the scope is full, the nest unreachable), and where the only row carrying the entry was written by a device that has since left the successor's fleet. **The retired schedule still opens rows in the bound nest's walk and nowhere else.** The fleet scope's predecessor arm opens relay rows in the reclamation pass, under a stated carve-out, because its walk holds the mint kind's keys alone (`account-data-taxonomy.md`, clause (3)(i)). This rule needs no carve-out. The delegable walk already opens every live predecessor row at every full pass, so the pass reads the walk's list and opens nothing itself. *Which walks carry* stands as written. **At the cap.** A retire inserts nothing and needs no headroom; the carry's put does. With this rule a pass carries as many rows as the scope has room for, the predecessor rows behind them are retired once their covers are listed, and a later pass carries on. Measured by hand through the existing retire door on the flow above: retiring the 2,047 covered predecessor rows was accepted for every one, the two pending rows then published, the last two predecessor rows were retired behind them, and the scope held 2,049 rows, all the successor's; the seed-only device read all 2,049 and both devices' writes landed again. The walk owes one thing for this: a carry whose put is refused `scope_full` does not stop it. The publish parks the row and sends it once the scope has room ([`../architecture/account-replica-posture.md`](../architecture/account-replica-posture.md) § The store device principal, refinement 11 → *A row refused for room is parked*). An aborted walk would bank no listing, and with no listing nothing is published as the pass saw it. **Who asks, and what the nest checks.** Every runtime whose bound-nest walk opens the row: a device that holds the predecessor's seed, and the seedless agent handed the schedules. A retire is idempotent, and a second asker's answer is its licence to forget its own copy. The retire door needs no change: it authorizes the account's session and never the row's writer, and the predecessor's devices hold no grant on the successor's account, so their walk marks count for nothing at the gate. The retire record carries each retire to the linked nests (`account-sync-plane.md` § The bind leg, ruling 5), behind that nest's own diff. **What this rule left open is ruled where the scope's own reclamation is** (2026-10-01; built 2026-10-02 but for parts (6) and (7)): [`../architecture/delegable-scope-reclamation.md`](../architecture/delegable-scope-reclamation.md) § Delegable-scope reclamation. The measurement met three things this rule does not settle: a removed or signed-out device's delegable rows also stay live for good, and such a row may be the only row of its item; a scope with no free pair cannot begin, because every carry is a new pair; and one row per device per item is the scope's ordinary shape. That section rules all three, and two of its parts change this rule's reach. A carry's put names the predecessor's row it was carried from (its part (2)), so at a nest that reads the list the cover and the retire are one transaction, and *At the cap* then needs no free pair. And the case *No cover, no retire* leaves standing, where the only row carrying the entry was written by a device that has left the fleet, is closed by its hand-over (part (3)). **Compatibility:** an existing retire, sent from an existing pass; no wire or at-rest change. A binary older than this rule retires nothing, which is today's residue. **Refused.** *Stating residual (c) as a bound:* each succession would leave P rows for good, a second succession would need room for a third copy, and past half the cap the carry cannot finish and no client can free the room. *The nest dropping the predecessor's delegable rows in the ceremony:* until a device has carried them they are the only copy of the user's preferences, seen-sets and read markers. *Retiring on merged state alone,* with no listed cover: the row that carries the entry may be one the nest does not hold. *Opening relay rows under the retired schedule in the pass,* as the fleet arm does: it would amend *Which walks carry* for nothing, since the walk already opens every such row. *Every device that holds the material re-authoring, so each holds its own licence:* one more live row per item per device, to license a retire the first device's row already licenses.

**Rejected.** *Reading under the predecessor's schedule for good and never re-authoring* leaves the corpus sealed to a retired key: the device-loss race the ratified blockquote above forbids, and rows no grant of the successor's can open. *Moving the preference cluster under the generation machinery*, so the re-escrow would carry it, takes the cluster off the grant-mintable generation-0 keys (a re-registration under new kind strings and a re-seal of every row) and makes reading a muted-word list depend on an escrow holder. *A leg that reads the re-keyed `__config`* is what the CAS-blob bridge was, and it went with the bridge ([`../architecture/config-dissolution.md`](../architecture/config-dissolution.md) § The `__config` dissolution schedule → *The closure order*, step (5)); the rail itself retired at step (6).

**Declared residuals of the carry.** (a) A device holding no predecessor material carries nothing; the rows stay unopened there until a device that holds the material walks. That is owed by another device, never damage, and no progress line renders it: the values arrive. (b) The carry is unadjudicated, as the `__config` re-key that carried these same sub-records was: the delegable rung is secret-free, recreatable state with no mark plane (§ Adjudicating what the aftermath carries across lists the planes that have one). What a walk can be served under a retired schedule is bounded by who can write into the scope, not by who holds the retired keys. The home nest refuses the retired identity from the ceremony's commit, and its walk is the only one that carries (*Which walks carry*, above), so the carry admits what rested on that nest at the ceremony; a predecessor-sealed row a peer or a linked nest relays is never opened. (c) The predecessor's own row stays on the feed beside the re-authored one until the rule above retires it (*The predecessor's own row is retired behind the carry*). What that rule leaves, stated: (i) where no successor device holds the predecessor's seed, nothing of that predecessor's is opened and all of it stays, P rows per succession, measured at about 290 B sealed per row for the mixed set and 261 B per read marker (3,487 B for one device's 12 rows; 534,789 B for 2,049 markers), and each item the successor's devices write again takes a second pair beside it; (ii) a row whose item no listed member row carries (*No cover, no retire*), until a member's hand-over writes that row ([`../architecture/delegable-scope-reclamation.md`](../architecture/delegable-scope-reclamation.md) § Delegable-scope reclamation, part (3); built 2026-10-02); (iii) a replica that holds no predecessor material, and walked these rows before they were retired, keeps its relay copies, since nothing tells a relay plane about another writer's retirement and it cannot open the rows to learn they are dead; a replica that joins afterwards is served none; (iv) the equal rows a second device re-authored (clause (3) of *What the carry buys*) are a live member's rows, one per item per such device, and stay until that section's part (4) retires the earlier of each equal pair. (The fleet scope's counterpart: [`../architecture/account-data-taxonomy.md`](../architecture/account-data-taxonomy.md) § The generation machinery → *Fleet-scope reclamation*, clause (3)(i).)

The table those rulings feed is § Adjudicating what the aftermath carries across, below.

### Adjudicating what the aftermath carries across

> **The aftermath re-confers authority from a ledger the adversary could have written, so every row it carries across is MARKED — re-registration is never gated, and never silent either. Ratified 2026-08-03.** `__config`, where the ledger then rested, was sealed under `BackupKey`, which derives from the identity seed, and the seed *is* account access (§ The identity seed) — and the plane the ledger rests on since its cut (`fauna.state.succession-ledger`, fleet-only, `GenerationTip`-sealed) changed the mechanism, not the reach (ruled 2026-10-02): `BackupKey` alone opens no tip-sealed row, but the seed also derives the escrow secret, the nest serves the escrow to whoever authenticates as the owner, and a seed-holder enrolls a device of their own that the fleet view admits — so a seed thief keys every generation a surviving holder serves and reads and writes every fleet-only row (the MSEK, the credentials, the period keys, the folder keys, this ledger) exactly as it read and wrote the blob; what ends that reach is the ceremony, which burns the predecessor's escrow and re-escrows generations ≥ 1 to the successor's target alone (the one key floor: [`../architecture/account-data-taxonomy.md`](../architecture/account-data-taxonomy.md) § The generation machinery, clause (b) of the plane-floor paragraph; the escrow doors and the burn: [`../architecture/owner-key-material.md`](../architecture/owner-key-material.md) § Path A-sibling-2 → *Rotation*). The seed thief this entire ceremony answers could therefore have added a `backup.destinations` row — or, once that leg exists, a capability-grant ledger entry — pointing at a box or a grantee of their choosing, at any point in the pre-succession window. Nothing distinguishes it from a row the owner added: the shape is identical and no provenance was recorded. The aftermath then re-registers it with no user gesture, and the progress line reports the leg done.
>
> **The answer is the one this section already gives for structurally identical planted state**, one table row below: the MLS sweep cannot vouch for leaves the thief may have seated, and its ratified treatment is neither *refuse* nor *carry silently* — it re-points what it can and then **reports** what it did not itself add, keyed `(person, raising event)`, and the user adjudicates. The same shape governs every plane the aftermath re-mints from the ledger it carries across: **re-register or re-mint immediately** — backups restarting and grants working again is the aftermath's whole point, and gating it behind a prompt would leave a successor's corpus unprotected for as long as they ignore it — **then mark each carried-across row un-adjudicated until the owner keeps or removes it**, rendered where those rows already render. A section that adjudicates planted group members while auto-re-minting planted authority rows, without saying why the two differ, is the outcome this ruling exists to prevent.
>
> **The mark is keyed by the raising event, never a permanent per-row boolean** — the same rule, for the same reason, as the unattested-member item (§ Propagation → *MLS groups*): a *Keep* is a verdict about the compromise window that raised it and cannot vouch for that row across a later one, so a second succession legitimately re-raises a row the owner kept after the first. It is written by the pass that carries the ledger across (the `__config` re-key when ratified; the successor's `chain` write since the ledger kind's cut, below), because that is the only point at which the raising identity is still known; the leg that re-registers sees a settled outcome and no predecessor at all. Marking is **not** a user edit and must not restamp the record's merge clock, or a carried-across config would win latest-wins against a concurrent device's genuinely newer edits.
>
> **The verdict rests ON the mark; a *Keep* never deletes it. One encoding for every plane. Ratified 2026-08-06.** The reflex shape — presence means open, *Keep* removes the row — makes an answered mark indistinguishable from one never raised, and that breaks in two independent ways. **A re-run of whatever raised it** re-raises everything already worked through: the member sweep has a re-run driver by design (the *"finish moving your groups"* retry), and the `__config` re-key ran at every sign-in on every device while the rail lived. **And two of the owner's devices working one backlog erase each other**: the only merge rule under which a *deletion* can win is a whole-field latest-wins, which discards the peer's adjudications wholesale — so a device still holding a pre-*Keep* copy resurrected the mark simply by writing `__config` later for an unrelated reason, which the aftermath itself did at every sign-in. Both adjudication planes therefore store the owner's verdict at rest and merge as a **per-key union in which a decided verdict beats a still-`Open` one**, keyed on the row and its raising event — lossless and resurrection-free at once (where each mark plane rests since the rail's retirement: [`../architecture/config-dissolution.md`](../architecture/config-dissolution.md) § The `__config` dissolution schedule → *The kinds*). With the verdict carried, *Keep* no longer needs to bump the merge clock either, which removes the one case where an adjudication on one plane could beat a peer's genuinely newer edits on another. An unrecognized verdict from a newer build renders **as still open**: re-asking is harmless, silently hiding a flagged row is the failure the whole surface exists to prevent. **The union's own tiebreak, settled 2026-08-10 when the merge was made convergent in bytes:** the surviving verdict is the **most restrictive** — *Removed* ▸ an unnameable verdict ▸ *Kept* ▸ *Open* — which subsumes "a decided verdict beats `Open`" and additionally settles the two arms that used to keep whichever device ran the merge. Two devices genuinely deciding one item differently converge on *Removed* (a person the owner removed is never silently re-admitted, and a *Kept* is re-givable where a wrongly-dropped removal leaves nothing to observe); an unnameable verdict outranks a *Kept* for the same fail-visible reason it renders as open — losing it would silently vouch where the newer build may not have.
>
> **The destination plane joined the rule 2026-08-11, and all three encodings are now one. What it settled beyond the lift itself:** the mark moved **off** the `BackupDestination` row into `UserConfig.unattested_destination_marks`, keyed `(destination_id, raising event)` — a mark living on the row could only merge the way the row does, which is the whole-record latest-wins the two failures above ride in on. **The rows themselves deliberately stay whole-record latest-wins**, and the wider "give `backup.destinations` a per-key union" change was considered and **rejected**: a union without tombstones cannot propagate a *removal*, and on this plane the row being removed is the one a thief may have planted — so the union would trade the failure this rule closes for a worse one. A destination row is a *recreatable* preference anyway (re-addable from any app's Backups page; nothing at the destination is lost with the row), which is the same test that keeps the muted-keywords list on latest-wins. **This plane's `Removed` verdict is therefore ENFORCED, not merely recorded:** on the member and grant planes the removal happens in another system (an MLS commit, a nest-side revoke) and the verdict is only its record, whereas here the removal *is* a deletion inside the record that rides latest-wins — so the merge prunes every destination row whose id carries a `Removed` verdict, and a stale peer's newer write can no longer carry the row back (which, with the verdict now at rest, would have carried it back rendering *clean*). Safe against a genuine re-add because `destination_id` is a fresh uuid per enroll. The row's `unattested_from_predecessor` field survives as a **legacy stamp**, still written and still read as a fallback for the raising event no mark answers, so a build older than the plane keeps rendering the mark instead of silently unflagging the row — re-asking is harmless, hiding is not, the same asymmetry that governs an unnameable verdict.
>
> **The email-filter remainder is the FOURTH plane, and it joins by the same encoding — ratified 2026-08-14.** The per-action disposition ([`email-filters.md`](email-filters.md) defers here; ruled 2026-08-12) moves `Discard` and `FileInto` rules intact and moves `Reject` disarmed, and it justifies that remainder with *"the successor's own filter list is its remedy"* — which is a surface **nothing routes the successor to**, the exact reasoning this section rejected for the autoreply one row earlier. A thief-armed suppression or diversion rule therefore survives the ceremony unannounced, which is precisely the *carried-across, thief-armable, re-conferred* shape the ruling above governs. So it is marked like the other three: `unattested_filter_marks` (a field of the blob then; `fauna.state.succession-ledger` rows since that kind's cut, below), keyed `(filter_id, raising event)`, the owner's `UnattestedVerdict` at rest, merged by the same per-key union with the same most-restrictive-wins tiebreak.
>
> **Why the mark goes in the owner's record (`__config` when ruled; a ledger row since, below) here is a DIFFERENT reason from the destination plane's, and the difference is the part worth keeping.** There the mark moved *off* the row because a mark on the row could only merge the way the row does. A filter row cannot merge at all: `email_filters` is nest-side SQL under a single authority, one copy every device reads over `fauna.email.filters.list`, so neither failure the verdict-at-rest encoding was ratified against is reachable on the row itself. What rules the mark off the row here is instead that **the verdict is a judgment the nest never acts on** — `Remove` reuses the deletion the filter list already offers (the no-second-removal-mechanism rule, one paragraph up), so the nest needs to know nothing, while a mark column would mean the schema + wire + 7-app change the `Reject` ruling already priced and rejected. Two consequences follow. This plane's `Removed` is **recorded, not enforced** — the opposite of the destination plane, whose enforcement exists only because there the removal *is* a deletion inside a latest-wins record; here it happens nest-side through the existing kind. And there is **no legacy row stamp to fall back to** (the destination plane's own was retired 2026-09-24 by the compat-remnant sweep ([`compat-remnant-sweep.md`](../architecture/compat-remnant-sweep.md) § Program 4)), so there was never a mixed-fleet fallback here: an older client renders no mark at all, which is a *narrower* reading rather than a wrong one, and the fail-visible asymmetry is preserved by the raise being idempotent — a newer client re-reads the same rows and renders them.
>
> **What raises it, and why the classification is stable.** The inherited set is the rows whose `created_at` is at or before the succession's recorded time — `created_at` is already on the wire `EmailFilter` and on the table, so **no nest change is required to detect the class**. `filter_id` is a durable key across the ceremony because the move re-points `owner` and leaves the primary key alone. The raise runs in the aftermath, beside the member-review roster and for the same reason (that is where the raising identity is still known), and it must stay **idempotent per `(filter_id, predecessor)`** rather than "every filter I own right now": the two are indistinguishable in the successor's first session and diverge on every later re-run, once the successor has authored filters of their own.
>
> **On the account-state plane the marks are ROWS — ruled 2026-09-29, built 2026-09-30 (§ Implementation status today).** Since `fauna.state.succession-ledger` was cut plane-only (the `__config` dissolution's closure step (3)), each mark of the grant, member and filter planes is one row keyed on the row and its raising event — the per-key union above becomes the row's own join, the raise a per-row put of an `Open` mark that is idempotent by the key itself, and the `__config` re-key that wrote them became the successor's `chain` write under its own tip. The key grammar, the arms, the read fold and the succession write are owned by [`../architecture/config-dissolution.md`](../architecture/config-dissolution.md) § Phases and gates → *Bounded rows* → *The ledger*; the destination plane's mark rides `fauna.state.backup`, that kind's own row.
>
> **Where the plane raises run, and how a refused raise stays owed — ruled 2026-09-30.** The post-auth aftermath (`run_succession_aftermath`) runs before, or unordered with, the successor's account-store handle on every host family — tui spawns it first among the post-auth hooks and `resolve_and_start` separately, linux calls it well before `account_runtime::install`, the `fauna-ffi` seat leaves the order to its caller, web runs it as the first `onActorChange` handler while the runtime starts once conversations exist — and the successor's first generation is minted by its own runtime, never by the ceremony, which only burns the predecessor's wraps (§ Implementation status today, the 2026-09-28 entry). So the ledger's plane writes cannot live in that pass. **They form a post-store-ready pass of their own — shared Rust in `fauna-client-recovery` beside the post-auth pass — that every host runs once at its store-ready edge**, the edge that already lends the offline-share seat record and wires the conversation seams (tui's `AccountStoreReady` arm, linux's `account_runtime::install` ready closure, the `fauna-ffi` seat's `HOST.finish` installed arm, web's `startAccountRuntime` after `wire_parts`), handing it the account-store handle (through the `SuccessionLedgerStore` seam the cut declares) plus the registry and the nest requester the raises already take; a host passes no identity and no ceremony fact per call. Its legs, in order: the chain re-point (`repoint_succession_ledger`, retired = each attested predecessor), the grant-mark raise (`raise_grant_marks` — an `Open` mark on every live grant **whose latest event that predecessor signed**, refined 2026-09-30 with the cut: the blob stamped every live grant because it ran once, at the re-key, before the successor could have minted anything, and a raise that re-runs at every store-ready would otherwise ask the owner to adjudicate grants they minted, renewed or re-minted themselves — the signature is the provenance the blob's timing stood in for), the member-item raise, the filter-mark raise, then leg 4's re-mint (it reads the grant log, which is now these rows), then leg 8's period-key rotation — and the review-surface re-read that used to fire between legs 1 and 2 fires after this pass instead. The post-auth pass keeps every leg whose plane is still the blob (1, 2, 7) until that plane's own kind is cut. **Leg 6 joined this pass with `fauna.state.mail`'s cut (2026-09-30):** the credentials it burns and the burn record that makes it idempotent are that kind's rows, so in the post-auth pass it read a store that was not up yet and, with a bounded wait run out, stayed owed until the next sign-in. It runs LAST — the only leg that takes something away — after leg 8, under leg 4's attested-predecessor gate, skipped with the rest when the chain put is refused (its rotation records Renew events on the ledger); it burns for the registry's attested predecessors and needs no `__config` outcome, since the credential list no longer rests in that blob. The host hands the pass a mail-settings machine over the account store (the burn drives the shared rotate-mail-keys flow, which lives on it): tui its Mail page's own machine, linux and the `fauna-ffi` seat `rpc_glue::build_mail_settings_machine_for_session` over the connection's own identity, web the machine `runSuccessionAftermath` builds and stashes beside its progress callback. **Leg 8 joined this pass with `fauna.state.subscriptions`' cut (2026-09-30):** its keys are this store's rows. It needs no park and no ceremony fact — what it owes is derived from each period's `minted_by` — and it rides leg 4's gate and leg 4's signing: it runs only where this device attests a predecessor, after leg 4, skipped with the rest when the chain put is refused, and it mints and signs the rotated blobs with the account's own stored seed (`AccountRegistry::secrets`). The host hands in the account store as the period-key custody (`fauna_client_subscriptions::PeriodKeyStore`, which the handle implements beside the ledger seam), and leg 4's post-tier re-mints read the same store — so no post-tier grant waits on a later pass. **The backup kind's two legs join this pass when `fauna.state.backup` is cut (ruled 2026-09-30):** after the filter-mark raise and before leg 4, (i) the destination-mark raise — `merge_destination_marks` with an `Open` mark per destination, keyed on the raising predecessor, idempotent by the mark row's join — **riding the park, refined 2026-09-30 with the cut:** it needs no roster and no seconds, but a destination carries no signed provenance the way a grant event does, so a raise re-run at every store-ready off the attested set would mark every destination the successor adds afterwards as carried across (the grant-mark refinement above, for the same reason); the blob raised once, at the re-key, and the park is the plane's once — the raise runs in the park drain beside the member and filter raises, and the park clears only after all three landed — and (ii) leg 2's `NestBackupKey` re-grant, reading the destination list off the handle's `backup_state`. **The two legs differ in which lists they read (ruled 2026-09-30),** because the list is kept per source box ([`backup-destinations.md`](backup-destinations.md) § State & data shape → *Destination data model*): the raise marks every destination the account lists on **any** box — the succession is the account's event, the predecessor enrolled all of them, and a mark names no box — so a box no device happens to be bound to still gets its marks; the re-grant reads only the list of the box the pass's connection is bound to, because the grant it owes is that nest's. Leg 2 moves as well as the raise because the cut leaves the blob's destination list unwritten: if leg 2 stayed in the post-auth pass, it would read an empty list, answer `NothingConfigured`, and backups would silently stop after a succession. **The pass does not wait for a tip — its first put IS the successor's first tip-sealed write.** At store-ready the assembly has carried the attested predecessors' generation keys into the successor's slot and published the successor's escrow target, and no receipt names the successor's target yet (the burn), so the chain put finds no tip and the writer door runs the **first-need mint** (trigger (a) — [`../architecture/owner-key-material.md`](../architecture/owner-key-material.md) § Path A-sibling-2 → *Rotation*; the plane's `resolve_or_mint`) exactly as a fresh account's first tip-sealed write does; the pump's succession rider then re-escrows the carried generations and, finding a tip this identity minted, mints nothing more. The door refuses only when that mint cannot run — offline, no escrow-target row yet, no trusted holder reachable — the transient no-tip refusal every tip-sealed writer meets; inside a pump pass the handle parks a tip-sealed put until the pass ends, so this pass needs no ordering against the pump. **What stays owed across a refusal, and where.** The chain re-point and the grant-mark raise need only the attested predecessor set, which the account registry holds durably (`record_succession`, written by the ceremony before the switch), so they run at EVERY store-ready and are idempotent by the row's own join (`Ok(false)` when the row already says so) — no park at all. The member-item raise needs the sweep's roster, which is ceremony-only (never re-derived — [`succession-propagation.md`](succession-propagation.md)'s 2026-08-06 bullet), and the filter-mark raise needs the nest's succession seconds; **both ride ONE durable park: `PendingCeremony`, written into the account registry as a per-successor JSON side-slot** (the `pending_factory_reset` pattern of `fauna-client-accounts` — `park_aftermath_ceremony` / `pending_aftermath_ceremony` / `clear_aftermath_ceremony`, erased with the successor's row) **by the post-ceremony folds beside their `record_succession` call** (tui `session.rs`, linux `client.rs`, `fauna-wasm` `succession.rs`, `fauna-ffi` `recovery.rs` — four copies of one fold; lift them into one shared fold when the cut touches all four). The registry is the one durable per-device store that exists at the park moment on every host — the park runs pre-switch, as the predecessor, when the successor's account store does not exist yet — and the ceremony already writes it in that act; the account store's meta table cannot hold it (not open yet) and the device-local `__config` replica, the retiring rail when this was ruled, retired with it on 2026-10-02 ([`../architecture/config-dissolution.md`](../architecture/config-dissolution.md) § The `__config` dissolution schedule → *The closure order*, step (6)). Crash-safety per [`../architecture/nest/common.md`](../architecture/nest/common.md) § Client-state recoverability: the slot write before the switch is the single durable decision point; the post-store-ready pass at every store-ready is the boot reconcile — it consumes the slot only when its raising predecessor is in the attested set (attribution is never guessed, as before), runs the raises, and **clears the slot only once every put of both raises landed**, so a crash or a door refusal anywhere in between leaves it parked and the next store-ready re-runs it. > **Which recorded time, settled at build (2026-08-14).** It is `actor_successions.succeeded_at` — the moment the **nest committed** the ceremony — and **not** the statement's own `IdentitySuccession.created_at`. The usual objection to the statement clock is that it is the *client's*; the decisive one here is narrower and adversarial: the statement is stamped when it is **authored**, and the seed thief keeps full authority until the **commit**, so a rule planted in that window has a `created_at` after the statement's stamp and a statement-clock bound would carry it across **unmarked** — the silently-hidden flagged row this whole section exists to prevent. The nest's stamp closes that window exactly and is the same clock that wrote every `created_at` being compared against. Two consequences bind any app implementing this. The stamp is in **seconds** where `email_filters.created_at` is in **milliseconds**, and it is rounded **up** to the end of its second: rounding down would hide a rule written part-way through the commit's own second, while rounding up can only over-mark a rule nobody is able to author (the thief has lost authority; the successor is not signed in yet) — the fail-visible direction this section takes everywhere. And the stamp is available on a **read** as well as in the submit reply (settled 2026-08-15, closing the residual this paragraph used to declare): **`fauna.recovery.succession.status`**, an authenticated, self-scoped kind whose request carries no actor id, so a ceremony finished through `reconcile_succession` classifies after all. ⚠ **The read is authenticated for a reason that binds any future edit:** the pre-identity `succession.lookup` answers about whatever actor id it is handed, and a *server-observed* commit stamp there would be an anonymous oracle for when any account's recovery-from-compromise happened — the statements `lookup` serves are signed artifacts, and this stamp is not one, so it inherits none of their disclosure licence. Raising **nothing** and saying so remains the honest answer where no bound can be obtained at all (a nest older than that kind, or unreachable) — never a guess. > **Severity, sized in both directions, so the ruling can be re-argued rather than inherited.** The cap: post-succession segments seal under a `NestBackupKey` derived from the *successor's* seed, which the source nest holds and the thief does not, so an attacker-controlled destination receives ciphertext it cannot open — this is unflagged, not invisible. The floor: the successor's whole corpus is continuously replicated to a box the adversary chose (volume, timing and growth metadata, plus a standing ciphertext copy that becomes plaintext-equivalent on any later seed compromise), and the one moment designed to break the thief's hold is the moment that restores their enrollment. For the **capability-grant** re-mint the cap does not apply at all: a grantee the thief added to the ledger would be handed live *read* capability by the successor's own client, which is a confidentiality break rather than a routing one — which is why the shape is ratified here, before that leg is built, rather than per-leg.

> **What a successor's replica restore may take from a predecessor's — ratified 2026-08-27, closing the finding the first unrefused retry press made.** **A successor's engine is seated only by its own joins.** The `__mls` `provider` snapshot is the crypto state of a *leaf*, and a leaf is an identity (§ Propagation → *MLS groups*: the successor joins as a **new member**, there is no in-place credential swap). After the ceremony the successor's `provider` path holds the **predecessor's** snapshot — ownership moved in the nest's succession transaction, and the re-seal leg re-keyed it so it opens — and restoring it into the successor's engine seats the successor as the **old** leaf: it then decrypts as the very credential remove-old exists to ratchet out, and its own remove-old answers `CannotRemoveSelf` (measured 2026-08-27 on the ceremony device, where the ceremony's in-process join had been persisted first and the launch restore swapped the whole provider KV over it). Three rules, each with its reason. **(1) A `provider` whose groups are seated under a leaf that is not this identity's is never restored** — the engine keeps its own state (the ceremony device's SQLite holds the join), and the first successor-side save replaces the occupant by CAS. The verdict is read off the snapshot's **own-leaf credential per group** (`ProviderReplica::seating_verdict`), never off the re-seal's outcome or the path: a first session that crashed before its own state replaced the snapshot re-runs the re-seal as `AlreadyCurrent`, while the leaf a snapshot seats is a fact of its bytes. ⚠ **And a snapshot whose bytes will not yield that fact establishes nothing, so it is not restored either** (hardened 2026-08-29): the verdict was a bare list of positively-identified foreign seats whose caller restored on *empty*, which read "could not examine a single group" as "clean" — fail-open on an absolute. It now carries an `unexaminable` count beside the list and only a snapshot that was **fully examined and wholly this identity's** is restored. Two distinctions are load-bearing there, and both are pinned: a group that **loads** but no longer seats this identity is *examined* (an eviction that already landed — counting it as unknown would refuse the restore of every user ever removed from one group, permanently) — the whole-KV door's answer only: the mid-session per-group door, which has no sibling groups to carry the identity question, refuses a blank seat (`devices.md` § Cross-device MLS group-state sync → *A sibling-joined group is adopted mid-session by a targeted import*); and a refusal on the *unexaminable* arm additionally **holds the provider save gate** (`MlsStateSync::hold_provider_saves`), because unlike the foreign-seat arm — where the successor's first save is *supposed* to take the path back — overwriting a snapshot this session merely could not read would destroy state that may well be its own. It is the succession-time instance of the restore chokepoint's standing invariant (`restore_into` swaps the whole KV, so a late restore "wipes groups joined since launch" — `restore_and_wire_with_retry`), applied to a snapshot another identity captured. ⚠ **And the question is asked by the restore door itself, not by its callers** (hardened 2026-08-30): rule (1) is an absolute over the *snapshot*, so a per-caller check is only ever as complete as the caller census — and that census failed, leaving the **mid-session** door (`MlsStateSync::resync_provider`, driven by `resync_channel` whenever the inbound loop meets an own-leaf commit this device did not author) swapping the very bytes the launch door had just refused, for the whole span the launch deliberately leaves them at the path. `ProviderReplica::restore_into` now takes the engine, reads the identity off it, and restores nothing unless the verdict is clean, returning the verdict; the un-checked swap survives only as a test-gated `restore_into_unchecked` that no production build compiles, so a third door cannot repeat the miss. **What a refusal costs is still each door's own answer**: the launch holds the provider save gate (above), while the resync returns "no replica restored", which its caller maps to `CommitApplyOutcome::Stalled` — Rule-2-safe, the ingest cursor stopping *before* the commit so nothing is folded or forked. That stall is self-healing on the foreign arm (the successor's first save takes the path back by CAS, and the next resync reads clean) and lasts the session on the unexaminable arm (saves are held precisely so unreadable bytes are not destroyed, so nothing replaces the snapshot) — the strictly better half of that trade, since the alternative is swapping the engine's whole KV for bytes whose leaf is unknown. A refused snapshot is also never recorded as the save baseline. **(2) History slices and thread bindings always restore.** Readable history — own-message plaintext, the folded reaction state, the channel↔thread binding — is the successor's; only the leaf is not. The re-seal keeps re-keying the predecessor's `provider` too, because it is the channel list the history re-seal walks and it must open under the successor's key for the CAS that replaces it. **(3) The ceremony device publishes the successor's own `provider` before the switch** (`MlsStateSync::publish_provider` — ungated, un-merged: the merge would re-import the retired leaf). Web has no other durable home for the successor's join (an in-memory engine; the page reloads as the successor), and it is what the successor's *other* devices restore from — the successor's leaf reaches them through the ordinary single-leaf state-sync, exactly as any second device's. Native carries the join in SQLite as well, so a native device that meets the predecessor's snapshot first (a crash before the publish, a sibling seat signing in first) skips it under rule (1) and publishes its own at the first flush. **Declared residual:** a channel the successor never joins keeps its history slice sealed to the predecessor's key until a retry joins it — the re-seal walks the successor's channel list once the publish lands. Pins: `fauna-mls` `state_replica` (the leaf a snapshot seats, and the re-seating demonstrated at the engine layer), `fauna-client-mls-sync` `orchestration` (the launch restore over a real in-process join, then a fresh successor engine restoring the published snapshot), and end to end `test_succession_sweep_retry.py`'s finishing tail — one press, every group re-pointed, the button retired, the wire grown.
