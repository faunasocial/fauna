- **The band (D2 — adopted as recommended).** A supervised account carries an **age band** — `U13 | 13-15 | 16-17 | 18+`, the same bands the store age APIs use — as per-account nest state on the guardianship plane, set **at admission by the admitting guardian** exactly where the supervised designation itself is set (§ The guardianship link; the admission carry grows the band beside `guardian_actor`). The band is a **defaults dial, never a fourth enforcement pillar**: it selects the guardian-policy *defaults* offered at admission — content-pillar floors, reach knobs, and the dynamic-features guardian tier ([`../architecture/dynamic-features.md`](../architecture/dynamic-features.md) § The rule-setter model, tier 4) — which the guardian then edits per-knob exactly as today; enforcement stays where the three pillars put it. An account with no guardianship link is `18+` by construction (the unsupervised-equivalent-defaults rule, § Wire & data shape). The band auto-graduates nobody (§ Graduation & transfer — an age-based automatic path stays future).
- **Provenance (D5).** The band records *how it was established*: `attested-ios | attested-android | guardian-asserted | none`. A guardian-set band is `guardian-asserted`; an open-mode self-registration is `none` (below); the attested values require the platform attestation below. Admission policy and audits key on provenance; enforcement never does — the band's value, not its pedigree, drives the defaults.
- **Attested age-at-admission (D5 — adopted).** The mobile app may carry the platform's age signal into the admission application, hardened by platform attestation: **Apple App Attest** / **Google Play Integrity** over `{nest-issued nonce, age band, application id}` — the verifying signature is Apple's/Google's, **never a key embedded in the Fauna app** (a key in a publicly shipped artifact is extractable and hardens nothing). The claim **corroborates the admitting adult's judgment and never overrides it**: an `18+` attestation must not silently override a guardian's judgment that the applicant is a child (hand-me-down and parent's-own-account devices make false-adult the common failure), and coverage is honestly partial (iOS 26+ / Play-serviced devices only; the platform APIs are declinable outside mandated regions; on android only the **Play-distributed build types** carry the two Google client libraries — the `foss` F-Droid / direct-download artifact links no Play code and makes no claim, [`../architecture/installers/android.md`](../architecture/installers/android.md) § Goal). **Play Integrity plumbing — RULED 2026-08-24 (user, closing the question the memo's Addendum item 7 left open): nest-local, no relay.** App Attest chains verify nest-locally against Apple's published root; Play Integrity *standard* tokens are Google-server-decode-only, so the android app makes a **classic** request (the mode Google reserves for infrequent high-value actions — account admission is exactly that) whose nonce is the **SHA-256 of the claim message** — Google's logs see a digest, never the band or the actor id, exactly as Apple sees only the App Attest client-data hash — and the nest opens the verdict **locally** with the app's Play Console **response-encryption keys pinned as nest constants** (`age_attest::FAUNA_ANDROID_PLAY_APPS`, one pair per pinned app — Play Console issues them per app — each `None` until the org downloads it — an unarmed arm is one the nest *cannot check*, the next bullet) — structurally the iOS arm: sovereign, works out of the box, no Fauna-org service in any admission. **Why those keys may live in a public tree:** Google signs the verdict with a key only Google holds; the pinned pair only *opens the envelope*, so publishing it costs verdict *secrecy* (a holder of a Fauna token can read it), never authenticity. Accepted costs, deliberately: the classic-request quota is fleet-wide per app (~10k/day across every nest; admissions are rare, and Google raises it on request); a key rotation is a nest release that swaps both constants. Rejected: a Fauna-org verification relay (the memo's lean — the org would have to operate it, every self-hosted nest would phone the org per android admission, and an outage would block android attested admissions fleet-wide), and distributing org Google-Cloud credentials to nests (a real secret, unlike the response keys). The verdict checks the nest makes are listed on `age_attest::verify_play_integrity`; the nonce contract is `fauna_protocol::age::age_claim_signed_message`'s.
- **An attestation the nest cannot check (ruled 2026-10-01 under D5 and [`../architecture/version-compatibility.md`](../architecture/version-compatibility.md) I2).** *Cannot check* is not *failed the check*. The nest sorts an attested claim into exactly three outcomes, by what **this nest build** holds for the attestation's `platform`. **(1) Verified** — the nest holds that platform's verifier (iOS: the pinned App ID; android: the pinned response keys) and the platform's signature checks out over the claim message and a live nest-minted nonce: the claim earns `attested-*`. **(2) Failed the check** — the nest holds the verifier and the attestation does not pass (an unknown, spent or expired nonce; a chain, binding or verdict that does not verify): the **whole admission is refused**, `fauna.account.age_attestation_invalid`, never downgraded — a claim presented as attested that fails a check the nest can run is tampering, a replay, or a transient the app heals by re-minting, and a silent downgrade would hide all three. **(3) Cannot check** — the nest holds **no verifier for that platform**: the platform is unarmed in this build, or its token is one this build does not know (a platform a newer app speaks): the attestation is **ignored unread** (no field of it parsed, its nonce not consumed) and the claim is handled **exactly as the declared-only claim it would be without it** — provenance `none` on the request row, no band row from a registration, the store-says-minor refusal still keyed on the band — and the admission proceeds. *Why this weakens nothing:* whoever sends the claim decides whether an attestation is attached, so ignoring an unverifiable one grants precisely what omitting it grants. What fails closed is the **provenance** — `attested-*` is never recorded without a verified platform signature — and the **require-knob**, which only outcome 1 satisfies: with the knob on, a cannot-check claim is refused as every declared-only claim is, `fauna.account.age_verification_required` ([`../architecture/nest/public-mode.md`](../architecture/nest/public-mode.md) § Registration Modes → *Age at registration*), so an admin who turns it on admits exactly the platforms their nest can verify. Refusing the admission instead bought no assurance and broke two cases in which nothing is forged: every Play-distributed android admission while the response keys are unset, and any app whose platform is armed talking to a nest built before the arming (I2 — the newer peer degrades, it does not fail). **The nest says what it can check, and the app attaches only that.** `fauna.account.age_nonce`'s reply carries the additive `attestation_platforms` — the `AgeAttestation.platform` tokens this nest holds a verifier for, computed from the same arming the verifier reads (a pinned value that fails to decode counts as unarmed, on both sides of that one function) — riding the ceremony's own reply rather than the connect-time capability set, because the fact is needed exactly when the app holds the nonce; an absent or empty list reads as **verifies nothing**. The shared onboarding machine is the one enforcement point: it puts an attestation on the wire only when its nonce is the one last minted from the nest the admission goes to and that reply listed the attestation's platform, and otherwise sends the claim declared-only. The mobile glue reads the same list off the nonce it was handed to skip its platform round (android's classic-request quota is fleet-wide) — an economy, never the guarantee. Together the two halves cover every pairing of releases: a nest from this ruling on degrades whatever a blind or newer app attaches, and an app from this ruling on attaches nothing to a nest that predates the list and would have refused it. Reading absence as *verifies nothing* costs one thing — a nest built between the iOS arming (2026-09-30) and this ruling verifies iOS without saying so and is sent declared-only claims; no shipped iOS build could attest in that window (the entitlements were pending, § Implementation status today), so no working pair regresses. **No app compiles its platform's arming in as a gate:** arming is a property of the nest being addressed, never of the app's own build, so android gains no client-side key-presence bit, and `fauna_ffi::ios_age_attestation_app_id` stays only what the signed message needs — its `application_id`. **The onboarding notice follows the claim that will be sent** (§ App surface → *Age-band surfaces*).
- **A half-armed android arm (ruled 2026-10-11 under D5 and the cannot-check ruling above).** Play Console issues the response-encryption keys **per app**, so the main app and Fauna Kids are pinned as separate entries and can be armed one at a time; *armed* is per pinned app, and the platform-level advertisement cannot say which. **`attestation_platforms` lists `android` when at least one pinned android app is armed** — listing it only when both are would leave the armed app's families declared-only for no reason. The nest picks the entry by **which armed key opens the verdict token's envelope** (the key unwrap is integrity-checked, so another app's key does not open it) — never by a package the client names. A token **no armed key opens while some pinned app is unarmed** is **outcome (3), cannot check**: it may be the unarmed app's genuine verdict, which the nest can no more read than an unarmed platform's, so it is ignored, its nonce not consumed, and the claim handled as declared-only — granting exactly what omitting the attestation grants, as above. Only the envelope's structure is read to decide this (its five parts and pinned key algorithm; a token off that profile is not a verdict from either app and is refused). Once **every** pinned app is armed, a token none of their keys opens is **outcome (2)**, refused. A token an armed key opens is that app's to verify in full — both verdict package fields and the signed message must name it — and any failure there is outcome (2) whatever the other entry's arming.

- **The store-law developer duties — Significant Change re-consent, the store age rating, the revocation notification (ruled by the user 2026-10-19 at decision gate D-S1…D-S8, every recommendation taken as written; D-S5's and D-S8's legal-review riders were decided without counsel on 2026-10-21 under public-texts ruling 20 — items (5) and (8)).** The platform facts (Apple's PermissionKit, DeclaredAgeRange and StoreKit; Google's Play Age Signals and Play Console) are sourced in that memo's § 1; what the state laws *require* is **unverified input throughout** — nothing in this bullet asserts compliance, and every reading of the law is collected as the legal-review items L1…L5 of item (8). **(1) A device-local store-consent gate, apart from the guardian plane (D-S1).** A parent's consent to a significant change is a store construct about one device: the store knows the parent, asks them, and tells the app on that device the answer. So the two store-distributed mobile apps carry a **store-consent gate** that keys on the **platform's own signal for the person on this device** — iOS `AgeRangeService.requiredRegulatoryFeatures`, android a Play `significantChangeStatus` — and never on `supervised_by` or the band (D2, D3): no nest state, no wire field, no org server, no compat obligation. Fauna hard-codes no state list, cohort date or age threshold — a new state, a stayed law or a moved cohort changes what the platform reports, not what Fauna ships. A Fauna ward outside the covered cohorts sees no gate (their guardian has Fauna's own tools); an unsupervised account on a device whose store reports a covered minor does see it; the same account on the five other apps is untouched — the declared limit of a store construct. **(2) The significant-change ledger (D-S2).** What counts as significant is **one hard-coded catalog in shared Rust** — a Rust constant, bucket 1 of the configuration invariant, never a file or an admin knob — of entries `{ id (monotonic), effective_from (a UTC date), description (an i18n key in `en.yaml`) }`, read by both mobile apps; the description is the one sentence a parent reads in Apple's dialog, in Play Console and on Fauna's own surface. **The user appends an entry at a release**, against four criteria — *data*: the release changes what categories of personal data leave the device for anyone other than the user's own nests; *rating*: the store questionnaire's answers or the rating itself change; *monetization*: a purchase or payment surface becomes reachable in a store-distributed build; *functionality*: the release adds a new class of feature or a new reach surface, not an improvement to an existing one — and a release tripping none appends nothing, so nobody is asked anything. Rejected: *every release is significant* (it would nag every covered family on every bugfix, and Play caps submissions at 3 per 90 days); *only a rating change is significant* (the one trigger Apple names, but the statute as read lists four). **(3) The whole-app lock at launch and foreground (D-S3 — ruled over the per-feature alternative).** At launch and on every return to the foreground, before any page — not in onboarding: a first install is already consented, the store obtained the parent's consent to the download of this very version — one **shared-Rust verdict**, fed by thin platform glue through injectable seams (the FaunaKit `StoreAgeClaimRound` shape, unit-tested without a device), runs in order: *does the duty apply?* (no → no gate, ever, for this person; a `foss` android build links no Play code and never gates — absent by data); *is anything pending?* (iOS: the ledger head is past this install's device-local `consented_through`, written at first run as the then-current head; android: an entry with `effective_from` on or before today and later than Play's `significantChangeApprovalDate`, or a `PENDING`/`DECLINED` status; plus the rating trigger of item (4)); *parental consent required* → the **store-consent lock**, one full-screen blocking surface — the `screen-time-lock` precedent, a conditionally-present global — carrying what changed (the entry's description) and the state (*waiting for your parent* / *your parent said no*), with on iOS a button that sends the system ask (PermissionKit's `AskCenter`, the answer arriving on the device) and on android no ask at all — Play raises the parent's request itself, so the surface says approval is requested through Google Play and re-checks on foreground; approved → `consented_through` advances to the head and the lock lifts; several pending entries coalesce into one ask. *Adult acknowledgment required* (iOS only) → the system sheet `showSignificantUpdateAcknowledgment`, no Fauna surface and no lock. No sign-out affordance: the lock is about the person the store knows on this device, not the Fauna account, so signing out would lift nothing. **Whole-app, not a per-feature hold,** because the statute as read frames the consent on the minor's *continued use of the application*, a per-feature hold needs a runtime device-local feature mask the product does not have ([`../architecture/dynamic-features.md`](../architecture/dynamic-features.md) § The rule-setter model — the store tier is compile-time, and a change that is not a registry member has no gate to hang on), and one surface is one thing to test; the accepted cost is that a covered child cannot open Fauna on that device until the parent taps approve, a few times a year at most. **Build rule: the mechanism ships at least one release before its first ledger entry** — an install with no stored `consented_through` cannot tell a fresh download of the version carrying entry N (consented) from an update into it (not consented); with the mechanism already on every device, every install has a baseline before any entry exists. ⚖ Whether a *pending* consent permits a grace period is legal-review item L3; the design assumes not. **(4) `AppStore.ageRatingCode` is the gate's runtime rating-change trigger, and nothing else (D-S4).** On iOS 26.2+ the app reads it at launch and compares it with the last value this install saw (device-local; the first read records a baseline silently); a change is a significant change of the whole app — the one trigger Apple and the statute both name — and enters the round of item (3) as a synthetic pending entry with a fixed description, for people the platform says the duty applies to and nobody else. It earns its place because a rating can change **without a Fauna release** (Apple re-rates on its own schedule) and a ledger compiled into a binary cannot know that. Deliberately not driven by it: no guardian- or user-facing readout (the integer's values are undocumented, and a guardian on tui or web could not see it anyway); no gate on the kids flavor (eligibility keys on *supervised*, the kids-app bullet); never an input to the band or its defaults (D2). Android has no equivalent property: a rating change there is an org-submitted Play Console change like any other (item (6)). **(5) The App Store server notification on consent revocation is NOT adopted (D-S5 — decided 2026-10-21 without counsel under public-texts ruling 20: the memo's L2 question is answered by the organisation's own reading below, recorded as a residual and never asserted compliant).** `RESCIND_CONSENT` is delivered to one HTTPS URL per app record, on a server the developer runs; acting on it would take three things that do not exist — an org endpoint, a registry mapping each install's store identity to the nest it signed in to (every iOS app reporting itself to the org to populate it), and a channel from the org to that nest to suspend the account: the org relay D5 rejected, with one thing worse — **the registry would be a central, org-held record of which minors installed Fauna and which nest each uses**, data that exists nowhere today and that the product's whole shape exists to avoid holding. What already happens without it: Apple itself prevents a revoked app from launching, so the revocation is enforced where it was made, by the party holding the consent, on the artifact the law is about — and nothing is left on that device for Fauna to poll (no on-device read of a revocation is documented, and a revoked app does not launch). The honest residual: the Fauna *account* stays usable from a browser, a desktop or another device after a store-level revocation — the same boundary D3 states for the store signal itself: the store governs its artifact on its device; the account-level acts are the guardian's (the policy, graduation) and the admin's (disable the account), in the app, on the nest the family chose. **The App Store Connect notification URL stays unset.** Recorded with the ruling: if Apple ships an on-device read of consent state, it feeds the lock of item (3) as one more input and this non-adoption of the *server* channel stands; Play's documentation names no developer notification for a Family Link revocation, so there is no android twin to rule on. **(6) Android adopts the Play analogue on the same ledger and surface (D-S6).** The change is submitted in Play Console on the app's *Age signals* page with the entry's `effective_from` and its en-US description — **one off-machine act per ledger entry, on the release checklist beside the entry itself** (a ledger entry whose Play submission was forgotten leaves android asking nobody); the app reads `significantChangeStatus` / `significantChangeApprovalDate` from the Play Age Signals library the store-age arm already links; Play-distributed build types only, the `foss` build makes no claim and shows no gate. The five other apps declare the absence (a store construct, like the kids door — `declared_absence`, never `skip_unbuilt`), so tui does not lead this journey: **shared Rust (ledger + verdict + message) first, then android, then iOS** — the D-K3 order, for the same reasons. **(7) Element IDs (D-S7 — user-approved 2026-10-19 under rule A, spelled as proposed, conditional on the whole-app lock of item (3), which holds):** `store-consent-lock`, `store-consent-lock-message`, `store-consent-lock-ask-button` — § App surface → *Store-consent surfaces*. **(8) The first ledger entry is the owner's own act (D-S8 — decided 2026-10-21 without counsel under public-texts ruling 20, replacing the legal-review gate the memo's § 6 proposed).** The first entry — the first significant change shipped to a store — is gated on nothing but the owner's significance call at that release (item (2), the release attempt's significant-change step); the step's record states, the first time, the reading the entry rests on — L1 the four significance criteria, L3 the whole-app lock while consent is pending (item (3)), L5 which accounts are covered — as the organisation's own assessment, never asserted compliant and re-decidable at any later release; L2 is item (5)'s reading; L4, outside these duties — a law-firm summary of the Texas act says store-provided personal data must be deleted after verification, which touches the already-built attested age band resting on the nest (D5) — is recorded as a residual reading: D5 stands and nothing waits on it.
