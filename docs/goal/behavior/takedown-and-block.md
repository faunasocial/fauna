## Legal takedown (the legal-compulsion carve-out)

The one nest-wide social-content removal, non-discretionary and legally bounded (Q5 decision owner: the frame; this doc owns the **mechanism**). Built for posts + conversations (2026-07-05, hardened 2026-07-07).

- **Wire:** `fauna.moderation.legal_takedown { content_id, content_type, legal_reference, restore }` — Admin-only (`bridge_method_allowlist.rs`). A takedown **requires** a non-empty `legal_reference`; structurally it can never be a silent or discretionary lever: every takedown produces the **transparency triple** — a visible tombstone, `fauna.moderation.appeal` by `content_id`, and a permanent `moderation:legal-takedown` audit row. (All three legs are built: the appeal affordance lands on the author's moderation queue — `appeal-button` on each nest-issued row, the reason form, the recorded-for-review outcome line — on **tui** since 2026-09-21, then web, linux, android and windows (macOS and iOS follow in the trickle-down); the nest-side gate that makes the handle honest is § Errors & edge cases' appeal bullet.) **Tombstone-not-delete:** `restore=true` clears the flag and re-serves (the takedown row stays as additive history) + audits the overturn.
- **Posts** (`content_type="post"`): one atomic SQLite transaction writes a nullable `content_meta.legal_takedown_ref` flag (schema v3, additive; the flag `UPDATE` asserted to match exactly one row) + an `ObligationAction::TakenDown` (=7) row (surfaced to the author via `fauna.moderation.actions`) + the audit row. Serve: `get_post_core` withholds the body from **every** viewer (incl. author/admin) and `PostGetReply.legal_takedown` carries the tombstone reference; feeds exclude the post (`db/feeds.rs`); the self-scoped author enumeration `fauna.posts.list` (`content-index-ingest.md` § Ingest triggers, v1) withholds the body text too, returning the row with an empty `body` — it is an **authenticated body-serving path**, so it is bound by the every-viewer rule exactly as `get` is, and the row is still enumerated because dropping it would make that corpus listing silently partial. ⚠ The authenticated paths are a different enumeration from the anonymous off-box one below: `PUBLIC_POST_SERVABLE` is scoped to an off-box audience and is deliberately *not* the predicate here, because quarantine and `gated_tier` are author-visible. **Any new path that serves post body content to its author belongs in this bullet on the day it is written.** **The HLS manifest doors serve post body content and are enrolled here (2026-09-09).** `GET /api/v1/video/{post_id}/master.m3u8` and `…/{variant}.m3u8` render a video post's segment list — every variant, every segment duration and an absolute URL per segment — and had read it through the deliberately flag-blind body primitive, so they answered 200 for a post this bullet withholds. They now route through `get_post_core` like the post read itself, inheriting both gates in its order: a taken-down post answers **451, no body** (the answer the HTTP twin and the blob door already give — a playlist carries no tombstone render of its own, so the reference reaches the client through the post's own read), and a quarantined one is author/admin-only, which is why these routes take an optional bearer and are no longer unconditionally public. The gate belongs on the serve path and **not** in the body primitive: `moderation_withhold` reads *flagged* posts through that primitive to compute the blob withhold set, so teaching it to withhold would silently empty that set. **The rest of that primitive's serve paths are enrolled here too (2026-09-10), and the enrolment is now checked rather than asserted.** A partition of every production caller of the flag-blind post-body reads (`segments::post::load_post_body` and the reads beneath it) found five more serve paths, four of them ungated: (1) **the author's web site** — the render turns each published post into `post/{slug}.html`, the index and `feed.xml` for the open internet, so its enumeration now drops any post a moderation flag withholds, and a takedown or its overturn re-renders the site before the reply (nothing re-renders on a schedule, so a page rendered before the takedown would otherwise keep serving it); a render that fails there clears the author's rendered site instead — derived output, and the clear leaves its restore owed; the takedown's own transaction also records that render as owed, so a nest that stops before rendering drains it at boot ([`web-content-hosting.md`](web-content-hosting.md) § Routing, render, serving → *A revoke is durable* and *A blanked site is owed its restore* own the mechanism) — and the overturn re-renders because the author's publish link is the consent and a takedown never withdrew it, the ActivityPub outbox's pull-surface behaviour; (2) **the account export's `posts` domain** — the author's own archive, so only the takedown binds (quarantine is author-visible): the entry stays, following `fauna.posts.list`, with an empty `data_hex` and the citation in `legal_takedown_ref`; (3) **`fauna.moderation.train`** — the model it trains is derived from the body and read back by the caller, so it reads through `get_post_core`, the takedown gate ahead of quarantine; (4) **Search** — every hit is a snippet of the indexed body, so the public phase applies all three moderation flags and the author's own phase the takedown alone; and (5) the **federation feed query**, which withheld a takedown but handed a peer naming an author that author's quarantined and suppressed posts, now applies all three; and (6) **the federated-follow approval backfill** (enrolled 2026-10-17), which queues an author's newest posts to a newly approved subscriber's home nest and enumerates them through the same fragment ([`federated-follow.md`](federated-follow.md) § Decision 1 owns the backfill). Every SQL gate among them is one named fragment, `db::public_servability::MODERATION_SERVABLE` — the moderation half of `PUBLIC_POST_SERVABLE`, which composes it — so the feed reads, the federation query, Search and the render cannot disagree about which flags exist. **The enforcement:** `segments::post`'s `every_flag_blind_post_body_read_is_partitioned` walks every production call of those reads and fails on a caller nobody has classified as a serve path naming its gate or a reader saying why nothing leaves the process, and on a serve path whose production body no longer calls the gate it names — so "on the day it is written" is a gate, not a promise. **The export's `post` segment pairs are enrolled too (2026-09-10)** — the `include_blobs` leg every app requests, which copies each `__post` segment file into the archive verbatim and so carried a taken-down body by a door none of those reads touch: a pair cannot be verbatim and withhold one record, so a `post` pair holding a currently taken-down post is withheld whole and declared in the manifest (`segment_store.withheld`, reason `legal_takedown`) for exactly as long as the flag stands — the ruling and its rejected alternatives are [`../architecture/account-data-plane.md`](../architecture/account-data-plane.md) § Nest-side requirements item 1, *Payload stores* decision (6). **And the withhold survives the author's own DELETE of a taken-down post (2026-09-11).** Every post-side withhold above keys on `content_meta.legal_takedown_ref`, and the delete verb removes exactly that row while tombstoning only the mirror — so compaction kept the compelled bytes and the next `include_blobs` export shipped the pair again, with the blob door re-serving the record's attachments until the GC sweep. **The delete is not the door to close**: it destroys where these verbs gate, it is the *user always controls their data* affordance ([`../ui/feed.md`](../ui/feed.md) § Post deletion — “three verbs, three owners, no overlap”), and the same core runs under account deletion and admin eviction, which a refusal would wedge forever on one taken-down post. So the FACT moves instead: the delete records the post, its citation and the blob digests it named in `legal_takedown_deleted_posts` (schema v64) before removing anything, and the export's pair set and the blob door's rebuild read that beside the live flag. It has to be captured there and nowhere later, for two independent reasons: the permanent `TakenDown` obligation row cannot answer the question afterwards — **an overturn writes no counter-row**, so *taken down → deleted* and *taken down → restored → deleted* are indistinguishable in that ledger, and keying a withhold to it would withhold a whole segment pair forever for a post legitimately restored first — and the blob digests are unreadable once the record is tombstoned, which is exactly why the blob rebuild's record walk cannot recover them. The row is permanent and inert: once compaction has reclaimed the record and the GC dropped the blobs, it withholds bytes that are already gone. Unlike the derived blob set below this one **is** a floor, and necessarily — after the flag row is deleted the box holds nothing left to recompute from. Federation **discloses** the withholding (HTTP twin → 451; WS peer fetch returns the additive `FedPostGetReply.legal_takedown` marker with an empty body) — deliberate, unlike policy-quarantine's existence-hiding `not_found` (ratified 2026-07-06). **The tombstone row is registered on all three per-actor axes (2026-09-11)** — retained at account deletion (the same delete core that writes it runs there, before the purge walk), moved by an identity succession (the export's withhold set is keyed by the exporting actor, and the corpus it withholds from moves), and exported verbatim (every column is the author's own compelled fact); the deletion and export reasons are the registry entry's (`db/actor_tables.rs`), the succession half is [`succession-repoint-axis.md`](succession-repoint-axis.md) § The declared re-point axis. **And the opposite ordering — deleted, then the order arrives (2026-09-13) — is accepted, not refused.** Before this, `legal_takedown_core` resolved a post's author (and its very existence) through the `content` row a prior delete had already removed, so a compelled order against an already-deleted post was answered `not_found`: no transparency triple, nothing recorded, and the compelled bytes rode the export pair and the blob door exactly as an ordinary deleted post's would until compaction and the next GC sweep caught up. The post arm now resolves the author from the segment's tombstone-inclusive scope lookup (`segments::post::lookup_scope_by_post_id_including_tombstoned`) and reads the digests through the matching tombstone-inclusive body read (`read_body_by_post_id_including_tombstoned` — a reader only, closed and test-enforced alongside the flag-blind primitives above) whenever the ordinary `content`-row lookup comes back empty; a post compaction has already reclaimed still answers `not_found`, same as one never stored. The write lands the obligation row (the author's queue still shows "taken_down") + the audit row + `legal_takedown_deleted_posts` in one transaction — never the `content_meta` flag (no row exists to carry it) and never the legs the delete already ran on its way past (the ATProto retraction witness, the Nostr kind-5, the web re-render). `restore=true` against such a row is audit-only: the floor stays exactly as permanent and inert as the rest of this bullet already rules it, since the bytes left with the author's own delete regardless of which order the compelled order arrived in.
- **The off-box publish surfaces withhold too (closed 2026-07-29; the Nostr relay joined them 2026-08-04).** The ActivityPub outbox + note dereference, the ATProto public-projection stream, and the **Nostr materializer** are serve paths that *publish to third-party public networks*, so a takedown must stop them or it is silently defeated where it matters most. **The Nostr materializer was the last to adopt the predicate (2026-08-04)** — `unmaterialized_exposed_post_ids` now gates on the same constant, pinned per-flag by `nostr::store`'s `a_flagged_post_is_never_materialized_onto_the_public_relay`. All four now apply **one** named predicate — `db::public_servability::PUBLIC_POST_SERVABLE`, the single owner of "may this post be served to an anonymous, off-box audience" — which gates on `legal_takedown_ref`, `quarantined` and `suppressed` exactly as the feed read does, and additionally requires the `content_meta` row to **exist**, so a post whose best-effort index write failed — reachable, see § State & data shape's `put_post` note — is withheld rather than read as ungated. Before this, each path carried its own copy of an incomplete filter; the shared constant is what stops them drifting apart again. **The two create-time push legs apply the predicate's twin (added 2026-09-07).** The ActivityPub `Create`-push and the Bluesky write-through publish a post the instant it lands, on the decoded post, before any `content`/`content_meta` row exists for a SQL predicate to filter — so each had carried its own one-arm copy (`gated` only) for four months, and ruling 1 of `archive-import.md` (an import is served on Fauna, never re-broadcast), landed in the predicate alone, would not have reached them. They now apply `db::public_servability::publishable_off_box_at_create`, kept beside the SQL: the two arms decidable from the post itself, `gated` and the archive-platform `source`. The moderation flags have no create-time meaning (a post cannot carry a takedown at the instant it is created), so the twin deliberately covers only those two; an arm added to the SQL that *has* a create-time meaning is added to the twin in the same change. **Retraction of an already-projected post (closed 2026-07-29).** The predicate alone stops only *future* publication, so a takedown now also writes the **same `tombstone/post` witness a user delete writes** — in the same transaction as the flag, obligation and audit rows, dated at the takedown instant and carrying the post's digest. The ATProto bridge's built retraction path resolves that digest against its record map and issues a real `deleteRecord`, so a post published to Bluesky *before* its takedown is withdrawn rather than left standing. The witness id is derived from the post digest, so it is the same row a later delete would write (at most one retraction per post), and a retraction the bridge has already applied re-reads as a clean no-op. **On the fediverse side, delivered Notes cannot be recalled at all** — an inherent limit, the same one § Legal takedown already records for the conversation plane. **On the Nostr side a takedown retracts — ruled + built 2026-08-04 (refutable while no deployment is live): it emits a NIP-09 kind-5 deletion request through the same leg a self-service delete uses** (`nostr::propagate_post_delete`: signed with the author's deposited key at the established signing position, NIP-09-applied to the nest's own relay store so the derived event stops being served locally, broadcast live, and pushed to the author's configured relays; the key-less degradation arm as for deletes). The takedown handler triggers it immediately, and the sync worker's tick reconciles any takedown whose propagation a crash swallowed — which also healed takedowns predating the ruling. Three grounds, in order: (1) **it is the already-ratified derived-event lifecycle honored on a second trigger, not a new admin power over the author's key** — a derived Nostr event must not outlive the post it was derived from ([`../ui/feed.md`](../ui/feed.md) § State & data shape → *Post deletion*), a takedown ends the post's servable life, and the kind-5's `content` stays empty: it asserts only that the source is gone, never a motive, and never leaks the legal reference. (2) **Parity across the off-box surfaces**: the same transaction's witness already drives a real ATProto `deleteRecord`; retract-where-expressible is the one consistent position, leaving the fediverse's structural inability as the sole exception. (3) **Under-publishing is the safe failure direction** (the overturn ruling's own ground) — a deletion request can only reduce distribution. **Honest reach, recorded so it is never overclaimed:** NIP-09 leaves honoring a kind-5 to each relay's discretion, and copies already fetched by clients or mirrored by non-honoring relays persist; what a takedown may claim is exactly that the nest's own relay no longer serves the derived event, and that a signed deletion request was broadcast to live subscribers and pushed to the author's configured relays — nothing stronger. **The overturn takes the same position as the ATProto restore below, enforced against inverted mechanics:** Nostr materialization is absence-driven (a servable post with no outbound map row materializes on the next sweep), so where the projection stream's watermark makes non-republication the ATProto default, the sweep makes *re*-publication the Nostr default — the materializer input query therefore excludes any post bearing a permanent `TakenDown` obligation row (written in the takedown transaction, kept on restore as additive history), so no-auto-republish holds by construction on every materialization path (the expose toggle, the periodic sweep, a re-expose after unlink) — stronger than the ATProto corollary, where a full re-derive does honor the cleared flag. The author's recourse is unchanged: posting again is a fresh post with a fresh digest, materialized normally. **Quarantine and suppression still only gate future materialization and never retract** — reversible policy levers must not take a permanent-intent act their own reversal cannot undo off-box. Proof: tier_3 `tests/nostr_legal_takedown_kind5.rs` (immediate leg incl. empty-content pin + restore-does-not-rematerialize + reconcile heal).

**An overturn does not re-publish off-box — a ruling, not an inherent limit (2026-07-29; refutable while no deployment is live).** `restore=true` clears the flag and the post re-serves on Fauna, but **no re-publication witness is written**, so the projection stream — whose watermark is long past the post's own row — never re-projects it. Three reasons, in order: (1) **a re-publication is a publication**, and publishing to a searchable, cache-persistent external network is exactly what [`atproto-pds-bridge.md`](atproto-pds-bridge.md) § Projection & backfill makes a deliberate user opt-in — an admin's overturn is not the user's consent to publish again. (2) For a legally compelled mechanism the safe failure direction is **under**-publishing: a bug in an auto-republish path re-publishes legally compelled content, which cannot be recalled once off-box. (3) The user keeps the recourse that matters — they can post again. **State this as a choice, not a limit:** the record key is derived deterministically from the post, so a restore witness *would* return the record to its original address; building one is an option this ruling declines, not a capability the protocol lacks. Two honest corollaries: the overturn *is* honoured by any future full re-derive of the bridge's (explicitly re-derivable) store, which re-walks from the user's consent floor where the cleared flag now passes the predicate; and consuming AppViews index at ingest time, so even a re-publication would surface as fresh activity rather than restore the post in place.
- **The linked Bluesky account's cross-post is retracted too — ruled 2026-10-21, built the same day; refutable by the owner.** A member who turned on `write_through` ([`bridges.md`](bridges.md) § Cross-posting) has the nest write each post's text into their *external* Bluesky repo with the OAuth session the nest holds for it, and that record's life is already tied to the post's by a ratified rule: a post must not outlive its derivations, the write-through record named among them ([`../ui/feed.md`](../ui/feed.md) § Post deletion → *Propagation*), which is why the author's own delete runs the delete leg (`bluesky::spawn_write_through_delete`, from `routes::delete_post_core`). A takedown ends the post's servable life, so the same leg runs on it as the second trigger — the Nostr ruling's ground (1) above, where the nest already signs a kind-5 with the member's deposited key and pushes it to the member's own configured relays. Three grounds, in order: (1) **it is the author's delete leg on a second trigger, not a new admin power over the member's account** — the admin acts on the post, never on the account: the leg touches the one record this nest itself wrote, does nothing the member's own delete does not, and carries no reason (a `deleteRecord` has no body, as the kind-5's `content` stays empty), so the legal reference never leaves the nest. (2) **Parity across the off-box surfaces, and across the two identity backings**: retract-where-expressible leaves the fediverse's structural inability as the sole exception, and Bluesky expresses the delete; and an external repo and a Fauna-hosted PDS are alternative backings for one member's ATProto presence ([`bridges.md`](bridges.md) § Bluesky bridge, the two-directions note), the hosted backing's copy already retracted by the witness above — a recall that stopped at the external backing would be one a member routes around by their choice of backing, which nothing justifies. (3) **Under-publishing is the safe failure direction** (the overturn ruling's own ground). The overturn takes the ATProto restore's position: nothing re-creates the record — the member posts again, and `write_through` cross-posts that. **Honest reach, so it is never overclaimed:** the leg recalls the copy THIS nest wrote with the session THIS nest holds; a copy the author's home nest wrote through their account there — a takedown served against a projected copy, *The author homed on another nest* below — is that nest's, and the delivered obligation row asks it to retract nothing (a recorded limit, not reopened here); and what Bluesky's AppViews and third-party caches keep after a `deleteRecord` is theirs, as a relay that ignores a kind-5 is. **Mechanism:** `legal_takedown_core` fires the leg beside its Nostr one, non-fatal; the hourly re-drive (`post_delete_redrive`) chases a mapping whose post is gone *or* carries `legal_takedown_ref` (`crosspost_delete_owed`) — the takedown writes the same `tombstone/post` witness the sweep reads the author from — and a restore clears the flag, after which a remaining mapping waits for the author's own delete.
- **Conversations** (`content_type="conversation"`): **best-effort MLS relay-withholding** — the flag is `segment_records.legal_takedown_ref`, keyed on the message's record Cid, and the withhold-on-`Some` check lives in the conv read primitives — `segments::conv::read_after_seq` (single-channel: `channel.fetch`, `federation.channel.fetch`) and its multi-channel twin `read_for_scopes_after_seq` (`mls.pull`), which duplicates the identical never-load-sealed-bytes gate rather than calling into the former — so **every** per-record serve path withholds it: the sealed envelope is withheld from **future** fetches and the tombstone reference is carried in-thread. Boundaries (documented, not bugs — the content is E2E-sealed): it cannot recall content already delivered before the takedown, and cannot reach client-sealed snapshot/replica blobs. **The author's queue row is the triple's appeal leg (ruled 2026-10-18, the design pass; built 2026-10-18).** The relay holds no plaintext, but it does hold the record's **nest-attested author** — `conv_record_authors`, the authenticated `channel.send` caller written in the mirror row's own transaction (schema 70; served as `ChannelFetchEntry.author`) — so "persists no sender", the ground on which no queue row was written, is stale. A takedown of an attested record writes the SAME `ObligationAction::TakenDown` row on the author's queue a post takedown writes (`content_type="conversation"`, `content_id` the record digest in lowercase hex, the author's `author_hex`), and the triple is the in-thread tombstone + the author's queue row with its appeal handle + the audit row — a post's exactly, and zero new app UI, since every app already paints the queue and its appeal. Why the queue and not the thread: the author is the only party who can be told, and their own devices never receive the tombstone — each keeps the real copy, and the cursor walk never re-fetches a synced seq — so the row is the author's one signal, as it is for a blocked post; no doorbell rings (§ Admin block → *What a block is not*). The appeal needs no record id on the wire: the author's own copy carries the same digest (`fauna_conversations::plane::plane_ref`), which is how an app matches a queue row to the message in the thread: the author's own copy paints the withheld badge (§ App render, the fourth surface). A record no attestation names — appended by a nest-side writer, or a send that authenticated nobody — writes no row and keeps the any-holder arm (§ Errors & edge cases, the appeal bullet). **Reach, stated rather than hidden:** the row lands on the CHANNEL's home nest under the author's actor id, and `fauna.moderation.actions`/`appeal` are served by the nest the caller is connected to, so an author homed on another nest would not see it — the issuing nest delivers the row to the author's home nest and the home relays the appeal back (*The author homed on another nest*, the next bullet, ruled 2026-10-18).
- **The author homed on another nest — the issuing nest DELIVERS the row to the author's home nest, and the home nest RELAYS the appeal back to the issuer (ruled 2026-10-18, the design pass; nest leg built 2026-10-19).** **The gap, stated for both kinds.** An obligation row is keyed by the author's actor id on the nest whose admin decided: the CHANNEL's home nest for a conversation record (*Conversations* above), and for a post the nest serving the copy the order was served against — for the local projection of a foreign post that is the *reporter's* nest, not the author's (§ User-initiated reporting → *Routing*: `content_meta` rows exist for projected posts, so the flag and the block apply to the local copy, and both takedown arms and the block key the row on the content row's author whether or not this nest hosts them — `legal_takedown_core`, `AdminBlockTarget::Post`). `fauna.moderation.actions` and `fauna.moderation.appeal`, though, are served by the nest the author's app is connected to — their home — so an author homed elsewhere reads an empty queue, and the triple's appeal leg stops at the issuer's border. **(1) Delivery — a push, never a poll.** The app's data plane runs only through its home nest ([`direct-messages.md`](direct-messages.md) § Technical Flow — Cross-Nest), and only the issuer knows where the author lives: the set of nests that might hold a row for an author is unbounded (every channel home they sit in, every nest projecting their posts), so a home-side or app-side query has nothing to enumerate, while the issuer resolves the author's home exactly as a forwarded report does (§ Routing → *Home-nest resolution*: a projected post → the nest it arrived from; a conversation record → the author's `channel_foreign_members` row on the channel home, its `home_nest_id`). The push is the shape the two existing legs that tell a user's home nest about their standing already take — the report outcome (the home writes the reporter's row, § What the reporter is told) and the federated follow's state push ([`federated-follow.md`](federated-follow.md) § The state leg back). So a takedown or block whose author this nest does not host queues `fauna.federation.moderation.obligation.deliver` to the author's home in the same act that writes the row, on the moderation plane's one durable queue (the report triad's `abuse_report_outbox`: an inline first try, backoff for seven days, idempotent on the receiver), carrying the row's fields — `content_type`, `content_id`, `author`, `action`, `category`, `timestamp` — plus the issuer's `ref` (its own row id: the dedupe key, and what the appeal names back) and the issuer's declared address, which the receiver records against the verified origin by the relayed-origin rule — how the appeal finds a nest the home may never have dialled. **The receiver's gate:** the named author is hosted here, else `not_hosted`; for a `post`, the post is that author's by the home's own tombstone-inclusive lookup (the home holds every own post, deleted ones as tombstones — a post it never held is refused, so a forged post row cannot land); a conversation record it cannot check — the home keeps no record of its members' foreign channels, the client names the channel home per relayed call — so there the gate is the hosted author plus the budgets. It stores the SAME `obligation_action_records` row with two additive nullable columns, `issuer_nest_id` (the verified origin; NULL = this nest's own decision) and `issuer_ref`, so `fauna.moderation.actions` serves it unchanged on the author's queue and every app's appeal handle is already on it — zero new app UI; the wire row gains the additive `issuer_nest: Option<String>` (the issuer's handle domain) so the queue can say *where*, which the lead app paints in the row's existing text, no new id. A re-delivery of a stored `(origin, ref)` is free; a new one spends two per-target budgets — the author and the whole nest — summed over every origin, past which it is refused `rate_limited` and the sender's queue retries ([`../architecture/federation.md`](../architecture/federation.md) § Security → *Rate limiting is the primary DoS defense*). **(2) The appeal — relayed by the home, decided and kept by the issuer.** The deciding admin's nest must hold the appeal: it is the record that admin reviews, and the pending bound lives beside the decisions it pends against — so the author's home never records a foreign appeal as its own. `fauna.moderation.appeal` on a row carrying an `issuer_nest_id` runs the local checks (the caller is the row's author; the reason non-empty and bounded) and relays `fauna.federation.moderation.appeal { ref, content_id, author, reason }` INLINE to the issuer — the `fauna.posts.get_remote` posture: the issuer's reply (`appeal_recorded` / `appeal_already_recorded`) comes back verbatim, its typed refusals through the peer-relay error map, and an unreachable issuer is the relay's error on `appeal-status`, the author holding their text and retrying; the home keeps no pending state. The issuer's gate is structural: the verified origin must be the nest the row was delivered to (recorded on the issuer's row at the acknowledged delivery — for a conversation record the author's `home_nest_id`, the foreign-member binding every channel relay uses) and `author` the row's `author_hex`; it then runs the body a local author gets — the pending bound per (appellant, `content_id`), the `moderation:appeal` audit row with the author as `actor_id`, the reason verbatim (§ Errors & edge cases) — and its admins review it as any other. Once the issuer acknowledged, the home records `moderation:appeal-relayed` on its own chain (actor the author, target the content id, detail the issuer and the text) — the author's act, which their account export carries as it carries a local appeal; never before the acknowledgement, so the home's chain never claims a relay that failed. **(3) What does not cross.** An overturn delivers nothing — as for a post, no counter-row: the row stays history on both nests and the handle stays, an overturn being a decision that re-opens it on the issuer; the tombstone and the audit row stay the issuer's, so the triple is the issuer's tombstone + the author's delivered row with its handle + the issuer's audit row; the issuer's admins decide, and the author's admins learn nothing from the delivery (a home admin told of a takedown or block is the retaliation vector § What the reporter is told keeps the author's side clear of). **Bounds, stated rather than hidden.** A verified but hostile nest can deliver a row it never acted on against a conversation record this home cannot check: the budgets bound the noise, the row names its issuer so the author knows which nest claims to withhold what, the author's own copy carries the digest an app may match it against (*Conversations* above), and the appeal text goes only where the author sends it — to the named issuer, by their own act. A post row is checked against the author's own store and a forged one is refused. **Rejected:** the author's app or nest querying each foreign nest for rows about the author (shape (b) of the design pass) — nothing bounds what to query, and a read of "rows about me" against every foreign nest at every queue refresh is a per-user fan-out the data plane has nowhere else; and a home-side appeal record standing in for the issuer's — it would leave the deciding admin's review with nothing to read and the pending bound with nothing to pend against. Built: the nest leg, and on tui, web, linux and android the issuer named on the queue row with the two-nest witness (2026-10-19, § Implementation status today); windows, macos and ios name it with their trickle-down.
- **The blob-serve door withholds the record's attachments too (ruled + built 2026-09-09).** A withheld record's body is withheld on every per-record serve path — for a conversation at the read primitive every such path flows through (§ Conversations below), for a post on each serve path in front of the deliberately flag-blind body primitive, a set that is closed and test-enforced (§ Posts above; the export's `post` segment-pair leg is the one open exception, recorded there) — but a record's *attachments* are not in that body. They are separate content-addressed blobs served by the unauthenticated `GET /api/v1/blob/{id}` (a post's photo, its thumbnail and a gated post's sealed body; a conversation's sealed attachment), named by a hash every recipient already holds: it is inside the message they received, or the post they saw before the flag. So until this ruling a takedown blanked the text and kept relaying the picture, and — since a withheld record is a LIVE mirror row (tombstone-not-delete) — the GC kept the bytes pinned for it to relay. **The rule: a blob is withheld from that door iff every LIVE record naming it is under a takedown — plus every blob named by a post its author deleted while taken down (2026-09-11, § Posts above).** That second clause is not an exception to the first but the same rule where the walk goes blind: such a record is neither live nor readable (its `content` row is gone and its tombstoned body answers `None`), so the rebuild below can neither enumerate it nor learn which blobs it named, and its digests come from what the delete captured. They join the **flagged** side only — a deleted record is not a live record, so it exempts nothing, and a blob some live unflagged record also names keeps serving exactly as the per-record exemption says. The exemption is load-bearing, not decoration: a takedown is compelled against *one record* and its transparency triple is per-record, so withholding a blob that a live *unflagged* record also stands behind would break that record's media with no tombstone, no appeal handle and no audit row — precisely the silent, discretionary removal this section exists to make structurally impossible. The nest can answer the question without reading anything sealed because the plaintext floor already records exactly which blobs a record names (`GatedInfo.attachment_refs` for posts, `conv_attachment_refs` for conversation records — [`../architecture/encryption-at-rest.md`](../architecture/encryption-at-rest.md) § Plaintext floor). **Answer shape: 451 Unavailable For Legal Reasons, no body** — the same disclose-rather-than-hide posture the federation post fetch takes (§ Posts above), and deliberately without the tombstone reference: a blob has no tombstone render of its own, and the reference reaches the client through the record's own serve path, which is what paints it. Both branches of the door are gated **on the resolved digest before either runs** — a hex id and its `b…` multibase CID address the same bytes, so a hex-only gate is bypassed by asking again in base32 — and the gate precedes the `?thumb=1` lookup, so a withheld original never serves a thumbnail of itself. **Tombstone-not-delete is untouched:** the GC pin is deliberately *not* gated on the flag, so the bytes stay on the box and `restore=true` re-serves the very same blob. *Where the predicate lives:* `nest::moderation_withhold`, over a derived `blob_legal_withhold` set — never a floor (every row is recomputable from the flag and the reference sets the box already holds) and never edited incrementally, because both sides of that set-difference move as records come and go and a missed hook would drift in the direction that keeps serving compelled bytes. It is rebuilt **wholesale** in two places: by the takedown/restore handler the instant a flag moves, synchronously before the reply, so an admin's confirm returning “taken_down” means the door is already shut; and by every blob-GC sweep, folded into the record walk it already performs ([`backup-restore.md`](backup-restore.md) § 9 steps 2f/2g), which is what keeps it true afterwards. **Completeness decides only whether a walk may RELEASE:** a walk that read every live record replaces the set, an incomplete one may only add to it — a run that could not read some record cannot know which blobs it named, so it fails toward over-withholding (which the next complete walk corrects) rather than back toward serving compelled bytes (which nothing corrects). What the reconcile buys: a new record naming a withheld blob releases it, and the removal of the last unflagged record naming a shared one withholds it. A per-request join was weighed and rejected — conversations have a real reverse index but posts deliberately have none ([`backup-restore.md`](backup-restore.md) § 9 step 2f walks live records rather than a table written at ingest), so answering per GET would hand an anonymous caller a full-store walk. **The withhold is a property of the BYTES, so it binds every route onto the blob store — not one door (2026-09-10).** `GET /api/v1/blob/{id}` is the door the rule was written for, but the video plane serves the same digests out of the same store at `GET /api/v1/video/segments/{hash}`, and a video post's manifest, thumbnail and every segment hash are in the withheld set (`Post::blob_refs`). Until this was closed a takedown was bypassed by a **path-prefix substitution on the very hex digest the requester already holds** — 451 at one path, 200 at the other. **The same substitution worked at two further paths until 2026-09-11**: `GET /api/v1/chunks/{hash}` and `GET /api/v1/manifests/{hash}` — the file-sync data plane's own content-addressed reads, mounted unauthenticated on the identical store on the rationale that a ciphertext hash is the capability — served the withheld digest 200 while the two doors above answered 451. Every such door calls the one named gate (`blob_routes::legal_takedown_gate`, over the predicate `blob_routes::is_legally_withheld`) rather than restating the posture, so the answer shape cannot drift between them. **The gate precedes the store read AND any arm that would fetch the bytes back from elsewhere**, and that ordering is load-bearing rather than tidy: the segment door's cache-on-fetch pulls a missing segment from a delivery-source peer and stores it back, and the chunk door's folder-hinted arm relays a missing chunk from one of the folder owner's seats and — for a full-residency folder — `store.put`s it back, so a gate placed after either would let that door *re-import* compelled bytes onto a box that no longer had them: the takedown running backwards. **The manifest→chunks walk is gated at the walk, not once per door.** `web_content::file_bytes::read_file_by_manifest` reassembles a file out of the digests a manifest names, and any registered user may upload a manifest naming a withheld digest (`POST /api/v1/manifests` is `BulkWriteAuth`, and for a single-chunk manifest the declared `file_hash` IS that digest, so crafting one needs nothing beyond the hex the requester already holds) — so the withhold is consulted on the manifest hash and on every chunk the walk reads, which binds its three caller families at once: the public share link (`share_routes::handle_share`), the author's own web site (`web_content::serve`) and the site render (`web_content::service`). The refusal is a 451 wherever the request names the withheld file, and the one arm that answers otherwise does so deliberately: when the withheld file is the site's own custom `404.html`, a request for some *other*, missing path gets the built-in 404 page, because that request never named the withheld bytes and a 451 there would misreport a path that simply does not exist — while a request for `/404.html` itself resolves to the same `web_files` row and answers 451. **One consequence, stated here rather than discovered later:** `/api/v1/chunks` is the file-sync data plane and the store is content-addressed, so a legitimate sync of a chunk whose bytes dedup to a withheld digest is answered 451 like any other request for those bytes. That is the rule working as written — the withhold is keyed on the BYTES, and a second namer is exactly the case the per-record exemption above already decides at set-rebuild time — not a defect of the gate. **How the next door is enrolled: mechanically, not by this paragraph.** The standing duty went unchecked long enough for four doors to ship ungated, each found only when somebody happened to look — and writing the mechanism found a fifth route that nobody ever had: the **mail byte plane's upward body reference** (`mail_body_plane::resolve_body_ref`), whose chunk digests come off the REQUEST and whose join is authenticated only against a declared total, so an authenticated bridge session could name a withheld digest as a body chunk, pad to the total, and read the compelled bytes back out as its own message. It is gated on the same predicate; its staged twin (`resolve_staged_body`) deliberately is **not**, because that join carries an AEAD tag under the key the caller's own reference supplies, so a blob they merely named can never satisfy it — which its witness asserts adversarially, by naming a withheld digest in a staged reference. So the duty is now a test rather than a promise: `backup::service`'s partition walks every production call site onto the store — every caller of `BackupService::local_blob_store` and, because the handle outlives the call that acquires it, every use of the handle a long-lived holder keeps (the chunk resolver, the payload store and the web-content service) and every caller of a holder method that hands stored bytes back — and requires each to be classified: a **reader-named** serve path, which must name the gate that withholds and whose production body the test checks actually calls it (a private helper names instead the doors it is reached from, and must be reached from nothing else); an **owner-scoped** serve path, which no caller can aim at a digest because the digest comes from a row this nest wrote or from bytes the caller itself supplied; a **primitive**, a holder method whose every caller is enrolled in its own right; or a **reader**, from which nothing derived from the store leaves the process. It fails on a caller nobody has classified and on a classified door whose gate call is gone, so a new door is enrolled on the day it is written and an enrolled one cannot quietly lose its gate. That partition, never a count written in prose, is the authority on how many doors there are; the gate is `pub(crate)` to make reuse the path of least resistance. **What the withhold binds on OWNER- and ADMIN-scoped routes (ruled 2026-09-12).** The partition's owner-scoped class holds routes no caller can aim at a digest, and "binds every route onto the store" cannot be read there as "gate them all" — gating an admin's own export or an owner's own backup mirror could destroy data the user is entitled to — so each such route needs a decision, and the decision follows from what a takedown removes. **A takedown removes a record's AVAILABILITY THROUGH THIS NEST, never the box's CUSTODY of the bytes**; tombstone-not-delete is the same statement from the storage side (the bytes stay on disk precisely so `restore=true` can re-serve them). Three questions, asked in order, decide any owner- or admin-scoped route — today's four and the next one: **(a) Is it custody moving with the box?** The box's own store copied whole, to the operator the order was served on (who already holds every byte under their own root) or into the deployment's own new backend — NOT bound, and MUST carry the bytes: the flag (`content_meta`), the order (`audit_log`) and the post-delete floor (`legal_takedown_deleted_posts`) ride beside them in the logical dump, and a copy that dropped the withheld bytes would turn the tombstone into a deletion at the next restore, so an overturn could re-serve nothing. **(b) Does it serve the record to a viewer the record's own serve path withholds it from?** Bound, in § *Posts*' shape: withheld AND declared, never silently dropped — and the author is such a viewer, since § *Posts* withholds a taken-down body from every viewer including the author, so the author's own archive is bound on every leg. **(c) Does it put the bytes behind a door this nest does not control?** A push that lands them, under the same digest, in another box's content-addressed store — whose own unauthenticated door holds no flag — is the path-prefix substitution above with the *host* substituted instead: bound, withhold-and-declare, and retract-where-expressible (the ATProto and Nostr legs' posture), because that door serves a copy pushed before the flag exactly as one pushed after. The four, decided: **(1) the author export's blob leg** (`export_routes::gather_export_data`; question (b)) — bound, and DORMANT: its extractor `scan_blob_refs` is a no-op, so the leg ships no store blob today and the archive's real blob carriage is the `post` segment-pair leg, already withheld whole and declared (decision (6)); the day an extractor lands, each gathered digest passes `blob_routes::is_legally_withheld` before the store read and a withheld one is declared in the manifest. A witness cannot be written against a leg that carries nothing, so the binding is recorded at the loop and in the partition entry rather than fabricated. **(2) the admin whole-store export** (`admin_export_routes::handle_admin_export_all`; question (a)) — not bound: carries, and must. **(3) the folder mirror** (`segment_backup::run_folder_once`; question (c)) — bound. A covered folder is mirrored latest-per-path into the destination's own store under the source's store keys ([`backup-destinations.md`](backup-destinations.md) § Ordinary-folder coverage), where `/api/v1/chunks/{hash}` would serve a public-audience folder's plaintext chunk to anyone holding the hex this nest answers 451 for. A folder file is never itself the subject of an order (`content_type` is `post` or `conversation`), so a withheld digest reaches a folder only by content-address dedup with a taken-down record's attachment — the same second-namer collateral the chunk door's 451 already carries, and bounded here to *delay*: a live path naming a withheld digest is skipped, counted in the pass report (`FolderRunReport::withheld_paths`), retracted at the destination if an earlier pass mirrored it (the ordinary path delete, retained under the destination's grace window), and re-tried every pass, so it mirrors the moment the flag lifts; the owner's local file is never touched. The set is read once per pass and tested in memory, so a box with no takedown pays nothing. **A per-(destination, folder) memo (`folder_withhold_checkpoint`) skips re-opening an already-mirrored manifest once its content is confirmed against the current withheld set, and is stamped only after BOTH the live loop and the dropped-paths teardown finish clean — never before the teardown — so a teardown failure never leaves a stamp claiming to have rechecked a path it did not.** Reachable, not hypothetical: a path can be deleted and later recreated with byte-identical content, which is the same manifest hash (a pure function of the bytes, uncorrelated with when they were written), so a stale-but-trusted stamp over such a path is a real failure mode, pinned directly. (The reserved-rail segment pass is not this route: `BACKED_UP_KINDS` is `mail`, sealed under a key the destination never holds.) **(4) the derived room-post view** (`room_post_view::index_room_post`; question (b), asked of the view rather than the read) — the reception pass's store read runs in the act that stores the post, before any flag can exist on it, and is not gated; what it derives IS a serve path for the body: a `room.search` hit is an existence oracle over the withheld text (search for a rare word, get the post id back) and a `posts.room_labels` verdict was scored from it, both served to the room's floor — a non-author audience. Both maps (`db::rooms::room_post_ids_for_docs` and `rooms_indexing_posts`, behind `ROOM_POST_VIEW_MODERATION`) apply `MODERATION_SERVABLE` over `content_meta` — the fragment every non-author surface shares — so a taken-down room post yields no hit and no verdict while the flag stands; the view rows stay (tombstone-not-delete — a re-derivation after the members rotated this nest out would be impossible), the overturn re-serves them with nothing recomputed, and the post's own delete still purges them through its unconditional walk. Not reopened here: the chunk door's dedup ruling above. - **Invocation surface (ruled 2026-08-16; first leg built on tui the same day):** the admin initiates a takedown or an overturn **through the app** — the legal-takedown console on the `admin-nest` page (`admin-nest-takedown-*`, beside the seed-rotation ceremony for the same reason: a rare, high-stakes, confirm-gated nest-wide admin act that adds no nav entry on any of the 7 apps). The ruling: the one-configuration-surface invariant (`principles.md` § One configuration surface) makes the app UI the only sanctioned human channel for an admin act — there is no operator role, so the raw-WS-RPC-only state was the gap, not a posture, and recording an ops-only exception would have minted a *new* deviation from an iron-clad invariant, which nothing licensed. The console is deliberately **not** on the moderation page — that page is the user's own training queue, never an admin console (§ Goal). Two-step dispatch: the arm button is live only when the shared form view allows it (content id present; `legal_reference` **required** for a takedown, optional on restore — the wire's own structural guard rendered client-side, so a citation-less takedown is never even armable), and the armed confirm **names the verb, the content id, and the citation** before anything dispatches — a compulsory act is never confirmed blind. *Where logic lives:* every decision is shared Rust — `fauna_client_moderation::takedown` (form gating, confirm summary, verdict wording) + `ModerationClient::legal_takedown` (the dispatch), exported over UniFFI and wasm so the six-app trickle-down is paint-only. tier_3 proof: `tests/e2e-unified/tests/test_admin_legal_takedown.py` — the whole triple through the UI: guard → named confirm → verdict → feed exclusion → the author's queue row → restore → re-serve, with the queue row surviving the restore as additive history.
- **App render (shared Rust, not 7×):** the tombstone face is `fauna_core::obligation::legal_takedown_tombstone` (UniFFI + wasm twins). **Three surfaces, one concept, one `legal_takedown_ref: Option<String>` field shape on each — each paints the tombstone IN PLACE OF the withheld content, never a blank surface and never a page-level error standing in for a body — and a fourth for the one party who still holds the content, its author:** quoted-post cards (`FeedManager::resolve_quoted_post` → `RenderBlock::QuotedPost.legal_takedown_ref`); conversation bubbles, which collapse to it when `MessageSnapshot.legal_takedown_ref` is set (no decrypt attempted — the envelope is empty), painted as the ui.yaml element `dm-message-legal-takedown`, the twin of `dm-message-deleted` (owner-approved 2026-10-16); and, since 2026-08-10, the **post-detail body area** when `PostSummary.legal_takedown_ref` is set — the deep-link door (`FeedManager::resolve_post`) is the one read that sees `PostGetReply.legal_takedown`, and it parks a `PostSummary::taken_down` tombstone in the snapshot's deep-link slot for the surface to find ([`../architecture/feed-read-model.md`](../architecture/feed-read-model.md) § The read model → *Opening a post the timeline never loaded* owns that resolve's properties). The third surface is now adopted on all seven apps: tui, linux, android, and web (2026-08-10/2026-08-14/15), then macOS+iOS (2026-08-25) and windows (2026-08-26) with their own search-navigation lift — corrected here to match [`../ui/feed.md`](../ui/feed.md) § Implementation status today, which previously understated this the same way. **The fourth surface — the author's OWN copy of a withheld conversation message (owner-approved 2026-10-19 as `dm-message-withheld-badge`; shared Rust and tui built 2026-10-19, web, linux and android the same day — `test_moderation_appeal_conversation.py` green `--app web` and `--app linux`, android compiled with its device run host-owed — windows, macos and ios follow in the trickle-down).** The author's devices keep the real copy and never receive the tombstone, so the bubble keeps its body and a badge beside it says what the author's queue row says, opening that row's appeal (the moderation page's own appeal form — one flow, the `appeal-*` ids). Shared Rust decides it: `ConversationsManager::refresh_withheld_messages`, called when a thread opens, reads the caller's `fauna.moderation.actions` through the thread's rail (`RailBackend::own_withheld_records` → `ConversationsRpc::own_withheld_records`, kept by the one filter `WithheldRecord::from_queue_row` — `content_type="conversation"`, a `TakenDown` or `Blocked` action, the newest row per record), and `thread_detail` projects `MessageSnapshot.withheld` onto each own message whose `plane_ref.record_digest` a row names — a delivered row from another nest (`issuer_nest`) matches the same way and the badge names that nest by the queue's own `issued_by` suffix. Never stored, and never through `legal_takedown_ref` (which collapses the bubble) or `admin_block_reason` (one ground only): `withheld` is its own read-time field, the `delivered_to` shape. Its words are `WithheldOwnMessage.lines` — the ground (the queue row's action label, or the block notice with its reason when the copy is the author-exempt fetch that carries one, the badge then REPLACING the beside-body notice so the bubble says it once), the issuer suffix, the appeal word. The badge follows the queue row, so it stands as long as the row does — through a restore or an Unblock too, as the row does. The author's moderation-queue row carries the deliberately off-list `category="illegal"` (an enforcement descriptor, not a classifier emission — renders as the `Other` badge) + `obligation_action_label(7)` "Removed under legal obligation".

## Admin block of a reported item (owner-ratified 2026-10-16; nest, tui, linux, web and android legs built 2026-10-17)

**The second by-hand withhold, and the one lever a report can become.** On 2026-10-15 the owner weighed an admin block of reported content and kept the levers at suspension, eviction and the cited legal takedown; on 2026-10-16, after the per-nest rules were redirected into device-applied blocking rules and the stores' "report content" requirement was put as a question, the owner directed a design pass for a manual content block and ratified it the same day with the three decisions below, each chosen from the pass's options in the owner's own words — *Disclosed*, *Yes*, *Withhold, no retraction*. This section owns the lever; § Legal takedown owns the withhold mechanism it reuses, and item 1 of § Categories & enforcement the invariant both sit under: no nest-wide removal by hand without a record.

- **The lever.** A fourth verb on a report-queue row, **Block**, Admin-only: it withholds the reported item from everyone this nest serves but the author, through the legal takedown's own withhold with a second **ground** — *blocked by this nest's admin after a report* — carrying the report's reason (one of the eight, § User-initiated reporting → *What a report carries*) as the stated explanation. **The transparency triple is not optional:** a visible notice in the item's place naming the ground and the reason ("blocked by this nest's admin after a report: harassment"), the author's appeal through the existing `fauna.moderation.appeal` by content id, and a permanent audit row. **Unblock** clears the flag, audits the overturn and re-serves the same bytes (tombstone-not-delete). Blocking never deletes. The confirm is the takedown console's two-step: armed only when the shared form view allows it, and the armed confirm names the verb, the item and the report's reason before anything dispatches — a lever on another user's content is never confirmed blind.
- **Only from a report.** The verb exists on a report-queue row and nowhere else: the report is the block's citation, as the legal reference is the takedown's — an admin who wants to block an unreported item files a report themselves, which the queue then shows under their own handle. Every block therefore has a reporter, a reason and a record; "no silent lever" is structural. A report with an `actor` subject cannot be blocked — a block is of one item; the account levers are [`admin.md`](admin.md)'s.
- **Nest-local — and decision 3 (the owner's, 2026-10-16): *Disclosed*.** The block binds what *this* nest serves — its own members' posts, the local projection of a foreign post, a conversation record it relays — and nothing on any other nest. A block on a home nest's own post is disclosed to federation the way a takedown's withholding is: the WS peer fetch returns an additive marker naming the ground and the reason with an empty body, and the HTTP twin answers **403** with the ground in the body — never `not_found` (the quarantine's existence-hiding shape), and never **451**, which stays the legal takedown's alone: honesty about the ground extends to the status code.
- **The author keeps their own view — decision 4 (the owner's, 2026-10-16): *Yes*.** Unlike the legal takedown, which withholds the body from every viewer including the author, a block is the admin's judgment about the nest's audience, not a compulsion against the author: the author's own reads serve the item with the notice beside it, the author keeps their copy on their devices, and the author-scoped paths (`fauna.posts.list`, the account export) are untouched by a block. Two limits, stated rather than hidden: a sealed **conversation record** is withheld from every future fetch, the author's other devices included (the author keeps the copy on the device that sent it) — stated 2026-10-16 as forced by a relay that persists no sender, which it does not: the record's nest-attested author (`conv_record_authors`, § Legal takedown → *Conversations*) names the author to the authenticated local `channel.fetch`, so the 2026-10-18 design pass rules the post's author-exempt arm (`get_post_core`) for the local fetch of a record under a block: the attested author's own devices receive the sealed envelope with the notice beside it, every other caller and every relayed fetch (`federation.channel.fetch`, `mls.pull` — their caller is a nest) the withheld entry; a takedown stays every-viewer, as for a post. It honours this decision's *Yes* more fully, it does not reverse it, and it is built (2026-10-19): the read primitive takes the viewer the caller authenticated, the account export passes its exporter the same way, and the author's app keeps the body with the notice beside it (§ Implementation status today); and a blocked record's **attachments** go through the anonymous blob doors, which cannot tell the author apart, so they are withheld from every requester — the author's app shows the text, the notice and whatever it already cached.
- **Off-box publication — decision 5 (the owner's, 2026-10-16): *Withhold, no retraction*.** A block withholds the item from everything this nest serves from the moment it lands, the off-box publish surfaces included — the author's web site render, the ActivityPub outbox, the ATProto projection, the Nostr materializer — through the one predicate family (`MODERATION_SERVABLE`, which `PUBLIC_POST_SERVABLE` composes; § Legal takedown → *Posts*), so the doors cannot disagree. It **retracts nothing already published**: no `tombstone/post` witness, no `deleteRecord`, no kind-5 — retraction is a permanent-intent act the block's own reversal cannot undo off-box, which is the reason quarantine and suppression never retract; the takedown retracts because it is compelled.
- **What a block is not.** Not a reputation signal, not a label, not an input to any feed or scorer, not a message to the reporter beyond the existing outcome (*acted*), not a notification to the author beyond the notice and the appeal (§ User-initiated reporting → *What the reporter is told*: a report-notified author is a retaliation vector), not a knob — no threshold, no auto-block count; a flood of reports still blocks nothing by itself (anti-abuse bound 1 stands) — and not a rule: a nest's published rules ([`nest-rules.md`](nest-rules.md)) block classes of content on the device and never a picked item.
- **Mechanism (the takedown's, with a second ground).** Wire: `fauna.moderation.admin_block { report_id, restore }` → `{ status: "blocked" | "restored", content_id, content_type }`, Admin-only (`bridge_method_allowlist.rs`), beside `legal_takedown`. One transaction: the flag — `content_meta.admin_block_report_id` for a post, `segment_records.admin_block_report_id` for a conversation record, each nullable and additive, the `UPDATE` asserted to one row — plus, for a post, an `ObligationAction::Blocked` (=10) row on the author's queue whose `category` is the report's reason (appealable: the appeal gate accepts it as a real enforcement record; a conversation record's row is keyed by its nest-attested author exactly as the takedown's is — `category` the report's reason — and the appeal gate's conversation arm reads the second flag beside the first for a record no attestation names, which any holder of the record id appeals (§ Legal takedown → *Conversations*, ruled 2026-10-18; built for both grounds 2026-10-18); an author this nest does not host — the local projection of a foreign post, a foreign member's record — receives either ground's row on their home nest's queue through § Legal takedown → *The author homed on another nest*, ruled 2026-10-18), the `audit_log` kind `moderation:admin-block`, and the report resolved `acted` (the reporter's notification exactly as today). A block cites an open report; a replay against the report it already resolved answers `blocked` and writes nothing. `restore=true`: the flag cleared (only while it still names this report — a block under a later report is that report's to lift), `moderation:admin-block-restore` audited, the obligation row kept as history; the four decision audits (the takedown's two and the block's two) each close the appeals pending against their target. A blocked item's report has left the open queue, so the blocked list reads `fauna.moderation.admin_block.list` (Admin-only): one row per currently-blocked item — the report id Unblock takes, the subject, the reason and the block's time (the report's resolution). Serving: `MODERATION_SERVABLE` gains `AND cm.admin_block_report_id IS NULL`, so feeds, Search, the federation query, the room views, the site render and every off-box surface inherit the withhold with no second copy; `get_post_core` withholds the body from every viewer except the author and carries `PostGetReply.admin_block { reason }` to all; the conversation read primitives withhold on the second flag as on the first, the in-thread marker carried; the blob withhold set (`moderation_withhold`) takes blocked records as a second input and records each withheld blob's ground (`blob_legal_withhold.ground`; a blob both reach is withheld as legal), and the one door gate answers 403 for a block and 451 for a takedown, every enrolled door inheriting it through the partition tests; the web-site re-render on a flag change runs for a block as for a takedown. Faces, shared Rust: `fauna_core::obligation::admin_block_notice(reason) -> LocalizedText` (`moderation.admin_block.notice`, `{reason}` the reason's localized name) beside `legal_takedown_tombstone`; `obligation_action_label(10)` → `moderation.action.blocked`; the admin client seam in the takedown console's `takedown` module shape (`block_form_view`, `block_confirm_summary`, `block_verdict`, `ModerationClient::admin_block`), exported over UniFFI and wasm so the six-app trickle-down after tui is paint-only.
- **App surface — IDs approved by the owner 2026-10-16 (rule A), in ui.yaml since the tui leg (2026-10-17).** On `admin-nest` (placement [`admin.md`](admin.md) § N. Nest): per open `admin-nest-report-item` with an item subject, `admin-nest-report-block-button`; the confirm `admin-nest-report-block-confirm-summary`, `admin-nest-report-block-confirm-button`, `admin-nest-report-block-cancel-button`; below the queue `admin-nest-blocked-section`, indexed `admin-nest-blocked-item` (subject, reason, age), `admin-nest-blocked-unblock-button`. The notice renders in the body position of the post and the message through the shared face — no new feed or conversation ID, as for the takedown tombstone. Strings in `en.yaml`, rendered through `LocalizedText`; no app hard-codes any of them.
- **Build order (rust-first):** shared Rust + nest → tui, the lead app, with the tier_3 witness through the UI — report → Block → notice in place → appeal → Unblock re-serves → the batched trickle-down: linux, web and android, macOS + iOS, windows. Every public text that states what an admin can do changes only through the public-texts request, never from a build row. The owner reworded their own statement of the third exception on ratifying ([`../architecture/public-texts.md`](../architecture/public-texts.md) § Words from the owner).
- **Read against the neighbours.** [`../principles.md`](../principles.md) § The user always controls their data is **confirmed, not amended**: a block removes the item's availability through this nest, never the author's custody of their content or their copy on their devices, and the author keeps their view (decision 4). [`../architecture/content-moderation-and-ranking.md`](../architecture/content-moderation-and-ranking.md) § Boundaries names the block beside the legal case as a bounded, recorded withhold — still "never a general admin lever". The stores' "report content" question reads this section as the lever its candidate (i) named, and its per-store mapping — recorded 2026-10-16 in [`terms-and-content-guidelines.md`](terms-and-content-guidelines.md) § The stores' requirements against the admin's levers — cites it rather than minting a second design, and binds the first store submissions on this section's nest and lead-app build rows.
