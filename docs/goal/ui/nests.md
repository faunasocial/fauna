## What this page is

The **Nests** page (Settings rail, renamed from "Linked nests" when this v1 lands) lists every nest the user's content lives on — their home nest and any linked nests — and, on each nest's row, shows a **trust facet**: what content-processing that nest has been **trusted to read** (the content capabilities the user's client has minted to it), with a **revoke** control, an **expiry / renewal** state, and a **Now / History** lens over the client-authoritative signed grant-event log.

It is the **nest-side sibling** of the client-device roster (`devices.md`) under one shared participant model (`participants.md` § Q-A — deliberately *sibling pages*, not a merged "Participants" page). The asymmetry the two pages render: a **client is the trusted mint** (needs no grant); a **nest is an untrusted holder** that receives a scope-limited, HPKE-wrapped capability grant (`participants.md:36`). Identity is never granted either way.

**Naming (binding, `participants.md` § Naming `:102`,`:107`):** the user-facing vocabulary is **"trust" / "trusted to read …"**. The words **"capability" and "grant" MUST NEVER appear in any rendered string** — they are internal-only. The `nest-trust-*` / `-grant-*` tokens below are **dev-facing ui.yaml element IDs** (never shown to users), so "grant"/"trust"/"nest" in an *ID string* is fine; every *i18n string* this page renders is checked against the naming table.

## Layout

A vertical list of nest rows (`nests-item`, indexed — one per `fauna.pair.list` row plus the home nest), preceded by the page heading, an error slot, and the "Link a nest" add affordance (unchanged from the linking surface). Each nest row carries, below its identity line:

1. **Identity line** (existing): label, abbreviated nest id, the sync capabilities it may pull (`mls_pull` / `namespace_sync` / `post_forward` / `mail_pull` / `nostr_push` — `linked-nests.md`'s `default_self_sync()` set, owner: `private-mode.md`), an optional pairing expiry, and Unlink.
2. **Trust facet** (net-new): a per-row **Now / History** lens toggle; in **Now**, the nest's current content-processing grants; in **History**, that nest's grant-event timeline. A nest that holds no grants shows an explicit "not trusted to read anything" empty state.

The Now/History lens is a **per-row** facet (`participants.md:41` — "each row carrying the trust facet … and the Now/History lens"), not a page-level toggle.

## Behavior

### Forward queue (ratified 2026-09-23)

A home nest behind a relay forwards the user's posts to that relay through a queue, and a forward the relay refuses stays queued and retrying for as long as the refusal lasts. **Whose page shows it, and why the user's, is owned by [`../architecture/nest/private-mode.md`](../architecture/nest/private-mode.md) § Post Forwarding** (*the queue is the user's to see*); this section owns only the page shape. The Nests page renders one **page-level** block, after the add form and before the nest rows, from the `forward_queue` the shared `LinkedNestsMachine` snapshot carries (sourced from `fauna.pair.list`, per author, additive — an older nest reports none and the block is absent): while the user's queued count is non-zero, the status line says how many of their posts are waiting to reach their relay and — when any are refused past the retry ceiling (about 8.5 hours of backoff) — what to check: that this nest is allowed to forward their posts on the relay, which linking the relay again from this page grants. Below it, the nest's own record of the latest failed attempt when one exists (never an empty reason line), then **Retry now** (`RetryForwards` → `fauna.pair.forward_retry`, then re-list) and **Stop forwarding these** (`DiscardForwards` → `fauna.pair.forward_discard`, then re-list — the posts themselves stay). Copy is the `S.nests.forward_*` strings; "relay" is the user word for the linked internet nest ([`../../guides/nest-relay-setup.md`](../../guides/nest-relay-setup.md)). The block is absent, not empty, on a public nest and on a home nest whose relay accepts everything. e2e: `test_forward_queue.py` — a private nest whose relay refuses, the post appears on this page, linking the relay from here delivers it, and a discarded one leaves.

### Linking (unchanged)

Add / list / unlink behavior is owned by `../behavior/linked-nests.md` — the `LinkedNestsMachine` (`Refresh` → `fauna.pair.list`; `Link` → `fauna.pair.add`; `Unlink` → `fauna.pair.revoke`). This page renders that machine's snapshot as the identity line; nothing about linking changes.

### Trust facet — grants (Now lens)

Each nest's current grants are the **Now** projection of the client-authoritative signed grant-event log (`grant_log::current_grants`, folding the succession ledger's grant events — `fauna.state.succession-ledger`, [`../architecture/config-dissolution.md`](../architecture/config-dissolution.md) § Phases and gates → *Bounded rows* → *The ledger*), filtered to grants whose holder is that nest. Per grant the row shows scope (which content kinds it may read), `lasts-until`, a status, the honest bound, and renew + revoke controls.

- **Mint** (adding trust) — **scope-first, holder derived (UX ratified 2026-07-13)**: the user picks a *use case* from `nest-trust-mint-scope-select`, whose options are the shared catalog `view_model::mint_options(cfg, holders)` → `LinkedNestRow.mint_options` — "Read and filter my mail" (`content.read{mail}` **bundled with** the keyless `content.label-write`, the production MDA grant shape — never `read{mail}` alone), "Read my calendar" (`content.read{calendar}`), and one "Serve paywalled posts — ‹tier›" option per held tier (`content.read{post, tier}`; tier folded into the option list, the `compose-gate-tier-select` idiom). The catalog **only offers what would actually mint**: each option's payload must derive (`derive_scope_payload` — mail enabled / tier period-key held) and a role-matching holder must be enrolled (mail/calendar → the `mda` holder; post-tier → the generic `content-processor` holder, e.g. web-serve). The **holder is derived from the scope choice**; `nest-trust-mint-holder-select` renders only when an option lists >1 candidate (unreachable today — every option derives exactly one). Rationale: the scope→holder mapping is 1:1 in practice, and two independent selects would let a user mint e.g. the mail key to the web-serve holder — a user-triggerable over-grant. Confirm derives the minimal per-scope key from material the client already holds, assembles an HPKE-sealed `GrantBlob` (`fauna_client_capabilities::mint_grant`), appends a signed `GrantEvent` to the log (`grant_log::record_mint`) and persists it, and **only then** deposits the blob via `fauna.capabilities.mint` (`CapabilitiesClient::mint`) — the record-then-deposit order below, which is not an implementation detail but the thing that keeps a minted capability revocable. **Deliberately single-holder, not "one grant per content-processor holder on the nest"**: the generic `content-processor` role (`CONTENT_PROCESSOR_ROLES`) now covers more than the MDA — e.g. the web-serve paywall holder (`monetization.md` § Pillar 2) — and role alone can't discriminate which holder a given scope is meant for, so `LinkedNestsAction::Mint` takes an explicit `holder_bridge_id` (`libs/fauna-client-pair` 2026-07-13). `content.read{folder}` is deliberately absent from this picker (dedicated mint flow, `derive_scope_payload` → `Err(FolderUsesDedicatedMint)`).
- **Renew**: `grant_log::record_renew` + a durable save, *then* `CapabilitiesClient::renew` (the window slide; `appended_keys` empty in the master-key regime) — see *Record-then-deposit* below for why the log leads on a widening verb.
- **Revoke**: `CapabilitiesClient::revoke` (deletes the `(owner, grant_id)` row; the holder's next fetch goes dark) *then* `grant_log::record_revoke` — the nest leads here, and only here, because revoke narrows.

The owner's audit view is **client-authoritative log state, never a nest read** — `fauna.capabilities.fetch` is holder-pubkey-scoped (a bridge pulls its *own* grants) and there is deliberately no owner-facing "list my grants" RPC (design § No modes `:302`; the grant table on a nest is forgeable and worthless for forensics). The one owner-side nest read that exists is **not an audit read**: the ids-only reconcile sweep (*Reconcile* below), whose answer can feed nothing but revokes aimed back at the answering nest.

**Record-then-deposit — the ordering the absent enumerate forces (ratified 2026-08-14).** Because the audit view is the log and *only* the log, and because no owner-side enumerate exists to discover anything the log omits, **a grant's signed `Mint` event must be durable — and, since 2026-10-06, acknowledged by the bound nest's state plane — before its blob is deposited on the nest.** The two stores cannot be updated atomically — the nest is a separate machine and its accept is the commit point — so the achievable invariant is a one-directional one: *whatever the nest ends up holding, the log already names* — and, in the fleet form, *the nest's own copy of the log already names it too*, which is what lets a sibling replica learn of the event before it can meet the row (*Reconcile* below, the *Exactly one consumer* bullet, says why local durability is not enough). The failure directions are not symmetric:

- Nest holds it, log does not → on no page, nameable by no `revoke` call, discoverable by nothing. **Unrecoverable**, and thus a breach of `principles.md` § The user always controls their data ("revocable, audited from the user's app") and `nest/common.md` § Client-state recoverability.
- Log holds it, nest does not → a **phantom row**: visible, and cleared by a revoke that is idempotent nest-side. **Recoverable.**

Generalized across the three verbs: **where the log and the nest can disagree, the log must be the MORE permissive of the pair.** So mint and renew (which *widen*) record first, then call the nest; revoke (which *narrows*) calls the nest first, then records. The rule is carried by a type rather than by convention — `grant_log::UndepositedGrant` holds the sealed blob, and only `release(&PublishedGrants)` reaches the bytes, where `PublishedGrants` is built from the ledger the bound nest acknowledged (`SuccessionLedgerStore::merge_published`: join, then the runtime's own publish step run to its ack, refusing while any ledger row stays unsent — so a merge that dropped the event and a publish the nest refused are both caught before any blob leaves the device). The pre-2026-10-06 form read back the locally stored ledger only (`RecordedGrants`), which proves the event durable on one device and on no nest. `Renew` keeps record-locally-then-call: it creates no row, and liveness is window-blind (`fauna_core::grant_event::latest_live_events` decides by the presence of a `Revoke`), so an unpublished `Renew` can strand nothing. Prior to 2026-08-14 all four minting sites ran the unsafe order and one had copied it from another citing it as canonical prior art, which is why this is enforced structurally.

**Reconcile — the one admissible owner-side nest read (RATIFIED 2026-08-15; resolves the open question filed with the record-then-deposit ruling above).** The *never a nest read* rule above governs reads that **inform** — anything a nest answer could feed into the audit view or the signed log, where believing a lying nest is fatal (fabricated or hidden history). A **reconcile** read has the opposite trust polarity: the client believes nothing in the answer — it acts only on *absence from its own log* — and the only action the answer can trigger is `fauna.capabilities.revoke` aimed back at the answering nest, i.e. the surrender of that nest's own holdings, which the nest could effect unilaterally by deleting its rows anyway. A lying answer therefore confers **zero new power**: a fabricated id buys an idempotent no-op revoke (revoking an absent grant replies `{ok: true}`); an omitted id leaves an orphan exactly as unreachable as it already was, and a *dishonest* holder ignores revocation regardless (the honest bound — `architecture/encryption-at-rest.md` § Capability tiering); a recognized id is skipped by construction. What an honest answer buys is the one thing the log alone cannot: the invariant above — *the log is the more permissive of the pair* — enforced **retroactively**, over rows stranded before record-then-deposit landed, and over any row a hostile or buggy nest resurrects after a revoke. This is not an exception to the audit rule; it is that rule's enforcement sweep, resolving every disagreement in the only safe direction (narrow the nest), never the forbidden one (widen the client's view from a nest read). Admissibility is conditional on all of the following, each load-bearing:

- **`fauna.capabilities.reconcile` returns grant ids and NOTHING else** — reply `{ grant_ids: [16-byte id, …] }`, owner-scoped nest-side (`WHERE owner_actor_id = caller`; all rows, expired included). Ids-only is not minimalism but the mechanism: with no scope, holder, or window in the reply, feeding the Now or History lens is *unrepresentable*, not merely forbidden. (A `GrantBlob` carries no owner signature, so richer reply fields would be unverifiable nest-authored display data — the exact thing the audit rule bans.)
- **Exactly one consumer: the revoke-the-unrecognized sweep, a step of the account runtime's full pump pass behind the fleet walk (re-ruled 2026-10-06 for the fleet).** Enumerate **first**; then let the pass's fleet walk complete — the listing that brings this replica every `fauna.state.succession-ledger` row the bound nest held *after* the enumerate; then read the ledger fresh; then revoke every returned id the log does not hold **live** (no event at all, or latest event `Revoke` — `grant_log::latest_live_events` is the predicate's home). **The guarantee, stated for every replica:** an honest nest's row is never judged unrecognized while any device's log holds its event — the event reached the nest before the row did (*Record-then-deposit* above, the published form), the walk served every event row the nest held at a listing taken after the enumerate, and no honest row exists without its event; so a false revoke needs a nest that answers the enumerate with a row whose event it withholds from the feed it serves this very device, which is the nest surrendering its own holding — zero new power, exactly the polarity above. The pre-2026-10-06 form — enumerate, then read the *local* replica, at the page refresh — held for one replica only: a sibling whose pump had not yet pulled a fresh `Mint` read the id as unrecognized and revoked it, silently, with both logs still showing the grant live (a laptop back from days offline did this to every grant the phone minted meanwhile). Shapes weighed and set aside: a two-sweep deferral a convergence window apart (time-based, and a non-holder has no convergence it can observe), a writer-only sweep (judged from the same lagging replica), a nest-side refusal of a young row's reconcile revoke (a nest-authored age, and the long-offline replica stays unguarded). An id whose latest event is Mint/Renew is the log's business — renewal and expiry judgments stay log-side.
- **Nothing from the answer enters client state.** A reconcile revoke appends **no** `GrantEvent`: a `Revoke` event for an id the log never minted would be nest-influenced content entering the signed log — the precise channel the audit rule exists to close. Local diagnostics may count sweep actions; the log and both lenses may not change.
- **Bounded by the existing per-owner cap.** The nest-side reply is ≤ `MAX_GRANTS_PER_OWNER` rows by construction; the client processes at most that many ids per sweep and fires at most that many revokes, all aimed at the answering nest — a hostile oversized answer is truncated, and a revoke storm lands only on its author.
- **Automatic and silent, and only where the walk is.** The sweep runs as a step of the engine holder's full pump pass ([`../architecture/account-client-lifecycle.md`](../architecture/account-client-lifecycle.md) § The client-side lifecycle, the pump bullet — the first pass, the backstop tick, a reconnect, `reconcile_now`) — never at a page refresh (the Nests page neither enumerates nor judges), never on a non-holder (its replica converges through a co-located holder it cannot observe), never after a fleet walk that did not complete, never from a ledger this device cannot fold (an unkeyed replica, a store refusal: the read errs and the sweep does nothing). The pass acts as the account's actor over the device-principal session, the same `User` admission the page's owner session had. No confirm step and no UI surface: an orphan is invisible by construction, so no user expectation can depend on it, and the sweep restores "what the page shows is what exists". Deliberately no surface renders a nest-claimed row.
- **Own rows only, per nest.** The sweep covers the caller's `(owner, grant_id)` rows on the answering nest, and its revokes go back to that same nest. Predecessor rows in a succession belong to the succession registry's rulings over `capability_grants`, not to this sweep.
- **Sequencing prerequisite for the client half — SATISFIED 2026-08-15 for one replica, RESTATED 2026-10-06 for the fleet.** Every minting site (the succession re-mint `fauna_client_capabilities::succession::remint_capability_grants` included) records before it deposits, which is what made the single-replica sweep safe: with `Mint` durable before the row exists, a log read taken after the enumerate on the *minting* device can only be newer than the row list. Across devices that durability is local to the minter, and a sibling's replica learns of it only through the nest's feed — so the prerequisite the fleet form needs is the published one (*Record-then-deposit* above: the event acknowledged by the bound nest before the deposit), and the order the sweep needs is enumerate → walk → read; reverse any two and this prerequisite buys nothing. (The nest-side kind never had this gate.)

**Bounded-mail regime marker (ratified 2026-07-19):** a **bounded** (crypto-time-boxed, content-sealing-epochs) mail grant's `Mint`/`Renew` log events carry `tier: "bounded"` on their `content.read{mail}` scope tuple (`GRANT_SCOPE_TIER_BOUNDED`; constructor `grant_log::bounded_mail_event_scope`, predicate `grant_log::is_bounded_mail_grant`) — required on every bounded mint event from day one (the bounded plane shipped with zero production callers, so no unmarked legacy events exist). This is the log-side regime discriminator the MSEK-rotation heal driver enumerates by and the regime copy renders from. It rides an *existing* signed field because the `GrantEvent` shape is **frozen for this major**: the signature covers the canonical encoding and `verify()` re-encodes the deserialized struct, so a net-new field would fail verification on every older device — and the succession ledger's merge (`fauna.state.succession-ledger`) *drops* non-verifying events, silently erasing the mint from pre-addition devices' logs. Log-only convention: the `GrantBlob`'s own `ScopeTuple` keeps `tier: None` (nest-side scope matching untouched), and the nest's regime-crossing renew guard — not the marker — remains the enforcer, so a wrong marker is fail-safe in both directions (a mismarked master grant's heal-renew is refused; an unmarked bounded grant heals at its next renew instead of at rotation). Holder metadata (`mlkem_ek`, role, `bridge_id`) stays **out** of the log per the roster rule below — the heal driver re-resolves it from the live roster exactly as mint does.

**The per-labeler grant's log twin (ratified 2026-09-27 — the client half).** A `wasm` mail-labeler subscription's grant (`content-moderation-and-ranking.md` § Tier-3 → *Subscribing = minting a capability*, the concept owner) is a bounded mail grant whose every blob tuple carries `factor = labeler:<hex>`. The log's `GrantEventScope` mirrors `class`/`kind`/`tier` only and cannot grow a field (the frozen-shape rule above), so the factor rides **folded into `kind`** after a single space — `content.read{kind: "mail labeler:<hex>", tier: "bounded"}` plus `content.label-write{kind: "labeler:<hex>"}` (`grant_log::bounded_mail_labeler_event_scope`; read back through `GrantEventScope::base_kind` / `::factor`, never by comparing `kind` raw; `grant_log::labeler_factor_of_grant` is the grant-level read and `current_labeler_grant` the subscription's lookup). **Why `kind`, not `tier`:** every consumer that acts on a mail tuple keys on `kind == "mail"` — the bounded predicate, the succession re-mint's payload derivation, the rotation-heal driver, this page's scope copy — so a device older than the fold sees an unknown kind and leaves the grant alone (its re-mint fails closed as an unknown read kind, its heal skips it as unbounded, its copy falls back to the raw text); folded into `tier`, that same older device would read a plain `content.read{mail}` and re-mint a **standing, factor-less** mail key to the MDA — a widening the owner never consented to. The fold lands where an older reader's ignorance is fail-safe. A current reader treats the grant as bounded (`is_bounded_mail_grant` looks through the fold), re-mints it through `mint_bounded_mail_labeler_grant`, heals and renews it with the factor on every wrap (`bounded_mail_rotation_heal_keys` / `bounded_mail_renewal_keys` take it), and renders each tuple naming the labeler's short id — *Mail — only to run community labeler ‹id› over it* / *Write labels for community labeler ‹id›* (`scope_label`; `nests.scope_mail_labeler` / `scope_labeler_labels`) — never a second anonymous mail row. The Now lens lists it beside the composed mail grant; unsubscribe is its `Revoke` in History.

### Trust facet — backup rows (home-nest row; ratified 2026-07-24)

The nest-side segment-backup redesign (`../behavior/backup-restore.md` § Background Tasks; `../behavior/backup-destinations.md` § State & data shape) mints two standing grants at destination-enroll, **both empowering the source (home) nest** — so both render on the **home nest's row** (user decision 2026-07-24: the trust facet lists powers *this nest* holds; the deferred Q-F destination badge stays deferred, `participants.md`):

1. **The `NestBackupKey` seal grant** — one row when `fauna.backup.status` reports `enrolled` (read over the already-open home connection via `BackupClient::status`). Scope copy: the nest is trusted to seal and upload the owner's message backups. Revoke dispatches `fauna.backup.nest_key.revoke` (the nest can no longer seal new segments; existing destination custody is untouched).
2. **One writer-grant row per backup destination** — the source nest's authorization to write custody at that destination, read via `fauna.backup.writer_grant.list` **over the destination's own authenticated connection** (never the federation channel), and revoked there too (`fauna.backup.writer_grant.revoke`) — which is what keeps the freeze-the-backup affordance operable with the source nest fully hostile. The destination set iterated is the client's own destination list, the `fauna.state.backup` kind's row for the home box (pinned client-side, source-untrusted). An unreachable destination renders `status: unreachable`; a configured destination whose writer list lacks the home nest renders `status: missing`. "The home nest" is its **connection-bound id**, never its own `fauna.nest.info` claim — rows and revoke alike (owner: [`../behavior/backup-destinations.md`](../behavior/backup-destinations.md) § Create step 3). A revoke the destination answers `revoked: false` is settled by re-reading its writer list over the same connection: the grant still listed ⇒ the revoke failed and the user is told; absent ⇒ nothing was left to revoke (a retry after a lost acknowledgement), not an error.

These rows are **live nest reads, not grant-event-log folds** — deliberately outside the signed `GrantEvent` log (its scope shape is frozen for this major, and both grants are verifiable/revocable against the live stores, which cannot drift the way a client-side mirror could). Consequences: no `lasts-until` / renew / auto-renew (both grants are standing until revoked), and no History-lens entries. Rendered copy uses trust vocabulary ("trusted to…"), never "capability"/"grant" (§ Naming). A home nest holding a backup row but zero content grants does **not** show `nest-trust-empty`.

### Trust facet — generation recovery (home-nest row; ratified 2026-07-29)

Revoking a rogue source's writer grant *freezes* the damage; it does not undo it. Because the custody writer is the owner's source nest and a writer's supersede power is delete power, the destination retains every superseded generation for the custody grace window `T` = 30 d (`../architecture/segment-backup-protocol.md` § *Custody grace window (T)* owns the mechanics). **These rows are the user-facing half of that mitigation** — what the owner can actually roll back to once they have revoked. They render on the **same home-nest row, immediately after the backup trust rows** (user decision 2026-07-29): one surface, so "who may write here" and "what can I roll back" are read together rather than on two pages.

- **Read** — `fauna.backup.generation.list` per configured destination, over the client's **own** authenticated connection to that destination, never through the source nest and never over the federation channel. That is the whole point: recovery must work with the source fully hostile. The destination set iterated is the client's own pinned destination list (the `fauna.state.backup` kind's row for the home box), source-untrusted by construction — the same set the writer-grant rows walk.
- **Restore** — `fauna.backup.generation.restore` promotes one generation back to live, spoken to the destination on that same connection.
- **Ordering is the destination's** — newest supersede first, deliberately preserved rather than re-sorted, so one nest-side ordering rule serves all 7 apps.
- **`expires_at` is derived from the window the destination reported** (`superseded_at + grace_secs`), never from a client-side copy of `T`. A client that hard-codes 30 d would lie the moment a destination ran a different window.

Three states the copy must distinguish — all three are ratified requirements, not flavor:

1. **A failed read is not an empty one.** A destination that could not be asked renders `nest-trust-generation-status: unreachable` with **no** restore affordance. An empty list rendered as "nothing to recover" is precisely the false reassurance a hostile source or a dropped connection buys, and it would be read by the one user who most needs the truth. (Enforced in shared Rust by `GenerationsStatus::Unreachable` being a distinct variant from an empty `Listed`; `DestinationGenerations::has_recoverable()` is what a shell branches on, never `generations.is_empty()`.)
2. **A path-less row is still shown.** `path_hash` is one-way, so a custody row predating the additive `path` column has no plaintext path; the shell renders the hash. It must never hide or skip such a row — the rows a rogue source produced are exactly the ones a user needs to see.
3. **"Too late" is not "broken".** A restore that returns `RestoreOutcome::NoSuchGeneration` means the generation is unknown or already reclaimed past `T`; the copy says *this version is past the recovery window*, never a failure message. Only one of the two is worth retrying, so they need different words.

**Required copy — the quota bound.** Retained generations are **quota-charged for the whole window** (deliberate: quota *is* the supersede rate cap, ratified 2026-07-23). A user near their cap on a destination can therefore see usage a supersede storm inflated until `T` elapses, and the surface that lists those generations is where that is explicable rather than mysterious.

Like the backup rows above, these are **live destination reads, not grant-event-log folds** — no `lasts-until`, no renew, no History-lens entries.

### Trust facet — custody rows (ratified 2026-08-15; W8 (account-data-plane.md § Workstreams) — built on tui 2026-08-17, § Implementation status today)

The nest-shaped half of the participant custody facet ([`../behavior/participants.md`](../behavior/participants.md) § The participant model → Custody facet): one row per **custodian nest** — a nest holding sealed copies of this account's planes under a keyless custody grant (a friend's nest, an archive or second nest). Mechanism + grant shape: [`../architecture/account-replica-posture.md`](../architecture/account-replica-posture.md) § Replica posture → *The custody grant + ceremony*.

- **These rows ARE grant-event-log folds** — the custody grant's mint/renew/revoke are signed `GrantEvent`s, so the Now/History lenses, `lasts-until`, renew/auto-renew, and revoke all come from the existing regime (unlike the backup and generation rows above). The scope copy is trust vocabulary: *"trusted to hold sealed copies — cannot read them"*.
- **Receipt freshness rides the row.** Beside the grant state the row renders the custodian's latest known custody receipt — held-bytes summary and *last confirmed ‹time›* — and it inherits the three-state honesty rule from the generation rows: **fresh / stale / no-receipt-yet are three different states with different words**; a custodian whose receipts have gone stale renders as *degraded redundancy the owner sees* (the A7 rule), never as an empty or omitted row.
- **Required copy — the custody floor at mint, the honest bound at revoke.** The offer/mint flow states what the custodian will see (scope ids, sizes, change timing — the shape, never content: *"choosing custodians is choosing who sees that shape"*), and revoke copy states its honest bound: revocation stops future carriage and serving on honest boxes; **copies already held stay held — and stay sealed forever** (the keyless tier never had read reach to lose).
- **Mint is the ceremony, not the content-grant picker.** Adding a custodian runs the offer → accept → mint ceremony (the accept happens on the *host's* app), a different gesture from `nest-trust-mint-scope-select`'s scope-first content mint — the picker is deliberately not reused. Default scope set = `Account` (single-principal planes; shared-audience scopes are explicit-only per the charter's carve-out).
- **Which custodies land here:** the ones the accept bound to a NEST — the nest-custodian identity fact ([`../architecture/account-replica-posture.md`](../architecture/account-replica-posture.md) § Replica posture → The custody grant + ceremony, the device-or-nest bullet) owns the split; device-bound custodies render on Devices instead, never both.
- **Element IDs:** the rule-A-approved set (2026-08-16) lives in ui.yaml — `nest-trust-custody-item` and children, all indexed.

### Trust facet — History lens

The **History** lens renders that nest's raw grant-event timeline (`grant_log::history_for_grant` / `history`), most-recent-first — mint / renew / revoke events, each self-describing (it carries the grant's scope + window as of that event). A signed `Revoke` event carries no scope of its own (the frozen `GrantEvent` shape), so its row names the scope that grant last carried — the newest scoped event for the same `grant_id`, resolved in the shared fold (`view_model::history_for_holders`), display-only, the signed log untouched. Now and History are two views of **one** event-sourced log (Now = projection, History = the log), which is exactly why the store is an append-only *signed* log and not a mutable table: the same store that must be client-authoritative for the current-grants view *is* the forensic record for free.

### Honest bound (required copy — spec requirement, not flavor)

Every grant row MUST state the honest bound of revocation/expiry, per regime (design § Phase 2 Step 1 (e); `2026-07-04-…-design.md:114`,`:119`; NEXT slice-3 requirement):

- **Standing-keyed kind (every content kind today):** revoking stops this nest **re-acquiring** the key and stops future sessions — *on an honest box*. It does **not** un-see what was already read, and does **not** cryptographically bound content that arrives after revocation until that kind adopts content-sealing epochs. So `lasts-until` bounds *future epoch acquisition only* — "nothing at all for a standing-keyed kind" beyond honest re-acquisition (`:104`).
- **Epoch-sealed kind (post-Phase-3):** the stronger, crypto-bounded wording (future content is cryptographically unreachable after the window).

The net sentence the copy must never over-promise past: *a content capability bounds future access and narrows a leak's suspect set; it never un-sees the past, and against a malicious operator it bounds future content only for a kind sealed under a rotating content-sealing epoch — a primitive that does not exist today* (`:119`). All strings live in `i18n/strings/en.yaml` (never per-app).

### Expiry / renewal — first-class states

Grants carry a finite default `lasts-until` (~90 days, a hard-coded Rust constant — no config surface; product invariant). The **minting client auto-renews** grants for nests the user has *blessed*; un-blessed / one-off grants get short (hours) windows by default (*Duration and blessing* below). `nest-trust-grant-status` renders the liveness state so a lapsed grant reads as "background processing paused — renew here", **never as silent feature loss**:

- **active** — inside its window.
- **expiring soon** — within the renew-ahead threshold.
- **expired** — window elapsed; the nest has gone dark; `nest-trust-grant-renew` is the recovery.
- **auto-renewing** — the blessed-box indicator; the minting client renews it in the background (app-foreground + periodic check; phones can't background reliably — same constraint as index building).

**Duration and blessing (ratified 2026-09-24).** The user makes exactly two choices, both here: **(1) a per-grant duration** on `nest-trust-mint-duration-select`, one of two hard-coded windows — *one-off* (`ONE_OFF_GRANT_WINDOW_SECS`, 8 hours) or *standard* (`DEFAULT_GRANT_WINDOW_SECS`, ~90 days); the picker pre-selects standard on a blessed nest and one-off on an un-blessed one (the row's `mint_default_duration`, computed in shared Rust), and a `Mint` that names no duration takes that same default; **(2) a per-nest blessing** on `nest-trust-blessed-toggle`, persisted in the synced `fauna.state.blessed-nests` account-plane kind (§ Data shape). The **auto-renew loop** (shared Rust, `LinkedNestsMachine`) renews every current grant that is (a) held by a blessed nest's holder, (b) *standing* — its mint-time window is longer than the renew-ahead threshold, so a one-off grant is never auto-renewed and lasts its hours — (c) inside the renew-ahead threshold, (d) not yet expired (an expired grant is the user's to renew, as `compute_liveness` already says) — each renewed with what its regime needs (ratified 2026-09-27; the pre-2026-09-27 keyless rule excluded bounded grants from both renew paths): a master-key grant as a pure window bump; a **bounded** mail grant (`grant_log::is_bounded_mail_grant`, the per-labeler subscription grant included) with the epoch wraps for the window it extends into — `bounded_mail_renewal_keys`, computed from the recorded old end, sealed to the holder as the nest's **live roster** names it (the log keeps the holder's pubkey, never its seal-target key material), every wrap carrying the grant's own labeler factor — and the manual renew does the same. The wraps are sealed **before** the `Renew` is recorded: a bounded grant whose holder the roster no longer lists is skipped by the loop and refused by the manual renew with its recorded end unmoved (a keyless bump would move that end, skipping the epochs in between — and the nest refuses such a bump outright, [`../architecture/encryption-at-rest.md`](../architecture/encryption-at-rest.md) § Capability tiering → *Content-sealing epochs*, the coverage guard). **Which nest is blessed is decided on the identity the connection proved, never on the nest's own `fauna.nest.info` claim** (`LinkedNestsNest::bound_nest_id`: the origin's login pin — SPKI-bound on native TLS, possession-verified on web, per [`../architecture/security.md`](../architecture/security.md) § Transport trust — or, with no pin, a possession proof over the connection): the home row's `nest_id`, its blessing and the one-tap blessing all read it, and the loop renews nothing when the nest's claim differs from it, so a box the user never blessed cannot borrow a blessed sibling's id. A renewal extends to now + that grant's own mint-time length and slides the window start to one mint-length behind now, never backward (`grant_log::renewal_window`; a manual renew does the same), log first then nest, like every widening call — the window *slides* rather than grows, so a bounded grant's epoch wraps never outgrow the calendar; the nest prunes the wraps below the new start in the same renew (the retention ruling, [`../architecture/encryption-at-rest.md`](../architecture/encryption-at-rest.md) § Capability tiering → *Content-sealing epochs*). **Auto-renewing** liveness is shown only on grants the loop will renew. The loop runs on every Nests-machine refresh and on the `AutoRenew` action every app dispatches at foreground and then every `AUTO_RENEW_CHECK_SECS` (1 hour) while running; phones renew only while foregrounded. It is silent and best-effort, like the reconcile sweep: a failed renewal changes nothing the page shows except that the grant keeps approaching expiry, and the liveness states above stay the honest signal. Its *due* decision reads the trust facet's render clock (`trust_clock`), so a test that moves that clock sees a renewal; the renewed window is stamped from the real clock. **The onboarding one-tap "trust this box" blesses the home nest** as part of the tap (its standard-length grants then renew themselves; [`../behavior/onboarding.md`](../behavior/onboarding.md) § 3b-ter).

An **MSEK hard-revoke re-derives the recipient-mail keypair**, so an outstanding `content.read{mail,calendar}` grant keeps opening OLD content but AEAD-fails on NEW content — the auto-renew loop treats MSEK rotation as a re-wrap trigger and the log records the rotation (design into the VM slice; this rotation-on-revoke is today's de-facto coarse crypto-expiry for mail grants).

## Where logic lives

Shared Rust view-model, thin per-app shells (priority #2). The Nests-page machine **extends / reuses `LinkedNestsMachine`** (`libs/fauna-client-pair`, per `linked-nests.md` § Where logic lives) — same snapshot + dispatch shape — adding the trust facet:

- **List** nests: the existing `Refresh` → `fauna.pair.list` (owner-implicit) — whose reply's additive `forward_queue` the machine projects to `LinkedNestsSnapshot.forward_queue` (§ Forward queue); the two queue actions `RetryForwards` / `DiscardForwards` are machine actions like every other mutation, so no shell holds queue logic.
- **A nest's holder set** (the load-bearing link): a grant's `holder` is a **content-processor service-user X25519 pubkey**, not the nest's Ed25519 identity (design § 2.4 — `holder_pubkey = bridge_service_users.x25519_pubkey`). A nest row therefore maps to *its* holders by enumerating that nest's enrolled content-processor service-users over the **bridges seam** (`fauna.bridges.list_service_users`, filtered `has_x25519`; the actual pubkey via `fetch_bridge_pubkey`) — the same reuse-enrollment the DKIM/TLS seal already rides. The holder→nest association is **not** stored in the grant log (the log stays kind-generic); it is recomputed from the live roster, so a lying nest merely fails to decrypt (the grant is sealed to the real pubkey) rather than forging a grouping. **`CONTENT_PROCESSOR_ROLES = ["mda", "content-processor"]`** — the MDA keeps its own specific role; every other content-processor variant (web-serve, a future scorer/indexer) shares the one generic `content-processor` role, distinguished only by `bridge_id` (e.g. `"mda-1"` vs `"web-serve"`), surfaced to the client as `LinkedNestRow.available_holders: Vec<AvailableHolder{bridge_id, role}>` for the mint picker.
- **Per-nest grants (Now lens)**: `view_model::trust_facet_for_holders(ledger, holders, now, blessed)` folds `grant_log::current_grants` to the grants whose `holder` is in that nest's holder set, each projected with a `now`-relative liveness (active / expiring-soon / expired / auto-renewing). **History lens**: `view_model::history_for_holders(ledger, holders)` — the same holder-filtering over the raw event log (`grant_log::history`), most-recent-first, keeping Revoke.
- **Blessing + auto-renew**: `SetBlessed{nest_id, blessed}` records the verdict through the machine's `BlessedNestsStore` seam — the account plane's `fauna.state.blessed-nests` kind, one row per nest, a local door-put the runtime publishes (the shared impl `fauna_account_seams::blessed_nests::PlaneBlessedNests`, wired by every app over its account-store handle; an unreadable verdict renders un-blessed); the renew-due fold is `view_model::grants_due_for_renewal(ledger, blessed_holders, now)` and the machine's sweep renews what it returns, in one ledger merge then one nest bump per grant (§ Expiry / renewal → *Duration and blessing*). `AutoRenew` runs the sweep and refreshes only when it renewed something.
- **Mint / Renew / Revoke** actions: compose `mint_grant` (holder pubkey from `fetch_bridge_pubkey`) + `CapabilitiesClient::{mint,renew,revoke}` (`libs/fauna-client-capabilities::rpc`) + `grant_log::record_*`, recording each event through the succession-ledger seam (`fauna_client_config::SuccessionLedgerStore` — a per-row join through the account store's writer door, no CAS) and signing each `GrantEvent` with the identity key held behind the signer seam. **The order of those two halves is load-bearing, not stylistic** — *Record-then-deposit* above; `grant_log::UndepositedGrant` enforces it for every minting site, in this crate and any other.

**v1 holder scope (resolved 2026-07-07):** v1 renders the trust facet for the **connected/home nest** — the nest the client holds a bearer connection to, whose service-user roster is directly enumerable and where the proven `content.read{mail}`→MDA flow lives (tier_3 `test_capability_rescore_drain.py`). Enumerating a *linked* (relay) nest's holders needs a second authenticated connection (the `LinkedNestsNest::connect_peer` machinery) and is a documented follow-on, not a v1 gap — a linked nest with no locally-known holders shows the `nest-trust-empty` state.

Exposed over UniFFI (native) + WASM (web); the per-app shell renders the snapshot and dispatches actions with no trust logic of its own. Renewal-policy constants (default lifetime, renew-ahead threshold `view_model::RENEW_AHEAD_SECS`, retry cadence, one-off window) are hard-coded Rust constants — the only user choices are per-grant duration + blessed-box status, made in this UI. A grant's `lasts_until` and a History-lens entry's `at` are raw unix-seconds; rendering them as a local wall-clock string is `fauna_core::format::format_unix_local` (`value-formatting.md` § Absolute local timestamp display) — the shell composes the surrounding label (`scope_label`/`status_label`/`history_line`) but does not hand-roll the timestamp-to-string step itself.

## Data shape

- The grant log: `fauna.state.succession-ledger`'s `event/<grant id>/<sig>` rows — one signed `GrantEvent` (`fauna_core::grant_event`) each, write-once, fleet-only and tip-sealed — read as the `SuccessionLedger` fold (owner [`../architecture/config-dissolution.md`](../architecture/config-dissolution.md) § Phases and gates → *Bounded rows* → *The ledger*).
- `BlessedNest { nest_id, blessed, at }` (`fauna_core::data`) — one per nest the user ever toggled, each its own `fauna.state.blessed-nests` row on the account plane (fleet-only, tip-sealed; [`../architecture/config-dissolution.md`](../architecture/config-dissolution.md) § The `__config` dissolution schedule owns the kind, its key shape and posture). The former `UserConfig.blessed_nests` blob field retired with the `__config` rail on 2026-10-02 (same doc's § The `__config` dissolution schedule → *The closure order*, step (6)). **Merge:** per `nest_id`, the newer `at` wins; on an equal `at` the un-blessed entry wins (the more restrictive verdict — blessing is what lets a box's read grants renew without the user). An absent entry is un-blessed.
- A grant's **chosen duration** is not stored separately: it is its `Mint` event's `window_end - window_start` (`grant_log::minted_window_secs`), which a renewal preserves because the chosen duration is read from the `Mint` event alone — a `Renew` slides both `window_start` and `window_end` (the retention ruling, [`../architecture/encryption-at-rest.md`](../architecture/encryption-at-rest.md) § Capability tiering → *Content-sealing epochs*), so the current window's width is not the chosen length.
- Folds: `grant_log::current_grants(ledger) -> Vec<CurrentGrant>` (Now — re-exported from `fauna_core::grant_event`, its current owner), `history(ledger)` / `history_for_grant(ledger, grant_id)` (History) (`libs/fauna-client-capabilities/src/grant_log.rs:339`,`:444`,`:454`).
- Per-nest projections (`libs/fauna-client-capabilities/src/view_model.rs`): `trust_facet_for_holders(ledger, holders, now, blessed) -> TrustFacet { grants: Vec<GrantView> }` (Now — the fold above filtered to a nest's holder set + `now`-relative `GrantLiveness` Active/ExpiringSoon/Expired/AutoRenewing; an empty facet is the `nest-trust-empty` state) and `history_for_holders(ledger, holders) -> Vec<HistoryEntry>` (History — the raw per-nest event timeline, most-recent-first, keeps Revoke).
- The row model is kind-generic (`holder` / `grant_id` are opaque identities), forward-compatible with the north-star participant row (`participants.md` § participant model) — v1 renders only the trust facet; role badges + quota display are Phase-2 facets (below).
