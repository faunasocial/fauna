## Goal

The Profile page is the **canonical per-user surface** — the one detail view of any user (your own or anyone else's, whether or not they are a contact). It shows that user's identity (avatar, display name, handle, actor_id), their posts, followers / following counts, the per-user **relationship actions** (start DM, request contact, follow, block) — and it is the **creator storefront**: the Tiers tab hosts subscription-tier management (own profile) and browse/subscribe (another's). Because it is the per-user *detail* view, it is also where Contacts navigates *to* — see § Relationship to Contacts. The non-subscription destinations remain placeholders; existence of this doc means future profile work must be checked against it (a hard stop: raise the gap before building against it) rather than assumed.

## Relationship to Contacts

Profile and Contacts are **one "user" concept in two roles** — *detail* and *list* — not two representations of a user:

- **Profile = the per-user *detail* view.** One page about one actor; unbounded (every actor has one); the home for every per-user action (DM, request-contact, follow/subscribe, block). Asymmetric-friendly: you can open and subscribe to a creator who will never be your contact.
- **Contacts (`contacts.md`) = a *list* you own** — your accepted contacts + the pending-knock inbox; a bounded subset of users (the *symmetric* mutual edges). Each row **links into** the actor's Profile and renders via the shared `user-header` component.

So the contact-edge **lifecycle** (knock / accept / block / confirm, the `ContactStatus` edge) is owned by `contacts.md` and lives nest-side; the Profile is a *caller* that surfaces those actions on the detail view and reads `contact_status` into its snapshot. Contacts does **not** re-implement per-user detail, and the Profile does **not** fork the edge model. This list→detail decomposition keeps "a user" a single concept across both surfaces (priorities #1/#3/#4).

## Layout & flow

**Reached via** a top-level `profile-tab` nav destination (sidebar on desktop, bottom-tab / "more" on mobile — the same `{page}-tab` model as every other page; ratified 2026-06-17), which opens the **viewer's own** profile. **Another user's** profile opens by tap-through — a contact row (`contacts.md`), a post author in the feed, or a search result (search already filters by `profile`). The viewed actor is the page's input; everything below branches on `is_self`.

The page header is the shared **`user-header`** component (avatar, display name, handle, `profile-actor-id-copy-btn`) — the *same* component a contact row renders, so the two surfaces never drift (priorities #1/#4). After the header:

- a **primary relationship action**: `profile-edit-button` on own profile; `profile-follow-button` (*follow = subscribe to the free "followers" tier*) on another's;
- on **another's** profile only, **secondary relationship actions** — start DM, request contact, block/unblock — reusing the contact-edge lifecycle owned by `contacts.md`. **IDs ratified 2026-06-21 (user-approved):** `profile-start-dm-button`, `profile-request-contact-button`, `profile-block-button` (one block/unblock toggle whose label flips on `contact_status`), in ui.yaml's `profile` block as OTHER-only elements alongside `profile-follow-button` — see § Element IDs. A fourth secondary action, `profile-report-button` (report this account), is **user-approved 2026-09-25 and in ui.yaml** (OTHER-only, beside `profile-block-button`; unbuilt on every app) — owner [`../behavior/moderation.md`](../behavior/moderation.md) § User-initiated reporting.

Below the header is a **tab strip**:

- **`profile-posts-tab`** (default) — the user's posts. **TBD** (forward-pointer; only the tab landmark is specced).
- **`profile-tiers-tab`** — the **subscription UX**, which branches on `is_self`:
  - **Own profile (author management)** — three sections: §1 **My tiers** (`subscription-tiers-section` — list + create/edit/delete), §2 **Pending requests** (`subscription-requests-section` — approve/reject; approve transparently mints + uploads a KeyBlob), §3 **Subscribers** (`subscription-subscribers-section` — per-tier roster + remove).
  - **Another's profile (subscriber browse)** — `subscription-offers-section`: the creator's offered tiers with per-row Subscribe (the free followers tier uses `profile-follow-button`), price hint, external payment link, and the viewer's status.

Followers / following tabs and bio/links are still TBD — settle before behavior-changing work on them. (Consumer-side "my subscriptions across creators" is **not** here — it's the separate `subscription-settings` page; `monetization.md` § Pillar 1.)

### The private section — your nickname, notes and labels on this person (ratified 2026-09-19; IDs approved 2026-09-25; built on tui 2026-09-26, linux 2026-10-01, android, macos and ios 2026-10-03)

On **another's** profile only, below the relationship actions and above the tab strip, a section visibly marked as private ("Only you can see this") edits the **private contact overlay** for the viewed actor. The overlay itself — record, merge, succession fold, where the nickname paints — is owned by [`contacts.md`](contacts.md) § The private overlay; this page owns only the edit surface, which lives here because the Profile is the home of every per-user action and because the overlay is **not gated on a contact edge** (a knock sender, a blocked account and a creator you merely follow all have a Profile; none need a roster row). It never renders on the viewer's own profile.

- **Fields:** a single-line nickname, a multi-line notes field, and the person's labels as a removable list with an add field (completion offers the labels already used on other people — the derived vocabulary).
- **One explicit Save, staged edits.** Nickname, notes, and label adds/removes are staged locally and committed together by the save button — the `profile-edit-form` precedent, and the shape a terminal UI can express. **Save writes only the registers the user changed**: the overlay merges per field on each register's stamp, so re-stamping an untouched field would let this device's stale copy beat another device's newer edit of it. Shared Rust owns the staging itself and that diff (`fauna_core::contact_overlay::OverlayEditor` — untouched fields follow the live overlay, the first edit snapshots the baseline, the staged form → changed-register set), the bounds validation and its `LocalizedText` refusals; app glue owns the text widgets only.
- **Errors** surface on the page's existing `error-message`: a bounds refusal, and the plane's refusal to seal while no generation tip resolves for this device ([`../architecture/account-data-taxonomy.md`](../architecture/account-data-taxonomy.md) § The audience ladder → *The contact-overlay rung*). A refused save keeps the staged edits on screen.
- **The header shows both names.** With a nickname set, the header's primary line (`profile-handle`) is the nickname and a secondary line carries the public name it replaced ([`../behavior/value-formatting.md`](../behavior/value-formatting.md) § Peer display label — `PeerLabel.public`), so the page never hides who this actually is. Clearing the nickname restores the one-line header.

**Element IDs (rule A — approved by the user 2026-09-25 exactly as listed; in ui.yaml since the tui build, 2026-09-26):** on this page, OTHER-only — `profile-public-name` (text; rendered only while a nickname is the primary line), `profile-private-section` (view), `profile-nickname-field` (input), `profile-notes-field` (input, multi-line), the `profile-label-list` component (`indexed: true` rows of `profile-label-chip` text + `profile-label-remove-button`), `profile-label-field` (input) + `profile-label-add-button`, and `profile-private-save-button`. On the contacts roster row ([`contacts.md`](contacts.md)) — `contact-public-name` (text, indexed; same render condition) and `contact-labels` (text, indexed; the person's live labels on one line, absent when there are none). "Nickname" is the product word throughout, deliberately not "alias", which the mail surfaces already use for address aliases.

## State & data shape

`profile_snapshot(actor_id)` → `ProfileSnapshot` is the observer-driven render model (no client-side caching — same rule as `feed.md` § Architectural rules):

```
ProfileSnapshot {
  actor_id, handle, display_name, avatar_url,   // identity (from the Profile record)
  bio, followers_count, following_count,         // placeholder fields — None/0 until those tabs land
  is_self: bool,                                 // drives edit-vs-follow + which tab branch renders
  contact_status: Option<ContactStatus>,         // from the contacts edge (pending/accepted/confirmed/blocked); None if no edge
  follow_status: FollowStatus,                   // derived from the subscription to the "followers" tier
  posts: Vec<PostSummary>,                        // empty until the Posts tab is built
}
```

`contact_status` is the *same* `ContactStatus` `contacts.md` defines (one source of truth for the edge). `follow_status` and the Tiers-tab data are the `SubscriptionsSnapshot` shapes owned by `monetization.md` § Pillar 1 — the profile *composes* them, it does not redefine them.

The `identity` fields (`handle`, `display_name`, `avatar_url`, `bio`) come from the `Profile` record via `fauna.profile.get`; the publish path (`fauna.profile.set` + the text-only edit form) is **built on all 7 apps** (§ Implementation status today). A fresh actor stays `not_found` until it saves the edit form (publish-on-first-edit — no onboarding auto-seed); until then the SELF profile shell renders `actor_id` + `handle` (from `fauna.account.get`) and the Tiers-tab author management (over the landed `SubscriptionsAuthor`), which is `monetization.md` § Pillar 1's Slice-A definition and needs neither `fauna.profile.get` nor the rich-identity fields.

## Where logic lives

All of it is shared Rust; the profile page is glue over existing seams plus one new fetch kind.

- **Profile fetch.** `fauna.profile.get(actor_id) -> Profile` WS-RPC kind — in the **existing** `fauna.profile.*` namespace, alongside `fauna.profile.handle.change` (do not fork a new `fauna.profiles.*`). The `Profile` record is signed plaintext (verify the Ed25519 signature on receipt; § Encryption at rest), wrapped by a thin `libs/fauna-client-profile` `ProfileClient<R>` (mirrors `fauna-client-posts` / `fauna-client-feed`). **LANDED** (read half) — the nest handler serves the stored `schema = 'profile'` content row verbatim as `ProfileGetReply::body`; `fauna-client-profile::decode_profile` decodes it (signed `EmbedAsBytes`, verified; signed-only — the bare fallback that once read pre-publish-path rows went with the 2026-09-24 compat-remnant sweep). The companion **publish/edit** path is the next bullet (ratified 2026-06-18); until it ships, `fauna.profile.get` returns `fauna.profile.not_found` and the shell falls back to `actor_id` + `handle`.
- **Profile publish/edit (`fauna.profile.set`).** RATIFIED 2026-06-18 (user-approved); **shared-Rust + nest half BUILT 2026-06-18** (wire types `libs/fauna-protocol/src/profile.rs`; nest handler `bins/fauna-nest/src/profile_handlers.rs::profile_set_handler`; shared decoder `fauna_core::encoding::decode_profile`; client `fauna-client-profile::{build_profile, ProfileClient::profile_set}`; tier_3 `conformance_profile.rs`). A **client-originated own-write** WS-RPC kind in the existing `fauna.profile.*` namespace (do **not** fork `fauna.profiles.*`), mirroring `fauna.posts.create`: the owner's app builds its `Profile`, `sign_and_pack`s it into the signed `EmbedAsBytes` wire, and uploads it as `ProfileSetRequest::body`; the nest **verifies the Ed25519 signature at ingest, asserts `profile.actor_id == authenticated caller`, then stores** the signed bytes as the `schema = 'profile'` content row. Wrapped client-side by a thin `fauna-client-profile::ProfileClient::profile_set`; auth gate `require_permission(actor_id, "fauna.profile.set")` (`User | Admin`, the personal-account gate its sibling `fauna.profile.handle.change` uses — the handler's caller-match keeps the write to the caller's own profile).
  - **Why client-originated (no nest-side seed).** The `Profile` is signed by its own `actor_id` (`fauna_core::encoding`'s `Signed for Profile`) and the nest never holds the user's secret key, so the nest **cannot** mint a signed profile — a nest-written profile would be unsigned, inventing a trust class § Encryption at rest does not sanction, and `activitypub::actor_routes` already serves a `minimal_person()` from account fields when no profile exists, so an empty seed buys nothing. Every genuine `Profile` therefore originates on the owner's app.
  - **Stored shape + the `actor_routes` reconciliation.** The stored/at-rest payload is the *signed `EmbedAsBytes`* (envelope + canonical `Profile`), and the **signed-only verify-and-decode is lifted into one shared helper** consumed by **both** `fauna-client-profile::decode_profile` (the `profile.get` read) **and** the nest's `activitypub::actor_routes` (federation serve) — so both verify the signature, and `actor_routes` is reconciled off its current bare-only `canonical_decode::<Profile>` (priority #2). (Storing bare `Profile` instead is rejected: `profile.get` could not then verify the signature, violating § Encryption at rest.)
  - **Write semantics.** *Immediate* — no pending-action delay, unlike its sibling `fauna.profile.handle.change` (which delays 6 h because a handle is a scarce shared namespace; display fields are free-form per-actor with no impersonation surface). The row is **appended** and `profile.get` reads latest-by-`created_at` (the landed read shape); the handler **prunes the author's superseded `schema='profile'` rows in the same transaction** (keep-latest-1) — the only row a profile-write drops is the author's own superseded display state (recreatable; carry a one-line "superseded by newer signed profile" comment per the no-user-data-loss carve-out).
  - **Field ownership (ruled 2026-09-19).** `fauna.profile.set` carries the whole signed `Profile`; the user *edits* `display_name` / `bio` / `links` / `avatar` / `banner`, and **every other field is carried read-modify-write from the stored profile the writer admits as its base** (empty defaults on first publish) — never populated from app state. Which bases a writer admits, and which fields survive from which base, is § Persistence → *After an identity succession, the successor RE-PUBLISHES* → the admission rule. The superseded wording (*"the app populates the non-display fields from state it already holds"*) described no writer that ever shipped: no app writes `inbox_mode`, `admin_nests` or `load_hint` onto its profile, so the profile's `inbox_mode` is a writer-less public field carrying whatever the last admitted base did (`Open`, from the edit form), while the authoritative inbox mode is the nest's per-actor `inbox_modes` row — written over `fauna.inbox.mode.set`, read by every gate that enforces it (§ Encryption at rest; owner [`../behavior/direct-messages.md`](../behavior/direct-messages.md) § Reach policy). `handle` is **not** on the `Profile` (it stays account state in the `users` table — no two-source-of-truth). The one producer outside the edit form is the `nests` field's primary entry: `fauna_client_profile::publish_recovery_head`'s landed-registration mirror fills it with the dialed home-nest URL when the list is empty and never displaces an authored list — mechanism + rationale owned by [`../behavior/identity-succession.md`](../behavior/identity-succession.md) § The succession statement (wire) → *the peer-profile harvest*.
    - **The avatar/banner sub-flow: shared-Rust half landed 2026-07-29, IDs approved 2026-07-29, tui leg landed 2026-07-29** (the other 6 apps lift the same path — see § Implementation status today). A save expresses each image field as a three-state `fauna_client_profile::ProfileImageEdit` (`Keep` / `Clear` / `Set(ContentHash)`) threaded through `build_edited_profile_with_images`, because over the read-modify-write base "leave the picture alone" and "remove the picture" are different edits that `Option<ContentHash>` cannot both express; `Keep`/`Keep` **is** the text-only behaviour, so the existing `build_edited_profile` delegates and every app's pre-existing call site is unchanged. `decode_profile_display` now also projects the two stored refs, so a form can render the current picture and offer a remove affordance — no app does yet (the edit form starts every image field at `Keep`/empty rather than pre-seeding the current picture's presence, same as `compose-file` starting empty; rendering the *stored* picture is the `user-header` component's job, unbuilt on every app pending its own ui.yaml ID). Faces: UniFFI `build_edited_profile_with_images` + `FfiProfileImageEdit`, wasm `build_edited_profile_with_images` (payload-less enums force each field's three states into a `clear: bool` + `hash_hex: Option<String>` argument pair — the `ProfileImageEdit::from_parts` adapter). The picture **bytes** are an ordinary blob upload through the public-post path, not a new mechanism — [`media.md`](media.md) § Encryption at rest owns that shape; tui's leg rides the identical `fauna_client::upload_public_post_blob` feed's `compose-file` uses, over a staged local path (no OS file picker on tui — § Element IDs).
  - **One write door, and it accepts a delegated signature (2026-07-29, F2.3).** The verify-then-store half is `profile_handlers::ingest_profile_core`, shared by `fauna.profile.set` and by the ATProto PDS's external-app profile round-trip — the same share-the-gate discipline `ingest_post_core` gives posts, so the second caller provably cannot store a profile the first would reject. The gate stays **strict signed-only**, like the read-side `decode_profile`, but the signature may now be the account's server-held authoring sub-key rather than the owner's identity key, when an identity-signed `DeviceAuthorization` granting `UpdateProfile` rides in the wire's `signer_auth`. Mechanism owner: [`../architecture/serialization.md`](../architecture/serialization.md) § Delegated authoring; the instantiation and its merge semantics (an ATProto record sets `display_name`/`bio` and preserves every field it cannot express) are owned by [`../behavior/atproto-pds-full.md`](../behavior/atproto-pds-full.md) § F2 detail → *`putRecord`*. Nothing about the owner's own client path changes: it still signs with the identity key, and the nest still holds no secret of the user's.
  - **Onboarding = publish-on-first-edit.** A fresh actor stays `not_found` until it saves the edit form (the SELF shell + others'-view fall back to `handle`/account fields); **no** auto-publish at onboarding.
  - **Federation receive-write is deferred.** A peer nest materializing a *remote* actor's `Profile` row is out of v1 — today inbound `activitypub` `handle_update` caches remote actors in the `remote_actors` table, not `schema='profile'`; only **local** actors need a publish path for the rich-identity header + the others'-profile browse. Adding it later is additive-everywhere (`version-compatibility.md` I4 — a new kind + new content rows raise zero wire/at-rest compat concern; no migration, no data loss).

- **Request contact / accept / block.** Reuse the contact-edge lifecycle owned by `contacts.md` (the shared knock/contacts client) — the profile is one *caller* of that logic, not a second implementation.
  - **Request contact routing (2026-09-25).** `profile-request-contact-button` sends the same signed knock `contacts-add-button` does (`build_knock_payload` → `fauna.inbox.send`), and its `recipient_nest_url` is shared Rust: `fauna_client_profile::knock_recipient_nest_url(actor, profile_body, own_nest_url)` over the profile this open already fetched. ### After an identity succession, the successor RE-PUBLISHES; it does not rewrite

**Ratified 2026-08-23; the admission rule amended 2026-09-19.** A succession re-points the profile row's *ownership*
nest-side (`content.author` is `Succession::Move(Plain)`) but nothing re-authors
its signed bytes, so the row a successor now owns still carries the
predecessor's `actor_id`. Every writer in `fauna-client-profile` fetches its
base with `profile_get(<its own actor id>)` and then preserved that decoded
`actor_id`, so the signing assertion refused — which made the recovery-head
mirror a permanent no-op for the one case it matters most in, and, more
seriously, **failed every ordinary profile edit a successor attempted**. The
rule:

- **A predecessor's base is adopted under the signer; any other base the nest
  serves is refused.** `fauna_client_profile::admit_base` is the one funnel
  every writer decodes its base through. **Amended 2026-09-19:** the 2026-08-23 text adopted *any*
  decoded base, reasoning that "the evidence is the nest's own: it answered
  *this row is yours*". That reasoning is retired. The home nest is exactly the
  party [`../behavior/identity-succession.md`](../behavior/identity-succession.md)
  § The succession statement → *the peer-profile harvest* refuses as an anchor,
  and a writer that re-signs whatever it serves hands that nest the owner's
  identity signature over a `recovery_head` and `nests` of its choosing, which a
  same-nest peer's harvest then seeds as the owner's succession anchor. The
  admission rule:
  1. **An unsigned base is refused.** The legacy bare shape is any host's
     fabrication.
  2. **A signed base is admitted only if it names the signer, or a predecessor
     the device's account registry records the signer succeeding from**
     (`AccountRegistry::predecessors_of` — the device's own record of a
     succession it took part in or restored; a row whose secret the device does
     not hold still counts, since admitting a base needs the link, not the
     key). Anything else — a stranger's genuine signed profile, or one the nest
     minted under an identity of its own — is refused with a typed error: never
     adopted, and never swapped for defaults, because the user's fields are
     unknown then and republishing empty ones is a loss of its own.
  3. **`recovery_head` and `nests` survive only a base the signer signed with
     its own identity key** (`AuthoringOrigin::Direct`). From a predecessor's
     base, or from a delegated one (an external-app edit signed by the
     nest-held authoring sub-key, [`../behavior/atproto-pds-full.md`](../behavior/atproto-pds-full.md)
     § F2 detail), both drop to absent: they are the harvest's anchor and dial
     domain, and neither is ever carried from another key's word. Absent is a
     cache miss that costs peers a chain fetch, never a poisoned anchor, and
     the landed-registration mirror writes both back from the device's own
     state at the next kit landing — or, after a delegated edit, at the
     owner's next sign-in ([`../behavior/identity-succession.md`](../behavior/identity-succession.md)
     § the peer-profile harvest, rule 1, owns that producer).
  4. **Everything else travels with any admitted base** — the display fields,
     `avatar` / `banner`, `admin_nests`, `load_hint` and `inbox_mode`. Nothing
     reads them to decide whom to trust, a delegated `UpdateProfile` grant
     already covers them, and resetting them would itself publish a loss
     (`inbox_mode` would read `Open`).

  A predecessor's base is re-labelled to the signer, so the user-authored
  fields travel with the account and only authorship changes.
- **A successor device with no recorded succession link LEARNS it from the
  landed statement (2026-09-20).** A device's registry can lack the link for
  ordinary reasons: it never held the predecessor's row (any second device —
  adding an account carries no link), or the user removed the retired account,
  which deletes that row. Left there, the inherited profile would have no writer
  on that device — and no writer anywhere once every device is in that state,
  which is a client-unrecoverable state
  ([`../architecture/nest/common.md`](../architecture/nest/common.md) § Client-state
  recoverability) and therefore a bug, not a residual. Two mechanisms close it:
  1. **The link lives on the successor's own registry row**, as well as on the
     predecessor's — the whole chain, nearest hop first — so it survives the
     retired account's removal and is recorded even where the predecessor's row
     never existed. It holds ids, never key material.
  2. **A link the registry lacks is proven, never taken on the nest's word.**
     Once per sign-in the client reads its own stored profile. If it is signed
     by an identity the registry cannot place, the client fetches the public
     succession path forward from that identity
     (`fauna.recovery.succession.lookup`) and accepts it only as an unbroken
     chain that **ends at the signer**, every link's `new_sig` verifying under
     that link's own successor. What it proves is recorded per (1). The
     mirror writer and the edit form's base load run the same proof in-line
     over a base they cannot place, so neither waits on the per-sign-in hop:
     that hop runs in the background, a save could overtake it, and a hop
     that failed would refuse every edit until the next sign-in. The base
     load records what it proves before the form can save.
     The check is **`new_sig`-only by design**: the question is "is this base
     mine", not "was this succession authorized" — a linkless device holds no
     chain head for the predecessor to run the full rule against, and the last
     link is the signer's own signature, which no nest can mint. Earlier links
     are the word of identities the signer's chain already vouched for; the
     weakest of them is a thief who held a predecessor's key, who could already
     sign any profile *as* that predecessor. That base is admitted on the same
     terms either way (rule 3 drops both anchors), so the proof admits nothing
     the recorded link would not. A reply that is broken, reordered, truncated
     or forged proves nothing and the base stays refused.
- **This is not a rewrite of signed history.**
  [`../behavior/succession-aftermath.md`](../behavior/succession-aftermath.md)
  § Re-key scope rules that posts, messages and **profile versions** stay
  attributed to the old actor and are never rewritten. Nothing here re-signs or
  re-attributes stored bytes: the new owner publishes a *new version* of a row
  it already owns, exactly as any edit does. The alternative preserves no
  history — it leaves a live row permanently unwritable by the only identity
  entitled to write it.
- **The defensive assertion stays.** A *hand-assembled* `Profile` naming another
  actor is still a caller bug and `build_profile` still refuses it. Only a
  decoded base is adopted, and only because of where that base came from —
  which is why the writers' docs require `base_body` to be the caller's own
  stored profile.

## Encryption at rest

`docs/goal/architecture/encryption-at-rest.md` owns the at-rest *property* (the plaintext routing floor, the per-mode ceiling, cross-flow, the trust property under compromise); this section owns the profile-specific mechanics. On conflict in that doc's domain, that doc wins.

The federated `Profile` record (`libs/fauna-core::data::Profile`: `display_name`, `bio`, `avatar`, `banner`, `links`, `nests`, `admin_nests`, `load_hint`, `inbox_mode`, `recovery_head`, plus the author signature) is **signed plaintext**, byte-identical on the wire and at rest — there is no storage mode (`encryption-at-rest.md`), so this has no per-box variance. The concrete stored/wire form is the signed `EmbedAsBytes` (envelope + canonical `Profile`); the nest verifies the Ed25519 signature when it ingests a `fauna.profile.set` write, and both the `fauna.profile.get` read and the `activitypub::actor_routes` federation serve decode through one shared signed-only verify helper (§ Where logic lives → *Profile publish/edit*). The profile is the user's identity-as-seen-by-others; every field is intentionally public, and the system treats it the same way it treats a public post. There is no sealed sub-record on the profile entity. `recovery_head` (`Option<ChainHead>`) is an additive mirror of the actor's RecoveryKey registration-chain head — a peer-verifiable cache, not owner-private data — owned by [`../behavior/identity-succession.md`](../behavior/identity-succession.md) § Registration.

**No "private profile fields."** Profile is not the home for owner-private data. Owner-private *profile-adjacent* configuration — notification preferences, bridge settings, blocked/muted lists, theme/language, anything that's the user's own knob and that only the user's own apps act on — lives in the account's **account-state plane** (fleet-only kinds sealed under the account's generation tip; formerly the whole-record `UserConfig` in the `__config` reserved folder, whose rail retired 2026-10-02 — [`../architecture/config-dissolution.md`](../architecture/config-dissolution.md) § The `__config` dissolution schedule → *The kinds*), not on the `Profile` record. See the "User settings" conformance row in `encryption-at-rest.md` for its at-rest property; `docs/goal/architecture/owner-key-material.md` § Path A-sibling-2 for the generation tip. **A setting the nest itself enforces is nest-held plaintext, with no sealed copy** (corrected 2026-10-02 — this paragraph had the sealed plane holding the authoritative `inbox_mode` and the nest mirroring it into the plaintext floor at update time; neither was ever built, and no plane kind holds the field): the inbox mode is the nest's per-actor `inbox_modes` row, written over `fauna.inbox.mode.set` and read by the DM-initiation, knock and federation-delivery gates (owner [`../behavior/direct-messages.md`](../behavior/direct-messages.md) § Reach policy); the spam preferences and the server-side email filter rules are nest rows of the same class. The nest cannot enforce what it cannot read, and a sealed twin would be a second source of truth — the `handle` rule above, applied. The `Profile.inbox_mode` field is the writer-less public mirror § Persistence describes, not the setting.

**Forward-pointer.** If a future profile concept genuinely needs an owner-only sub-record on the profile entity itself (e.g., a private "notes-to-self about my own profile" field, draft profile edits prior to publication), it follows the **drafts pattern** — sealed under the owner's read key, owner's client seals before upload, owner's clients unseal — and earns its own row in the conformance table at that time. Until then there is no such sub-record, and the table reflects that.

**Where the crypto lives.** Nowhere new: `Profile` is signed-and-verified by the existing `libs/fauna-core::data::Profile` Ed25519 signature pipeline (no per-mode branch). The owner-private settings' seal is the account-state plane's (see `owner-key-material.md` § Path A-sibling-2).

The predecessor list comes from `AccountRegistry::predecessors_of`: linux, tui and the kit-mirror paths resolve it in Rust; the FFI edit builders take it as a `predecessors` argument that apple, android and windows fill from their registry's `predecessorsOf`; web's wasm face resolves it from the browser's own registry, so the SPA's call sites are unchanged. Pinned by five `fauna-client-profile` unit tests, each of which fails when its own guard is mutated. **The linkless successor — shared Rust, 2026-09-20**: `AccountEntry::succeeded_from` keeps the chain on the successor's own row (`record_succession` writes it, so it survives `remove` of the retired account; `record_predecessors` merges a proven link; `predecessors_of` answers the union). `fauna_client_profile::proven_predecessors` is the `new_sig`-only chain walk (`SignedIdentitySuccession::verify_new_sig`), `learn_inherited_predecessors` the read-and-lookup hop, and `publish_recovery_head` runs the same proof in-line. The per-sign-in seam is `fauna_client_recovery::ceremony::learn_succession_link`, called from tui's and linux's post-auth hooks and from `fauna-ffi`'s `run_succession_aftermath` ahead of its gate (so apple, android and windows need no app change); web's `run_aftermath_web` does the same two steps over the browser registry. Pinned in `fauna-client-profile` (the linkless mirror written red-first, the hostile-nest statement, multi-hop, broken/forged chains, zero lookups over a placeable base) and `fauna-client-accounts` (the link survives removal, an absent-row succession, the proven merge). **The edit form's in-line proof — all 7 apps, 2026-09-23:** `fauna_client_profile::fetch_own_profile_base` returns the base together with what it proves, and `fauna_client_recovery::ceremony::load_profile_edit_base` records that before handing the base back. Its body, `ceremony::read_own_profile_learning_link`, is wasm-clean and generic over the transport, so it is also the body of every sign-in hop (native `learn_succession_link`, web's `run_aftermath_web`). tui and linux call it directly; apple, android and windows through `FfiProfileClient::load_edit_base` over the registry they hand `run_succession_aftermath`; web through the wasm `loadProfileEditBase` face. Wired in all 7 apps: apple's `ProfileEditVM.open` reads through the new `APIClient.loadProfileEditBase`, pinned by `ProfileEditVMTests`. windows' `ProfileViewModel.OpenEditFormAsync` reads through the new `NestRpcClient.LoadProfileEditBaseAsync` (a thin pass-through over `FfiProfileClient.LoadEditBase`, mirroring `ProfileGetAsync`'s own shape), pinned by `ProfileViewModelTests`. Pinned in `fauna-client-recovery` (`the_edit_base_load_records_the_link_its_save_then_reads`, plus the first-publish and failed-read answers). End to end: `tests/e2e-unified/tests/test_profile_linkless_successor.py` — `test_a_device_that_never_held_the_predecessor_saves_a_profile_edit` on tui, linux, web and windows (a second device with no link learns it and saves) and `test_a_profile_edit_still_saves_after_the_retired_account_is_removed` on tui, linux, web and windows (the link survives removing the retired account).
