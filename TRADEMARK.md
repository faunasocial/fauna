# Fauna trademark policy

Fauna is free software. Anyone may use, study, change and redistribute the
code under its licences (Apache-2.0 or MIT, at your option; see
`LICENSE-APACHE` and `LICENSE-MIT`). The **names and artwork** that identify
the project are a separate matter: they tell people that what they are
installing comes from the Fauna project, and this policy exists so that they
keep meaning that.

This policy covers only the trademarks. It does not restrict, and cannot be
read as restricting, anything the code licences allow you to do with the
code.

Together, "the marks". Whatever copyright may or may not subsist in the
artwork, this policy governs its use as an identifier of the project.

## The rule in one sentence

You may say that something *is* Fauna when it is, and that it *works with*
Fauna when it does; you may not present something as Fauna when it is
something else, or suggest an affiliation with or endorsement by the project
that does not exist.

## What you may do without asking

1. **Redistribute the official software unchanged.** Copy, mirror, host and
   hand out the official source and the official builds, under their own
   names, in any quantity.

2. **Package it for a platform or a distribution.** A package built from
   unmodified source may keep the name if the only changes are the ones
   packaging needs: build settings and flags, install paths, dependency
   versions, packaging metadata, platform integration files, and security
   fixes taken from upstream. State who made the package.

3. **Run it.** Anyone who runs Fauna — a nest for a family, a club or a
   company, or the apps on their devices — may call it what it is: a Fauna
   nest, the Fauna app.

4. **Fork it in order to contribute.** A branch or fork whose purpose is to
   develop changes intended for the project may keep the name while that
   work is in progress, as long as it is not marketed, published as a
   product, or distributed to end users as a release.

5. **Describe your own work accurately.** Statements of fact are free:
   "built on Fauna", "compatible with Fauna", "a client for Fauna",
   "connects to Fauna nests", "hosting for Fauna". Put your own name first
   and the marks second, in plain text rather than in the Fauna artwork, and
   do not make the marks the most prominent element of your name, page or
   listing.

6. **Talk about it.** Use the names in articles, reviews, talks, teaching
   material, documentation, research, bug reports and social media, in any
   language, to refer to the project.

## What needs your own name instead

1. **A modified version.** If you distribute a version whose changes go
   beyond the packaging exceptions above, give it its own name and identity.
   Remove the marks from the product name, the app names and icons, the
   package and bundle identifiers, the store listings, the support and
   update links, and the user-visible text; do not ship the Fauna artwork as
   your icons or branding. Your version may still say that it is *based on*
   or *derived from* Fauna. This is the ordinary free-software arrangement:
   the code is yours to change; the identity is not.

2. **A confusingly similar name.** Names that fold a mark into another name
   — "FaunaSomething", "Something-Fauna", "Fauna Social Plus", a domain,
   a company or a product name that a casual observer could take for the
   project or an official part of it — need written permission whether or
   not the code is modified.

3. **Anything that implies official status.** Do not present a build,
   service, event, account, domain or publication as official, affiliated
   with or endorsed by the project unless it is. "Official" means published
   by the association through its own release channels.

4. **The artwork as your brand.** The Fauna artwork identifies the project.
   Do not use it, or artwork designed to resemble it, as the icon, logo or
   branding of anything that is not the official software.

5. **Selling merchandise** carrying the marks. Making a few items for
   yourself or a local community, not for sale, is fine.

## Asking for permission

Where this policy says written permission is needed, write to
**hello@fauna.social**. Say what you want to do and how the marks would
appear. Permission, when given, is given in writing, may carry conditions,
and may be withdrawn if the conditions stop being met. Silence is not
permission.

## Scope and enforcement

- This policy is issued by Fauna Social, which holds the marks. It is
  separate from the code licences: the copyright in the code stays with its
  contributors, who license it to everyone under the terms above, and this
  policy takes nothing from that grant and adds nothing to it. The
  Apache-2.0 licence's own trademark clause (section 6) and this policy say
  the same thing; this policy applies whichever licence you choose.
- The association may update this policy. The current version is the one at
  the root of the project's source repository.
- Failure to act on a use that this policy does not permit is not a waiver.

## Questions

If you are unsure whether a planned use is covered, ask at
hello@fauna.social before you rely on it — the answer is usually quick, and
usually yes.
